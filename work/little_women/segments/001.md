A procession of titles and little pictures: "Christmas won't be Christmas without any presents"; Beth puts slippers to warm; the girls sing—"Merry Christmas"; theatricals and neighbors; "Face to face with the Laurence boy"; talk over the fence; schoolroom scrapes—"He took her by the ear! by the ear!"; plans for "Being neighborly." Parties and partners, papered locks, drawing lessons; "We'll work like bees"; "Beth was post-mistress"; mischiefs and repentances—"Please give these to your mother"; Mr. Laurence's kindness; "O sir, they do care very much." Letters and losses—"It didn't stir, and I knew it was dead"; fever and watchings; "Amy's Will"; patient hands, "Gently stroking her head as her mother used to do"; slow recovery—"Beth was soon able to lie on the study sofa all day"; and farther prospects—"The Jungfrau." New ventures: "The First Wedding"; "Artistic Attempts"; "Literary Lessons"; "Domestic Experiences"; "Calls"; bouquets and hansom-cabs; foreign scenes, odd English, and homely thrift—"Thou shalt haf thy Bhaer"; fireside courage—"Jo stuffed the whole bundle into the stove"; partings and returns—"O Jo, can't you?" Reconciliations and harvest—"Under the umbrella"; "Harvest time"; children’s voices—"Me loves evvybody"; and "Leaving Mrs. March and her daughters under the festival tree."
