In his suite at Claridge's, Kramenin was informed that a Mr. Julius P. Hersheimmer was below. The name promised a millionaire several times over.

"Bring him up."

Julius was escorted in. "Monsieur Kramenin? I've got some very important business I'd like to talk over with you, if I can see you alone."

The secretary was dismissed. Julius watched him go, closed the door, and came back to the centre of the room.

"Now, Mr. Hersheimmer, perhaps you will be so kind as to come to the point."

"I guess that won't take a minute." Then, with an abrupt change of manner: "Hands up — or I shoot."

Kramenin stared into the big automatic, then with almost comical haste flung up his hands. In that instant Julius had taken his measure: an abject physical coward — the rest would be easy.

"This is an outrage!" cried Kramenin in a high hysterical voice. "Do you mean to kill me?"

"Not if you keep your voice down. I'm not proposing to kill you this trip — that is, if you're reasonable."

"What do you want? Money?"

"No. I want Jane Finn."

"Jane Finn? I — never heard of her."

"You're a darned liar. And Little Willie here is just hopping mad to go off."

The Russian wilted. "Granted I do know who you mean — what of it?"

"You will tell me where she is to be found."

"I daren't. Mr. Brown — he is a man to fear. I have seen him. Spoken to him face to face. I did not know it until afterwards. He was one of a crowd. He knows everything, and his vengeance is swift. Even I — Kramenin! — would not be exempt."

"He'll never know," said Julius. He raised the revolver. "I'm going to count five. One — two — three — four—"

"Do not shoot! I will do all you wish. The girl is at Gatehouse, in Kent. Astley Priors, the place is called. The little fool has lost her memory, curse her."

"And the other girl, the one you decoyed away over a week ago?"

"She's there too."

"Isn't it all panning out beautifully? And a lovely night for the run."

"What run?"

"Down to Gatehouse. I'm not such a kid as to leave you here to ring up your friends."

The Russian was hustled into a coat and down to the waiting car. Julius warned him that the chauffeur — a naval man whose brother had been murdered by Bolsheviks — would not assist any escape. To pass the time on the run, Julius narrated, with picturesque inaccuracy, a tale of a man he had once shot in Arizona, a tough from 'Frisco, and an episode in the Rockies. By the end of the hour, Kramenin was more dead than alive.

The car swept up the drive of Astley Priors.

"Turn the car first. Keep the engine going, and be ready to scoot like hell."

The front door opened. Kramenin felt the muzzle pressed against his ribs.

"It is I — Kramenin," he managed. "Bring down the girl at once. There is no time to lose."

Whittington came down the steps, astonished. "You! What's up? Surely you know the plan—"

"We have been betrayed. The girl! And at once. The other little fool had better come too."

"You have orders — from *him?*"

"Naturally. Hurry."

Whittington turned and ran back into the house. The agonizing minutes went by. Then two figures hastily huddled in cloaks were hustled into the car. The smaller resisted; Whittington shoved her in unceremoniously. As Julius leaned forward, the light from the open door lit his face. A man on the steps gave a startled exclamation.

"Get a move on!" shouted Julius.

The car bounded away. A flash, a report — the bullet just missed the taller girl by an inch.

"Get down, Jane!" Julius cried, thrusting her flat. He took aim and fired back. "Hit him — not killed."

"Where's Tommy?" cried Tuppence. "And who's this?"

"Tommy's making tracks for the Argentine. He thought you'd turned up your toes. May I present Monsieur Kramenin? I persuaded him to come on the trip for his health."

"But what made them let us go?"

"Monsieur Kramenin asked them so prettily they couldn't refuse."

The Russian burst out: "Curse you! They know now that I betrayed them. My life won't be safe an hour in this country."

"I'd advise you to make tracks for Russia right away. Pull up — the gentleman's not making the return trip."

Before the car had halted the Russian had swung out and disappeared into the night.

"How did you 'persuade' him?" asked the smaller girl — the first time she'd spoken.

Julius tapped his revolver. "Little Willie here takes the credit."

"Splendid!" The colour surged into her face.

"Annette and I didn't know what was going to happen," said Tuppence. "We thought it was lambs to the slaughter."

"Annette," said Julius. His mind seemed to be adjusting itself to a new idea.

"It's her name."

"Shucks. She may think it's her name, because her memory's gone, poor kid. But it's the one real and original Jane Finn we've got here."

"What?"

A bullet embedded itself in the upholstery just behind Tuppence's head.

"Down! It's an ambush." The car leapt forward; three more shots went wide. Julius raised his hand to his cheek.

"You are hurt?" said Annette quickly.

"Only a scratch."

The girl sprang up. "Let me out! It is me they're after. I'm the one they want. You shall not lose your lives because of me."

Julius caught her by both arms. She had spoken with no trace of foreign accent.

"Sit down, kid. I guess there's nothing wrong with your memory. Been fooling them all the time, eh?"

She nodded, and burst into tears.

"You're from home," she said indistinctly. "I can tell by your voice. It makes me homesick."

"Sure I'm from home. I'm your cousin — Julius Hersheimmer. I came over to Europe on purpose to find you — and a pretty dance you've led me."

The car slackened. "Cross-roads here, sir. I'm not sure of the way."

As it slowed, a figure climbed suddenly over the back, and plunged head first into the midst of them.

"Sorry," said Tommy, extricating himself. "Was in the bushes by the drive. Hung on behind. Now then, you girls, get out. There's a station up that road. Train due in three minutes."

"What the devil are you driving at?" demanded Julius. "You can't let those girls go off alone."

"You and I aren't going to leave the car. Only the girls. Get out at once, Tuppence. Take her with you. Take the train to London. Go straight to Sir James Peel Edgerton."

"Darn you! Jane, you stay where you are."

With a sudden swift movement Tommy snatched the revolver from Julius's hand, and levelled it at him.

"Now will you believe I'm in earnest? Get out, both of you, or I'll shoot."

Tuppence sprang out, dragging the unwilling Jane after her. "Come on. If Tommy's sure — he's sure."

They started running.

"What the hell—" Julius's pent-up rage burst forth.

"Dry up. I want a few words with you, Mr. Julius Hersheimmer."
