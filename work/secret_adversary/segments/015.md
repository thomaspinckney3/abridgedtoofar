A CONSULTATION

Nothing was more bewildering to Tuppence than the ease with which everything was arranged through Sir James's skilful handling. The doctor accepted that Mrs. Vandemeyer had taken an accidental overdose of chloral and doubted whether an inquest would be necessary. A nurse arrived to take charge, and they left the ill-omened building.

"And what now?" asked Julius, with a gesture of despair.

"There is still the chance that Dr. Hall may be able to tell us something," said Sir James. "I told you he is staying at the Métropole. I suggest we call upon him after a bath and breakfast."

A little after eleven they drew up before the Métropole. The little doctor came hurrying.

"Ah, my young friend of the tree episode! Ankle all right, eh?"

"Cured owing to your skilful treatment, doc."

"Can we have a word with you in private?" asked Sir James.

He led them to a quiet room, and Sir James came at once to the point.

"Dr. Hall, I am very anxious to find a young lady who has been at one time in your establishment at Bournemouth. She would almost certainly have been sent to you under an assumed name. Are you acquainted with a Mrs. Vandemeyer?"

"Mrs. Vandemeyer, of 20 South Audley Mansions? I know her slightly."

"You are not aware that she is dead? She took an overdose of chloral last night."

"Dear, dear, I had no idea. Very sad."

"Is it not a fact that she committed a young relative of hers to your charge?"

Julius leaned forward eagerly.

"That is the case," said the doctor. "She came to me in June or July of 1915, under the name of Janet Vandemeyer. I understood her to be a niece. Mrs. Vandemeyer told me the girl had been on the _Lusitania_ when that ill-fated ship was sunk, and had suffered a severe shock in consequence."

"We're on the right track, I think?" Sir James said.

"As I said before, I'm a mutt!" returned Julius.

"You spoke of wanting a statement from her," said the doctor. "Supposing she is not able to give one? She is perfectly sane—but if you want a statement concerning any events prior to May 7, 1915, she will not be able to give it to you. Janet Vandemeyer is suffering from a complete loss of memory."

_"What?"_

"Quite so. After May 7, 1915, her memory is as good as yours or mine. Before that, nothing. She did not know her own name, or where she had come from. She couldn't even speak her own tongue."

"Sooner or later," he went on, "the memory will return—as suddenly as it went. But in all probability the girl will have entirely forgotten the intervening period, and take up life where she left off—at the sinking of the _Lusitania_."

"And when do you expect this to happen?"

The doctor shrugged. "Sometimes a matter of months, sometimes as long as twenty years. Sometimes another shock does the trick. One restores what the other took away."

"Another shock, eh?" said Julius thoughtfully. He fell into a brown study, then suddenly hit the table such a resounding bang that everyone jumped.

"I've got it! Doc, what about your medical opinion on this. Say Jane was to cross the herring pond again, and the same thing was to happen—the submarine, the sinking ship, every one taking to the boats. Wouldn't that give a mighty big bump to her subconscious self, and start it functioning again?"

"In my own opinion, it would be successful. Unfortunately there is no chance of the conditions repeating themselves."

"Not by nature, doc. But I'm talking about art. Hire a liner, hire some passengers, hire a submarine—it's done every day in the States for the movies. By the time she's got a life-belt on her, with a well-drilled lot of artistes doing the hysterical stunt on deck—why, she ought to be right back where she was in May, 1915."

"But the expense, my dear sir!" Dr. Hall's voice rose. "It will be _colossal!_"

"Money doesn't worry me any," explained Julius simply.

Dr. Hall turned an appealing face to Sir James, who smiled slightly. "Mr. Hersheimmer is very well off—very well off indeed."

The doctor's glance came back to Julius with a new deference.

"Very remarkable plan. The movies—of course! And you really mean to carry it out?"

"You bet your bottom dollar I do. Can we get on the long distance to your place right away, and ask them to send her up?"

The doctor stared. "I beg your pardon. I thought you understood. Miss Vandemeyer is no longer under my care."
