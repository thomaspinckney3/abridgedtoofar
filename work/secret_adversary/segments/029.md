**AND AFTER**

At a celebratory supper at the Savoy, Mr. Carter raised a glass to the Joint Venture and read brief passages from Sir James's notebook—revealing a man who had chosen absolute power outside the law and worn borrowed personalities. The treaty had been burnt; the conspiracy had collapsed.

In the Rolls-Royce returning to the Ritz, Julius spoke abruptly.

"Jane—I want you to marry me. I've loved you from the moment I set eyes on your photo. You shall take your own time, but I want the right to look after you."

"That's what I want," said Jane wistfully. "Some one who'll be good to me. Oh, you don't know how lonesome I feel."

A small hand slipped into his.

"I love you now, Julius," said Jane Finn. "I loved you that first moment in the car when the bullet grazed your cheek."

In another taxi, the Young Adventurers sat bolt upright, very stiff and ill at ease. A terrible constraint had settled down between them. They were tongue-tied—paralysed. All the old _camaraderie_ was gone.

At last Tuppence made a desperate effort. "Rather fun, wasn't it?"

"Rather."

"I like Julius."

Tommy was suddenly galvanized. "You're not going to marry him, do you hear? I forbid it."

"Oh!" said Tuppence meekly.

"Absolutely, you understand."

"He doesn't want to marry me—he really only asked me out of kindness. He's head over ears in love with Jane. I expect he's proposing to her now."

"She'll do for him very nicely," said Tommy condescendingly.

"But I suppose you prefer sterling worth," said Tuppence demurely.

"I—oh, dash it all, Tuppence, you know!"

Tuppence stole a glance at him sideways. "It has been fun, hasn't it, Tommy? I do hope we shall have lots more adventures."

"You're insatiable. I've had quite enough adventures for the present."

"Well, shopping is almost as good," said Tuppence dreamily. "Think of buying old furniture, and bright carpets, and futurist silk curtains, and a polished dining-table, and a divan with lots of cushions."

"Hold hard. What's all this for?"

"Possibly a house—but I think a flat."

"Whose flat?"

"You think I mind saying it, but I don't in the least. _Ours_, so there!"

"You darling!" cried Tommy, his arms tightly round her. "I was determined to make you say it."

The taxi proceeded round the north side of Regent's Park.

"You haven't really proposed now," pointed out Tuppence. "Not what our grandmothers would call a proposal. But after listening to a rotten one like Julius's, I'm inclined to let you off."

"You won't be able to get out of marrying me, so don't you think it."

"Marriage is called all sorts of things—a haven, a refuge, a crowning glory, a state of bondage. But do you know what I think it is?"

"What?"

"A sport!"

"And a damned good sport too," said Tommy.
