As the Lusitania sinks in 1915, a dying courier entrusts American girl Jane Finn with secret Allied papers.  
Years later in London, cash-strapped friends Tuppence Cowley and Tommy Beresford launch “The Young Adventurers, Ltd.” and hear rumors of Jane Finn.  
Their search draws out the menacing Mr. Whittington and the unseen criminal overlord “Mr. Brown,” who will kill to get the treaty back.  
Tuppence infiltrates Mrs. Vandemeyer’s household while Tommy traces spies to a Soho hideout, is captured, and is mysteriously aided by a French girl, Annette.  
They team up with wealthy American Julius P. Hersheimmer, claiming to be Jane’s cousin, and with eminent lawyer Sir James Peel Edgerton, while British spymaster Mr. Carter explains the treaty’s explosive stakes.  
Clues point to radical agitators and foreign agents all orchestrated by Mr. Brown, with Jane’s amnesia masking the truth.  
Mrs. Vandemeyer dies suddenly before she can confess, proving Brown’s lethal reach and deepening the mystery.  
Tommy escapes, the trio follow leads to a nursing home, and at last find Jane alive but memoryless and under guard.  
Her memory returns: she hid the treaty behind a Marguerite picture in the Soho room where Tommy was held.  
A first recovery yields only a taunting “With the compliments of Mr. Brown,” showing the mastermind is always a step ahead.  
A fake telegram lures Tuppence into danger, then a melodramatic “rescue” suggests an inside plot, and the hunters race back to Soho.  
They find the real papers—but Sir James reveals himself as Mr. Brown, the double-dealing genius who has used friend and foe alike.  
Overpowered by Tommy and Julius, Sir James evades justice by killing himself with poison from a signet ring.  
With Brown dead and his records seized, the conspiracy collapses, the general strike fizzles, and Jane is vindicated.  
Julius wins Jane’s heart, Tommy and Tuppence confess their love and plan marriage, and the Young Adventurers’ first great case ends in triumph.
