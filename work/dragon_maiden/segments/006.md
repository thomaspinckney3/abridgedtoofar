It was forty-five minutes before I got to my apartment building. Night beat me to the stoop, and with it came shadow and darkness. An alley separated my building from its neighbor.

I paused at the alley. Had I heard something clattering in there? Hulking shadows of trashcans stared back. Nothing moved. I shrugged and kept going.

My keys rattled in my hand—then I froze. A rattle had mimicked my own. Something flickered in the corner of my sight, but disappeared before I could catch it. I fumbled, shoved the key in the lock, and fell inside.

"Easy there, Miriam," I whispered. "Just your imagination."

Still, I couldn't shake the feeling of being watched. I hurried up to my third-floor apartment, tossed my purse on the table, and plopped into my favorite—and only—recliner.

"Now this is the way to live," I murmured. "Just me and my chair."

I noticed a sweet smell that shouldn't have been there. None of my incense candles were lit. I tried to rise. My arms wouldn't pull me forward.

The feeling of being watched returned. I glanced at the window to the fire escape. Three figures stood on the other side of the glass. I gasped, but the scream stuck in my throat.

One knelt and fidgeted with the sill. The window popped up; he drew it to the top and slipped inside. The other two followed.

Two were burly men in ragged overcoats. They were followed by a familiar face. Blake.

The pair strode over. One grabbed my legs, the other pulled a rope from his coat, and together they bound me. I couldn't fight, couldn't manage more than a garbled whisper. Blake stood by my head and studied my face.

"What a lucky break your friend introduced me to you today. You're just in time to join a few other girls on an exciting journey."

"With all expenses paid," one of the men joined in.

Blake chuckled. "Come, come. It's an honor for this young woman to join in this age-old ritual." His eyes flickered back to me. "She may even be good enough to be his consort."

"She ain't pretty enough."

"Beauty isn't everything, and I think this one might have something special." He leaned close and reached into his shirt collar. My eyes widened as he peeled away the fake flesh that clung to his brown skin. His nostrils bulged like a pig's, his teeth yellow and sharp as fangs. He was a monster. "—she saw through my disguise."

I wanted to run. All I could manage was a shudder.

"Stop fooling around," one of the men insisted.

Blake tucked the mask back in. "All right, but don't damage the merchandise." He pulled a cloth from his pocket, and a stench of rotten plants attacked my nostrils. "I'll take care of that now. It'll prove whether she's one of them or not, too."

He shoved the cloth over my mouth and nose. Tears pooled in my eyes as the room began to spin.

In a moment I was knocked clean out.
