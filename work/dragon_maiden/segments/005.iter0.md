“Weekend at last!”

I stretched as the fifty-floor herd poured onto the sidewalk, a tide of tired bodies surging toward dinner and couches. I only had the dinner part—pizza—but the crowd still reminded me how ordinary my life was.

“Hey, Mir!” Heather waved me over. Beside her stood the recent transfer everyone whispered about—a cute guy with slick confidence and eyes a shade too dark for comfort.

“This is the sexy woman I’ve been telling you about, Blake,” Heather announced.

He offered his hand. It was clammy, like his skin didn’t quite fit. “Nice to meet you.”

“Likewise,” I said, already regretting the forced meet-cute. “Don’t let her talk about my love life. It’s practically non-existent.”

Heather huffed. “Do you both have to be so old-fashioned with the handshake? I’m trying to hook you up here.”

Blake laughed. “I don’t think this is going to work, Heth. She’s on to me.” He gave me a look that felt like a test, then waved and slipped into the river of commuters.

I watched him go. “This is the third time this month, and it’s only the seventh day.”

“I’m trying to get you a good man before you fossilize,” Heather said, then studied my frown. “He wasn’t that bad. A little strange, but who isn’t?”

“Strange isn’t a synonym for eligible,” I muttered, already drifting down the street.

“Hey! Wait up!” She caught my arm and brightened. “Food before you hole up all weekend?”

“Only if you’re buying.”

“Done. But I expect a dance out of it.”

“You know I hate that.”

“Which is why I’m insisting.” She flagged a taxi. “Practice for the wedding. The bride’s gotta dance.”

“You’re impossible,” I sighed as we slid in.

“To the Bar Room,” she told the driver. “And step on it.”

The city blurred past in colored swaths. “I was thinking of a hike with some girls,” she said. “You should come.”

“I don’t fit in,” I admitted. “Like I’m always an outsider.”

“Then stop saying no. Live a little, Miriam, before the whole life is gone.”

Lights smeared against the glass. “I just feel like I’m supposed to wait. Like my time will come.”

The driver glanced up. “Ladies, I think someone’s following you.”

Heather twisted to the back window, grinning like a game-show contestant. “Can you lose them?”

“For extra,” he said, already turning.

“Wait—” The cab slung around a corner, the world tilting. Heather whooped. I braced and tried not to throw up.

It didn’t last long. A few sharp turns, a narrow alley, the smell of damp trash. We tumbled out behind a dumpster at an intersection while the cab shot away with our cash. A red car howled past, chasing his taillights.

“Wow,” Heather panted. “That was close.”

I staggered along the alley, aiming for the streetlights and the bus stop that would take me home. “I’m going to find my stomach,” I said. “Maybe call you tomorrow about bar-hopping.”

“Fine!” she shouted, ankles splashing through a puddle. “If you don’t want my help, then go find your own guy!”

I waved without turning around. It wasn’t a bad suggestion, if Fate hadn’t already filed other plans.
