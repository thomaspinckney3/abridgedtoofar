CHAPTER 1

“Weekend at last!”

I stretched as the fifty-floor building emptied, a herd of tired humanity spilling onto the bright, busy sidewalk. Laughter, office quips, a wave of plans. I only had the dinner part, but pizza awaited me. Still, being among such a large crowd of my fellow humans was a bitter reminder of how normal my boring life was.

“Hey, Mir!” Heather waved me over—beside her, Blake, the cute transfer I’d seen around. My heart sank, but I smiled anyway.

“This is the sexy woman I’ve been telling you about, Blake,” she announced.

He looked me over. Something dark flickered in his eyes. He held out his hand. “Nice to meet you.” I shook it. His fingers were clammy like his skin didn’t quite fit.

“Likewise, and don’t let Heather tell you about my love life. It’s practically non-existent.”

Heather rolled her eyes. “I’m trying to hook you up here.”

Blake laughed. “I don’t think this is going to work, Heth. She’s on to me.” He shook his head, waved, and walked off.

“This is the third time this month, and it’s only the seventh day,” I told her.

She sighed and shrugged. “I’m just trying to get you hooked up. You’re too nice not to deserve a good man and reproduce.”

“Then stop trying to help me. The guys you keep showing me are getting creepier.”

“A little strange, yeah, but who isn’t?” She hurried after me as I escaped down the sidewalk. “Wanna get a bite before you hole up all weekend?”

“Only if you’re buying.”

“Fine, but I expect a dance.”

“You know I hate that.”

“The bride’s gotta dance at her wedding,” she sing-songed, already stepping off the curb. “Taxi!”

We slid into the back seat. “To the Bar Room, and step on it. We need to get drunk,” she told the driver.

He grinned. “Yes, ma’am.”

Heather leaned back. “I was thinking of a hike with a few girls one weekend. Wanna come?”

“I’m not much for nature walks.”

“It’s the society, isn’t it?”

I winced. “It’s not that. It’s just—well, I just don’t feel like I fit in. Like I’m an outsider or something, you know what I mean?”

Heather shook her head. “No, and I won’t take ‘no’ for an answer the next time I ask you to go with me on an adventure. You need to live a little, Miriam, before your whole life is gone.”

I glanced out the window and sighed. The world sped by us as a shadow play of colorful lights and figures. “I know. I… I just feel like I’m supposed to wait for something. Like my time will come.”

“Hey, ladies,” the driver interrupted, peering at his mirror. “I think someone’s following you.”

We looked back. A red car hung behind us.

“You sure it’s not for you?” Heather asked.

“I ain’t picked up no one but you in the last half hour.”

Heather clapped. “You think you can lose ‘em?”

“No problem, but for the risk it’ll be extra.”

“I’ll take that risk!”

“Wait—ah!” The driver cut hard into a side street, hurling me into Heather’s arms.

“Isn’t this great?” she yelled.

“Yeah, wonderful,” I managed, extracting myself. “It’s going to be great sitting in jail, too.”

“This is living!” she crowed.

“This isn’t living! This is the preliminaries before death!”

We tore through another alley, bounced over ruts, skidded ninety degrees. “Just another block, misses!” the driver shouted.

“Stop there,” Heather ordered, pointing at the next cross-alley. “We’ll get out, then book it like we’re still aboard.”

“What about my fare?”

She flung bills onto the seat. “That should cover it.”

He braked hard. Seat belts dug into us; we slid to a stop mid-intersection. Heather yanked mine free, shoved open the door, and pulled me into the shadows. “Thanks for the fun!”

We ducked behind a dumpster as the red car screamed past and chased our former taxi.

“Wow, that was close,” Heather panted.

I clung to the dumpster, stomach in knots. I staggered back the way we’d come.

“Hey, wait! Where are you going?”

“Home.”

“But what about finding a guy at the bar?”

“I need to find my stomach back here first,” I quipped. “Then maybe I’ll call you tomorrow about bar-hopping.”

Heather stamped into a puddle, spraying herself. “Fine! If you don’t want my help, then go and find your own guy!”

I waved without looking back. It wasn’t such a bad suggestion if Fate hadn’t had other plans.
