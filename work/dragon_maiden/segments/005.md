"Weekend at last!"

That was my cry as I stretched my arms above my head. Around me throngs of coworkers spilled from the fifty-floor office building onto the sidewalk—a tired, eager humanity heading home to family and dinner.

"Amen!" one called out.

"Anybody seeing the game?" another asked.

"I'm just seeing my eyelids," a hefty guy quipped.

That got a laugh that extinguished much of the exhaustion on a lot of faces. I only had the dinner part, but pizza awaited me. Still, being among such a large crowd of my fellow humans was a bitter reminder of how normal my boring life was.

"Hey, Mir!" My friend Heather waved. Beside her stood a cute guy I'd seen around the office, a recent transfer. My heart sank. Heather jerked her head toward me. "This is the sexy woman I've been telling you about, Blake."

Blake looked me over. There was something dark about his eyes I didn't like. He held out his hand. "Nice to meet you."

I shook it. His fingers were clammy, like his skin didn't quite fit. "Likewise, and don't let Heather tell you about my love life. It's practically non-existent."

Blake laughed. "I don't think this is going to work, Heth. She's on to me."

Heather blinked. "On to you?"

"It's nothing. Anyway, nice to have met you." He waved and walked off.

I turned to my friend. "This is the third time this month, and it's only the seventh day."

She sighed. "I'm just trying to get you hooked up. You're too nice not to deserve a good man and reproduce."

I glanced at Blake's retreating back. "Then stop trying to help me. The guys you keep showing me are getting creepier."

"I don't think he was that bad. A little strange, yeah, but who isn't?" She caught up as I walked off. "Wanna get a bite before you hole yourself in your apartment for the weekend?"

"Only if you're buying."

Heather rolled her eyes. "Fine, fine, but I expect to get a dance out of this."

"You know I hate that."

"The only way you'll dance is with someone you know, and I'll probably be the only one you know." She stepped into the street and raised her hand. "Taxi!"

A cab pulled up. We slipped inside.

"I was thinking of going hiking one of these weekends with a few other girls. Wanna come?"

I shook my head. "I'm not much for nature walks."

She frowned. "It's the society, isn't it?"

I winced. "It's not that. It's just—I don't feel like I fit in. Like I'm an outsider, you know what I mean?"

Heather shook her head. "No, and I won't take 'no' for an answer next time. You need to live a little, Miriam, before your whole life is gone."

I glanced out the window and sighed. The world sped by as a shadow play of colorful lights and figures. "I know. I… I just feel like I'm supposed to wait for something. Like my time will come."

The driver's voice cut in. "I think someone's following you—that red car." Heather whipped around and squealed; the driver took a hard corner into an alley and sped up. I clutched the oh-shit handle. After a few hair-raising turns Heather tossed bills into the front seat and yanked me out at an intersection. We crouched behind a dumpster as the red car zoomed past, still chasing our former taxi.

My stomach was in knots. I staggered past Heather toward home.

"Hey! Where are you going?"

"I need to find my stomach back here first."

"Fine!" she called. "If you don't want my help, then go and find your own guy!"

I waved without looking back. It wasn't such a bad suggestion, if Fate hadn't had other plans.
