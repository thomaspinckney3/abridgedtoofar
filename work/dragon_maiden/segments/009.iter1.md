CHAPTER 5

The guards flanked me back up a narrower stair to a corridor lined with small doors. A room shy of the end, they turned me into a spacious chamber with a hearth, a four-poster heaped in plush sheets, a dresser, and three wide windows that swung inward. Two panes were open, letting in the chirp of crickets and the night air.

An elderly woman stood at the bed’s foot and turned as we entered. I recognized her from the stables. “Is this to be His Lordship’s Maiden?” she asked. The guards bowed. “Yes, Maid Darda.” She had my hands freed and dismissed them to wait outside.

She circled me, studying. “Take a picture. It’ll last longer,” I snapped. The corners of her mouth lifted. “There are no cameras here, nor the electricity to use such devices.” I frowned. “So, is this place in the past?” “It is, but not in the way you believe,” she said. “The time in this realm is in sync with your world, but the technology hasn’t advanced near as far.” I arched a brow. “So I’m in a different dimension?” “Yes.”

I drifted to a window and looked out over fields sloping to a gap in the trees. “So how’d the other girls and I get here?” “You would do well to forget that life and think of your new one here,” she scolded. I turned and glared. “And what’s that supposed to mean?” “You have been chosen for a very honorable position,” she said evenly. “Even if he does not choose you as a mate, you will be given a job for life as a servant in the household.” “Is that supposed to be a good thing?” She only raised an eyebrow. “You are fatigued from your long journey. Perhaps you should lie down for a spell.”

“I’m not going to ‘lie down for a spell’ or do anything that’ll make me comfortable,” I told her. “This place isn’t my home, and never will be.” Her lips twitched. “Your thoughts are easy to read. I’m afraid escape is possible from the Castle, but not from the woods. You would not get far without better means of travel than your feet. The communications within the castle are closely guarded, so do not look to the other servants for help. As for outsiders, the nearest human habitation apart from the small village around the castle is over fifteen miles of rough road.”

I gripped the sill rather than reveal whatever plan I didn’t yet have. She stepped close and set a shrunken hand on my shoulder. “Forgive me for this.” “Forgive you for—ah!” Heat knifed beneath her palm. I jerked away, stumbling, and she watched me with sorrowful eyes. “The pain will soon subside.”

I yanked down my collar. A black scorch marked my skin in the crest I’d seen on the guards’ armor. “Are you insane? What did you do to me?” “Merely the Marking. All Maidens must be marked,” she said. “The Marking is to protect you from other, less scrupulous lords, though the true bonding will take a few hours to complete.” She opened the door. “If you have no other questions, then I will bid you good evening. Tomorrow I will ensure food is brought to you at the appropriate times as you adjust to your new eating schedule.” “How do you expect me to eat when you’re branding me all the time?” “The Marking is only done once. As for the meals, the dragon lords are regularly daytime creatures, but during the Marking their schedule will be rather erratic. In time you will learn their peculiarities.” “Not likely…” “Pardon?” “I didn’t say anything.” She bowed. “Sleep well.” The lock clicked after her.

I pulled my sleeve down over the ache and rubbed the bruise. They were mad—lords and ladies, damsels in distress, “dragon lords” and “Maidens.” I had to get out, find the authorities, free the others.

I paced the room for exits. The door was a guess. The windows were certainty. I leaned out. The drop was sixty feet to hard ground—too far to jump, not too far to climb. The bed’s sheets would be my rope.

I worked knots. In half an hour I had a length of linen. Nothing moved outside but a cool breeze. No sentries paced. Beyond the fields, the forest lay a hundred yards off; from there I could double back to the road, skirt the village, and keep going until civilization and sanity. I fed the rope between the meeting edges of the shutters to hide it, tied the other end to the bedstead, and paused at a ripple of distant revelry. They were celebrating their triumph over six women. I had to move before this dragon lord paraded me for his drunken friends.

I swung over the sill and slid. I stopped level with a lit window, the ground a tantalizing twenty feet below. Voices drifted out. “My Lord, you must see to your guests,” Renner urged. “I have only been away a short while, Renner, and will return in a moment. I’m sure they understand that the danger in the borderlands is more pressing,” the lord replied. “Has something new been heard?” “Yes. The scouts have brought news of more raids by these new clans.” Papers rustled, a chair scraped, and a shadow loomed at the window. I held breath and rope. His voice dropped. “Five villages were lost during this last raid.” “And still we know nothing about these dragons?” “They are barbarians from the north driven down by unknown forces. That is all the scouts could surmise.” The shadow moved away. “Let these matters rest for a while, My Lord, and enjoy yourself,” Renner said. A deep sigh. “Perhaps I have. I will join the others and see their choices. I believe the young Cayden had his eyes set on your favorite.” “Merely an oversight on my part, My Lord. A lapse of bad judgment that shan’t happen again.”

The candlelight died. A door shut. I slid the last feet, dropped to packed earth, and crouched. The grounds lay empty; the laughter was far off. They celebrated and traded their insane talk of rival dragon clans while I slipped into the dark.
