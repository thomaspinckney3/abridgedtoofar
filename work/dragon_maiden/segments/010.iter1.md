CHAPTER 6

I fled straight across the grounds and into the woods, the shouts behind me telling me they had found the empty bed and the sheets.  Torches spilled from the gate; men spread across the lawns and road.  I slid down the muddy hillside, forced my way into the brambles until I found a narrow animal path that curved away from the road and brought me at last to a sunken glen where a creek fell into a round pool.  I sank against a boulder, aching and hungry, meaning to close my eyes only for a moment.

“What do you mean by this transgression?”

I jumped up.  Across the pond stood the dragon lord, most of him in shadow, his eyes an unnatural green, one hand resting on the hilt of a sheathed sword.

I snorted.  “You’re the one who’s transgressed.  Several kidnapping laws, if I’m not mistaken.”

“You cannot escape me.  Return with me to the castle at once,” he ordered.

I looked around for a weapon of my own and snatched a smooth river stone from the ground.  I held it up in a throwing position.  “Get away!  I know how to use this!” I shot back.

His eyes widened.  His grip tightened.  “Let down the stone.  You don’t know what trouble it can cause here.”

“Like I’m doing that.”  He stepped into the open, and shock stole my breath: a pair of large, red, dragon-like wings folded at his back.

Steel flashed; he dashed toward the bank.  I threw.  My aim, as ever, betrayed me.  The stone plopped into the pool near the base of the fall.  Bubbles surged, the earth trembled; I stumbled and fell.  A column of water burst upward, twisted into a spinning spindle, narrowed, and took the form of a woman before the excess fell away in a rain that soaked us both.

She hovered above the pond in a blue dress that moved like waves.  Her alabaster skin shone; dark hair streamed; eyes the color of the clearest waters fixed on me.  When she spoke, her voice held a cavern’s echo.  “Why have you transgressed me?”

The dragon lord knelt, set his sword before him, bowed his head.  “She meant no harm, My Lady.  She is ignorant of this world.”

The woman half-turned to him, eyes still on me.  “Then she is a Maiden.”

“Yes, chosen only this evening by me.”

A ghost of a smile.  “I see.  She must be very bold to have escaped the Castle so quickly.”

“Yes, very bold, but I will punish her in a fitting manner.”

Anger makes one forget their predicament, and mine was no exception.  I jumped to my feet and glared at him.  “Like hell you are!”  I started on a tirade that was cut short by the twinkling of laughter.

The woman’s smile deepened as she regarded me.  “My, my, Dragon Lord, but you have chosen a wild one.  She has escaped your castle, eluded your men, violated my waters, and now berates you before me, all in one night.”

“I am sorry, My Lady.  I swear it will never happen again.”

She arched an eyebrow.  “It won’t?  What a pity, for it amuses me.”  She drifted to my shore; I tried to shrink back, but her gaze held me.  “Don’t be afraid, little Maiden, for I wish to give you a gift.”  Cool and warm at once, like deep and shallow waters, her palm cupped my cheek.  “May your body grow with your love, and let it accept your master in all his forms as your form.”

Blue light bloomed from her hand, soaked through my skin, and spread a blanket-warmth through me, settling hottest at the brand on my shoulder as though it sank deep beneath the flesh.

My legs gave; I dropped to my knees.  She stepped back, bare feet just misting the surface as she glided to the center of the pond.  “Good luck, little Maiden.  Sleep well, my darling.”  A cocoon of water rose and wrapped her; it slipped back into the pool, and she was gone, leaving only the dragon lord’s astonishment.

I didn’t get to savor his astonishment as a wave of disbelief and fatigue swept over me.  That was no mirage of an insane person.  The world spun around me as I clutched my head and swayed from side-to-side.  The last I knew was of the dragon lord gliding over the pond toward me and catching me in his strong arms.
