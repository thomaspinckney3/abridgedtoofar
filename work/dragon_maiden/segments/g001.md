## Chapter 1

"Weekend at last!"

That was my cry as I stretched my arms above my head. Around me throngs of coworkers spilled from the fifty-floor office building onto the sidewalk—a tired, eager humanity heading home to family and dinner.

"Amen!" one called out.

"Anybody seeing the game?" another asked.

"I'm just seeing my eyelids," a hefty guy quipped.

That got a laugh that extinguished much of the exhaustion on a lot of faces. I only had the dinner part, but pizza awaited me. Still, being among such a large crowd of my fellow humans was a bitter reminder of how normal my boring life was.

"Hey, Mir!" My friend Heather waved. Beside her stood a cute guy I'd seen around the office, a recent transfer. My heart sank. Heather jerked her head toward me. "This is the sexy woman I've been telling you about, Blake."

Blake looked me over. There was something dark about his eyes I didn't like. He held out his hand. "Nice to meet you."

I shook it. His fingers were clammy, like his skin didn't quite fit. "Likewise, and don't let Heather tell you about my love life. It's practically non-existent."

Blake laughed. "I don't think this is going to work, Heth. She's on to me."

Heather blinked. "On to you?"

"It's nothing. Anyway, nice to have met you." He waved and walked off.

I turned to my friend. "This is the third time this month, and it's only the seventh day."

She sighed. "I'm just trying to get you hooked up. You're too nice not to deserve a good man and reproduce."

I glanced at Blake's retreating back. "Then stop trying to help me. The guys you keep showing me are getting creepier."

"I don't think he was that bad. A little strange, yeah, but who isn't?" She caught up as I walked off. "Wanna get a bite before you hole yourself in your apartment for the weekend?"

"Only if you're buying."

Heather rolled her eyes. "Fine, fine, but I expect to get a dance out of this."

"You know I hate that."

"The only way you'll dance is with someone you know, and I'll probably be the only one you know." She stepped into the street and raised her hand. "Taxi!"

A cab pulled up. We slipped inside.

"I was thinking of going hiking one of these weekends with a few other girls. Wanna come?"

I shook my head. "I'm not much for nature walks."

She frowned. "It's the society, isn't it?"

I winced. "It's not that. It's just—I don't feel like I fit in. Like I'm an outsider, you know what I mean?"

Heather shook her head. "No, and I won't take 'no' for an answer next time. You need to live a little, Miriam, before your whole life is gone."

I glanced out the window and sighed. The world sped by as a shadow play of colorful lights and figures. "I know. I… I just feel like I'm supposed to wait for something. Like my time will come."

The driver's voice cut in. "I think someone's following you—that red car." Heather whipped around and squealed; the driver took a hard corner into an alley and sped up. I clutched the oh-shit handle. After a few hair-raising turns Heather tossed bills into the front seat and yanked me out at an intersection. We crouched behind a dumpster as the red car zoomed past, still chasing our former taxi.

My stomach was in knots. I staggered past Heather toward home.

"Hey! Where are you going?"

"I need to find my stomach back here first."

"Fine!" she called. "If you don't want my help, then go and find your own guy!"

I waved without looking back. It wasn't such a bad suggestion, if Fate hadn't had other plans.

## Chapter 2

It was forty-five minutes before I got to my apartment building. Night beat me to the stoop, and with it came shadow and darkness. An alley separated my building from its neighbor.

I paused at the alley. Had I heard something clattering in there? Hulking shadows of trashcans stared back. Nothing moved. I shrugged and kept going.

My keys rattled in my hand—then I froze. A rattle had mimicked my own. Something flickered in the corner of my sight, but disappeared before I could catch it. I fumbled, shoved the key in the lock, and fell inside.

"Easy there, Miriam," I whispered. "Just your imagination."

Still, I couldn't shake the feeling of being watched. I hurried up to my third-floor apartment, tossed my purse on the table, and plopped into my favorite—and only—recliner.

"Now this is the way to live," I murmured. "Just me and my chair."

I noticed a sweet smell that shouldn't have been there. None of my incense candles were lit. I tried to rise. My arms wouldn't pull me forward.

The feeling of being watched returned. I glanced at the window to the fire escape. Three figures stood on the other side of the glass. I gasped, but the scream stuck in my throat.

One knelt and fidgeted with the sill. The window popped up; he drew it to the top and slipped inside. The other two followed.

Two were burly men in ragged overcoats. They were followed by a familiar face. Blake.

The pair strode over. One grabbed my legs, the other pulled a rope from his coat, and together they bound me. I couldn't fight, couldn't manage more than a garbled whisper. Blake stood by my head and studied my face.

"What a lucky break your friend introduced me to you today. You're just in time to join a few other girls on an exciting journey."

"With all expenses paid," one of the men joined in.

Blake chuckled. "Come, come. It's an honor for this young woman to join in this age-old ritual." His eyes flickered back to me. "She may even be good enough to be his consort."

"She ain't pretty enough."

"Beauty isn't everything, and I think this one might have something special." He leaned close and reached into his shirt collar. My eyes widened as he peeled away the fake flesh that clung to his brown skin. His nostrils bulged like a pig's, his teeth yellow and sharp as fangs. He was a monster. "—she saw through my disguise."

I wanted to run. All I could manage was a shudder.

"Stop fooling around," one of the men insisted.

Blake tucked the mask back in. "All right, but don't damage the merchandise." He pulled a cloth from his pocket, and a stench of rotten plants attacked my nostrils. "I'll take care of that now. It'll prove whether she's one of them or not, too."

He shoved the cloth over my mouth and nose. Tears pooled in my eyes as the room began to spin.

In a moment I was knocked clean out.

## Chapter 3

"Let me out! God damn fucks! Let me out!"

My dark world was receding, and that was my introduction back to the real one. I forced my eyes open.

I was in the back of a metal truck. Floor and walls were plates of steel; the ceiling and rear were canvas, tied so tight no light came through. Tires crunched on gravel. On either side ran wooden benches, and on one I sat—not alone. Four other women sat around me, all roughly my age, hands bound behind their backs, ankles chained. I shifted; chains rattled on my own feet.

The one pounding the side of the truck had a scowling face, mascara run down like an undead mask.

"Let me out, you stupid fucks!" she screamed.

"Will you stop that?" one of the others snapped. "You've been trying for fifteen minutes. They don't give a damn."

Mascara-face took a deep breath. "Heellppppp!"

The truck slowed. A door opened; boots hit gravel. The canvas was shoved aside and Blake revealed himself in all his piggy glory. Behind him stretched a vast forest under night's shroud; distant lights spoke of a small town, nothing like the city I knew.

"What the hell's going on here?"

Mascara-face gathered her wits. "I—I want you to release me."

Blake snorted—the noise even sounded piggish. "Like hell we will. You shut up. We can't hear ourselves think up there."

"What do you want with us?" the woman beside me asked.

"You'll see. We should be there in a little bit, and then you'd better keep your mouths shut. The lords don't like loud women unless they're bedding them."

He dropped the canvas. The truck lurched forward again.

A few sniffles broke the silence. The girl beside me—maybe twenty—shook with soft sobs.

"You okay?" I asked.

She shook her head. "W-where are they taking us? Why did they take us?"

"I don't know. What do you last remember?"

"We've all got the same story," the irate one cut in. "Everybody was grabbed out of their homes or off the streets and drugged. We woke up in this truck, and that brat's been crying since."

"You don't have to be an asshole," I told her. "We're all going through this together."

"It's everybody for themselves. I'm not dragging a brat with me."

"That's enough," another woman said. She turned to me. "Some of us introduced ourselves before you woke up. I'm Alexandra—Alex. That's Stephanie, and Cindy." She jerked her head. "And that's Olivia."

"Miriam," I said.

"Shut up back there!" Blake called through the cab.

We fell quiet. The truck swayed more heavily, then stopped. The engine shut off; doors slammed. Three sets of feet walked alongside, and the rear flap was thrown aside. Blake and his two compatriots stood there—human faces gone, snout-nosed true identities revealed.

"Climb out, or we drag you out."

One by one we shuffled forward in single file. Stephanie hesitated at the edge of the bed; one of the men grabbed her arm and yanked her down. She cried out as she hit the gravel.

A flash of red blinded my vision. I lunged at the closest asshole. My shoulder hit his face and we both toppled. Alex gave a war whoop and threw herself at the other. Cindy charged Blake. He side-stepped and smacked her on the back of the head. She crumpled.

"Handle these wenches or you're fired!" Blake snapped.

My victim wrapped me in a bear hug and stood. Alex was subdued. Blake stalked over and glared.

"You two are almost more trouble than you're worth. One more stupid move and you'll be thrown out into this world that you wouldn't last five minutes in."

My eyes followed his arm. Twenty feet away stood a long stone stable with a thatched gable roof. Beyond it, a hundred acres of open ground stretched to the surrounding forest. A castle occupied five front acres, six towers accenting its rectangular design; soft lights lit its tall, narrow windows. Cows and sheep grazed in fenced fields. A branch of the gravel road led to a great stone arch whose massive wooden doors bore carved figures I couldn't make out. Behind us, a small village of stone huts lined the road, smoke curling from their chimneys.

We were marched into the stables to three empty stalls. Alex, Stephanie, and I were shoved into one; the others into the next. Blake stood between them.

"Take the manacles off all the girls except those two troublemakers, and then go check if they're ready up there."

One of them frowned. "But that'll make 'em look lame for the presentation."

Blake grinned. "Some of our buyers like them bound."

They laughed as they unlocked the ankle manacles; hands remained tied. Alex and I were passed over. One pig-man disappeared through a door at the end of the stables.

Blake paced the straw. "Now listen up. I won't be repeating this. You lot are to be presented to some very influential—" he chuckled, "—we'll call them men. Some of them will want to take you. You'd better hope they do. Otherwise you're discarded to the servant's quarters of this castle and left to fend for yourself. If you're good girls you'll be chosen and given a life of luxury in their homes."

"But I want to go home!" Stephanie insisted.

He stopped and sneered. "Nobody gets back through the Portal. Nobody." The sly grin slipped onto his lips. "At least not alive."

"What the hell are you talking about? What portal?" Alex demanded.

"That's for you girls to find out, but after you're presented to the Lords of the Air."

"Let us go!"

"Shut up!" Blake snapped. "You're staying here, so stop your whining and get used to it!"

The door opened a sliver and the henchman slipped back in. He whispered at Blake's shoulder.

"We've got a problem."

Blake narrowed his eyes. "What kind of problem?"

A soft female voice spoke up. "There is no problem."
