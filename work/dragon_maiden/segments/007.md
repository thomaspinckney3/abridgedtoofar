"Let me out! God damn fucks! Let me out!"

My dark world was receding, and that was my introduction back to the real one. I forced my eyes open.

I was in the back of a metal truck. Floor and walls were plates of steel; the ceiling and rear were canvas, tied so tight no light came through. Tires crunched on gravel. On either side ran wooden benches, and on one I sat—not alone. Four other women sat around me, all roughly my age, hands bound behind their backs, ankles chained. I shifted; chains rattled on my own feet.

The one pounding the side of the truck had a scowling face, mascara run down like an undead mask.

"Let me out, you stupid fucks!" she screamed.

"Will you stop that?" one of the others snapped. "You've been trying for fifteen minutes. They don't give a damn."

Mascara-face took a deep breath. "Heellppppp!"

The truck slowed. A door opened; boots hit gravel. The canvas was shoved aside and Blake revealed himself in all his piggy glory. Behind him stretched a vast forest under night's shroud; distant lights spoke of a small town, nothing like the city I knew.

"What the hell's going on here?"

Mascara-face gathered her wits. "I—I want you to release me."

Blake snorted—the noise even sounded piggish. "Like hell we will. You shut up. We can't hear ourselves think up there."

"What do you want with us?" the woman beside me asked.

"You'll see. We should be there in a little bit, and then you'd better keep your mouths shut. The lords don't like loud women unless they're bedding them."

He dropped the canvas. The truck lurched forward again.

A few sniffles broke the silence. The girl beside me—maybe twenty—shook with soft sobs.

"You okay?" I asked.

She shook her head. "W-where are they taking us? Why did they take us?"

"I don't know. What do you last remember?"

"We've all got the same story," the irate one cut in. "Everybody was grabbed out of their homes or off the streets and drugged. We woke up in this truck, and that brat's been crying since."

"You don't have to be an asshole," I told her. "We're all going through this together."

"It's everybody for themselves. I'm not dragging a brat with me."

"That's enough," another woman said. She turned to me. "Some of us introduced ourselves before you woke up. I'm Alexandra—Alex. That's Stephanie, and Cindy." She jerked her head. "And that's Olivia."

"Miriam," I said.

"Shut up back there!" Blake called through the cab.

We fell quiet. The truck swayed more heavily, then stopped. The engine shut off; doors slammed. Three sets of feet walked alongside, and the rear flap was thrown aside. Blake and his two compatriots stood there—human faces gone, snout-nosed true identities revealed.

"Climb out, or we drag you out."

One by one we shuffled forward in single file. Stephanie hesitated at the edge of the bed; one of the men grabbed her arm and yanked her down. She cried out as she hit the gravel.

A flash of red blinded my vision. I lunged at the closest asshole. My shoulder hit his face and we both toppled. Alex gave a war whoop and threw herself at the other. Cindy charged Blake. He side-stepped and smacked her on the back of the head. She crumpled.

"Handle these wenches or you're fired!" Blake snapped.

My victim wrapped me in a bear hug and stood. Alex was subdued. Blake stalked over and glared.

"You two are almost more trouble than you're worth. One more stupid move and you'll be thrown out into this world that you wouldn't last five minutes in."

My eyes followed his arm. Twenty feet away stood a long stone stable with a thatched gable roof. Beyond it, a hundred acres of open ground stretched to the surrounding forest. A castle occupied five front acres, six towers accenting its rectangular design; soft lights lit its tall, narrow windows. Cows and sheep grazed in fenced fields. A branch of the gravel road led to a great stone arch whose massive wooden doors bore carved figures I couldn't make out. Behind us, a small village of stone huts lined the road, smoke curling from their chimneys.

We were marched into the stables to three empty stalls. Alex, Stephanie, and I were shoved into one; the others into the next. Blake stood between them.

"Take the manacles off all the girls except those two troublemakers, and then go check if they're ready up there."

One of them frowned. "But that'll make 'em look lame for the presentation."

Blake grinned. "Some of our buyers like them bound."

They laughed as they unlocked the ankle manacles; hands remained tied. Alex and I were passed over. One pig-man disappeared through a door at the end of the stables.

Blake paced the straw. "Now listen up. I won't be repeating this. You lot are to be presented to some very influential—" he chuckled, "—we'll call them men. Some of them will want to take you. You'd better hope they do. Otherwise you're discarded to the servant's quarters of this castle and left to fend for yourself. If you're good girls you'll be chosen and given a life of luxury in their homes."

"But I want to go home!" Stephanie insisted.

He stopped and sneered. "Nobody gets back through the Portal. Nobody." The sly grin slipped onto his lips. "At least not alive."

"What the hell are you talking about? What portal?" Alex demanded.

"That's for you girls to find out, but after you're presented to the Lords of the Air."

"Let us go!"

"Shut up!" Blake snapped. "You're staying here, so stop your whining and get used to it!"

The door opened a sliver and the henchman slipped back in. He whispered at Blake's shoulder.

"We've got a problem."

Blake narrowed his eyes. "What kind of problem?"

A soft female voice spoke up. "There is no problem."
