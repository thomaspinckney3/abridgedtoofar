CHAPTER 2

The bus took longer than I hoped, and night beat me to the stoop of my apartment building. The street was deserted. I reached the alley that split my place from the next and paused. Had I heard something clatter? Hulking trashcans, broken boxes, and black puddles stared back; the few streetlights only scratched at the dark. Nothing moved. Nothing clattered.

I shrugged and went on. My keys rattled in my hand as I dug them out—and I froze. A rattle had mimicked my own. Something flickered at the alley’s mouth, then vanished. My pulse quickened. I fumbled the right key free, shoved it into the lock, fell inside, and slammed the door. The entrance rattled, the echo booming through the lonely lobby.

I held my chest and panted. “Easy there, Miriam. It was just your imagination. . .” Still, the feeling of being watched clung to me as I hurried up to my third-floor one-bedroom with the lovely view of the fire escape and the neighboring brick.

I tossed my purse on the little table and dropped into my favorite—and only—recliner. The sensation faded in the safety of my locked apartment. I leaned back. “Now this is the way to live…” I murmured. “Just me and my chair.”

A sweet smell hung in the air. None of my incense candles were lit. I tried to rise, but my arms wouldn’t pull me forward.

The feeling of eyes returned. Movement at the fire-escape window caught my gaze. Three figures stood beyond the glass. I gasped, but the scream stuck in my throat.

One knelt, fidgeted with the bottom of the sash. The window popped up an inch, then rose. He slipped in; the other two followed. The overhead light showed them: two burly men in ragged coats—and Blake.

The pair strode to me. One grabbed my legs; the other pulled a rope from his coat. Together they began to bind me. I couldn’t fight. I couldn’t do more than whisper a garbled plea. Blake moved to my head. My terrified eyes met his smile.

“What a lucky break your friend introduced me to you today,” he said, leaning close. “You’re just in time to join a few other girls on an exciting journey.”

“Yeah. A regular cruise line,” one of the men snorted.

“With all expenses paid,” the other added.

Blake chuckled. “Come, come, my dear fellows. It’s an honor for this young woman to join in this age-old ritual.” His eyes flicked to me, the smile slipping a little. “She may even be good enough to be his consort.”

“She ain’t pretty enough,” one of them muttered.

Blake shook his head. “Beauty isn’t everything, my friend, and I think this one might have something special about her. After all—” He reached into his shirt collar and peeled away a strip of flesh-colored mask clinging tight to his brown skin. His nostrils bulged like a pig’s; his teeth were yellow, sharp at the tips—fangs in a too-wide grin. “—she saw through my disguise.”

I wanted to run. To push him away. All I managed was a shudder.

“Stop fooling around and help us,” one of the men snapped.

Blake tucked the mask back and grinned. “All right, but don’t damage the merchandise.”

“What about knocking her out? She’s going to struggle,” the other pointed out.

Blake drew a cloth from his pocket. A stench like rotten plants hit me. “I’ll take care of that now. It’ll prove whether she’s one of them or not, too.”

He clamped the cloth over my mouth and nose. The reek invaded my senses. My body wouldn’t respond; tears pooled and slid hot down my cheeks. Thought unraveled; the room began to spin.

In a moment I was knocked clean out.
