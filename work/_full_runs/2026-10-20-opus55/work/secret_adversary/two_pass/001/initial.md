IT was 2 p.m. on the afternoon of May 7, 1915. The _Lusitania_, struck by two torpedoes in succession, was sinking rapidly, and the women and children were being lined up for the boats. One girl stood alone, slightly apart. She was not more than eighteen. She did not seem afraid, and her grave, steadfast eyes looked straight ahead.

“I beg your pardon.” A man’s voice beside her made her turn. She had noticed him amongst the first-class passengers. There was a hint of mystery about him: he spoke to no one, and he looked over his shoulder with a swift, suspicious glance. Now he was greatly agitated, evidently in a state of overmastering fear. And yet he did not strike her as the kind of man who would be afraid to meet death!

He stood looking at her with a kind of desperate irresolution. “It must be!” he muttered to himself. “Yes--it is the only way.” Then aloud he said abruptly: “You are an American?”

“Yes.”

“A patriotic one?”

The girl flushed. “I guess you’ve no right to ask such a thing! Of course I am!”

“I’ve got to trust some one--and it must be a woman.”

“Why?”

“Because of ‘women and children first.’” He lowered his voice. “I’m carrying papers--vitally important papers. They may make all the difference to the Allies. They’ve more chance with you than with me. Will you take them?”

The girl held out her hand.

“Wait--I must warn you. There may be a risk--if I’ve been followed. I don’t think I have, but one never knows. Have you the nerve to go through with it?”

The girl smiled. “I’ll go through with it all right. And I’m real proud to be chosen! What am I to do with them afterwards?”

“I’ll advertise in the personal column of the _Times_, beginning ‘Shipmate.’ At the end of three days if there’s nothing, take the packet to the American Embassy, and deliver it into the Ambassador’s own hands.”

He took her hand in his. “Good-bye. Good luck to you,” he said in a louder tone.

Her hand closed on the oilskin packet that had lain in his palm.

As the _Lusitania_ listed to starboard, the girl went forward to take her place in the boat.
