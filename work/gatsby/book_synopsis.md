Nick Carraway, a Yale graduate and World War I veteran from the Midwest, moves to West Egg, Long Island in 1922 to learn the bond business. 
He rents a modest house next to the opulent mansion of the enigmatic Jay Gatsby and reconnects with his cousin Daisy Buchanan and her domineering, unfaithful husband Tom in East Egg. 
Nick learns of Tom’s affair with Myrtle Wilson, the wife of a struggling garage owner in the ash-strewn wasteland between the Eggs and New York. 
Night after night Gatsby hosts extravagant parties filled with strangers and rumor, yet remains curiously apart from his guests. 
Nick is one of the few actually invited, meets Gatsby, and hears whispers of shady business ties to the gambler Meyer Wolfshiem. 
Through Jordan Baker, Nick learns that Gatsby and Daisy fell in love before the war and that Gatsby has built his life and house across the bay to win her back. 
At Gatsby’s request, Nick arranges a tea that rekindles Gatsby and Daisy’s romance and opens Gatsby’s mansion as a stage for Daisy’s admiration. 
Gatsby’s carefully curated past is revealed as James Gatz, a poor Midwesterner who reinvented himself under the tutelage of millionaire Dan Cody and later through illicit enterprises. 
Tension mounts when Tom and Daisy attend one of Gatsby’s parties and Daisy is repelled by West Egg’s gaudy spectacle. 
On a sweltering day in the city, Tom confronts Gatsby at the Plaza Hotel, exposing his criminal dealings and pressuring Daisy to deny she ever loved Gatsby. 
Driving back, Daisy, at the wheel of Gatsby’s car, strikes and kills Myrtle, and Gatsby resolves to take the blame. 
Believing Gatsby was both Myrtle’s lover and the driver, the grief-crazed George Wilson tracks him to his estate, shoots him in the pool, and then kills himself. 
In the aftermath, Gatsby’s partygoers and associates vanish, Daisy and Tom flee without a forwarding address, and only Nick, Gatsby’s father, and a few servants attend the funeral. 
Disgusted by the Buchanans’ careless destruction and the moral emptiness he has witnessed, Nick ends his relationship with Jordan and decides to return to the Midwest. 
Reflecting on Gatsby’s incorruptible dream and the green light across the bay, Nick mourns a nation’s faith in the American Dream and concludes that our efforts to move forward are constantly pulled back by the past.
