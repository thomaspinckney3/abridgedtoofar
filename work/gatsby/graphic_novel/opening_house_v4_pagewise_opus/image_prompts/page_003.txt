Create one finished graphic novel page as clean, text-free art.

Hard constraints:
- No readable text anywhere.
- No speech balloons, caption boxes, labels, signs, maps, or lettering.
- Do not combine panels or remix the order.
- Keep every panel visually tied to its listed beat only.
- Do not add motifs, props, animals, objects, or symbols just because they appear elsewhere in the book.
- Reserve the explicit in-panel lettering slots listed for each panel.
- Keep those slots as simple empty background; keep faces, mouths, hands, active gestures, and key props out of them.
- Do not reserve lettering space in gutters or outside panel frames.
- Page has 4 panels, read left-to-right, top-to-bottom.

Style:
- Treat these as rendering/style directions only, not as a content inventory. Panel art prompts control what appears.
- visual period: Early 1920s American Jazz Age, post-WWI Midwest and East Coast
- line and color: Clean inked linework with muted watercolor washes; sepia and dusty blues for memory, warmer ambers for present
- lighting: Soft window light and lamp glow; long shadows suggesting introspection

Page 3 summary: Nick arrives alone at the weather-beaten West Egg bungalow when his coworker is reassigned, settles into a small household routine with a dog, a Dodge, and a Finnish cook, then is stopped on the road by a newcomer asking directions, after which his loneliness lifts; back at the bungalow he opens a stack of new banking books in the summer light, resolving to become a well-rounded man in what he already senses is one of the strangest communities in North America.
