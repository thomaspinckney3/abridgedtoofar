Create one finished graphic novel page as clean, text-free art.

Hard constraints:
- No readable text anywhere.
- No speech balloons, caption boxes, labels, signs, maps, or lettering.
- Do not combine panels or remix the order.
- Keep every panel visually tied to its listed beat only.
- Do not add motifs, props, animals, objects, or symbols just because they appear elsewhere in the book.
- Reserve the explicit in-panel lettering slots listed for each panel.
- Keep those slots as simple empty background; keep faces, mouths, hands, active gestures, and key props out of them.
- Do not reserve lettering space in gutters or outside panel frames.
- Page has 4 panels, read left-to-right, top-to-bottom.

Style:
- Treat these as rendering/style directions only, not as a content inventory. Panel art prompts control what appears.
- visual period: Early 1920s American Jazz Age; muted period authenticity in dress and interiors
- line and color: Clean inked linework with soft watercolor washes; sepia and dusk-blue palette with cream paper tones
- lighting: Warm interior lamplight against cool window light; long shadows suggesting memory and retrospection

Character continuity:
- Nick Carraway: Lean young man, early thirties, neatly parted light-brown hair, thoughtful gray eyes, narrow jaw Costume: White shirt with rolled sleeves, loosened dark tie, suspenders, high-waisted wool trousers Continuity: Stepping outside his bungalow to take in the geography of West Egg for the first time.
- Nick's Father: Older man, silver-streaked hair, trim mustache, kind but stern eyes Costume: Dark three-piece Midwestern suit, watch chain, stiff collar Continuity: Appears briefly in a remembered conversation; render with softer linework and warmer wash.
- Finnish Housekeeper: Stout middle-aged woman, ruddy cheeks, hair in a low bun, capable hands Costume: Plain dark dress, white apron Continuity: New minor character; appears only in the bungalow kitchen.
- Lost Stranger: Young man newer to the area than Nick, hat in hand, uncertain stance Costume: Light summer suit, straw boater, walking shoes Continuity: One-off speaker on the country road.

Page 3 summary: Nick moves alone into the weather-beaten West Egg bungalow, settles into solitary routines with a Finnish cook, is asked directions by a newer arrival on the road (which converts his loneliness into a sense of belonging), and begins his summer of self-improvement by shelving a stack of finance volumes—setting up his arrival in one of the strangest communities in North America.
