## Scope

Opening narration through Nick’s move to West Egg, early summer settling-in, and spatial orientation culminating in the reveal of Gatsby’s mansion.

## Pacing Strategy

- **Summary:** Anchor the reflective voice to clear, physical moments of Nick acting: writing, remembering, deciding, moving, looking. Compress aphorisms into select captions riding over visualized actions. Stage geography through Nick’s vantage rather than diagrams.
- **Target Reader Experience:** Feel Nick’s cautious, witty voice while swiftly arriving in West Egg, sensing summer’s restart, and seeing the Eggs and Gatsby’s mansion without bogging down in exposition.
- **Compression Ratio:** moderate
- **Main Risks:** Over-weighting narration and slowing early pages; Blurring narrator-frame (after the events) with story-present (spring–summer 1922); Letting geographic exposition stall action; Diluting the distinctiveness of Nick’s aphorisms if over-trimmed

## Time Frames

- **Narrator Retrospective (after the East):** chronological position: Post-1922 return; Nick writing/reflecting after the novel’s main events; reader cue: Past-tense reflections, moral framing, references to having ‘come back from the East’; visual cue: Nick at a desk or window with notebook and lamplight; quieter palette
- **Midwest Background to Departure:** chronological position: Pre-1922: family background, Yale (1915), Great War, decision to go East; reader cue: Concise backstory beats moving toward spring of ’22; visual cue: Sepia or cooler tones; family portrait, Yale cap, trench flashes, a ticket to New York
- **Spring–Early Summer 1922: Settling in West Egg:** chronological position: Nick’s first weeks in West Egg, early summer mood; reader cue: Present-tense of place; neighbors, errands, sunshine, self-improvement kick; visual cue: Warm greens and golds; roadside exchange; red-and-gold finance books
- **Orientation: The Two Eggs and Neighbors:** chronological position: Still spring–summer 1922; Nick physically situates home, bay, and mansions; reader cue: Pointing out shorelines, ‘West Egg’ vs ‘East Egg’, and Gatsby’s mansion; visual cue: Nick on shoreline or porch sightlines across the Sound; dusk light for contrast
