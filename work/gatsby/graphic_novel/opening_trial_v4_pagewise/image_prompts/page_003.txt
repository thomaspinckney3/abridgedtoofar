Create one finished graphic novel page as clean, text-free art.

Hard constraints:
- No readable text anywhere.
- No speech balloons, caption boxes, labels, signs, maps, or lettering.
- Do not combine panels or remix the order.
- Keep every panel visually tied to its listed beat only.
- Leave some uncluttered negative space where lettering can be added later.
- Page has 5 panels, read left-to-right, top-to-bottom.

Style:
- visual period: Early 1920s America with flashbacks to the 1910s Midwest
- line and color: Clean ink lines with selective hatching; muted, slightly desaturated palette with warm sepias for memory and cooler neutrals for present reflections
- lighting: Bright, crisp spring light for the move East; soft, interior daylight for domestic moments
- recurring motifs: porch railings and straight lines (order/reserve); train platforms and suitcases (transit between East/West); seasonal foliage: spring blossoms for new start; windows and thresholds (observation vs participation)

Character continuity:
- Nick Carraway: Lean, tidy side-parted hair, watchful eyes; often framed slightly apart as an observer Costume: Conservative dark three-piece suit, white shirt, slim tie; hat and overcoat when outdoors; modest shirtsleeves at home Continuity: Narrator situated in a modest West Egg bungalow; this page places his home at the tip of the Egg and contrasts it with neighboring mansions.
- Nick's Father: Middle-aged gentleman with neatly trimmed mustache and steady gaze Costume: Early 1910s–1920s Midwestern attire: shirt, vest, jacket; pocket watch chain visible Continuity: Appears in memories and moments of guidance/support; presence signals Nick’s moral frame and practical support
- Jay Gatsby (glimpsed): Elegant male silhouette or distant figure implied by the immaculate estate; features not revealed Costume: Suggested immaculate tailoring through clean, poised silhouette if shown at all Continuity: Identified only by the mansion to Nick’s right; avoid concrete identifiers—keep Gatsby as an idea more than a person.
- Young Man at the Office: Clean-cut clerk in shirtsleeves and tie, energetic posture Costume: 1920s office wear: white shirt, suspenders, narrow tie, sleeve garters Continuity: Colleague who proposes sharing a house in a commuting town
- Finnish Woman: Sturdy, middle-aged with hair in a bun or kerchief; practical, focused demeanor Costume: Plain 1920s house dress with apron; sensible shoes Continuity: Housekeeper who makes Nick’s bed and cooks breakfast; often near an electric stove
- Man on the Road: Youngish newcomer with suitcase, slightly disoriented posture, hat in hand Costume: Simple 1920s travel wear: light suit, tie, soft hat; scuffed shoes from travel Continuity: Recent arrival who asks Nick for directions, triggering Nick’s sense of belonging

Page 3 summary: Nick takes the modest bungalow alone, briefly assembles a life with a runaway dog, an old car, and a Finnish housekeeper. A stranger’s request for directions banishes his loneliness, and in spring’s surge he embraces study, health, and a renewed—yet focused—ambition, hinting at the strange community he has chanced into.

Panels:

Panel 1:
Beat: Nick arrives at the weather-beaten bungalow alone after his would-be housemate is sent to Washington.
Shot: establishing
Art prompt: A modest, weather-worn 1920s bungalow with peeling paint and a small porch railing under bright spring light. A narrow front yard with sparse grass and a lone suitcase at Nick’s feet. Nick stands at the threshold, hat in hand, taking in the place with quiet resolve; no one else is present. Hints of trees budding around neighboring cottages; a gravel lane and distant telegraph poles. Leave clear sky space for captions.

Panel 2:
Beat: Nick’s brief domestic setup: runaway dog, old car, and Finnish housekeeper.
Shot: medium
Art prompt: Interior of the bungalow kitchen in daylight: a simple electric stove glowing; the Finnish woman in an apron stirs a pan, quietly muttering to herself. Nick leans in the doorway, sleeves slightly rolled, watching with a faint half-smile. A scruffy small dog noses a food bowl on the floor. Through a window, an old 1920s Dodge sits in the small drive. Warm, lived-in clutter on counters; leave wall space for caption.

Panel 3:
Beat: A newcomer stops Nick on the road to ask for directions to West Egg village.
Shot: medium
Art prompt: Sunny roadside bordered by leafy trees. Nick stands with hands in pockets; a slightly rumpled newcomer with a suitcase and hat looks up at him, hopeful and a bit lost. A split-rail fence and dirt verge suggest the fringe of a village. Clear negative space between them for dialogue and caption.

Panel 4:
Beat: After helping, Nick feels claimed by the neighborhood and buoyed by summer’s beginning.
Shot: wide
Art prompt: Nick walks away along a tree-lined lane under a canopy of fresh green leaves, shoulders lighter, stride purposeful. Sunlight flickers through the foliage like a fast reel; a few cottages and gardens recede behind him. Space above and ahead for layered narration.

Panel 5:
Beat: Nick arms himself with books and a renewed, focused outlook, hinting at the strange community he’s entered.
Shot: medium
Art prompt: Inside the bungalow’s small sitting room: a narrow shelf lined with gleaming red-and-gold spined volumes; Nick, thoughtful, stands by a single window, light falling across his face and the books. Beyond the window, a distant shoreline with large houses implied across the water, blurred and indistinct. A small table with papers and a fountain pen. Ample wall space for dense captions.
