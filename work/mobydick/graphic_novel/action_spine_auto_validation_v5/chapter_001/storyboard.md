# Loomings — Choosing the Sea

Source chapter: Moby-Dick; or, The Whale — Chapter 1: Loomings

Adaptation principle: Reduce the chapter’s meditations to the concrete arc of a choice: I grow grim, decide to sea, choose my station as common sailor, accept its thumps and rewards, feel fated toward whaling, and welcome it for the whale.

## Action-Oriented Synopsis

Ishmael grows melancholy and decides to go to sea at once. He chooses to ship as a simple sailor rather than as a passenger, officer, or cook. He accepts the indignities and orders of shipboard life as part of a universal lot. He favors being paid and the fresh forecastle air, reinforcing his plan to sail before the mast. He feels compelled by fate to make this voyage a whaling one. He welcomes the whaling voyage, driven by fascination with the great whale.

## Caption Spine

1. Whenever it is a damp, drizzly November in my soul, I quietly take to the ship.
2. No, when I go to sea, I go as a simple sailor, right before the mast.
3. They rather order me about some, but I rub my shoulder-blades and am content with the universal thump.
4. I go as a sailor because they make a point of paying me, and for the wholesome exercise and pure air of the forecastle.
5. But wherefore it was that I should now take it into my head to go on a whaling voyage, the invisible police of the Fates can better answer—yet I feel their dogging me.
6. By reason of these things, then, the whaling voyage was welcome—my curiosity about the great whale having swung wide the flood-gates of the wonder-world.

## Omitted Material

- **Extended panorama of New York water-gazers at the Battery and along slips.:** Evocative tableau that illustrates a general attraction to water but does not change Ishmael’s situation; folded into the decision to go to sea.
- **Artist’s Saco landscape, prairies without water, Niagara-as-sand, and the many rhetorical questions about Persians, Greeks, and Narcissus.:** Analogies and philosophical examples; not present-tense action; their meaning (water draws us) is carried in captions over real action beats.
- **Jokes about broiled fowl, Egyptian mummies, and shipboard cook glory.:** Colorful digressions that support the choice not to be a cook; consolidated into the single decision frame about shipping as a simple sailor.
- **Detailed riff on paying versus being paid and the orchard thieves, perdition, and monied men entering heaven.:** Supporting argument, not a separate action; its gist (I prefer being paid) is compacted into one decision frame.
- **The mock playbill of Providence and references to the Grand Contested Election and Afghanistan battle.:** Figurative framing that does not alter the immediate path; its function is represented by the Fate-driven choice toward whaling.
- **Hypothetical broom-sweeping example and archangel Gabriel reference.:** Hypotheticals; instead we depict the accepted reality of being ordered about (climbing the rigging) without staging the hypothetical task.

## Visual Safety Notes

- Do not depict hypothetical or allegorical figures or scenes (e.g., Cato, archangel Gabriel, Narcissus, invisible police of the Fates); keep them in captions only.
- No readable text anywhere: avoid legible signs, ship names, documents, playbills, currency markings, or labels.
- Do not show specific named places as labeled objects (e.g., the Battery); use generic waterfront and room interiors.
- Do not visualize the imagined ‘endless processions’ or the ‘hooded phantom like a snow hill’; keep whales and visions out of the art for this chapter.
- Keep props generic to 19th-century seafaring (rigging, rope, sea chest, seabag) without introducing whaling-specific gear (e.g., harpoons) not present in the scene.
