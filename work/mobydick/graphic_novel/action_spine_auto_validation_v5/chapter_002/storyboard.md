# Moby-Dick Ch. 2 — The Carpet-Bag: Finding a Bed in New Bedford

Source chapter: Chapter 2. The Carpet-Bag

Adaptation principle: Track Ishmael’s concrete path from arrival in New Bedford to the Spouter Inn’s threshold, folding digressions and histories into captions that motivate his frugal search, and staging only on-the-ground turns that change his situation.

## Action-Oriented Synopsis

I arrive in New Bedford on a bleak Saturday night and discover the Nantucket packet has sailed, leaving me to wait until Monday. Short of money in the freezing dark, I resolve to be frugal and pace waterward seeking a cheap bed, passing bright, costly inns. Taking an open doorway for a public place, I stumble over an ash-box and blunder into a negro church, then back out. I come upon the Spouter Inn sign—Peter Coffin—and, despite the ominous name, judge it the very spot for cheap lodgings. I step to the threshold, scrape the ice from my feet, and prepare to see what sort of place the Spouter may be.

## Caption Spine

1. With my old carpet-bag under my arm I came to New Bedford on a Saturday night in December, only to learn the Nantucket packet had already sailed, and I must wait till Monday.
2. Bitingly cold, with only a few pieces of silver, I told myself—be sure to inquire the price, and don’t be too particular—and took the waterward streets, shunning the jolly, expensive doors of the Crossed Harpoons and the Sword-Fish.
3. Mistaking a low, wide building with an open door for public lodging, I stumbled over an ash-box, choked, and found a negro church within—a hundred black faces turning—so I muttered wretched entertainment at the sign of the Trap, and backed out.
4. Presently I came to a swinging sign of a white spout—The Spouter Inn: Peter Coffin; ominous names, but the dim light and poor, creaking look promised the very spot for cheap lodgings and the best of pea coffee.
5. Let us scrape the ice from our frosted feet at the threshold, and see what sort of a place this Spouter may be.
