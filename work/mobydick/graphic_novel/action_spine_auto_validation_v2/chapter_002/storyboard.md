# Moby-Dick — Chapter 2: The Carpet-Bag (Action Spine, Frames 3–8)

Source chapter: Chapter 2. The Carpet-Bag

Adaptation principle: Track only Ishmael’s concrete actions on a cold Saturday night in New Bedford: arrival and delay, decision to find cheap lodging, rejection of costly inns, mistaken entry into a church, and choosing the Spouter Inn. Fold rhetoric and digressions into concise first-person captions without staging hypotheticals.

## Action-Oriented Synopsis

Ishmael reaches New Bedford on a winter Saturday night, misses the Nantucket packet and must wait till Monday. Short on money, he searches the waterfront for cheap lodging, passes merrier, pricier inns, accidentally wanders into a black church and retreats, then finds the Spouter Inn and decides to go in.

## Caption Spine

1. I stuffed a shirt or two into my old carpet-bag, quitted Manhatto, reached New Bedford on a Saturday night, and, finding the Nantucket packet already sailed, I must bide till Monday.
2. With only a few pieces of silver and my patched boots stopping the way, I paced the bitter streets, shunning the jolly, expensive inns and following my instinct waterward for cheaper quarters.
3. Blundering into a low, wide building, I stumbled over an ash-box and opened upon a negro church—the great Black Parliament in Tophet—so I muttered and backed out.
4. At last I spied the creaking sign with a misty white spray—the Spouter Inn—and though ‘Coffin’ and ‘Spouter’ sounded ominous, I chose its cheap shelter and scraped the ice from my feet to go in.

- Depict only the spaces actually visited: New Bedford streets, tavern exteriors, church interior, Spouter Inn exterior.
- Portray the black congregation respectfully and historically grounded; no caricature.
- Do not invent characters like Peter Coffin onstage; only the sign and the building are shown.
