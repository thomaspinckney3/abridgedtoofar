# Moby-Dick — Chapter 1: Loomings — Action Spine (Frames 3–8)

Source chapter: Chapter 1. Loomings

Adaptation principle: Boil Melville’s reflective preface down to the concrete turns: I decide to go to sea, fix my lowly berth, narrow the voyage to whaling, and welcome the choice with awakened wonder; fold examples and philosophy into first-person captions over present-moment postures.

## Character Continuity

- Ishmael (narrator and prospective sailor): Young adult man, lean build, medium height, dark wavy hair, clean-shaven, thoughtful expression; plain dark wool coat, rough trousers, simple boots; no uniform or insignia.

## Prop Continuity

- seabag: Plain off-white canvas seabag with rope drawstring and shoulder strap, slightly worn and stained from use. Avoid confusion: Do not depict as a modern zippered duffel or backpack.
- whaling_ship: Square-rigged wooden whaler with dark hull, open whaleboats hung from davits along the sides, low brick try-works visible on deck, stout masts and rigging. Avoid confusion: Do not render as a modern steel ship, naval frigate, clipper with sleek lines, or steam vessel; no readable name on the hull.

## Action-Oriented Synopsis

Ishmael resolves to go to sea to cure his low spirits. He chooses to ship as a common sailor rather than as a passenger or an officer. He fixes specifically on a whaling voyage for this departure. He welcomes the choice with a rising sense of wonder and readiness.

## Caption Spine

1. Call me Ishmael: whenever a damp, drizzly November settles in my soul and my hypos would set me to knocking people’s hats off, I take it for high time to get to sea.
2. So I do not go as a passenger, nor as a Commodore, Captain, or Cook, but as a simple sailor before the mast.
3. Counting the universal thump, the pay, and the fore-castle air as part of the bargain, I take it into my head to go on a whaling voyage.
4. By reason of these things, the whaling voyage was welcome; the flood-gates of the wonder-world swung open within me.

## Omitted Material

- **Extended panoramas of Manhattan’s water-gazers and Sabbath circumambulation of the city.:** They illustrate a general impulse rather than change Ishmael’s situation; their meaning is folded into the first decision frame.
- **Country paths leading to water, the Saco valley painter, Prairies without water, Niagara as sand, the Tennessee poet, Persians and Greeks, Narcissus.:** Rhetorical and comparative meditations that support the sea’s allure but do not advance a concrete action; avoided per the no-hypothetical/analogy frame rule.
- **Detailed complaints about passengers, honor, being ordered about, and the 'universal thump.':** Motives and attitudes are folded into the role-choice and whaling-decision captions instead of separate frames.
- **Stage-managed 'programme' of Fate with elections and a battle in Afghanistan.:** Allegorical explanation of destiny without present action; kept as tone within the whaling decision, not visualized.
- **Egyptian mummies and broiled ibis/river horse digression.:** Comic aside attached to the rejected cook’s role; does not change the action.

- Do not literalize metaphors (Narcissus, hooded phantom, snow hill, the Fates); keep visuals grounded in present physical settings.
