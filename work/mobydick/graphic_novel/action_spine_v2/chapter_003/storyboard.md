# The Spouter-Inn: A Bed Fellow Found

Source chapter: Chapter 3: The Spouter-Inn

Adaptation principle: Track Ishmael’s lodging problem from offer to acceptance, compressing décor and tavern revelry into set dressing while staging only the decisions and encounters that change his night: conditional agreement, refusal and bench fiasco, retreat to the room, the harpooneer’s return and rite, the midnight scare, and Ishmael’s final acceptance.

## Character Continuity

- Ishmael (narrator) (wandering sailor and narrator): Young adult sailor of average build, travel-worn; dark monkey jacket or coarse wool coat and knit cap in the public room; later in shirt-sleeves in the cold upstairs chamber; plain, unadorned look.
- Peter Coffin (landlord) (innkeeper of the Spouter-Inn): Lean, middle-aged New England innkeeper with a weathered face; shirtsleeves, vest, and apron; old silk handkerchief at his neck; practical, quick with tools; carries and lights a tallow candle.
- Queequeg (harpooneer) (South Seas harpooneer and future shipmate): Tall, powerfully built; purplish-yellow complexion; face, chest, back, arms, and legs tattooed with large dark square patterns; little scalp-knot on the forehead; wears a heavy sea-coat (grego) when entering, later bare-chested; calm, self-possessed; carries a tomahawk-pipe and a large seaman’s bag; handles an embalmed New Zealand head and a small ebony idol.

## Action-Oriented Synopsis

I ask the landlord for a bed and, with the house full, agree to share with a harpooneer if he proves decent. Growing wary after supper, I refuse the arrangement and choose the bar-room bench instead. The bench proves impossible and, after the landlord explains the head‑peddling business, I take the big upstairs bed alone. As I doze, the harpooneer returns, and I lie still watching him unpack a preserved head, bare his tattooed body, and worship his little idol at the fireplace. When he leaps into the dark bed with a lighted tomahawk-pipe and threatens, I shout for the landlord. The landlord makes peace, and I have Queequeg put away his pipe and tomahawk, accept him as my bedfellow, and sleep soundly.
