## Scope

From Ishmael’s opening reflection and decision to go to sea through his arrival in New Bedford, entry into the Spouter-Inn, the landlord’s ‘harpooneer’ arrangement, the Grampus crew’s arrival and Bulkington cameo, Ishmael’s uneasy night preparations, Queequeg’s return and idol ritual, the bed panic, and the final accord to share the bed.

## Pacing Strategy

- **Summary:** Center the action spine on Ishmael’s concrete moves: walk toward water, decide to ship, travel to New Bedford, choose a cheap inn, negotiate a bed, attempt to sleep, witness Queequeg’s return and ritual, panic, and reconcile. Philosophical passages ride as concise captions over strong physical anchors. Decorative digressions and rhetorical volleys are dropped or compressed.
- **Target Reader Experience:** A brisk, atmospheric prologue that moves from wry, melancholy narration to tactile, odd, and tense comedy-horror, ending in a humane bond.
- **Compression Ratio:** aggressive
- **Main Risks:** Over-trimming Melville’s voice and losing tonal wit; Confusing time and place transitions amid essayistic passages; Exoticizing Queequeg without context—must present him with dignity; Cluttering pages with props (painting, jaw-bone bar, idol) without clear action spine
