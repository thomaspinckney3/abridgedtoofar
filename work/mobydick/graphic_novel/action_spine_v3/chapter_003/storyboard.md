# Chapter 3: The Spouter-Inn — Action Spine

Source chapter: Moby-Dick, Chapter 3: The Spouter-Inn

Adaptation principle: Track only the narrator’s concrete moves: entering, asking for a bed, refusing and improvising, confronting the landlord and understanding the 'head,' accepting the room, witnessing the harpooneer’s rites, the bed-springing confrontation, and the final acceptance that closes the chapter’s turn.

## Character Continuity

- Ishmael (narrator) (Narrator, traveler seeking a bed): Young adult sailor, average build, clean-shaven, dark hair, wearing a worn dark monkey jacket over a plain shirt, rough trousers, and sea-scuffed boots; carries himself wary but curious.
- Peter Coffin (landlord) (Innkeeper of the Spouter-Inn): Lean New Englander, late middle-age, angular face with a wry grin, hair tied back or under a cap; simple waistcoat and rolled-up shirtsleeves; practical hands that plane wood or whittle.
- Queequeg (harpooneer) (South Seas harpooneer and Ishmael’s future bedfellow): Tall, powerfully built; skin with a purplish-yellow cast; face, torso, and limbs tattooed in dark square patterns; mostly bald with a small scalp-knot; wears a heavy grego/wrapall and a shaggy poncho-like cloak with quill-like tags when arriving; carries a tomahawk-shaped pipe, a seaman’s bag, a preserved head in trade, and a small polished wooden idol.

- harpoon by bed: Tall iron harpoon standing upright at the bed’s head. Avoid confusion: Keep as background prop; not used as weapon in scene.

- papered fireboard: Thin panel with printed paper covering the fireplace opening. Avoid confusion: Do not include readable text or imagery; it is removed for the idol.
- shaggy poncho-like cloak: Thick, door-mat-like garment with a central slit and small tinkling quill-like tags on the edge. Avoid confusion: Garment, not an actual mat; worn as a poncho.

## Action-Oriented Synopsis

I entered the Spouter-Inn and, after close study, made out the smoke-dark painting as a ship in a gale with a whale about to impale himself upon her masts. I asked the landlord for a bed and, told the house was full, conditionally agreed to share a harpooneer’s blanket. I then changed my mind about sharing, resolved to try the bench, and found my makeshift bed foiled by knots and bitter draughts. Irritated by talk of “selling his head,” I demanded the truth and learned the harpooneer had been peddling embalmed New Zealand heads. Accepting I would not see him that night, I let the landlord lead me to the prodigious bed and looked over the harpooneer’s gear. Half-dozing, I watched the harpooneer enter, reveal his tattooed body, set up a little wooden idol, burn shavings and biscuit, and pray in strange gutturals. Tomahawk-pipe in mouth, he sprang into bed with me, I cried out, and the landlord rushed in to assure me Queequeg meant no harm. I decided better sleep with a sober cannibal than a drunken Christian, had him stash the tomahawk-pipe, turned in again, and slept soundly.

## Caption Spine

1. I entered the Spouter-Inn and, after much pondering and throwing open the little window, I made out the smoke-dark painting: a half-foundered ship and an exasperated whale springing upon her three masts.
2. I sought the landlord for a room, and being told the house was full, I consented—if the harpooneer were not decidedly objectionable—to share a blanket rather than wander further on so bitter a night.

4. Provoked by his bamboozling yarn of selling his head, I demanded the truth, and heard it plain—embalmed New Zealand heads, peddled on a Saturday night.
5. Seeing it was Sunday and he’d come to anchor elsewhere, I let the landlord lead me to the prodigious bed and I eyed the room and the harpooneer’s outlandish gear.
6. Dozing at last, I saw the harpooneer come in with a New Zealand head, undress to tattooed squares, set a little wooden idol in the fireplace, burn shavings and biscuit, and sing out in strange gutturals.
7. Then, tomahawk-pipe between his teeth, he sprang into bed with me; I sang out, and the landlord burst in with a light to swear Queequeg wouldn’t harm a hair of my head.
8. Better sleep with a sober cannibal than a drunken Christian, thought I; he stashed the tomahawk-pipe, I turned in again, and never slept better in my life.
