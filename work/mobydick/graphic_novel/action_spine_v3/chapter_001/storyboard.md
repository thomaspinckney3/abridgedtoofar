# Moby-Dick — Loomings (Action Spine)

Source chapter: Chapter 1: Loomings

Adaptation principle: Condense the chapter to Ishmael’s concrete decisions: go to sea, choose the common-sailor role, and fix on a whaling voyage; fold the chapter’s analogies and reasons into first-person captions without staging hypotheticals.

## Character Continuity

- Ishmael (narrator and prospective sailor): A lean young man in plain 1840s working clothes: dark wool pea coat, simple shirt, rough trousers, worn boots, knit watch cap; clean-shaven or light stubble; thoughtful, reserved demeanor.

## Prop Continuity

- coin_purse: A small, worn brown leather coin purse with a simple clasp; only a few coins inside. Avoid confusion: Not a modern wallet or billfold; do not show paper currency.
- dock_spiles_and_ropes: Weathered wooden pier posts with coiled tarred hemp ropes and mooring lines. Avoid confusion: Not decorative nautical props; avoid modern synthetic lines or bright plastic buoys.

## Action-Oriented Synopsis

I decide to go to sea to cure my gloomy restlessness. I choose to ship as a common sailor rather than as a passenger, officer, or cook. I set my course specifically for a whaling voyage, drawn by the great whale and remote seas.

## Caption Spine

1. Call me Ishmael; finding a damp, drizzly November in my soul, I take to the ship and set my course for the watery part of the world.
2. No, when I go to sea, I go as a simple sailor right before the mast, content to be ordered about and to be paid for it.
3. By reason of these things, I fixed on a whaling voyage—the great whale and the wild, distant seas swayed me, and the wonder-world swung open.

## Omitted Material

- **Panoramas of Manhattan’s water-gazers, silent sentinels along the Battery, and crowds pacing to the water.:** Mood and social tableau; no change to Ishmael’s situation.
- **Country paths to water, the metaphysical professor in the desert, and the maxim that meditation and water are wedded forever.:** Rhetorical illustration; does not advance the action.
- **The painter of the Saco valley, the waterless prairies, and the Niagara comparison.:** Analogical support; not an action turn.
- **Cato’s sword, pistol and ball, Persians and Greeks holding the sea holy, and Narcissus drowning in his image.:** Philosophical and mythic comparisons; not present-time events.
- **Hypotheticals about being ordered to sweep decks and the archangel Gabriel’s opinion.:** Imagined scenarios; not enacted in the chapter.
- **Extended digression on paying versus being paid and cheerful perdition.:** Reasoning folded into the sailor-role decision; not a separate action.
- **Pythagorean maxim, leaders and commonalty air, and second-hand atmosphere.:** Essay aside; no decision or path change.
- **The Fates as stage managers and the program list (“Grand Contested Election... Whaling Voyage... Bloody Battle in Affghanistan”).:** Figurative device; not a visible event.
- **Catalog of Patagonian sights and the thousand marvels attending the whale.:** Color and inducement folded into the whaling-choice frame.

## Visual Safety Notes

- Do not literalize metaphors or similes (no Fates, no Narcissus, no stage programs, no pistols, no Gabriel).
- No whales should appear in these frames; the whale is a motive, not present in Chapter 1.
- Avoid readable text on ships, signs, posters, or papers.
- Keep background figures indistinct so the driver action remains Ishmael’s decision and resolve.
- Maintain Ishmael’s consistent 1840s working-clothes look across all frames; no officer insignia or cook’s apron.
- Do not introduce anachronistic props (no modern ropes, bags, or clothing).
