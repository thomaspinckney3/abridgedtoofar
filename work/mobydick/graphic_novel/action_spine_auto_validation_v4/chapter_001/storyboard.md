# Loomings — Action Spine (Frames 3–8)

Source chapter: Moby-Dick; or, The Whale — Chapter 1. Loomings

Adaptation principle: Condense Ishmael’s reflective prelude into concrete, sequential choices: from gloom to the waterfront, from going to sea to choosing a common berth, then fixing on whaling and welcoming it, while keeping the visuals grounded in present-city moments and placing philosophy in first-person captions.

## Action-Oriented Synopsis

Sunk in a grim mood, I decide it is high time to go to sea again. I walk the Manhattan waterfront among crowds of water-gazers, confirming my pull toward the ocean. I choose to ship as a common sailor rather than as a passenger, officer, or cook, accepting the humbling work. I resolve on a whaling voyage in particular, feeling led by inscrutable Fates even as I call it my choice. I welcome the voyage, stirred by the great whale and the lure of forbidden seas.

## Caption Spine

1. Whenever it is a damp, drizzly November in my soul, I account it high time to get to sea as soon as I can.
2. I circumambulate the insular city of the Manhattoes on a dreamy Sabbath afternoon and look at the crowds of water-gazers.
3. No, when I go to sea, I go as a simple sailor, right before the mast—never as passenger, Commodore, Captain, or Cook—and I grin and bear the ordering about.
4. I take it into my head to go on a whaling voyage, though the invisible police officer of the Fates seems to dog and influence me.
5. By reason of these things, the whaling voyage was welcome, as endless processions of the whale and one grand hooded phantom floated into my soul.

## Omitted Material

- **Extended analogies about rivers, lakes, prairies, the Saco valley artist, and Niagara.:** Rhetorical examples that elaborate the pull of water but do not cause a new action; their meaning is folded into waterfront observation captions.
- **Philosophical asides about passengers paying versus being paid, and cheerful perdition.:** Motivational color, not a separate decision; subsumed under the choice to ship as a common sailor.
- **Hypothetical deck-sweeping, archangel Gabriel, and the ‘universal thump.’:** Hypotheticals and generalizations; rules prohibit staging them as visible events when not in the story-present.
- **Invocation of Persians, Greeks, Narcissus, and the ‘ungraspable phantom of life.’:** Philosophical and mythic justification without discrete action; retained as tone within captions but not visualized.
- **Providential playbill (“Grand Contested Election… WHALING VOYAGE… BLOODY BATTLE…”).:** Metaphorical program note; no present action and would introduce unreadable/on-screen text.
- **Opening self-introduction “Call me Ishmael.”:** Iconic identification but not a driving action; space is reserved for the chapter’s decision spine.

## Visual Safety Notes

- Do not depict whales, phantoms, or mythic imagery; they are inner visions and should remain in captions only.
- Avoid readable signage, ship names, flags with text, or any legible lettering in the harbor scenes (No readable text).
- Do not stage hypothetical events (e.g., a captain ordering sweeping, archangels) as literal visuals.
- Keep ships and waterfront generic and unnamed; no invented specific entities or labels.
- Use crowds and sailors as indistinct, non-specific figures to avoid inventing named people.
