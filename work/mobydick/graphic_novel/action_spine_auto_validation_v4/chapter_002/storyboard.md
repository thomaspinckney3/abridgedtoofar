# Moby-Dick, Ch. 2 — The Carpet-Bag: Action Spine and Frames

Source chapter: 2. The Carpet-Bag

Adaptation principle: Condense the chapter to Ishmael’s concrete movement: arrival, delay, frugal lodging hunt, misstep into a church, and resolve for the Spouter Inn; fold digressions and comparisons into captions or omit them when they don’t change the action.

## Action-Oriented Synopsis

I arrive in New Bedford with my carpet-bag and discover the Nantucket packet has sailed, forcing me to wait until Monday. With little silver, I decide to find cheap lodging for the intervening nights and to ask prices without being particular. I pass the jolly, costly inns and head waterward, limping on patched boots toward the cheapest quarters. I blunder into a negro church, choke on ash, take in the fierce sermon, and back out again. I come upon the Spouter Inn, weigh its ominous sign against my purse, judge it cheap, and resolve to try it.

## Caption Spine

1. Quitting old Manhatto with my carpet-bag, I came to New Bedford, only to learn the Nantucket packet had sailed, and I must wait till Monday.
2. With but a few pieces of silver, I set about finding where to eat and sleep, resolving to inquire the price and not be too particular.
3. I passed the Crossed Harpoons and the Sword-Fish—too expensive and jolly—and by instinct followed the streets waterward for the cheapest inns, my patched boots weary on the flinty frost.
4. Blundering through an open door, I stumbled over an ash-box into a negro church, a black Angel of Doom beating a book, and, muttering wretched entertainment at the sign of the Trap, I backed out.
5. I came upon the Spouter Inn with its creaking sign and the name Peter Coffin—ominous enough—yet I judged it the very spot for cheap lodgings, scraped the ice from my feet, and resolved to see what sort of a place it might be.
