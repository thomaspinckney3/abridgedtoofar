# Moby Dick Chapter 1 Four-Frame Storyboard

Source chapter: Chapter 1, "Loomings"

Adaptation principle: one first-person spine sentence per frame. The image may be stylized and simplified, but it must not add unsupported narrative content. Do not show a whale unless the scene physically contains one; named concepts in narration should be carried by caption and by character response, not by invented visual entities.

## Frame 1

Caption: Call me Ishmael.

Shot: medium portrait / establishing

Caption zone: top_left

Image prompt: A lean young man in a threadbare dark coat and seaman's cap stands alone at the edge of a winter city street, anonymous passersby blurred behind him, harbor masts faint in the distance. He is not posing heroically; he looks observant, dry, and inward. Muted ink-and-wash style, cold gray light, simplified background. Leave clean negative space in the upper-left for the caption. No readable text.

Purpose: Establish Ishmael as the narrator before any plot movement.

## Frame 2

Caption: Whenever I feel that damp, drizzly November in my soul, I know it is high time to get to sea.

Shot: wide

Caption zone: top_center

Image prompt: Ishmael walks through a damp, wintry Manhattan street toward the Battery, shoulders slightly hunched, hands in pockets, breath visible. The street is wet and gray, with sparse pedestrians and distant ship rigging pulling the eye toward the water. Stylized but source-grounded; no symbolic objects beyond weather, city, harbor, and Ishmael's movement. Leave clean negative space at the top-center for the caption. No readable text.

Purpose: Turn melancholy into a physical movement toward the sea.

## Frame 3

Caption: I do not go as a passenger, nor as an officer, but before the mast, where I may be ordered about, paid for my pains, and knocked into right temper.

Shot: medium

Caption zone: bottom_right

Image prompt: Dockside beside a working ship: Ishmael stands among common sailors, sleeves and coat ready for work, looking toward ropes, tar bucket, coils, and the foremast rather than toward a passenger gangway or officer's quarterdeck. A sailor's hand gestures him toward the work. Keep it practical and humble, not grand. Leave clean negative space in the bottom-right for the caption. No readable text.

Purpose: Show his chosen role: work before the mast, not comfort or command.

## Frame 4

Caption: The water draws me, and of all voyages, the whaling voyage draws me most: the great whale opens the wonder-world before me.

Shot: wide / visionary harbor

Caption zone: top_right

Image prompt: Ishmael stands on a cold harbor pier looking out toward a whaling ship and open water. Rigging, cold mist, and the dark hull of the ship carry the sense of danger and mystery; Ishmael's posture and intent gaze show that his desire has narrowed toward whaling. Do not show a whale, whale silhouette, sea creature, hunt, violence, or other imagined entity. Stylized ink-and-wash, simplified scenery, source-grounded content only. Leave clean negative space in the upper-right for the caption. No readable text.

Purpose: Make the final turn from general sea-longing to the specific pull of whaling.
