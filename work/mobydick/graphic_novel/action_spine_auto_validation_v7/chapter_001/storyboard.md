# Moby-Dick — Loomings (Chapter 1) — Action Spine

Source chapter: Chapter 1. Loomings

Adaptation principle: Preserve the narrator identity hook and compress the chapter’s movement into three concrete choices: go to sea now, choose the common-sailor role, and fix on a whaling voyage. Fold the many rhetorical examples and reasons into the captions over real, present-tense postures; omit tableaux that only illustrate general attraction to water or philosophy.

## Action-Oriented Synopsis

I introduce myself as Ishmael. I decide it is high time to go to sea to cure my spleen. I choose to ship as a simple sailor rather than as a passenger or officer. I resolve on a whaling voyage, welcoming its peril and wonder.

## Caption Spine

1. Call me Ishmael.
2. Whenever I find myself growing grim, I account it high time to get to sea, so I thought I would sail about a little and see the watery part of the world.
3. No, when I go to sea, I go as a simple sailor, right before the mast, not as a passenger, nor a Commodore, Captain, or Cook, for I abominate respectable toils and prefer being paid, the wholesome exercise, and the pure air of the forecastle.
4. Now I take it into my head to go on a whaling voyage; the great whale and the wild distant seas sway me, and by reason of these things the voyage is welcome.

## Omitted Material

- **Crowds of water-gazers around the Battery and across the city waterfront.:** Context tableau illustrating a general human attraction to water; does not change Ishmael’s situation or decision.
- **Country paths leading to lakes, the metaphysical professor, and the maxim that meditation and water are wedded.:** Rhetorical examples supporting the sea’s pull; no present action.
- **Artist’s landscape with the magic stream, Prairies without water, Niagara as sand.:** Hypothetical and comparative imagery; not a story-present event.
- **Persians, Greeks, and Narcissus reflections.:** Philosophical and mythic justification; folded into the decision to seek the sea.
- **Cato’s sword and pistol-and-ball comparison.:** Analogies for mood relief; the actionable part is choosing to go to sea.
- **Universal thump, New Testament scales, and obedience to old sea-captains.:** Attitude toward hierarchy; supports the single choice to be a common sailor.
- **Act of paying vs. being paid, and jokes about money and perdition.:** Reasons for choosing the sailor role; integrated into that one frame.
- **Stage-manager Fates and the printed "programme" of history.:** Metaphorical framing of destiny; not a physical event in the scene.
- **Broiled fowl and Egyptian mummies in pyramids.:** Humorous aside tied to refusing the cook’s role; extraneous to the action spine.

## Visual Safety Notes

- Do not depict whales, phantom figures, mythic scenes, or any imagined tableaux; keep visuals to Ishmael’s present world.
- Avoid readable signage, ship names, posters, or street text in backgrounds; include the note No readable text in prompts.
- Do not place Ishmael actively aboard ship performing duties; he is deciding and approaching the water, not yet shipped.
- Do not show labeled uniforms or ranks (Commodore, Captain, Cook); convey his choice through posture and setting only.
- Do not invent specific named locations (e.g., Battery, Manhattan) or show maps; keep the waterfront generic.
- Use ships, piers, ropes, and masts as general maritime props only, without logos or legible markings.
