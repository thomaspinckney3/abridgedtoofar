# Loomings — Action Spine

Source chapter: Moby-Dick; or, The Whale — Chapter 1: Loomings

Adaptation principle: Condense Ishmael’s reflective prelude into concrete first-person decisions and acceptances that move him from malaise toward a whaling voyage. Fold catalogues and analogies into motive lines; stage Ishmael in simple, source-grounded waterfront settings while keeping abstractions and hypotheticals in captions only.

## Action-Oriented Synopsis

Feeling low and restless, Ishmael decides it is time to go to sea. He refuses to travel as a passenger or take rank and instead chooses to ship before the mast. He swallows his pride about being ordered around, prefers being paid to paying, and, pushed by fate and a longing for remote dangers, fixes on a whaling voyage, which he welcomes with mounting wonder.

## Caption Spine

1. Growing grim and drizzly in my soul, I account it high time to get to sea.
2. I never go as passenger, nor captain, nor cook; when I go, I go as a simple sailor, right before the mast.
3. Though they order me about and touch my honor, I submit, for the universal thump is passed round and I am content.
4. They pay me for my trouble; and there is all the difference in the world between paying and being paid.
5. Dogged by the invisible police of the Fates and tormented with an itch for things remote, I take it into my head to go on a whaling voyage.
6. So the whaling voyage was welcome; the flood-gates of the wonder-world swung open, and inmost I carry the great whale.

## Omitted Material

- **Extended catalogue of water-gazers around Manhattan and Sabbath promenades.:** Rhetorical tableau that illustrates a general human draw to water without advancing Ishmael’s specific action.
- **Country paths leading to pools, the Saco valley painter, Prairies in June, and Niagara comparisons.:** Analogies supporting the water-meditation theme; folded into the opening decision to go to sea.
- **Broiled fowls, Egyptian mummies, and cook’s glory digression.:** Comic aside that does not change the decision to ship before the mast.
- **Detailed moral-philosophical weighing of sweeping decks and New Testament scales.:** Hypothetical example; the acceptance of being ordered is captured without staging the hypothetical act.
- **The Providence playbill with the Presidency and Afghanistan battle.:** Figurative framing of Fate; the essential pull of Fate is kept within the whaling decision caption.
- **Theological and ironic riff on money as root of ills versus the pleasure of being paid.:** Philosophical flourish; the action turn is simply choosing the paid sailor’s berth.

## Visual Safety Notes

- Do not depict hypotheticals (e.g., Ishmael being ordered to sweep decks) as literal events; show acceptance via posture only.
- Avoid visualizing imagined whales or metaphors; keep whales and Fate in captions, not in imagery.
- No readable text anywhere: no ship names, signs, tickets, newspapers, or labels.
- Use generic ships, officers, and sailors; do not introduce named vessels, harpoons, or specific whaling gear not present in the chapter scene.
- Keep settings generic to a mid-19th-century waterfront; avoid identifiable modern elements.
