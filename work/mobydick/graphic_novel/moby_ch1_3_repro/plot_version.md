## Scope

Ch. 1–3 excerpt from Ishmael’s opening reflections through his first night at the Spouter-Inn with Queequeg.

## Pacing Strategy

- **Summary:** Compress Melville’s reflective essays into selective captions riding over purposeful action. Privilege concrete movement: Ishmael walking to water, traveling to New Bedford, entering the Spouter-Inn, barroom business, the late-night room scene, and the bed mix-up with Queequeg. Preserve iconic voice lines and key motifs while trimming rhetorical digressions.
- **Target Reader Experience:** A brisk, atmospheric arrival sequence that moves from melancholic pull of the sea to comic unease and finally humanizing acceptance, with Melville’s voice surfacing in sharp captions.
- **Compression Ratio:** moderate
- **Main Risks:** Losing Melville’s essayistic music and humor if over-trimmed; Confusing time and place without strong anchors between reflections and present action; Othering Queequeg if staging leans into fear without the humanizing turn; Overloading barroom exposition and object-lore (painting, weapons) at the cost of forward motion

- Across pages, balance dense VO passages with silent or near-silent panels to preserve pace and allow art to carry mood.
