## Chapter None. ETYMOLOGY

Supplied by a pale, threadbare Usher dusting his lexicons, a reminder that words, like men, carry their histories to the grave. He quotes Hackluyt: leave out the H in whale and you “deliver that which is not true.” Then the dictionaries roll the word from vault to vault—hval, hvalt, wallow—until all tongues converge upon the vast, round-rolling creature.

## Chapter None. EXTRACTS

Here the poor devil of a Sub-Sub Librarian burrows up quotations, sacred and profane, in which Leviathan gleams: “And God created great whales,” says Genesis; Job beholds his “path to shine,” Isaiah sees a crooked serpent; Pliny weighs his acres-long back; Lucian meets a maw that “swallows all.” Sailors, kings, poets, philosophers, and naturalists speak: Hobbes christens his Commonwealth Leviathan; Montaigne sleeps safe within his mouth; Milton lenses his hugeness; Paley measures his heart; Scoresby times his line. From Nantucket songs to Burke’s Parliament, from Bacon’s Psalms to a Nantuckois harpooneer, the whale swims through the world’s thought—an omen and an engine, myth and commodity. “Give it up, Sub-Subs!” cries Ishmael’s commentator, yet sets these shards before us like a glittering midden by the sea.

## Chapter 1. Loomings

Call me Ishmael. Some years ago—never mind how long precisely—whenever a drizzly November grows in my soul, I go to sea. To knock hats off on shore would be poor philosophy; water is my pistol and ball. Men are magnetized by the ocean; churches, cities, meadows all run to its edges. “Meditation and water are wedded for ever.”

Not as a passenger, nor Commodore, nor Cook: as a common sailor. They pay me for my trouble; they take the discipline out of me. The “universal thump” is better rubbed in among tar and tackle than at a counting-house stool. Yet why whaling rather than coasting? Some invisible stage-manager wrote it in Providence’s playbill; and chiefly, it was the overwhelming idea of the great whale himself—his portentous bulk and nameless perils—that unmoored me. I itch for things remote; “I love to sail forbidden seas.” So in my mind two by two there floated processions of whales, and among them all one grand hooded phantom—snow-hilled in the air.

## Chapter 2. The Carpet-Bag

I packed a shirt or two and went to New Bedford, gateway to Nantucket when the packet’s sailed. It was a black December night and I was near-penniless, nosing the waterfront for a cheap bed. The Spouter Inn creaked under a sign like a geyser; its keeper one Peter Coffin—an omen I chose to ignore. Inside: wainscots like a condemned ship; a smoky painting of chaos wherein, by study, you discern a ship impaled by a leaping whale; harpoons and pagan clubs bristling like a museum of war. The landlord told me all beds were taken, save I would share a harpooneer’s blanket. So I supped and waited, suspicious of a “dark complexioned” fellow out peddling preserved heads.

## Chapter 3. The Spouter-Inn

A riotous crew stormed in from a three-year voyage, icicles in their beards; among them a tall, burnt man called Bulkington, whose sober gaze I marked. Midnight neared; still no harpooneer. The landlord revealed he sold “’balmed New Zealand heads,” but would not bring them to church on the Sabbath. My prejudices wrestled with my poverty; the fire burned low. At last he came: Queequeg of Rokovoko—tattooed over face and limb, tomahawk pipe in hand, a small black idol cradled like a child. He made a shrine of the fireplace and burned a biscuit for his god, then blew out the lamp and leapt into bed—his harpoon between his teeth. After some parley and the landlord’s assurance that “Queequeg here wouldn’t harm a hair of your head,” I turned in beside a sober cannibal. “Better sleep with a sober cannibal than a drunken Christian.”

## Chapter 4. The Counterpane

At dawn his tattooed arm embraced me like a patchwork quilt, and for a moment I took it for the counterpane itself. Memories of childhood terrors brushed me, but I woke to friendship. He shaved with his harpoon, dressed with the unstudied modesty of a savage gentleman, and we went to breakfast.

## Chapter 5. Breakfast

Harpooneers, mates, coopers—an unshorn company fed in abashed silence. Only Queequeg, at the head, was cool, drawing beefsteaks toward him with his harpoon like a marshal’s baton. Afterward he sat among them with his hat on, smoking the hatchet-pipe.

## Chapter 6. The Street

New Bedford is a Babel of Lascars, Feegeeans, sailors and green Vermont boys in bombazine cloaks. The harpoons around a mansion’s gate tell what dowers the daughters of this city carry. Patrician houses grew from “the bottom of the sea”; women bloom like red roses; and the purse of New Bedford smells of “spermaceti” and iron hoops.

## Chapter 7. The Chapel

In the Whaleman’s Chapel a drove of silent men and widows sit under marble tablets carved with loss: a boat’s crew “towed out of sight by a Whale,” a captain killed “in the bows of his boat,” a youth “lost overboard.” The “speechlessly quick chaotic bundling of a man into Eternity” is the business of whaling, yet Ishmael feels merry: “Take my body—’tis not me.”

## Chapter 8. The Pulpit

Father Mapple—a sailor turned preacher—climbs the rope ladder to a ship’s-bow pulpit. On the wall a painting of a stormed ship with an angelic light above; the Bible rests on a fiddle-headed scroll like a prow. The pulpit is the world’s prow: from thence the storm is first descried.

## Chapter 9. The Sermon

He prays deep as a bell tolling undersea, then sings Jonah’s hymn of deliverance. The text: “And God had prepared a great fish to swallow up Jonah.” Mapple’s lesson runs double: to sinners, obedience is hard but commanded; to preachers, dread to flee your Nineveh. Jonah’s sin was not his failure, but his disobedience; his repentance true, leaving “deliverance to God,” content to look towards the Holy Temple in the belly of hell. Then he lashes the pulpit’s timbers with woe on timorous pilots who “pour oil upon the waters when God has brewed them into a gale,” and blesses that delight which comes to him who “acknowledges no law or lord but the Lord his God.”

## Chapter 10. A Bosom Friend

Back at the Inn, Queequeg sat whittling his idol and counting a book’s pages by fifties with delight. Savage or no, “you cannot hide the soul.” I admired his self-possessed solitude among strangers; we smoked and shared the tomahawk. He pressed his forehead to mine and declared us “married”—bosom friends. He split his silver into two heaps and shoved half to me. That night he made an altar; I bowed with him: “Pagans and all included—can God be jealous of a little bit of black wood?” Better to do unto him as I’d have him do.

## Chapter 11. Nightgown

We sat up smoking beneath a blue canopy of smoke, and he told me, in his broken idiom, of Rokovoko—an island “not down in any map; true places never are.”

## Chapter 12. Biographical

He was the son of a chief, with a king for a father and a priest for an uncle. Refused passage by a Sag Harbor ship, he ambushed it in a strait, swam aboard, and would not let go the ring-bolt until shipped as a hand. He meant to bring back Christian arts to his people but learned instead “Christians could be both miserable and wicked.” Still, he would sail with me to Nantucket.

## Chapter 13. Wheelbarrow

We wheeled our gear to the packet Moss. Queequeg told how he once shouldered a wheelbarrow with his chest aboard, and how a ship’s captain washed his hands in a sacred punchbowl at a Rokovoko wedding. The Moss ran down the Acushnet, the air tartar-bracing. A greenhorn mocked Queequeg’s look; the harpooneer picked him up and popped him into the air like a cork, then later saved him from a parted boom and the sea. “It’s a mutual, joint-stock world,” said Queequeg.

## Chapter 14. Nantucket

An elbow of sand, all beach, “without a background”; yet from this bare scoria, naked islanders issued and conquered the watery world—“two thirds of this terraqueous globe are the Nantucketer’s.”

## Chapter 15. Chowder

At the Try Pots, under a gallows-like sign of two pots, Mrs. Hussey served clam, then cod chowder, then chowder for breakfast. Fish bones paved the yard, a cow fed on fish heads, and Mrs. Hussey confiscated Queequeg’s harpoon before bed.

## Chapter 16. The Ship

Yojo, Queequeg’s god, appointed me to choose our ship alone. Three lay at the wharf: Devil-dam, Tit-bit, Pequod. The Pequod—old-school, jaw-bones for arches, whale teeth for pins—was owned by Peleg and Bildad, Quaker sea-kings. Peleg swore and bargained; Bildad mumbled Scripture and would have set my lay at the seven hundred and seventy-seventh; Peleg made it the three hundredth. Ahab, their captain? “Has he not got a leg?” said Peleg; “chewed off by the monstrousest parmacetty.” Ahab is “a moody—good—man,” learned and ungentle, “above the common”; “stricken, blasted… has his humanities.”

## Chapter 17. The Ramadan

Queequeg kept a day’s fast, humbling himself to Yojo. I respected his rite; when he sat all day like a carved image, I chafed; when he woke, I lectured him on hygienic religion, then begged breakfast.

## Chapter 18. His Mark

Peleg demanded conversion papers; I swore him a deacon of the “great First Congregation of this whole worshipping world.” Queequeg signed with his tattooed round “X,” while Bildad thrust tracts into his hands “for the soul.”

## Chapter 19. The Prophet

A pock-pitted figure named Elijah hailed us: had we shipped in that ship? “Anything down there about your souls?” He hinted of Old Thunder—Ahab—and vanished and reappeared like a tatter of omen down the waterfront.

## Chapter 20. All Astir

The Pequod filled with spars, hoops, staves, coil upon coil; Aunt Charity bustled with flannel and needles; Bildad ticked off items; Peleg roared at men; no Ahab yet. We were to sail soon.

## Chapter 21. Going Aboard

Before dawn Elijah stopped us again, cracked with warnings: did we see men slip aboard? We thought we had. On deck, all was still but for an old rigger asleep and the ship’s gentle creak. “Ahab came on board last night,” the man said, and Starbuck’s voice soon rang clear: “Eight bells there!”

## Chapter 22. Merry Christmas

Peleg and Bildad mustered the crew. With psalmody on one side and curses on the other, they drove the capstan; anchor up, royals set; the Pequod slid out into the icy Atlantic. In the offing, Peleg and Bildad went down the side; the pilot boat sheered off. Bildad blessed us with molasses and prayers; Peleg swore a hot supper in Nantucket when we returned—this day three years.

## Chapter 23. The Lee Shore

Bulkington stood at the helm the first night out—a soul forever seaward. Better to perish in the “howling infinite” than be dashed safe against the lee: true thinkers keep the sea of independence.

## Chapter 24. The Advocate

Whaling is not unpoetical nor base. Who lights the world’s lamps? Who first broke through jealous coasts to open harbors? The whale-ship—pioneer and mother to commerce and missions. Cook has fame, but a score of nameless Nantucketers faced fiercer perils. Whaling is imperial; the whale itself has figured in kingly pageants and constellations. If in me there is aught worth, “I prospectively ascribe all the honor and the glory to whaling; for a whale-ship was my Yale College and my Harvard.”

## Chapter 25. Postscript

At coronations, kings are anointed: with what oil but sperm’s sweetest, unpolluted drop? Loyal Britons—remember.

## Chapter 26. Knights and Squires

Starbuck, the chief mate, is a New England Quaker—thin, steadfast, conscientious; brave where reason reigns, but he dreads the “more spiritual terrors… menacing from the frown of an enraged and mighty man.” He will have “no man in my boat who is not afraid of a whale.” Stubb, the second, is easy as a stage-driver, whistling at death; his pipe disinfects all mortal tribulations. Flask, short and pugnacious, thinks whales “magnified mice” and follows for the fun. Their squires: Queequeg for Starbuck; Tashtego, a Gay-Head Indian, for Stubb; Daggoo, an imperial African, for Flask.

## Chapter 27. Knights and Squires

The cosmopolitan crew—Isolatoes from all isles—federate under Ahab’s keel: a world’s deputation bound to lay humanity’s grievances before one bar.

## Chapter 28. Ahab

Days out of port Ahab appeared at last: bronze, scarred by a livid seam from face to foot; his right leg a carved ivory from a sperm jaw; his stance pinned in a hole by the mizzen shrouds, his gaze fixed “beyond the ship’s ever-pitching prow.” He paced and stood, a crucifixion in his face, silent as a figurehead; the crew felt a troubled master-eye. Then, slowly, the weather mellowed; he came more often to the air; a faint blossom of look flowered on his winter face.

## Chapter 29. Enter Ahab; to Him, Stubb

At night Ahab’s bone leg jarred the deck like a cannonball. Stubb, with his good-humored insolence, begged for a plug of tow to muffle it. Ahab flared: “Down, dog, and kennel!” Stubb, for once, warred with a thought—pray for him, or strike him? He went to sleep, his eleventh commandment being “Think not.”

## Chapter 30. The Pipe

Ahab found even his pipe no soothed charm and tossed it into the sea. “What business have I with this pipe? … Here have I been unconsciously toiling, not pleasuring.” He lurched away, slouched hat over brow.

## Chapter 31. Queen Mab

Stubb dreamed that Ahab had kicked him with his ivory leg, but since “’twas but a whalebone cane,” the insult was small. A hump-backed sea-fiend called him “wise Stubb”: let Ahab kick—account it honor; but “never kick back,” for “you can’t help yourself.” From the quarter-deck came Ahab’s cry: “If ye see a white one, split your lungs for him!”

## Chapter 32. Cetology

Ishmael, soberly mad, undertakes a classification of whales—Folios (Sperm, Right, Fin-back, Hump, Razor-back, Sulphur-bottom), Octavos (Grampus, Black-fish, Narwhal, Killer, Thrasher), Duodecimos (Porpoises). He laughs at his “draught of a draught,” but reminds, “the whale is a fish”—at least by Jonah and common sense—and no full-length portrait can be had of him.

## Chapter 33. The Specksnyder

The old Dutch rank gave the chief harpooneer sway in whale-matters; now he’s a senior spear, eating aft but living forward. All pomp aside, the Pequod keeps the quarter-deck’s punctilio. Ahab, least showy of captains, yet uses these “external arts” to entrench his sultanism. “Oh, Ahab! what shall be grand in thee… must be plucked at from the skies.”

## Chapter 34. The Cabin-Table

Ahab dines as a lion among cubs; the mates sit like schoolboys, cutting their beef noiselessly. Mates depart; the harpooneers take the cabin by storm, impaling steaks with iron, sharpening knives on whetstones, terrifying poor Dough-Boy the steward. Yet in truth they live forward; Ahab lives alone.

## Chapter 35. The Mast-Head

Man at a mast-head, the world gliding beneath, is lulled into such dreamy metaphysics he might forget whales entirely. Beware hiring “lean-browed” philosophers for your look-outs; they will “tow you ten wakes round the world and never make you one pint of sperm the richer.”

## Chapter 36. The Quarter-Deck

One afternoon Ahab nails a Spanish ounce of gold to the mast. “Whosoever of ye raises me a white-headed whale with a wrinkled brow and a crooked jaw… he shall have this gold ounce!” It is Moby Dick he names—the whale that “dismasted” him. Starbuck protests: “I came here to hunt whales, not my commander’s vengeance.” Ahab’s soul flames: “I will strike the sun if it insulted me!” and declares the white whale the “inscrutable malice” he hates. He needles the mates into complicity; grog is poured; harpoons serve for chalices. The harpooneers swear death to Moby Dick; Starbuck pales and prays, “God keep me!—keep us all!”

## Chapter 37. Sunset

Ahab alone in his cabin: the iron crown of Lombardy splits his brow. He will be “prophet and fulfiller”—dismember his “dismemberer.” His path is “laid with iron rails… naught’s an angle to the iron way.”

## Chapter 38. Dusk

Starbuck leans by the mainmast: “My soul is more than matched; she’s overmanned; and by a madman!” Yet still he vows to obey and fight grim futures.

## Chapter 39. First Night-Watch

Stubb, mending a brace, laughs the world into predestination: “Come what will, one comfort’s always left—that unfailing comfort is, it’s all predestinated.”

## Chapter 40. Midnight, Forecastle

A chorus of sailors—Nantucket men, Maltese, Lascars, Danes—dance, quarrel, sing in a babel of languages as the ship steers into squalls. Pip, the little black cabin-boy, shrinks under the windlass, fearing whiteness—the White Whale—and prays to the “big white God aloft.”

## Chapter 41. Moby Dick

Few had seen him; fewer fought him and lived. Moby Dick had a high pyramidical hump and a snow-white wrinkled brow; more than bulk, it was “intelligent malignity” that struck terror. Ahab’s leg was stove by him “chewed up, crunched,” and in long, mad convalescence “his torn body and gashed soul bled into one another.” He came to hate not just a brute but an abstracted malice made concrete. “He piled upon the whale’s white hump the sum of all the rage and hate felt by his whole race from Adam down.” Tools he needs—men—so he lures them; but his motive is “mad,” even as his means are lucid. To many the white seemed ubiquitous, immortal: his very spout reported in far latitudes at once. Yet whales’ mysterious migrations allow such superstition to root.

## Chapter 42. The Whiteness of the Whale

Whiteness refines beauty—the bride, the ermine, the albatross—and consecrates power—the Persian flame, St. John’s robes. Yet in the white bear and white shark it appalls; in pallor of the dead it chills; in ghosts and Death’s pale horse it terrifies. It is both “visible absence of color” and “concrete of all colors.” In that dumb blankness the soul feels the “heartless voids and immensities of the universe,” or the leprous atheism of a world stripped of painted veils. Thus Moby Dick becomes the symbol whose whiteness “heightens that terror to the furthest bounds.” Wonder you then at the fiery hunt?

## Chapter 43. Hark!

Night; men pass water to the scuttle-butt in silence. From below a cough, a sleeper’s turn? Archy whispers—something down in the after-hold—unseen men. Stubb tells him it is only three biscuits in his belly. Yet the rumor seeps.

## Chapter 44. The Chart

Ahab pores over wrinkled sea-charts, log-books, and currents; he pencils a maze of conjectural lines to thread the whale’s food, seasons, and veins. He knows he must use men, the most capricious of tools; he must still prosecute the ordinary voyage, tempering his monomania lest it break them. The Season-on-the-Line is his best chance, for there Moby Dick has been seen, but meanwhile he will lose no “chance-like” encounter. He is haunted even in sleep; the spirit in him recoils from the frantic thing he has made of his mind, yet cannot dethrone it.

## Chapter 45. The Affidavit

Lest landsmen cry fable, Ishmael testifies: whales have stove in ships; harpoons have been found years later in bodies; the Essex was sunk; an English sloop-of-war was struck; Langsdorff saw a whale lift a vessel like a raft. Credulity need not be asked: the whale will stand no nonsense.

## Chapter 46. Surmises

Ahab must keep the voyage “regular” to mask his private end. Starbuck’s soul abhors Ahab’s quest; Ahab studies such “minute atmospheric influences” as will hold men to duty while leading them to his revenge. He wisely makes room for oil and wages alongside vengeance.

## Chapter 47. The Mat-Maker

We wove a sword-mat for the boat, Queequeg beating home the woof with his oaken sword. The ship’s stillness wove me into fatalism: warp necessity, woof free will, chance the indifferent sword that “rules either and has the last featuring blow.” Tashtego’s cry split the reverie: “There she blows!”—then, “There go flukes!”—and Ahab’s “Time! time!”

## Chapter 48. The First Lowering

We lowered; Fedallah—the swart, turbaned man whose ghostly crew had slipped aboard—handled Ahab’s bow. Three mates’ boats pulled; the captain’s mysterious boat flashed from windward. A school of Right Whales cannot be hunted by us; but a lone spout beckoned. In the squall, Starbuck’s boat was dashed, we were swamped and rescued. Ishmael, chilled and laughing like a hyena, later made his will; but Ahab had shown his own private crew—five “tiger-yellow” oarsmen with Fedallah at their head.

## Chapter 49. The Hyena

“I have survived myself,” says Ishmael, making his testament on the greasy deck. The sea despises reckoning: predestination and posture both righted by laughter.

## Chapter 50. Ahab’s Boat and Crew. Fedallah

The owners had not meant Ahab to lower; he had taken private measures. The phantoms were “muffled mysteries”; Fedallah—white-turbaned, with one white tooth—neither to be known nor gainsaid. Ahab’s extra sheathing and special thigh-cleat bespoke his personal lowering: he would ride the whale “with frigate’s anchors for bridle-bitts.”

## Chapter 51. The Spirit-Spout

Night after night a solitary, motionless, silvery jet hovered ahead, alluring us on; we chased; it slid away like a spirit. Some swore it was Moby Dick himself beckoning us into his ambush; others maintained the same whale’s spout had been seen in far latitudes in the same hour.

## Chapter 52. The Albatross

We hailed a ship named the Goney; her captain dropped his trumpet as we called, “Have ye seen the White Whale?” The wind rose; no answer came; Ahab shouted to “address all future letters to the Pacific ocean… this time three years,” as the birds deserted our wake.

## Chapter 53. The Gam

Two whale-ships meeting on the high seas must gam—exchange news, letters, whaling intelligence—more sociable than men-of-war or pirates. Sometimes a gam is a parlor and a pulpit by boat between rolling worlds.

## Chapter 54. The Town-Ho’s Story

Told at Lima, under the Golden Inn’s moon: The Town-Ho leaked near the Line and made for an island haven. Radney, the ugly chief mate and part-owner, drove the men hard; Steelkilt, a tall Lakeman (inland sailor from Lake Erie) refused to sweep decks out of turn. Radney shook a hammer in his face; Steelkilt warned him “one graze” and he’d murder; the hammer grazed; Steelkilt stove in Radney’s jaw. The crew mutinied, then retreated to the forecastle; were enticed out, gagged, seized up, flogged. Three held out; Steelkilt planned to run amok with mincing-knives; two traitors bound him and gave him up; Radney flogged him despite warning. Release came; soon after, the cry: “There she rolls!”—Moby Dick. Radney stood in the bows; the boat struck; he fell upon the whale’s back; the white whale seized him in his jaws, lifted and plunged. Later, the mark of his red shirt hung in Moby Dick’s teeth. The mutineers deserted the ship at a savage isle; the captain fled to Tahiti for men; Steelkilt’s canoe intercepted him, exacted an oath to lie onshore six days, then sailed to freedom on French ships. “Where Steelkilt now is none know; but on Nantucket a widow still turns to the sea.”

## Chapter 55. Of the Monstrous Pictures of Whales

Whales as men have pictured them are monstrous: half-man, half-fish Hindu carvings, Guido’s sea-dragon, Hogarth’s corpulent beast, book-binders’ dolphins in armor. Scientific plates err as boldly: Dutch prints with perpendicular flukes; Captain Colnett’s whale with a five-foot bow-window eye; even Cuvier’s “sperm whale” a squash. You cannot fairly sketch the Leviathan: stranded skeletons mislead; the living bulk is mostly hidden beneath; he cannot be hoisted entire “to preserve all his mighty swells and undulations.”

## Chapter 56. Of the Less Erroneous Pictures… and the True Pictures of Whaling Scenes

Beale is best among draughtsmen; Scoresby shows the Right Whale, too small. Garnery’s French plates are truest to time and blood: one shows a sperm whales’ impalement of a boat—wrecked planks on his back, bobbing harpoon-poles and men’s heads; another, a Right Whale rolled like a weedy rock, his soot-black jets, the cutting-in smoke rising like a village of smithies.

## Chapter 57. Of Whales in Paint; in Teeth; in Wood; in Sheet-Iron; in Stone; in Mountains; in Stars

A beggar on Tower-hill hangs a painted loss: “whale crunches boat,” his stump proof to incredulity. Sailors skrimshander whales on teeth and busks; carve them in South Sea wood; hang brass whales for knockers; bend them into weathercocks. Hills and skylines mimic their undulations; constellations bear them—Cetus haunted beneath the Argo’s sail.

## Chapter 58. Brit

We sailed through meadows of brit—minute yellow feed for the Right Whale—“boundless fields of ripe and golden wheat.” Right Whales, secure from a Sperm Whaler, swam with open jaws, their fringe acting a Venetian sieve. The sea is a fiend to its own: secret, treacherous, cannibal; yet within us lies an “insular Tahiti… encompassed by all the horrors of the half known life.” God keep thee; push not off.

## Chapter 59. Squid

One blue morning a cream-coloured mass rose and fell—vast, furlongs wide—a nest of anaconda arms lazily writhing. “The great live squid,” Starbuck whispered, “which few whalemen beheld and lived to tell.” We pulled back in silence. The sailors’ superstitions knot around it: the Kraken might be but its fable grown vast; the sperm whale perhaps armed with teeth to rend this bed-clinging behemoth in dark zones below our sight.
