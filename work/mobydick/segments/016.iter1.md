Next morning, Monday, after disposing of the embalmed head to a barber for a block, I settled our bill—with Queequeg’s money; whereat landlord and boarders grinned at our sudden intimacy. We borrowed a wheelbarrow for my carpet-bag and his canvas sack and hammock, and down we went to the little Nantucket packet, the Moss, wheeling by turns, he pausing now and then to adjust the sheath on his harpoon barbs. Why lug it ashore, I asked, when ships find their own irons? He said he loved his own: of assured stuff, well tried, intimate with whales’ hearts; as inland mowers go with their own scythes, so he with his steel.

He told of the first wheelbarrow he ever saw at Sag Harbor—how, lashing his chest upon it, he shouldered the barrow and marched up the wharf. “Didn’t the people laugh?” I cried. He answered with another story: at Rokovoko, where the High Priest first dips his consecrating fingers in the calabash before the wedding feast, a punctilious sea-captain, thinking himself above a mere king in the king’s own house, coolly washed his hands in the sacred punchbowl. “Now,” said Queequeg, “what you tink now?—Didn’t our people laugh?”

Aboard at last, the Moss slipped down the icy Acushnet. New Bedford rose in glittering terraces; casks heaped; ships silent; hammers and forges melting pitch—one perilous voyage ending only to begin another; such the endlessness, yea, the intolerableness of all earthly effort. Gaining open water, the breeze freshened; the little Moss tossed foam like a colt his snortings. How I snuffed that Tartar air!—how I spurned that turnpike earth!—that common highway all over dented with the marks of slavish heels and hoofs; and turned me to admire the magnanimity of the sea which will permit no records. Sideways leaning, we darted; every ropeyarn tingled; the masts buckled like Indian canes in land tornadoes.

Passengers—boobies and bumpkins, green to the core—jeered at our companionship, as though a white man were anything more dignified than a whitewashed negro. One sapling mocked Queequeg; I thought the lad’s doom come; but the savage only tossed him heels-over-head, lit his tomahawk pipe, and passed it to me. The Captain stormed; Queequeg twisted his tattooed face: “him bevy small-e fish-e; Queequeg no kill-e so small-e fish-e; Queequeg kill-e big whale!” Then the mainsail’s weather-sheet parted; the boom flew; the greenhorn went overboard; all hands panic-struck. Queequeg slid to his knees, lassoed the boom; then, stripped to the waist, sprang into the freezing foam, dived, and rose with the lifeless form. They were picked up; the bumpkin restored; the captain begged his pardon. From that hour I clove to Queequeg like a barnacle—yea, till poor Queequeg took his last long dive.

Was there ever such unconsciousness? He did not seem to think that he at all deserved a medal from the Humane and Magnanimous Societies. He only asked for water—fresh water—something to wipe the brine off; then, dry clothes, pipe, and mild eyes that seemed to say—“It’s a mutual, joint-stock world, in all meridians. We cannibals must help these Christians.”
