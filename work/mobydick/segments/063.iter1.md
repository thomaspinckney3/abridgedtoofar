For the better understanding of whaling scenes, speak now of the magical, sometimes horrible whale-line. Once of the best hemp, slightly vapored with tar, it is now in the American fishery almost entirely of Manilla—though not so durable as hemp, stronger, and far more soft and elastic; and I will add (since there is an æsthetics in all things), is much more handsome and becoming to the boat, than hemp. Hemp, dusky as an Indian; Manilla, a golden-haired Circassian.

Only two-thirds of an inch thick, its one and fifty yarns each suspend one hundred and twenty pounds; the whole rope bears nearly three tons. In length, the common sperm line measures a little over two hundred fathoms. Towards the stern it is spirally coiled in the tub as a round, cheese-shaped mass of densely bedded sheaves; the least kink, in running out, would infallibly take somebody’s arm, leg, or entire body off; hence the utmost precaution in stowing. In English boats, two smaller tubs hold one continuous line; the American single tub, three feet in diameter, makes a prodigious freight for a craft with a half-inch bottom—like critical ice which bears a distributed but not a concentrated weight. With the painted canvas cover clapped on, the boat looks as if pulling off with a great wedding-cake for the whales.

Both ends of the line are exposed. The lower end, eye-spliced, hangs disengaged over the tub’s edge—first, to bend on an additional line from a neighboring boat should the whale sound beyond the first supply; second, for common safety’s sake, lest the doomed boat be dragged down after him. Before lowering, the upper end runs aft round the loggerhead, forward the boat’s length, jogs every oarsman’s wrist, passes between the men to the leaded chocks in the prow, pinned from slipping, hangs in a slight festoon over the bows, returns within, and, after a box-coil, is attached to the short-warp and thence to the harpoon.

Thus the whale-line folds the whole boat in its complicated coils, twisting and writhing round it in almost every direction. To the timid landsman’s eye, the crew seem Indian jugglers, festooned with deadliest snakes. Nor can any son of mortal woman, for the first time, seat himself amid those hempen intricacies, and, while straining his utmost at the oar, bethink him that at any unknown instant the harpoon may be darted, and all these horrible contortions be put in play like ringed lightnings; he cannot be thus circumstanced without a shudder that makes the very marrow in his bones to quiver like a shaken jelly. Yet habit—strange thing! what cannot habit accomplish?—Gayer sallies, more merry mirth, better jokes, and brighter repartees, you never heard over your mahogany, than over the half-inch white cedar; and, like the six burghers of Calais, the six men pull into the jaws of death with a halter round every neck.

Hence those repeated disasters of men taken out by the line. When it darts out, to be seated is like being among the whizzings of a steam-engine in full play; for the boat rocks like a cradle, pitching you without warning; and only by a buoyant simultaneousness of volition and action can you escape being made a Mazeppa of, and run away with where the all-seeing sun could never pierce you out.

More awful than the storm is the profound calm that precedes it—for, indeed, the calm is but the wrapper and envelope of the storm; and contains it in itself, as the seemingly harmless rifle holds the fatal powder, and the ball, and the explosion—so the graceful repose of the line before play carries more of true terror than any other aspect. But why say more? All men live enveloped in whale-lines. All are born with halters round their necks; but it is only when caught in the swift, sudden turn of death, that mortals realize the silent, subtle, ever-present perils of life. And if you be a philosopher, though seated in the whale-boat, you would not at heart feel one whit more terror than before your evening fire with a poker, and not a harpoon, by your side.
