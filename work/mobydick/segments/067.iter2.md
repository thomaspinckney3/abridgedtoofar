Stubb’s whale had been killed far from the ship; in a flat calm we made a tandem of three boats and, with eighteen men, thirty-six arms, and one hundred and eighty thumbs and fingers, slowly towed the inert, heaving bulk toward the Pequod. Three dim lights in the main-rigging guided us; drawing nearer we saw Ahab drop more lanterns, issue the usual orders for securing it for the night, hand his light to a seaman, and vanish to his cabin. Though hot in the chase, he now showed some obscure impatience or despair, as if the dead body only reminded him that Moby Dick yet lived, and a thousand other whales were nothing to his monomaniac end. Tied by the head to the stern and by the tail to the bows, the black hull of whale and ship lay close, yoked like colossal bullocks—one reclining, one standing.

A word for the flukes: the surest and most reliable hold is the tail; but as, from its greater density and death’s flexibility, it sinks low, you cannot with the hand get at it from the boat to put the chain round it. The difficulty is overcome by a small line with a float and a mid-weight, led so the float rises on the far side; having girdled the mass, the chain follows and is locked fast round the smallest part, hard by the lobes.

If Ahab grew still, Stubb—flush with conquest—bustled; the staid Starbuck even yielded him the deck. One small, helping cause of all this liveliness was soon made manifest. Stubb was a high liver; he was somewhat intemperately fond of the whale as a flavorish thing to his palate. “A steak, a steak, ere I sleep! You, Daggoo! overboard you go, and cut me one from his small!” Here be it known, that though these wild fishermen do not, as a general thing, and according to the great military maxim, make the enemy defray the current expenses of the war (at least before realizing the proceeds of the voyage), yet now and then you find some of these Nantucketers who have a genuine relish for that particular part of the Sperm Whale designated by Stubb; comprising the tapering extremity of the body.

About midnight that steak was cut and cooked; and lighted by two lanterns of sperm oil, Stubb stoutly stood up to his spermaceti supper at the capstan-head, as if that capstan were a sideboard. Nor was Stubb the only banqueter on whale’s flesh that night. Mingling their mumblings with his own mastications, thousands on thousands of sharks, swarming round the dead leviathan, smackingly feasted on its fatness. The few sleepers below in their bunks were often startled by the sharp slapping of their tails against the hull; peering over the side you could just see them wallowing in the sullen, black waters, turning on their backs as they scooped out huge globular pieces, leaving hollows like a carpenter’s countersink.

Though amid all the smoking horror and diabolism of a sea-fight, sharks will gaze up like hungry dogs round a carving-table; though, were the whole affair turned upside down, it would still be a shocking sharkish business enough; and though they are the invariable outriders of slave-ships—yet never will you find them in such countless numbers, and gayer spirits, than around a dead sperm whale moored by night to a whaleship. If you have never seen that sight, suspend your decision about the propriety of devil-worship, and the expediency of conciliating the devil.

But Stubb heeded not the mumblings so nigh him. “Cook, cook!—where’s that old Fleece?” he cried, widening his legs for a surer base, and stabbing his fork into the dish as with a lance. The old black, roused from his warm hammock at a most unseasonable hour, came shambling from his galley—this old Fleece, this old Ebony—shuffling and limping, assisting his step with his tongs made of straightened iron hoops; in obedience to the word of command, he came to a dead stop opposite Stubb’s sideboard, bowed his arched back lower, and sideways inclined his head to bring his best ear into play.

“Cook,” said Stubb, rapidly lifting a rather reddish morsel, “don’t you think this steak is rather overdone? Don’t I always say that to be good, a whale-steak must be tough? See those sharks—prefer it tough and rare! Go talk to ’em: welcome to help themselves civilly, and in moderation—but they must keep quiet. Blast me, if I can hear my own voice. Away, cook—and preach to ’em!” Sullenly taking the lantern, Fleece leaned over the bulwarks and began: “Fellow-critters: I’se ordered here to say dat you must stop dat dam noise. Massa Stubb say you can fill your dam bellies up to de hatchings; but—” “Cook!”—a slap—“you mustn’t swear when you’re preaching.” “Well, den—Belubed fellow-critters:” Stubb approved—“Right! coax ’em to it”—and Fleece went on with a flourish of his tongs, but was collared again: “Talk to ’em gentlemanly.” Once more the sermon proceeded.

“Your woraciousness, fellow-critters, I don’t blame ye so much for; dat is natur, and can’t be helped; but to gobern dat wicked natur, dat is de pint. You is sharks, sartin; but if you gobern de shark in you, why den you be angel; for all angel is not’ing more dan de shark well goberned. Now, look here, bred’ren, just try wonst to be cibil, a helping yourselbs from dat whale. Don’t be tearin’ de blubber out your neighbour’s mout. Is not one shark dood right as toder to dat whale? And, by Gor, none on you has de right to dat whale; dat whale belong to some one else. I know some o’ you has berry brig mout, brigger dan oders; but den de brig mouts sometimes has de small bellies; so dat de brigness of de mout is not to swaller wid, but to bit off de blubber for de small fry ob sharks, dat can’t get into de scrouge to help demselves.” “Well done, old Fleece!” cried Stubb, “that’s Christianity; go on.” “No use goin’ on; de dam willains will keep a scougin’ and slappin’ each oder, Massa Stubb; dey don’t hear one word; no use a-preachin’ to such dam g’uttons as you call ’em, till dare bellies is full, and dare bellies is bottomless; and when dey do get ’em full, dey wont hear you den; for den dey sink in de sea, go fast to sleep on de coral, and can’t hear not’ing at all, no more, for eber and eber.” “Give the benediction, Fleece.” He lifted both hands over the fishy mob and cried: “Cussed fellow-critters! Kick up de damndest row as ever you can; fill your dam’ bellies ’till dey bust—and den die.”

“Now, cook—attend.” Stubb plied his fork, then cross-questioned him on age and birthplace; with sudden gravity: “Do you belong to the church?”—“Passed one once in Cape-Down.” “And where do you expect to go to, cook?” He mumbled of bed soon; Stubb held him: “Avast! heave to! I mean when you die.” “When dis old brack man dies,” said the negro slowly, changing his whole air and demeanor, “he hisself won’t go nowhere; but some bressed angel will come and fetch him.” “Fetch him? How? In a coach and four, as they fetched Elijah? And fetch him where?” “Up dere,” said Fleece, holding his tongs straight over his head. “So, then, you expect to go up into our main-top, do you, cook? But don’t you know the higher you climb, the colder it gets? Perhaps you expect to get into heaven by crawling through the lubber’s hole; but, no, no—regular way, round by the rigging; ticklish business, but must be done. Drop your tongs, cook, and hear my orders.”

“Well—for the future, when you cook another whale-steak for my private table here, the capstan, hold the steak in one hand, and show a live coal to it with the other; that done, dish it. To-morrow, when we are cutting in, stand by for the tips of his fins—put them in pickle. Have the ends of the flukes soused.” As Fleece limped off: “Cutlets for supper to-morrow in the mid-watch!—Avast! Whale-balls for breakfast—don’t forget.” And old Fleece, vanishing to his hammock, muttered: “Wish, by gor! whale eat him, ’stead of him eat whale. I’m bressed if he ain’t more of shark dan Massa Shark hisself.”
