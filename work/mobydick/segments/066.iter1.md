Out of the trunk, the branches grow; out of them, the twigs. So, in productive subjects, grow the chapters.

The crotch—a notched stick, two feet long—stands in the starboard gunwale by the bow, a rest for the harpoon’s wooden end, its naked, barbed point projecting from the prow. Thereby the weapon lies to hand; the hurler snatches it up as readily from its rest as a backwoodsman swings his rifle from the wall. Two harpoons—the first and second irons—lie there, each on its own cord to the line, so both may be darted in succession into the same whale; if one draw, the other holds. But the whale’s convulsive run often forbids the second; yet, already bent to the running line, it must be tossed overboard, else most terrible jeopardy to all hands. Thenceforth a dangling, sharp-edged terror, it curvets about boat and whale, entangling or cutting lines, not to be secured till the fish is fairly a corpse. Imagine four boats on one strong, knowing whale, and eight or ten loose second irons dancing at once about him.
