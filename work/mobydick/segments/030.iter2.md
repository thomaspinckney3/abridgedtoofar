Stubb, the second mate, a Cape-Cod-man, was happy-go-lucky; neither craven nor valiant; taking perils as they came with an indifferent air; good-humored, easy, and careless, he presided over his whale-boat as if the most deadly encounter were but a dinner, and his crew all invited guests. In the very death-lock of the fight he handled his unpitying lance as a whistling tinker his hammer; humming old rigadig tunes while flank and flank with the exasperated monster. Long usage had converted the jaws of death into an easy chair for him. What he thought of death itself, there is no telling—whether he ever thought of it at all; but if he ever did chance to cast his mind that way after a comfortable dinner, like a good sailor he took it to be a sort of call of the watch to tumble aloft about something he would find out when he obeyed, and not sooner. What, perhaps, with other things, made Stubb such an easy-going, unfearing man, so cheerily trudging off with the burden of life in a world full of grave pedlars, all bowed to the ground with their packs; what helped to bring about that almost impious good-humor of his; that thing must have been his pipe. For, like his nose, his short, black little pipe was one of the regular features of his face; he kept a row of them ready loaded in a rack, and whenever he turned in, he smoked them all out in succession, lighting one from the other to the chapter’s end. When Stubb dressed, instead of first putting legs into trowsers, he put his pipe into his mouth. For every one knows that this earthly air, whether ashore or afloat, is terribly infected with the nameless miseries of the numberless mortals who have died exhaling it; and as in time of the cholera some go with a camphorated handkerchief to the mouth, so Stubb’s tobacco smoke might have operated against all mortal tribulations as a sort of disinfecting agent.

The third mate was Flask, of Tisbury in Martha’s Vineyard; short, stout, ruddy; very pugnacious concerning whales, who somehow seemed to think that the great leviathans had personally and hereditarily affronted him; and therefore it was a sort of point of honor with him, to destroy them whenever encountered. So utterly lost was he to all sense of reverence for the many marvels of their majestic bulk and mystic ways, and so dead to anything like an apprehension of any possible danger from encountering them, that in his poor opinion the wondrous whale was but a species of magnified mouse, or at least water-rat, requiring only a little circumvention and some small application of time and trouble in order to kill and boil. This ignorant, unconscious fearlessness made him a little waggish in the matter of whales; he followed the fish for the fun of it; a three years’ voyage round Cape Horn only a jolly joke. They called him King-Post on board the Pequod, like that short, square timber in Arctic whalers; made to clinch tight and last long.

These three—Starbuck, Stubb, Flask—were momentous men, headsmen of the three boats. In Ahab’s grand order of battle, they were as captains of companies; armed with long keen spears; their harpooneers as flingers of javelins; squire and Knight.

First among the harpooneers was Queequeg—Starbuck’s squire.

Next, Tashtego, pure-blooded Indian of Gay-Head, high cheek-boned, black rounding eyes—Oriental in largeness, Antarctic in glitter; the infallible arrow of his sires replaced by the unerring harpoon. He was Stubb’s squire.

Third, Daggoo, gigantic, coal-black, lion-treading; two golden hoops in his ears like ring-bolts; six feet five in his socks, erect as a giraffe; the corporeal humility of looking up at him made a white man seem a white flag suing truce of a fortress. Curious to tell, this imperial negro, Ahasuerus Daggoo, was the Squire of little Flask, who looked like a chess-man beside him.

As for the residue, at the present day not one in two before the mast in the American fishery are Americans born, though pretty nearly all the officers are. Islanders make the best whalemen: Azores, Shetlands; they were nearly all Islanders in the Pequod—Isolatoes, I call such, each living on his own separate continent; yet now, federated along one keel, an Anacharsis Clootz deputation from all the isles of the sea, and all the ends of the earth, accompanying Old Ahab to lay the world’s grievances before that bar from which not many ever come back. Black Little Pip—he never did—oh, no! Poor Alabama boy! Soon ye shall see him beating his tambourine on the grim forecastle; prelusive of the eternal time when bid strike in with angels in glory; called a coward here, hailed a hero there!
