The Chase.—Third Day.

Fair and fresh the third day dawned. Look-outs dotted every mast.

“D’ye see him?”—“Not yet.”—“In his infallible wake, though; but follow that wake, that’s all. Helm there; steady, as thou goest, and hast been going. What a lovely day again! were it a new-made world, and made for a summer-house to the angels, and this morning the first of its throwing open to them, a fairer day could not dawn upon that world. Here’s food for thought, had Ahab time to think; but Ahab never thinks; he only feels, feels, feels; that’s tingling enough for mortal man! to think’s audacity. God only has that right and privilege. Thinking is, or ought to be, a coolness and a calmness; and our poor hearts throb, and our poor brains beat too much for that. ... How the wild winds blow it; they whip it about me as the torn shreds of split sails lash the tossed ship they cling to. A vile wind that has no doubt blown ere this through prison corridors and cells, and wards of hospitals, and ventilated them, and now comes blowing hither as innocent as fleeces. Out upon it!—it’s tainted. ... Would now the wind but had a body; ... And yet, I say again, and swear it now, that there’s something all glorious and gracious in the wind. These warm Trade Winds, at least, that in the clear heavens blow straight on, in strong and steadfast, vigorous mildness; ... And by the eternal Poles! these same Trades that so directly blow my good ship on; these Trades, or something like them—something so unchangeable, and full as strong, blow my keeled soul along! To it! Aloft there! What d’ye see?”—“Nothing, sir.”—“Nothing! and noon at hand! The doubloon goes a-begging! See the sun! Aye, aye, it must be so. I’ve oversailed him. How, got the start? Aye, he’s chasing _me_ now; not I, _him_—that’s bad; I might have known it, too. Fool! the lines—the harpoons he’s towing. ... About! about! Man the braces!”

Reversed, she sailed hard upon the breeze, rechurning her own white wake. Starbuck coiled the main-brace, bones damp within him. “Against the wind he now steers for the open jaw. God keep us, but already my bones feel damp within me, and from the inside wet my flesh. I misdoubt me that I disobey my God in obeying him!”

“Stand by to sway me up!” cried Ahab; and once more he swung on high. An hour—gold beaten to ages. At last, three points off the weather bow, a spout; three shrieks up from three mast-heads. “Forehead to forehead I meet thee, this third time, Moby Dick! Brace sharper up—crowd her into the wind’s eye. Too far yet to lower; but let me have one more round look. An old, old sight, and yet somehow so young; aye, and not changed a wink since I first saw it, a boy, from the sand-hills of Nantucket! The same!—the same!—the same to Noah as to me. There’s a soft shower to leeward. Such lovely leewardings! ... Leeward! the white whale goes that way; look to windward, then; the better if the bitterer quarter. What’s this?—green? aye, tiny mosses in these warped cracks. No such green weather stains on Ahab’s head!—we both grow old, old mast, yet thou outlast’st me. ‘He should still go before me, my pilot; and yet be seen again’—where? Have I eyes at the bottom? Aye, Parsee, thou told’st direful truth of thyself; there thy shot fell short. Good-bye, mast-head—keep a good eye while I’m gone.” He gave the word and was lowered.

As he hovered in his stern, he waved to Starbuck to pause. “Starbuck!”—“Sir?”—“For the third time my soul’s ship starts upon this voyage, Starbuck.”—“Aye, sir, thou wilt have it so.”—“Some ships sail from their ports, and ever afterwards are missing.”—“Truth, sir: saddest truth.”—“Some men die at ebb tide; some at low water; some at the full of the flood;—and I feel now like a billow that’s all one crested comb, Starbuck. I am old;—shake hands with me, man.” Their hands met; eyes fastened; Starbuck’s tears the glue. “Oh, my captain, my captain!—noble heart—go not—go not!—see, it’s a brave man that weeps; how great the agony of the persuasion then!” “Lower away!” cried Ahab, tossing the mate’s arm; and the boat leaped on.

“The sharks! the sharks!” cried a voice; but Ahab’s own voice rode higher, and he heard not. Numbers of sharks snapped at the blades every dip, following as vultures banners. “Heart of wrought steel!” murmured Starbuck, gazing after—“lowering thy keel among ravening sharks; this the critical third day. For when three days flow together in one continuous intense pursuit; be sure the first is the morning, the second the noon, and the third the evening and the end of that thing—be that end what it may. Oh! my God! what is this that shoots through me, and leaves me so deadly calm, yet expectant,—fixed at the top of a shudder! Future things swim before me, as in empty outlines and skeletons; all the past is somehow grown dim. Mary, girl! thou fadest in pale glories behind me; boy! I seem to see but thy eyes grown wondrous blue. Strangest problems of life seem clearing; but clouds sweep between—Is my journey’s end coming? My legs feel faint; like his who has footed it all day. Feel thy heart,—beats it yet? Stir thyself, Starbuck!—stave it off—move, move! speak aloud!—Mast-head there! See ye my boy’s hand on the hill?—Crazed;—aloft there!—keep thy keenest eye upon the boats:—mark well the whale!—Ho! again!—drive off that hawk! see! he pecks—he tears the vane”—pointing to the red flag flying at the main-truck—“Ha! he soars away with it!—Where’s the old man now? see’st thou that sight, oh Ahab!—shudder, shudder!”

A downward pointed arm from aloft—Moby Dick had sounded. Ahab held on a little sideways from the ship, the becharmed crew mute as the head-beat waves hammered the bow. “Drive, drive in your nails, oh ye waves! to their uttermost heads drive them in! ye but strike a thing without a lid; and no coffin and no hearse can be mine:—and hemp only can kill me! Ha! ha!” The waters slowly swelled in broad circles; upheaved; a subterraneous hum; then, bedraggled with ropes and irons, a vast form shot obliquely from the sea, hovered in a thin veil of mist rainbowed in the air, then fell swamping back; the circling surface creamed like new milk round the marble trunk.

“Give way!” The boats darted. Maddened by fresh irons corroding in him, Moby Dick came head-on, flailing the mates’ boats apart, leaving Ahab’s almost without a scar. As Daggoo and Queequeg stopped strained planks, the whale turned and showed one whole flank. A cry: lashed round and round upon his back, pinioned in the involutions wound during the night, the half-torn body of the Parsee stared—distended eyes full on Ahab. The harpoon dropped from his hand. “Befooled, befooled!—Aye, Parsee! I see thee again. Aye, and thou goest before; and this, this then is the hearse thou didst promise. But I hold thee to the last letter of thy word. Where is the second hearse? Away, mates, to the ship! those boats are useless; repair them if ye can and return; if not, Ahab is enough to die—Down, men! The first thing that but offers to jump from this boat I stand in, that thing I harpoon. Ye are not other men, but my arms and my legs. Where’s the whale? gone down again?”

Not seeking them, as Starbuck cried—“Oh! Ahab, not too late even now to desist. See! Moby Dick seeks thee not. It is thou that madly seekest him!”—the whale swam steadily forward, almost past the ship, intent on his own straight path. Ahab, sliding by the vessel, hailed to have her follow not too swiftly, at a judicious interval. He saw Tashtego, Queequeg, Daggoo run to the mast-heads; men hammering in the staved boats; Stubb and Flask busy among bundles of new irons. As he saw and heard those hammers, far other hammers seemed driving a nail into his heart. But he rallied. Noting the vane gone from the main, he shouted Tashtego down for another flag and hammer, to nail it fast.

Whether fagged by three days’ chase and the hamper he bore, or moved by latent malice, the White Whale’s way abated as Ahab once more neared him; but the sharks pertinaciously stuck to the boat, biting till the oar-blades grew jagged. “Heed them not! those teeth but give new rowlocks to your oars. Pull on! ’tis the better rest, the shark’s jaw than the yielding water.”—“But at every bite, sir, the thin blades grow smaller and smaller!”—“They will last long enough! pull on!—The helm!—let me pass.” Helped to the bows, Ahab, within the smoky mountain mist of the whale’s spout curled round his Monadnock hump, arched back, arms high to the poise, darted his fierce iron—and his far fiercer curse. Moby Dick writhed sideways, rolled his nigh flank against the bow, suddenly canted the boat; three oarsmen flew; two clutched and hurled themselves inboard again; the third dropped astern, still afloat. Ahab shouted to take new turns with the line, and tow up to the mark; the double strain snapped it in the empty air. “What breaks in me? Some sinew cracks!—’tis whole again; oars! oars! Burst in upon him!”

Hearing the sea-crashing rush, the whale wheeled to present his blank forehead at bay; but catching sight of the nearing black hull—seemingly its larger and nobler foe—he bore down upon the advancing prow, jaws smiting amid fiery foam. “I grow blind,” gasped Ahab. “Hands!—is’t night?—The whale! the ship!” The crew forced the boat through sledge-hammering seas; but two whale-smitten bow-planks burst in; the boat lay almost level with the waves; men strove to stop the gap and bale.

For one beholding instant, Tashtego’s hammer hung suspended at the main-truck; the red flag half-wrapping him streamed straight, as his own forward-flowing heart; Starbuck and Stubb, on the bowsprit beneath, caught sight of the down-coming monster. “The whale, the whale! Up helm! Oh, all ye sweet powers of air, now hug me close!” cried Starbuck. “Steady! helmsman, steady. ... Nay! Up helm again! He turns to meet us! Oh, his unappeasable brow drives on towards one, whose duty tells him he cannot depart. My God, stand by me now!” “Stand not by me, but stand under me, whoever you are that will now help Stubb; ... I grin at thee, thou grinning whale! ... Why fly ye not, O Ahab! For me, off shoes and jacket to it; let Stubb die in his drawers! ... cherries! cherries! cherries!”—“Cherries? I only wish that we were where they grow. Oh, Stubb, I hope my poor mother’s drawn my part-pay ere this; if not, few coppers will now come to her, for the voyage is up.”

From the ship’s bows, nearly all the seamen hung inactive, eyes enchanted upon the whale; retribution, swift vengeance, eternal malice were in his whole aspect; the solid white buttress smote the starboard bow; men and timbers reeled; through the breach waters poured like torrents down a flume. “The ship! The hearse!—the second hearse!” cried Ahab from the boat; “its wood could only be American!”

The whale dived beneath the settling ship, quivered along her keel, shot to the surface far off the other bow, near Ahab’s boat, and lay a moment quiescent. “I turn my body from the sun. What ho, Tashtego! let me hear thy hammer. Oh! ye three unsurrendered spires of mine; thou uncracked keel; and only god-bullied hull; thou firm deck, and haughty helm, and Pole-pointed prow,—death-glorious ship! must ye then perish, and without me? ... Oh, now I feel my topmost greatness lies in my topmost grief. ... Towards thee I roll, thou all-destroying but unconquering whale; to the last I grapple with thee; from hell’s heart I stab at thee; for hate’s sake I spit my last breath at thee. Sink all coffins and all hearses to one common pool! and since neither can be mine, let me then tow to pieces, while still chasing thee, though tied to thee, thou damned whale! Thus, I give up the spear!”

He darted. The line ran with igniting velocity;—ran foul. Ahab stooped; cleared it; but the flying turn caught him round the neck, and, voiceless as Turkish mutes bowstring their victim, shot him from the boat. The eye-splice at the rope’s end flew out, smote the sea, and vanished.

The tranced crew turned. “The ship?—where?” Through dim, bewildering mediums they saw her sidelong fading phantom; only the upper masts out of water; the pagan harpooneers still, fixed by fate, on their sinking look-outs. Concentric circles seized boat and men and every lone chip; spinning, all went in one vortex out of sight.

As the last whelmings poured over the sunken head at the mainmast, a few inches of the spar yet showed; long streaming yards of the flag undulated with ironical coincidings over the destroying billows; and, at that instant, a red arm and a hammer hovered uplifted, nailing the flag faster to the subsiding spar. A sky-hawk, that had tauntingly followed the truck, pecking and incommoding Tashtego, chanced to intercept its broad wing between hammer and wood; feeling the aetherial thrill, the submerged savage, in his death-gasp, kept his hammer frozen; and the bird of heaven, with archangelic shrieks, imperial beak thrust upward, folded in Ahab’s flag, went down with his ship, which, like Satan, would not sink till she had dragged a living part of heaven, and helmeted herself with it. Small fowls screamed over the yet yawning gulf; a sullen white surf beat its sides; then all collapsed, and the great shroud of the sea rolled on as it rolled five thousand years ago.
