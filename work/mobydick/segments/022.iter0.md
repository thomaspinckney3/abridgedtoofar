“Shipmates, have ye shipped in that ship?” said a ragged stranger, levelling his massive forefinger at the Pequod. Small-pox had ribbed his face like a torrent’s bed; a black rag knotted his neck.

“Anything down there about your souls?” he added. “Oh, perhaps ye hav’n’t got any. A soul’s a sort of a fifth wheel to a wagon.”

“What are you jabbering about?” said I.

“About him—Old Thunder. Ye hav’n’t seen him yet, have ye?”

“Who’s Old Thunder?”

“Captain Ahab.”

He thrust out his finger like a bayonet. “When Captain Ahab is all right, then this left arm of mine will be all right; not before. Ye heard nothing of Cape Horn, where he lay like dead three days?—nor the deadly skrimmage afore an altar in Santa—? nor the silver calabash he spat into? And nothing about his leg—lost last voyage by a parmacetti—according to the prophecy?”

“They didn’t tell much,” said I. “Only that he’s a good whale-hunter and a good captain.”

“That’s true—both true. But jump when he gives an order. Step and growl; growl and go.”

He stood pointing at the Pequod, as if in troubled reverie. “Ye’ve shipped, have ye? Well, what’s signed is signed; and what’s to be, will be; and then again, perhaps it won’t. Anyhow, some sailors must go with him—God pity ’em! Morning to ye, shipmates; the ineffable heavens bless ye! I’m sorry I stopped ye.”

“If you have anything important, out with it,” said I.

“Ye are just the man for him—the likes of ye. Morning! Oh! when ye get there, tell ’em I’ve concluded not to make one of ’em.”

“Stop—your name?”

“Elijah.”

Elijah! We went on, counting him a humbug bugbear. Yet, turning a corner, I glanced back and saw him dogging us at a distance. I said nothing to Queequeg, but watched if he would turn where we turned. He did. Coupled with his shrouded hints—Ahab; the Cape Horn fit; the silver calabash; the squaw Tistig’s prediction; and a hundred other shadowy things—the sight stirred vague wonderments. Crossing over, we retraced our steps to try him. He passed by, not seeming to notice us. This relieved me, and finally, in my heart, I pronounced him—a humbug.
