About mid-day, swathed in a shark-skin apron, Perth stood between forge and anvil, when moody Ahab came with a rusty leathern bag. Perth drew the iron from the coals and struck it on the anvil—the red mass sending off the sparks in thick hovering flights, some of which flew close to Ahab. “Are these thy Mother Carey’s chickens, Perth? they burn; but thou—thou liv’st among them without a scorch.” “Because I am scorched all over,” said Perth; “not easily can’st thou scorch a scar.” “Well, well; no more. Thy shrunk voice sounds too calmly, sanely woeful to me. In no Paradise myself, I am impatient of all misery in others that is not mad. Thou should’st go mad, blacksmith; say, why dost thou not go mad? How can’st thou endure without being mad? Do the heavens yet hate thee, that thou can’st not go mad?”

Ahab’s voice turned: “Can’st thou smoothe almost any seams and dents?” “Aye; all but one.” Ahab leaned both hands on his shoulders, sweeping one hand across his ribbed brow: “Look ye here—here—can ye smoothe out a seam like this? if thou could’st, blacksmith, glad enough would I lay my head upon thy anvil, and feel thy heaviest hammer between my eyes. Answer!” “Oh! that is the one.” “Aye, blacksmith, it is the one; aye, man, it is unsmoothable; for though thou only see’st it here in my flesh, it has worked down into the bone of my skull—that is all wrinkles! But, away with child’s play;”

“I, too, want a harpoon made; one that a thousand yoke of fiends could not part; something that will stick in a whale like his own fin-bone. There’s the stuff.” He spilled nail-stubbs of racing horses’ steel shoes. “The best and stubbornest stuff we work,” said Perth. “I know it, old man; these stubbs will weld together like glue from the melted bones of murderers. Quick! forge me the harpoon. And forge me first, twelve rods for its shank; then wind, and twist, and hammer these twelve together like tow-line yarns.”

A flaw rejected; then Ahab himself took the welding. The forge shot up its intense straight flame; the Parsee passed silently, bowing toward the fire, as if invoking some curse or some blessing on the toil. But, as Ahab looked up, he slid aside. “That Parsee smells fire like a fusee; and smells of it himself, like a hot musket’s powder-pan,” muttered Stubb. The shank received its final heat; to temper it, Perth plunged it hissing into the cask; the steam scalded Ahab’s face. “Would’st thou brand me, Perth?—have I been but forging my own branding-iron, then?” “Pray God, not that; yet I fear something. Is not this harpoon for the White Whale?” “For the white fiend!”

“Now for the barbs; thou must make them thyself. Here are my razors.” For a moment Perth eyed them; “Take them, man: I now neither shave, sup, nor pray till—” To temper: “No—no water; I want it of the true death-temper. Ahoy, there! Tashtego, Queequeg, Daggoo! Will ye give me as much blood as will cover this barb?” Dark nods: three punctures. The barbs were baptized. “Ego non baptizo te in nomine patris, sed in nomine diaboli!” deliriously howled Ahab, as the malignant iron scorchingly devoured the baptismal blood.

A hickory pole was fitted; a new tow-line strained like a harp-string—“Good! and now for the seizings.” Rope unstranded, yarns woven round the socket; the pole driven home; the line traced and bound along its length—pole, iron, and rope—like the Three Fates—remained inseparable, and Ahab moodily stalked away with the weapon; the sound of his ivory leg, and the sound of the hickory pole, both hollowly ringing along every plank. But ere he entered his cabin, light, unnatural, half-bantering, yet most piteous sound was heard. Oh, Pip! thy wretched laugh, thy idle but unresting eye; all thy strange mummeries not unmeaningly blended with the black tragedy of the melancholy ship, and mocked it!
