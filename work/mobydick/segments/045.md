What the white whale was to Ahab has been hinted; what, at times, he was to me remains unsaid. Aside from those more obvious considerations, there was another thought, or rather vague, nameless horror concerning him, which at times by its intensity completely overpowered all the rest; and yet so mystical and well nigh ineffable was it, that I almost despair of putting it in a comprehensible form. It was the whiteness of the whale that above all things appalled me. But how can I hope to explain myself here; and yet, in some dim, random way, explain myself I must, else all these chapters might be naught. Though in many natural objects, whiteness refiningly enhances beauty—as in marbles, japonicas, and pearls; and though various nations have recognised a certain royal pre-eminence in this hue, the grand old kings of Pegu placing the title Lord of the White Elephants above all their other ascriptions, and the modern kings of Siam unfurling the same; and though the Hanoverian flag bears a snow-white charger, and the great Austrian Empire, Cæsarian, heir to overlording Rome, has for the imperial colour the same imperial hue; and though, besides all this, whiteness has been made significant of gladness—the Roman white stone marking a joyful day—and of many touching, noble things—the innocence of brides, the benignity of age, the ermine of the Judge, and the daily state of kings and queens drawn by milk-white steeds; and though in the higher mysteries it is the Persian white forked flame, Great Jove incarnate in a snow-white bull, and to the noble Iroquois the midwinter sacrifice of the sacred White Dog; and though directly from the Latin for white all Christian priests derive the name of the alb or tunic, and among the holy pomps of the Romish faith white is specially employed in the celebration of the Passion; and though in the Vision of St. John white robes are given to the redeemed, and the four-and-twenty elders stand clothed before the great white throne, and the Holy One that sitteth there white like wool; and though, finally, this pre-eminence in it applies to the human race itself, giving the white man ideal mastership over every dusky tribe; yet for all these accumulated associations, there lurks an elusive something in the innermost idea of this hue which strikes more of panic to the soul than that redness which affrights in blood.

Divorced from kindly associations and coupled with what is terrible, whiteness heightens terror to the furthest bounds. Witness the white bear and the white shark—their ghastly whiteness imparting an abhorrent mildness to their dumb gloating. Not the heraldic tiger so staggers courage as the white-shrouded ghostliness of those pale marauders. The French, alluding to the white, silent stillness of death, call that shark Requin—from Requiem.

Bethink thee of the albatross: in its wondrous bodily whiteness chiefly lurks the spell. Not Coleridge first threw that spell; but God’s great, unflattering laureate, Nature. I remember the first I ever saw—regal, unspotted, hooked Roman bill; at intervals it arched forth its vast archangel wings as if to embrace some holy ark; wondrous flutterings shook it; though bodily unharmed it uttered cries. Through its inexpressible, strange eyes, methought I peeped to secrets which took hold of God. As Abraham before the angels, I bowed myself; the white thing was so white, its wings so wide, and in those for ever exiled waters, I had lost the miserable warping memories of traditions and of towns. “Goney!” cried a sailor; the truth was in the bird’s whiteness.

So, too, the White Steed of the Prairies—a most imperial and archangelical apparition of that unfallen, western world—whose spiritual whiteness clothed him with divineness that enforced a nameless terror. And yet there are other whitenesses that lose strange glory and acquire a repelling chill: the Albino, invested in all-pervading pallor, more hideous than deformity; the White Squall; the White Hoods of Ghent masked in their snowy faction; the marble pallor of the dead, the shroud’s hue, and ghosts rising in a milk-white fog; yea, even Death, riding his pallid horse.

Why to the man of untutored ideality, who happens to be but loosely acquainted with the peculiar character of the day, does the bare mention of Whitsuntide marshal in the fancy such long, dreary, speechless processions of slow-pacing pilgrims, down-cast and hooded with new-fallen snow? Name alone can summon a spectralness: the White Tower; the White Mountains—whence, in peculiar moods, a gigantic ghostliness steals over the soul—while the thought of Virginia’s Blue Ridge is full of a soft, dewy, distant dreaminess; the White Sea exerts such a spectralness over the fancy, while that of the Yellow Sea lulls us with mortal thoughts of long lacquered mild afternoons on the waves; and “the tall pale man” of the Hartz.

Nor is it earthquakes and stampedoes alone that make tearless Lima the strangest, saddest city: for Lima has taken the white veil; and there is a higher horror in this whiteness of her woe. Old as Pizarro, this whiteness keeps her ruins for ever new; admits not the cheerful greenness of complete decay; spreads over her broken ramparts the rigid pallor of an apoplexy that fixes its own distortions.

To the common apprehension this phenomenon is not prime in terror; yet often the very thing that appals is the whiteness. A mariner, called by night to view his ship sailing through a midnight sea of milky whiteness, feels a silent dread, as if shoals of combed white bears swam around him; the lead assures soundings, but heart and helm go down till blue water is under him again. The backwoodsman views with indifference a prairie sheeted with snow; but not so the sailor in Antarctic scenes, where boundless churchyards grin with icy monuments and splintered crosses.

“White-lead chapter,” sayest thou? “White flag hung out from a craven soul?” Tell me, then, why a strong colt, smelling only the musk of a buffalo robe, starts and snorts with bursting eyes though he never knew the goring bisons of Oregon. No: but here thou beholdest even in a dumb brute, the instinct of the knowledge of the demonism in the world. So to me the milky sea, the frost-festoons, the windrowed snows—are the shaken robe to the frightened colt.

Though neither knows where lie the nameless things of which the mystic sign gives forth such hints; yet with me, as with the colt, somewhere those things must exist. Though in many of its aspects this visible world seems formed in love, the invisible spheres were formed in fright.

But not yet the incantation is solved. Why should that which is emblem of the Christian’s Deity be also intensifier of the appalling? Is it that by its indefiniteness it shadows forth the heartless voids and immensities of the universe, and thus stabs us from behind with the thought of annihilation, when beholding the white depths of the milky way? Or that whiteness is not so much a colour as the visible absence of colour; and at the same time the concrete of all colours; is it for these reasons that there is such a dumb blankness, full of meaning, in a wide landscape of snows—a colourless, all-colour of atheism from which we shrink? And when we remember the philosophers who tell us all other earthly hues—every stately or lovely emblazoning—the sweet tinges of sunset skies and woods; yea, and the gilded velvets of butterflies, and the butterfly cheeks of young girls; all these are but subtile deceits, not actually inherent in substances, but only laid on from without; so that all deified Nature absolutely paints like the harlot, whose allurements cover nothing but the charnel-house within; and when we consider further that the mystical cosmetic which produces every one of her hues—the great principle of light—for ever remains white or colorless in itself, and if operating without medium upon matter, would touch all objects, even tulips and roses, with its own blank tinge—pondering all this, the palsied universe lies before us a leper; and like travellers in Lapland refusing coloured glasses, the infidel gazes himself blind at the monumental white shroud that wraps all the prospect round him. And of all these things the Albino whale was the symbol. Wonder ye then at the fiery hunt?
