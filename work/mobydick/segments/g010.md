## Chapter 1

Stubb’s whale had been killed far from the ship; in a flat calm we made a tandem of three boats and, with eighteen men, thirty-six arms, and one hundred and eighty thumbs and fingers, slowly towed the inert, heaving bulk toward the Pequod. Three dim lights in the main-rigging guided us, and as we neared, Ahab dropped more lanterns, issued the customary orders for mooring, handed his light to a seaman, and vanished to his cabin. Though hot in the chase, he now showed some obscure impatience, as if the dead body only reminded him that Moby Dick yet lived, and a thousand other whales were nothing to his monomaniac end. Tied by the head to the stern and by the tail to the bows, the black hull of whale and ship lay close, yoked like colossal bullocks—one reclining, one standing.

A word for the flukes: the surest hold is the tail, but, being denser, it sinks beyond hand-reach; so a small line with a float and a mid-weight is led cleverly around, girdling the mass so the chain can follow and lock at the smallest part, hard by the lobes.

If Ahab grew still, Stubb—flush with conquest—bustled. “A steak, a steak, ere I sleep! You, Daggoo! overboard, and cut me one from his small!” About midnight it was cooked; standing at the capstan-head, lit by sperm-lanterns, Stubb set to his spermaceti supper, while thousands of sharks, wallowing blackly, turned on their backs to gouge out globular mouthfuls the size of a human head, leaving countersunk hollows as by a carpenter’s screw. The slapping of their tails jarred sleepers in their bunks. If ever you saw that sight, you would suspend judgment on the propriety of devil-worship.

But Stubb heeded neither sharks nor their smackings. “Cook, cook!—where’s that old Fleece?” The old black hobbled from the galley, tongs for a cane, and stood bowed before Stubb’s sideboard.

“Cook,” cried Stubb, “this steak is overdone. Don’t I always say a whale-steak must be tough? See those sharks prefer it tough and rare! Go preach to ’em: help themselves civilly, and keep quiet.” Taking a lantern, Fleece leaned over the bulwarks and began: “Fellow-critters: I’se ordered here to say dat you must stop dat dam noise. Massa Stubb say you can fill your dam bellies up to de hatchings; but you must stop dat dam racket!” “Cook!”—a slap—“you mustn’t swear when you’re preaching.” “Well, den—Belubed fellow-critters: you is all sharks, and by natur wery woracious; but to gobiern dat wicked natur, dat is de pint. You is sharks, sartin; but if you gobiern de shark in you, why den you be angel; for all angel is not’ing more dan de shark well goberned. Don’t be tearin’ de blubber out your neighbour’s mout. And, by Gor, none on you has de right to dat whale; dat whale belong to some one else.” Soon he despaired: “No use a-preachin’ to such dam g’uttons till dare bellies is full; and when full, dey sink on de coral and can’t hear not’ing, for eber and eber.” “Give the benediction, Fleece.” He lifted his hands over the mob: “Cussed fellow-critters! Kick up de damndest row as ever you can; fill your dam’ bellies ’till dey bust—and den die.”

“Now, cook—attend.” Stubb plied his fork. He cross-questioned Fleece on his age and birthplace; then, with sudden gravity, “Do you belong to the church?”—“Passed one once in Cape-Down.” Ahab-like catechism followed on heaven: Fleece pointed “up dere” with his tongs; Stubb mocked the lubber’s-hole to glory—“you don’t get there except the regular way, round by the rigging.” Then orders: “Next time, hold the steak in one hand, and show a live coal to it with the other; that done, dish it. To-morrow, when we are cutting in, stand by for the tips of his fins—put them in pickle. Have the ends of the flukes soused.” As Fleece limped off: “Cutlets for supper to-morrow in the mid-watch!—Avast! Whale-balls for breakfast—don’t forget.” And old Fleece, vanishing to his hammock, muttered: “Wish, by gor! whale eat him, ’stead of him eat whale. I’m bressed if he ain’t more of shark dan Massa Shark hisself.”

Outside, the sharks’ feast went on beneath the mild stars, and the ship and her yoked dead lay in their black communion till morning.

## Chapter 2

That mortal man should feed upon the creature that feeds his lamp, and, like Stubb, eat him by his own light—outlandish, yet historical. Three centuries ago the Right Whale’s tongue was prized in France; in Henry VIII’s day a court cook was rewarded for a sauce for barbecued porpoises. Porpoises are still eaten; their meat made into seasoned balls. The old monks of Dunfermline were fond of them.

Among his hunters the whale would be a noble dish, were there not so much of him: a hundred-foot meat-pie takes away the appetite. The Esquimaux live upon whales and quaff vintages of train oil; their Zogranda prescribes strips of blubber for infants. Long ago some Englishmen, left in Greenland, lived for months on mouldy “fritters”—the crisp, brown scraps left after trying-out.

What further depreciates the whale as a civilized dish is his unctuous richness: great prize ox of the sea, too fat to be delicately good. The hump would rival buffalo’s but for its solid fat; the spermaceti, bland and creamy as the third-month cocoanut’s white meat, is too rich for butter—yet sailors dip ship-biscuit into the try-pots and fry their suppers there. In a small Sperm Whale the brains—two plump, whitish lobes—are mixed with flour and cooked, resembling calves’ head; and some young bucks, by dining on calves’ brains, by and by get so much of their own.

It is not wholly the unctuousness that makes landsmen abhor whale-eating; rather some scruple that man should eat a newly murdered thing of the sea, and eat it by its own light. But the first butcher of an ox was surely deemed a murderer. Go to market on Saturday night, and see live bipeds staring at dead quadrupeds—does not that sight take a tooth out of the cannibal’s jaw? Cannibals? who is not a cannibal? It will be more tolerable for the provident Fejee who salted a lean missionary than for thee, gourmand of paté-de-foie-gras.

And thou that callest Stubb’s supper insult added to injury—look at thy knife-handle: bones of the brother of the ox thou eatest. Thy toothpick? a feather of the same goose. With what quill did the Secretary of the Society for the Suppression of Cruelty to Ganders indite his circulars? Only of late did they resolve upon steel pens.

## Chapter 3

Commonly, when a Sperm Whale is brought alongside at night, all sail is taken in and the crew sent below till daylight; but on the Line so many sharks gather that, left six hours, little but skeleton would float by morning. So, when the anchor-watch came on—Queequeg and a forecastle-man—they slung cutting-stages and lowered three lanterns that cast long gleams over the turbid sea, and began an incessant murdering of sharks, darting whaling-spades to the skull, their only vital part. In that foamy mêlée, mis-aims were frequent, and revealed a ferocity incredible: they snapped not only at each other’s disembowelments, but bent like bows to bite their own, swallowing the entrails again and again, to void them by the gaping wound. Even dead, they were perilous; a pantheistic vitality lurked in joints and bones. One hoisted on deck for his skin almost took off Queequeg’s hand while he tried to shut the stiff lid of the murderous jaw.

“Queequeg no care what god made him shark,” said the savage, lifting his hurt hand; “wedder Fejee god or Nantucket god; but de god wat made shark must be one dam Ingin.”

## Chapter 4

Saturday night bled into a Sabbath of butchery; the ivory Pequod turned shamble, every sailor a butcher. The green-clustered cutting-tackles were swayed to the main-top and lashed at the lower mast-head, the strongest point aloft; their hawser-like fall rove to the windlass; the huge lower block swung over the whale, and to it the hundred-pound blubber-hook. From stages over the side, Starbuck and Stubb cut a hole above the side-fin, scarved a broad semicircle, and the hook was set. Then, to a wild chorus, all hands heaved. The ship careened; bolts started like frosty nail-heads; she quivered and nodded her mast-heads to the sky. A swift snap; a great swash; and the triumphant tackle rose, dragging the semicircular end of the first strip.

As the orange’s rind spirals from the fruit, so the blubber peels in one uniform strip along the scarf, the windlass ever rolling the whale, the blanket-piece rising until it grazes the main-top and sways blood-dripping like a mass let down from the sky. The boarding-sword slices a hole low; the second tackle hooks; then, with sidelong, lunging cuts, the swordsman severs the strip in twain—short lower part still fast, the long blanket-piece clear for lowering. While one tackle peels and hoists anew, the other slackens, and down the strip goes through the main hatch to the blubber-room, where nimble hands coil it away like a great live braid of serpents. So the work proceeds: tackles hoisting and lowering; whale and windlass heaving; heavers singing; mates scarfing; ship straining—and oaths for oil to ease the friction.

## Chapter 5

What and where is the skin of the whale? You can scrape from the unmarred body an infinitely thin, transparent substance—isinglass-like, soft as satin until dried; I have used such shreds for markers in my whale-books, reading, as it were, through the whale’s spectacles. But that is rather the skin of the skin. The only dense enveloping layer you can raise is the blubber: firm, elastic, compact, eight to fifteen inches thick. Assuming the blubber for skin, then, when a great Sperm yields a hundred barrels of oil, which is but three-fourths of its expressed substance, conceive the enormity of the mass whose mere integument pours such a lake.

In life, the Sperm’s visible surface is marvellously graven—crossed with numberless straight marks like the finest Italian line-engraving, as if seen through the isinglass. On some, hieroglyphical suggestions appear; I recall one plate like the old Indian characters on the Upper Mississippi palisades. Often, too, rude random scratches efface the lines—hostile contacts, I deem, among the large, full-grown bulls; New England rocks scored by ice may resemble him there.

Blanket-pieces they call the long strips—aptly; for he is wrapt in his blubber as in blanket or Indian poncho. By this cosy surtout he keeps comfortable in all weathers, in all seas. Other fish—cold-blooded, lungless refrigerators—may bask under the lee of an iceberg; but the whale, like man, has warm blood and lungs: freeze his blood, and he dies. Yet he lives immersed to his lips amid Arctic waters; experiment proves a Polar whale’s blood warmer than a Borneo negro’s in summer.

Herein, admire the virtue of strong vitality, thick walls, interior spaciousness. Oh, man! admire and model thyself after the whale! Do thou, too, remain warm among ice. Do thou, too, live in this world without being of it. Be cool at the equator; keep thy blood fluid at the Pole. Like the great dome of St. Peter’s, and like the great whale, retain, O man! in all seasons a temperature of thine own.

## Chapter 6

“Haul in the chains! Let the carcase go astern!” Peeled and beheaded, the vast white body flashed like a marble sepulchre; still colossal, still floating, torn by sharks and vexed by screaming fowls—beaks like insulting poniards. Beneath a mild azure and joyous breeze, that great mass of death drifted till lost in infinite perspectives. Oh, horrible vultureism of earth! from which not the mightiest whale is free.

Nor is this the end. His ghost survives to scare. Espied afar by timid craft, the white mass and heaping spray are logged—shoals, rocks, and breakers hereabouts: beware! Years after, ships shun the place—There’s your law of precedents; there’s your utility of traditions;—There’s orthodoxy!

## Chapter 7

Before stripping, the whale was beheaded—no neck to speak of; the thickest part where head and body seem to join; the surgeon cutting deep from above, eight or ten feet off, steering blind to divide the spine at a critical point by the skull. The Pequod’s head, too large to hoist aboard, was hove half out of water and hung, blood-dripping, to the ship’s waist, like Holofernes’s from Judith’s girdle. The crew went to dinner; a copper calm unfolded its lotus-leaves upon the sea.

Ahab came alone; took a few turns; then, in the main-chains, set Stubb’s spade crutch-wise and leaned over the black, hooded mass. It seemed the Sphynx’s head in a desert. “Speak, thou vast and venerable head,” he muttered, “which, though ungarnished with a beard, yet here and there lookest hoary with mosses; speak, mighty head, and tell us the secret thing that is in thee. Of all divers, thou hast dived the deepest. Where unrecorded names and navies rust, and untold hopes and anchors rot; where this frigate earth is ballasted with bones of millions; there was thy most familiar home. Thou saw’st locked lovers leaping from their flaming ship; thou saw’st the murdered mate tossed by pirates to the insatiate maw. O head! thou hast seen enough to split the planets and make an infidel of Abraham, and not one syllable is thine!”

“Sail ho!” cried a voice from the main-mast-head. Ahab straightened; thunder-clouds swept from his brow. “Better and better, man.—Where away?”—“Three points on the starboard bow, and bringing down her breeze!” “Would now St. Paul would come that way, and to my breezelessness bring his breeze! O Nature, and O soul of man! how far beyond all utterance are your linked analogies!”

## Chapter 8

Hand in hand, ship and breeze blew on; soon the Pequod rocked, and through the glass the stranger proved a whaleship. Too far to windward, she shot by; so signals were set. She answered: the Jeroboam of Nantucket. Squaring her yards, she ranged abeam and lowered a boat; but as Starbuck rigged the side-ladder, the captain in the stern waved it off. A malignant epidemic raged aboard; though himself untainted and a rifle-shot off, Captain Mayhew held to shore-quarantine punctilio and would not come aboard. His boat kept parallel a few yards off, now shoved ahead by a rolling wave, now dropping back; and with these see-sawings came another interruption.

At an oar sat a small, short, youngish man, freckled, redundant yellow hair, in a long-skirted, cabalistic coat; delirium fanatic in his eyes. “That’s he!” cried Stubb. Gabriel—so ran the Town-Ho’s tale—had been a great prophet among the Neskyeuna Shakers, descending from heaven through a trap-door with the seventh vial. He shipped as green-hand, and once off soundings declared himself the archangel Gabriel, vicar-general of Oceanica, commanding the captain to jump overboard. His preternatural earnestness, and the terrors of real delirium, invested him with sacred awe among the ignorant crew; they feared him, and protected him; the captain could not land him. Since the epidemic, he had carried a higher hand, declaring the plague his own to loose or bind; the poor devils cringed, sometimes rendering him homage as to a god.

“I fear not thy epidemic,” Ahab called. “Come on board.” Gabriel sprang up: “Think of the fevers! Beware the horrible plague!” A headlong wave shot the boat ahead, drowning Mayhew’s words. When she drifted back Ahab hailed, “Hast thou seen the White Whale?”—“Think of thy whale-boat, stoven and sunk! Beware the horrible tail!” Again a wave tore them on.

When the sea’s caprice abated, Mayhew began the dark story of Moby Dick—Gabriel breaking in whenever the name arose. Not long from home, the Jeroboam had been warned of the White Whale’s havoc. Gabriel denounced any attack, pronouncing him the Shaker God incarnate. Yet, a year or two later, when Moby Dick was fairly sighted, Macey, the chief mate, burned to engage. The captain, not unwilling, let him go; five men manned his boat. Gabriel, at the main-royal, tossed his arm and hurled prophecies of doom. Macey stood in the bow, reckless and exulting, essaying his lance, when a broad white shadow rose; its quick fanning took the oarsmen’s breath. Next instant the mate, so full of furious life, was smitten bodily into the air, and fell fifty yards off. Not a chip of the boat was harmed, nor a hair of any head; but the mate for ever sank. Often it is thus in the fishery; sometimes the body, when found, shows no visible mark—stark dead.

“The vial! the vial!” shrieked Gabriel; he called the crew from further hunting; and the terrible event clothed him with added influence, as if he had fore-announced it specifically. He became a nameless terror aboard.

Mayhew ending, he asked Ahab if he intended to hunt the White Whale. “Aye,” said Ahab. Gabriel sprang up, pointing down: “Think of the blasphemer—dead, and down there!—beware of the blasphemer’s end!” Ahab turned stolidly aside. “Captain, my letter-bag—there is a letter for one of thy officers,” he said; and Starbuck fetched a sorely tumbled, mould-spotted missive, damp from a dark locker—as if Death had been post-boy. Ahab spelled it out: “Mr. Harry Macey, Ship Jeroboam.”—“It’s Macey—and he’s dead!” sighed Mayhew. “From his wife—let me have it.” “Nay, keep it thyself,” cried Gabriel to Ahab; “thou art soon going that way.” “Curses throttle thee!” yelled Ahab. Starbuck split the end of a long spade-pole to hold the letter clear of contagion; Ahab reached it toward the boat. The oarsmen, expectant, ceased rowing; the boat drifted astern; and, as if by magic, the letter ranged along Gabriel’s eager hand. He clutched, impaled it on a boat-knife, and sent it, thus loaded, back to the Pequod. It fell at Ahab’s feet. Gabriel screamed to give way, and the mutinous boat shot from our side.

The men, returning to the whale’s jacket, muttered many a strange hint of this wild affair, while the hoisted head jogged and the cut blubber bled.

## Chapter 9

In cutting-in there is much scampering; everything to be done everywhere at once. We must go back a little. Upon first breaking ground in the whale’s back, my friend Queequeg, as harpooneer, descended upon the monster to insert the blubber-hook. Oftentimes he must stay there till the whole flensing is done, the whale lying mostly submerged, the worked-on parts alone awash. So, ten feet below the deck, half on the whale and half in the sea as the vast mass revolved like a tread-mill, Queequeg floundered—in Highland costume of shirt and socks, to uncommon advantage—while I, his bowsman, held him from the ship’s steep side by the monkey-rope: a strong strip of canvas around his waist, and—newly improved by Stubb—tied at the other end to my own belt.

It was a humorously perilous wedding. For better or worse we were wedded; should Queequeg sink, usage and honor demanded I be dragged after. So an elongated Siamese ligature united us. So strongly, and metaphysically, did I feel it that my individuality seemed merged in a joint-stock of two; my free will mortally wounded; another’s mistake might plunge innocent me into disaster and death. Here, I thought, was an interregnum in Providence. Pondering still—jerking him from between ship and whale when the roll threatened to jam him—I saw it was every mortal’s lot; each tied, one way or other, to a plurality of others. If your banker breaks, you snap; if your apothecary sends poison in your pills, you die. Handle the monkey-rope heedfully as I would, sometimes he jerked so that I came near sliding overboard; and do what I would, I had but one end of it.

Unappalled by last night’s massacre, sharks swarmed like bees, freshly allured by the pent blood now flowing. Right among them was Queequeg, pushing them aside with his feet; the miscellaneously carnivorous shark, drawn by such prey, will seldom touch a man. Nevertheless, wise to look sharp: besides my jerks, Tashtego and Daggoo, from the stages above, flourished keen spades over his head, slaughtering what they could reach—disinterested, benevolent, and indiscreet; for, half-hidden in blood-muddled water, their zealous steel came nearer amputating a leg than a tail. Poor Queequeg, iron hook in hand, strained and gasped, and, I suppose, prayed to Yojo.

“Well, well, my twin-brother,” I thought, slacking and drawing to every swell; “is not this the image of us all in the whaling world? That unsounded ocean is Life; those sharks, your foes; those spades, your friends; and between sharks and spades you are in a sad pickle.” But courage—good cheer came when, blue-lipped and blood-shot, the exhausted savage climbed the chains and stood dripping and trembling; the steward advanced and handed him—what? Not hot Cognac, but a cup of tepid ginger and water.

“Ginger?” sniffed Stubb. “Is ginger the fuel, Dough-Boy, to kindle a fire in this shivering cannibal? Ginger! sea-coal? firewood? lucifers? tinder? gunpowder?” He suspected a sneaking Temperance Society. “The steward had the face to offer calomel and jalap to Queequeg off the whale. Is he an apothecary?” Starbuck owned it poor stuff. “It was Aunt Charity,” cried Dough-Boy; “she bade me never give the harpooneers any spirits, only this ginger-jub.” “Ginger-jub! you gingerly rascal!—but I hope I do no wrong, Mr. Starbuck; the captain’s orders—grog for the harpooneer on a whale.” “Enough,” said Starbuck; “only don’t hit him—go down with him, and get what thou wantest thyself.” Stubb returned with a dark flask for Queequeg, and Aunt Charity’s tea-caddy—for the waves.
