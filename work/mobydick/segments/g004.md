## Chapter 1

“Shipmates, have ye shipped in that ship?” said a ragged stranger, levelling his massive forefinger at the Pequod. Small-pox had ribbed his face like a torrent’s bed; a black rag knotted his neck.

“Anything down there about your souls?” he added. “Oh, perhaps ye hav’n’t got any. A soul’s a sort of a fifth wheel to a wagon.”

“What are you jabbering about?” said I.

“About him—Old Thunder. Ye hav’n’t seen him yet, have ye?”

“Who’s Old Thunder?”

“Captain Ahab.”

He thrust out his finger like a bayonet. “When Captain Ahab is all right, then this left arm of mine will be all right; not before. Ye heard nothing of Cape Horn, where he lay like dead three days?—nor the deadly skrimmage afore an altar in Santa—? nor the silver calabash he spat into? And nothing about his leg—lost last voyage by a parmacetti—according to the prophecy?”

“They didn’t tell much,” said I. “Only that he’s a good whale-hunter and a good captain.”

“That’s true—both true. But jump when he gives an order. Step and growl; growl and go.”

He stood pointing at the Pequod, as if in troubled reverie. “Ye’ve shipped, have ye? Well, what’s signed is signed; and what’s to be, will be; and then again, perhaps it won’t. Anyhow, some sailors must go with him—God pity ’em! Morning to ye, shipmates; the ineffable heavens bless ye! I’m sorry I stopped ye.”

“If you have anything important, out with it,” said I.

“Ye are just the man for him—the likes of ye. Morning! Oh! when ye get there, tell ’em I’ve concluded not to make one of ’em.”

“Stop—your name?”

“Elijah.”

Elijah! We went on, counting him a humbug bugbear. Yet, turning a corner, I glanced back and saw him dogging us at a distance. I said nothing to Queequeg, but watched if he would turn where we turned. He did. Coupled with his shrouded hints—Ahab; the Cape Horn fit; the silver calabash; the squaw Tistig’s prediction; and a hundred other shadowy things—the sight stirred vague wonderments. Crossing over, we retraced our steps to try him. He passed by, not seeming to notice us. This relieved me, and finally, in my heart, I pronounced him—a humbug.


## Chapter 2

A day or two passed; all was astir aboard the Pequod. Old sails were mended, new canvas bent, rigging coiled in heaps; bolts, hoops, staves, beef, bread, water—endless fetching and carrying. Captain Peleg scarce went ashore, roaring from his whalebone den; Bildad ranged the stores with a long list, marking each arrival.

At the inns, word ran that chests must be on board by night; yet still the sailing was delayed; there is no telling how many things must be thought of before a whaler is fully equipped. Whaling is three years’ housekeeping on the wide ocean; spare boats, spare spars, spare lines and harpoons—spare everythings almost, but a spare Captain and duplicate ship.

Chief among the fetchers was Captain Bildad’s sister—Aunt Charity—lean, indefatigable, kindhearted; now a jar of pickles; now quills for the mate’s log; now flannel for some rheumatic back; last of all, she came with a long oil-ladle in one hand and a still longer lance in the other.

Often, visiting the craft, I asked after Captain Ahab: “Getting better—expected aboard every day,” they said. If I had been downright honest, I would have owned my half-fancying misgivings at being bound for so long a voyage without once seeing the absolute dictator of it; but involved men sometimes cover their suspicions from themselves. I said nothing, and tried to think nothing.

At last it was given out the ship would certainly sail next day. So, at early dawn, Queequeg and I set forth.


## Chapter 3

Grey, misty dawn; sailors flitted ahead. “She’s off by sunrise,” said I.

“Avast!” cried a voice; hands fell on our shoulders; a figure peered between us. Elijah.

“Going aboard?”

“Yes; and what’s that to you?”

“Coming back afore breakfast?” he wondered. Then, stealing after: “Did ye see anything like men going towards that ship a while ago?”

“I thought I saw four or five, but it was dim.”

“Very dim, very dim. See if you can find ’em now, will ye?—Morning to ye! I was going to warn ye against—but never mind—sharp frost—Good-bye! Shan’t see ye again soon, unless it’s before the Grand Jury.”

We left him and stepped on board. Profound quiet; cabin locked; hatches on. In the forecastle, a tattered old rigger lay face-down across two chests, sleeping the profoundest sleep. We sat near—Queequeg, after feeling if the sleeper’s rear was soft enough, even perched upon him in his “country way.”

“Gracious!—don’t sit there,” said I. He shifted by the head, lit his tomahawk-pipe, and flourished its hatchet-side over the sleeper.

“What’s that for, Queequeg?”

“Perry easy, kill-e; oh! perry easy!”

The heavy vapor filled the hole; the man breathed muffledly, rolled, and sat up rubbing his eyes. “Holloa! who be ye smokers?”

“Shipped men. When does she sail?”

“She sails to-day. The Captain came aboard last night.”

“What Captain?—Ahab?”

“Who but him indeed?”

A noise on deck—“Holloa! Starbuck’s astir,” said the rigger. “Good man, and a pious.” He turned to. We followed.

Clear sunrise now; the crew came aboard by twos and threes; riggers bestirred; mates engaged; shore-folk hurried last bundles. Meanwhile Captain Ahab remained invisibly enshrined within his cabin.


## Chapter 4

Towards noon, the riggers were dismissed, the Pequod hauled from the wharf; thoughtful Charity came off with her last gifts—a night-cap for Stubb and a spare Bible for the steward. Then Peleg and Bildad issued forth.

“Now, Mr. Starbuck, are you sure everything is right? Captain Ahab is all ready—just spoke to him. Call all hands—blast ’em!” cried Peleg.

“No need of profane words, friend Peleg,” said Bildad; “away, Starbuck, and do our bidding.”

Peleg did most of the roaring; Bildad, a licensed pilot, looked forward for the anchor, now and then chanting a dismal stave of psalmody to cheer the windlass hands, who roared a chorus about the girls in Booble Alley, though three days before he had told them no profane songs should be allowed. Charity had placed a small Watts in each berth.

“Man the capstan! Blood and thunder!—jump!” Peleg cried, stumping along the bar, using his leg very freely. I paused a moment on my handspike, till a sudden sharp poke in the rear startled me: my first kick. “Spring, thou sheep-head!—spring thy eyes out!” roared Peleg, while imperturbable Bildad led off with his psalm.

At last the anchor up, sails set—we glided out into a short, cold Christmas. The freezing spray cased us in ice as in polished armor; from the bows, vast curving icicles depended like ivory tusks. Lank Bildad headed the first watch, and ever and anon his steady notes were heard:

“Sweet fields beyond the swelling flood,
Stand dressed in living green.
So to the Jews old Canaan stood,
While Jordan rolled between.”

Never did those words sound more sweetly. Spite of the frigid night and my wet jacket, there seemed many a pleasant haven in store.

We gained an offing; the pilots were needed no more; the stout sail-boat ranged alongside. Loath to depart a ship bound beyond both stormy Capes, poor old Bildad lingered; paced; peered windward and landward; coiled a rope; grasped stout Peleg’s hand; held up a lantern, as if to say, “Nevertheless, friend Peleg, I can stand it.” Peleg took it more like a philosopher; yet when the lantern came too near, a tear twinkled.

“Captain Bildad—come, old shipmate, we must go. Back the main-yard! Boat ahoy!—Luck to ye, Starbuck—Mr. Stubb—Mr. Flask—good-bye and good luck to ye all—and this day three years I’ll have a hot supper smoking for ye in old Nantucket. Hurrah and away!”

“God bless ye, and have ye in His holy keeping,” murmured Bildad. “Mind the cooper—don’t waste spare staves. Don’t whale it too much a’ Lord’s days; but don’t miss a fair chance either. Beware of fornication if ye touch at the islands. The sail-needles are in the green locker!—and—”

“Come, come, Captain Bildad; away!” cried Peleg, hurrying him over.

Ship and boat diverged; a screaming gull flew overhead; the two hulls wildly rolled; we gave three heavy-hearted cheers, and blindly plunged like fate into the lone Atlantic.


## Chapter 5

On that shivering winter’s night when the Pequod thrust her vindictive bows into the malicious waves, who should stand at her helm but Bulkington! Newly landed from a four years’ voyage, he pushed unrestingly off again. The port would fain give succor; there is safety, comfort, hearthstone there; but in such a gale, the land is the ship’s direst jeopardy; one graze, and she shudders through and through; with all her might she crowds off shore—seeks the lashed sea’s landlessness again—her only friend her bitterest foe!

Know ye now, Bulkington? As in landlessness alone resides highest truth—shoreless, indefinite as God—so better perish in that howling infinite, than be ingloriously dashed upon the lee! Take heart, O Bulkington! Up from the spray of thy ocean-perishing—straight up, leaps thy apotheosis!


## Chapter 6

We are now fairly embarked in whaling; and since landsmen count it unpoetical, butcherly, unclean and disreputable, I am all anxiety to right us.

Butchers we are—true; but butchers also—of the bloodiest badge—are all Martial Commanders whom the world delights to honor. As for uncleanliness, ye shall be initiated ere long into certain facts; but even granting the charge—what are slippery decks to the carrion of the battle-field, from which so many soldiers return to drink in all ladies’ plaudits? And if peril ennobles the soldier, let me assure ye many a veteran who has marched up to a battery would quickly recoil at the apparition of the sperm whale’s vast tail fanning into eddies the air over his head. For what are the comprehensible terrors of man to the interlinked terrors and wonders of God!

Though the world scouts us, yet unwittingly it pays us profound homage: almost all the tapers, lamps, and candles that burn round the globe, burn—as before so many shrines—to our glory!

Look in other lights. Why admirals of whaling fleets in De Witt’s day? Why Louis XVI fitting out ships from Dunkirk, inviting Nantucket families? Why Britain paying bounties by the million? How comes it that we Americans outnumber all others; seven hundred vessels; eighteen thousand men; millions of dollars afloat; and every year a harvest brought home—if there be not something puissant in whaling?

But this is not the half. The whale-ship has been the pioneer to remotest seas; she explored archipelagoes where no Cook or Vancouver sailed; first interpreted between men-of-war and savages; showed them the way to once savage harbors. Your Cooks and Krusensterns are trumpeted; I say anonymous captains out of Nantucket were as great, and greater. Until the fishery rounded Cape Horn, scarce any but colonial commerce touched the opulent Spanish Pacific; the whaleman broke the jealous policy of the crown, and in good time—if space permitted one might track the chain—helped eventuate the liberation of Peru, Chili, and Bolivia. Australia—blunder-born by a Dutchman—other ships shunned; the whale-ship touched there, mothering that mighty colony, yea, once feeding its infancy with her benevolent biscuit. Polynesia confesses the same. And if that double-bolted land, Japan, is ever to become hospitable, it is the whale-ship alone to whom the credit will be due; for already she is on the threshold.

“No æsthetic dignity,” you say? Who first wrote of Leviathan but mighty Job? Who first chronicled a whaling voyage but Alfred the Great, taking down the words of Other the Norwegian? Who pronounced our eulogy in Parliament but Edmund Burke?

“No good blood in our veins?” Better than royal there. Benjamin Franklin’s grandmother was Mary Folger of Nantucket—kith and kin to harpooneers—noble Benjamin’s own stock darting the barbed iron round the world.

“Not respectable?” Whaling is imperial. By old English law the whale is “a royal fish.” “Only nominal?” In a Roman triumph, the bones of a whale, brought from the Syrian coast, were the most conspicuous object in the cymballed procession. “No dignity?” The heavens attest it: Cetus is a constellation in the South! No more! Drive down your hat in presence of the Czar, and take it off to Queequeg!

And as for me—if there be any undiscovered prime thing in me; if ever I deserve repute; if, at my death, any precious MSS. be found—then I prospectively ascribe all the honor and the glory to whaling; for a whale-ship was my Yale College and my Harvard.


## Chapter 7

An advocate, after embattling his facts, may season them with a not unreasonable surmise. Kings and queens, at coronation, are solemnly oiled—as a head of salad. What kind of oil? Not olive, nor macassar, nor castor, nor bear’s, nor train, nor cod-liver. What then can it be but sperm oil in its unmanufactured, unpolluted state—the sweetest of all oils?

Think of that, ye loyal Britons! we whalemen supply your kings and queens with coronation stuff!


## Chapter 8

The chief mate was Starbuck, a Nantucketer and of Quaker descent; a long, earnest man; hard as twice-baked biscuit; the condensation of the man rather than any wasting blight. Looking into his eyes you seemed to see the lingering images of thousand-fold perils calmly confronted.

Uncommonly conscientious for a seaman, and endued with a deep natural reverence, the wild, watery loneliness of his life inclined him to that superstition which in some organizations springs from intelligence rather than ignorance. Outward portents and inward presentiments were his. Far-away domestic memories—his young Cape wife and child—bent him further from native ruggedness, restraining the gush of dare-devil daring so often evinced in the fishery. “I will have no man in my boat,” said Starbuck, “who is not afraid of a whale.” He meant, the most reliable courage rises from a fair estimation of peril; the utterly fearless man is a more dangerous comrade than a coward.

Courage with him was not a sentiment, but a thing useful, laid by and ready. He had no fancy for lowering after sun-down; nor for persisting with a fish that too much persisted with him. He was here to kill whales for his living, not to be killed by them for theirs; and that hundreds had so perished he well knew. What doom was his own father’s? Where, in the bottomless deeps, the torn limbs of his brother?

Thus tempered, his courage must have been extreme; yet there was in him that element which, under a suitable circumstance, might break confinement and burn his bravery up: those more spiritual terrors which sometimes menace you from the concentrating brow of an enraged and mighty man. Still, for all men—meanest mariners and renegades—I claim that abounding dignity which has no robed investiture; that democratic dignity which radiates from God Himself. If hereafter I weave round such men tragic graces, and even the most abased shall at times lift to the exalted mounts—bear me out in it, thou just Spirit of Equality!


## Chapter 9

Stubb, the second mate, a Cape-Cod-man, was happy-go-lucky; neither craven nor valiant; taking perils as they came with an indifferent air; in the very death-lock of the fight handling his unpitying lance as a whistling tinker his hammer; humming old rigadig tunes while flank and flank with the exasperated monster. Long usage had converted the jaws of death into an easy chair for him. What made him so cheerily impious against mortal tribulations?—his pipe, perhaps. Like his nose, his short, black little pipe was a regular feature. He kept a whole row of pipes ready loaded in a rack, and when he turned in, he smoked them all out in succession, lighting one from the other to the chapter’s end; loading them again against his next waking. When Stubb dressed, instead of first putting legs into trowsers, he put his pipe into his mouth.

The third mate was Flask, of Tisbury in Martha’s Vineyard; short, stout, ruddy; very pugnacious concerning whales, who in his poor opinion were but magnified mice—water-rats—to be circumvented and boiled. He followed the fish for the fun of it; three years round the Horn a jolly joke. They called him King-Post, he being like that short, square timber in Arctic whalers against icy concussions—wrought, made to clinch tight and last long.

These three—Starbuck, Stubb, Flask—were momentous men, headsmen of the three boats. In Ahab’s grand order of battle, they were as captains of companies; armed with long keen spears; their harpooneers as flingers of javelins; squire and Knight.

First among the harpooneers was Queequeg—Starbuck’s squire.

Next, Tashtego, pure-blooded Indian of Gay-Head, high cheek-boned, black rounding eyes—Oriental in largeness, Antarctic in glitter; the infallible arrow of his sires replaced by the unerring harpoon. He was Stubb’s squire.

Third, Daggoo, gigantic, coal-black, lion-treading; two golden hoops in his ears like ring-bolts; six feet five in his socks, erect as a giraffe; the corporeal humility of looking up at him made a white man seem a white flag suing truce of a fortress. Curious to tell, this imperial negro was squire to little Flask, who looked like a chess-man beside him.

As for the residue, at the present day not one in two before the mast in the American fishery are Americans born, though pretty nearly all the officers are. Islanders make the best whalemen: Azores, Shetlands; they are nearly all Islanders in the Pequod—Isolatoes, I call such, each living on his own separate continent; yet now, federated along one keel, an Anacharsis Clootz deputation from all the isles of the sea, and all ends of the earth, accompanying Old Ahab to lay the world’s grievances before that bar from which not many ever come back. Black Little Pip—he never did—oh, no! Poor Alabama boy! Soon ye shall see him beating his tambourine on the grim forecastle; prelusive of the eternal time when bid strike in with angels in glory; called a coward here, hailed a hero there!


## Chapter 10

For several days after leaving Nantucket, nothing above hatches was seen of Captain Ahab. The mates regularly relieved each other; yet, ever and anon, they came from the cabin with sudden, peremptory orders: they but commanded vicariously. Their supreme lord and dictator was there, unseen in the now sacred retreat.

My vague disquiet touching the unknown captain, heightened by Elijah’s diabolical incoherences, grew at sea to perturbation. Yet the aspect of the three officers allayed misgivings: better sea-men could scarce be found. Running south, winter bit us less and less; one grey, gloomy morning of transition, I mounted to the deck—foreboding shivers—and reality outran apprehension: Captain Ahab stood upon his quarter-deck.

No common illness marked him. He looked like a man cut away from the stake when the fire has wasted all the limbs without consuming them; his high, broad form seemed solid bronze, shaped in an unalterable mould, like Cellini’s Perseus. Threading from among his grey hairs, and down one side of his tawny, scorched face and neck, disappeared in his clothing, a slender, livid, rod-like mark—as when upper lightning peels and grooves a lofty trunk, leaving it greenly alive but branded. Whether birth-mark or the scar of some desperate wound, no one could say. Some whispered of elemental strife; a grey Manxman muttered that whoever should lay him out would find a birth-mark from crown to sole.

Then, too, the barbaric white leg upon which he partly stood—the ivory fashioned from the polished jaw-bone of a sperm whale. He steadied it in an auger-hole bored near the mizzen shrouds; one arm held a shroud; his face fixed straight out beyond the ever-pitching prow. In that fearless forward dedication of glance was an infinity of firmest fortitude; in his face, a crucifixion. He spoke not; nor did the officers address him; yet in all their minutest gestures, they owned the uneasy consciousness of a troubled master-eye.

Ere long he withdrew; but thereafter he was daily visible—at his pivot-hole, upon an ivory stool, or heavily pacing. As the sky grew genial, he grew less recluse, as if the dead wintry bleakness alone had sequestered him. For a time, he seemed as unnecessary as another mast; the Pequod was but making a passage; the mates sufficed. Yet gradually the warm, warbling persuasiveness of the holiday weather charmed him a little from his mood; and more than once he put forth the faint blossom of a look which, in any other man, would have soon flowered out in a smile.
