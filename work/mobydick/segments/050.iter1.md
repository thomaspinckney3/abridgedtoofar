It was a cloudy, sultry afternoon; the seamen lazily lounged about the decks, or vacantly gazed into the lead-coloured waters. Queequeg and I were weaving a sword-mat for our boat’s lashing. So subdued and preluding was the scene that each silent sailor seemed resolved into his own invisible self.

I, the attendant, passed the woof of marline between the warp with my hand for shuttle; Queequeg, standing sideways, slid his heavy oaken sword between the threads and, idly looking off, drove home every yarn. The dull intermittent sound of the sword ruled the ship and sea; it seemed the Loom of Time, and I the shuttle weaving at the Fates. There lay the fixed threads of the warp, subject to but one ever-returning vibration. This warp seemed necessity; and here, thought I, with my own hand I ply my own shuttle and weave my own destiny into these unalterable threads. Meantime, Queequeg’s impulsive, indifferent sword—sometimes hitting slantingly or strongly, sometimes weakly—shaped the fabric; this easy, indifferent sword must be chance—aye, chance, free will, and necessity—nowise incompatible—all interweavingly working together. The straight warp of necessity, not to be swerved from its ultimate course—its every alternating vibration, indeed, only tending to that; free will still free to ply her shuttle between given threads; and chance, though restrained in its play within the right lines of necessity, and sideways in its motions directed by free will, though thus prescribed to by both, chance by turns rules either, and has the last featuring blow at events.

Thus we were weaving and weaving away when a sound so strange, long, and musically wild and unearthly came that the ball of free will dropped from my hand, and I stood gazing up at the clouds whence that voice dropped like a wing. High in the cross-trees that mad Gay-Header, Tashtego, leaned forward, hand stretched like a wand; hovering half in air, he might have been some prophet beholding the shadows of Fate.

“There she blows! there! there! there! she blows! she blows!”

“Where-away?”

“On the lee-beam, about two miles off! a school of them!”

Instant commotion. The Sperm Whale blows as a clock ticks—same undeviating uniformity—and thereby whalemen know him. “There go flukes!” cried Tashtego, and the whales disappeared. “Time! time!” called Ahab. Dough-Boy ran, glanced at the watch, and reported the minute. The ship kept away; cranes thrust out; line tubs fixed; mainyard backed; the three boats swung over the sea like samphire baskets over cliffs; outside the bulwarks their eager crews clung and poised.

At that instant a sudden exclamation drew every eye from the whale. Dark Ahab stood surrounded by five dusky phantoms, fresh-formed out of air.
