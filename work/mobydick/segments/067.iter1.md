Stubb’s whale had been killed far from the ship; in a flat calm we made a tandem of three boats and, with eighteen men, thirty-six arms, and one hundred and eighty thumbs and fingers, slowly towed the inert, heaving bulk toward the Pequod. Three dim lights in the main-rigging guided us; drawing nearer we saw Ahab drop more lanterns, issue the usual orders for securing it for the night, hand his light to a seaman, and vanish to his cabin. Though hot in the chase, he now showed some obscure impatience or despair, as if the dead body only reminded him that Moby Dick yet lived, and a thousand other whales were nothing to his monomaniac end. Tied by the head to the stern and by the tail to the bows, the black hull of whale and ship lay close, yoked like colossal bullocks—one reclining, one standing.

A word for the flukes: the surest and most reliable hold is the tail; but as, from its greater density and death’s flexibility, it sinks low, you cannot with the hand get at it from the boat to put the chain round it. The difficulty is ingeniously overcome: a small line with a float and a mid-weight is led adroitly so the float rises on the far side; having girdled the mass, the chain readily follows and is locked fast round the smallest part, hard by the lobes.

If Ahab grew still, Stubb—flush with conquest—bustled; the staid Starbuck even yielded him the deck. One small, helping cause of all this liveliness was soon made manifest. Stubb was a high liver; he was somewhat intemperately fond of the whale as a flavorish thing to his palate. “A steak, a steak, ere I sleep! You, Daggoo! overboard you go, and cut me one from his small!” Here be it known, that though these wild fishermen do not, as a general thing, and according to the great military maxim, make the enemy defray the current expenses of the war (at least before realizing the proceeds of the voyage), yet now and then you find some of these Nantucketers who have a genuine relish for that particular part of the Sperm Whale designated by Stubb; comprising the tapering extremity of the body.

About midnight that steak was cut and cooked; and lighted by two lanterns of sperm oil, Stubb stoutly stood up to his spermaceti supper at the capstan-head, as if that capstan were a sideboard. Nor was Stubb the only banqueter on whale’s flesh that night. Mingling their mumblings with his own mastications, thousands on thousands of sharks, swarming round the dead leviathan, smackingly feasted on its fatness. The few sleepers below in their bunks were often startled by the sharp slapping of their tails against the hull, within a few inches of the sleepers’ hearts. Peering over the side you could just see them (as before you heard them) wallowing in the sullen, black waters, and turning over on their backs as they scooped out huge globular pieces of the whale of the bigness of a human head. This particular feat of the shark seems all but miraculous. How at such an apparently unassailable surface, they contrive to gouge out such symmetrical mouthfuls, remains a part of the universal problem of all things. The mark they thus leave on the whale, may best be likened to the hollow made by a carpenter in countersinking for a screw.

Though amid all the smoking horror and diabolism of a sea-fight, sharks will be seen longingly gazing up to the ship’s decks, like hungry dogs round a table where red meat is being carved, ready to bolt down every killed man that is tossed to them; and though, while the valiant butchers over the deck-table are thus cannibally carving each other’s live meat with carving-knives all gilded and tasselled, the sharks, also, with their jewel-hilted mouths, are quarrelsomely carving away under the table at the dead meat; and though, were you to turn the whole affair upside down, it would still be pretty much the same thing, that is to say, a shocking sharkish business enough for all parties; and though sharks also are the invariable outriders of all slave ships crossing the Atlantic, systematically trotting alongside, to be handy in case a parcel is to be carried anywhere, or a dead slave to be decently buried; and though one or two other like instances might be set down, touching the set terms, places, and occasions, when sharks do most socially congregate, and most hilariously feast; yet is there no conceivable time or occasion when you will find them in such countless numbers, and in gayer or more jovial spirits, than around a dead sperm whale, moored by night to a whaleship at sea. If you have never seen that sight, then suspend your decision about the propriety of devil-worship, and the expediency of conciliating the devil.

But Stubb heeded not the mumblings so nigh him. “Cook, cook!—where’s that old Fleece?” he cried, widening his legs for a surer base, and stabbing his fork into the dish as with a lance. The old black, not in any very high glee at having been previously roused from his warm hammock at a most unseasonable hour, came shambling along from his galley; this old Fleece, as they called him—this old Ebony—came shuffling and limping, assisting his step with his tongs, which, after a clumsy fashion, were made of straightened iron hoops; and, in obedience to the word of command, came to a dead stop on the opposite side of Stubb’s sideboard, bowed his arched back lower, and sideways inclined his head to bring his best ear into play.

“Cook,” said Stubb, rapidly lifting a rather reddish morsel, “don’t you think this steak is rather overdone? Don’t I always say that to be good, a whale-steak must be tough? See those sharks prefer it tough and rare! Go talk to ’em: welcome to help themselves civilly, and in moderation—but they must keep quiet. Blast me, if I can hear my own voice. Away, cook—and preach to ’em!” Sullenly taking the lantern, Fleece leaned over the bulwarks and began: “Fellow-critters: I’se ordered here to say dat you must stop dat dam noise. Massa Stubb say you can fill your dam bellies up to de hatchings; but—” “Cook!”—a slap—“you mustn’t swear when you’re preaching.” “Well, den—Belubed fellow-critters: you is all sharks, and by natur wery woracious; but to gobiern dat wicked natur, dat is de pint. You is sharks, sartin; but if you gobiern de shark in you, why den you be angel; for all angel is not’ing more dan de shark well goberned. Don’t be tearin’ de blubber out your neighbour’s mout. Is not one shark dood right as toder to dat whale? And, by Gor, none on you has de right to dat whale; dat whale belong to some one else.” Soon he despaired: “No use a-preachin’ to such dam g’uttons till dare bellies is full; and when full, dey sink on de coral and can’t hear not’ing, for eber and eber.” “Give the benediction, Fleece.” He lifted his hands over the fishy mob: “Cussed fellow-critters! Kick up de damndest row as ever you can; fill your dam’ bellies ’till dey bust—and den die.”

“Now, cook—attend.” Stubb plied his fork, then cross-questioned him on age and birthplace; with sudden gravity: “Do you belong to the church?”—“Passed one once in Cape-Down.” On heaven: Fleece pointed “up dere” with his tongs; Stubb mocked the lubber’s-hole to glory—“you don’t get there except the regular way, round by the rigging.” Then orders: “Next time, hold the steak in one hand, and show a live coal to it with the other; that done, dish it. To-morrow, when we are cutting in, stand by for the tips of his fins—put them in pickle. Have the ends of the flukes soused.” As Fleece limped off: “Cutlets for supper to-morrow in the mid-watch!—Avast! Whale-balls for breakfast—don’t forget.” And old Fleece, vanishing to his hammock, muttered: “Wish, by gor! whale eat him, ’stead of him eat whale. I’m bressed if he ain’t more of shark dan Massa Shark hisself.”
