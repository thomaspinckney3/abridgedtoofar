First, a plain statement touching the living bulk. On careful calculation (partly upon Scoresby’s seventy tons for a sixty-foot Greenland), a Sperm Whale of largest magnitude—eighty-five to ninety feet long, somewhat under forty in girth—will weigh at least ninety tons. Think you not then that brains, like yoked cattle, should be put to this leviathan, to make him at all budge to any landsman’s imagination?

But as the colossal skull embraces so very large a proportion of the entire extent of the skeleton, and is most complicated, you must not fail to carry it in your mind, or under your arm, as we proceed, otherwise you will not gain a complete notion. At Tranque, the skeleton measured seventy-two feet; fully invested in life, he must have been ninety, for the skeleton loses about one fifth. Of the seventy-two, skull and jaw comprised some twenty, leaving fifty feet of back-bone, and, for something less than a third of that, the mighty circular basket of ribs. To me this vast ivory-ribbed chest, with the long, unrelieved spine, extending far away from it in a straight line, not a little resembled the hull of a great ship new-laid upon the stocks, when only some twenty of her naked bow-ribs are inserted, and the keel is otherwise, for the time, but a long, disconnected timber.

The ribs were ten a side; the first near six feet; the second, third, and fourth successively longer, to the climax of the fifth—eight feet and some inches; thence they diminished, the tenth spanning a little over five. The skeleton is by no means the mould of the invested form. Where the fish is greatest in depth, the corresponding rib conveyed but half the living magnitude. Besides, for some way, where I now saw but a naked spine, all that had been once wrapped round with tons of added bulk in flesh, muscle, blood, and bowels. Still more, for the ample fins, I here saw but a few disordered joints; and in place of the weighty and majestic, but boneless flukes, an utter blank!

How vain and foolish, then, thought I, for timid untravelled man to try to comprehend aright this wondrous whale, by merely poring over his dead attenuated skeleton, stretched in this peaceful wood. No. Only in the heart of quickest perils; only when within the eddyings of his angry flukes; only on the profound unbounded sea, can the fully invested whale be truly and livingly found out.

But the spine—pile its bones on end with a crane; it looks like Pompey’s Pillar. There are forty and odd vertebræ, not locked; they lie like great knobbed blocks in Gothic courses. The largest, a middle one, is under three feet in width, more than four in depth; the smallest, where the spine tapers to the tail, but two inches wide—like a white billiard-ball. Some smaller were lost by little cannibal urchins who played marbles with them. Thus the spine of the hugest of living things tapers off at last into simple child’s play.
