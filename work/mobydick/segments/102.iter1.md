Ahab, pacing between binnacle and mainmast, often paused—now nailing his glance to the compass, now to the riveted gold coin he had set in the mast. One morning he newly studied the strange figures: And some certain significance lurks in all things, else all things are little worth, and the round world itself but an empty cipher, except to sell by the cartload, as they do hills about Boston, to fill up some morass in the Milky Way. Now this doubloon was of purest, virgin gold, raked somewhere out of the heart of gorgeous hills, whence, east and west, over golden sands, the head-waters of many a Pactolus flows. Nor, though placed amongst a ruthless crew and every hour passed by ruthless hands, and through the livelong nights shrouded with thick darkness which might cover any pilfering approach, nevertheless every sunrise found the doubloon where the sunset left it last. For it was set apart and sanctified to one awe-striking end; and however wanton in their sailor ways, one and all, the mariners revered it as the white whale’s talisman. Now those noble golden coins of South America are as medals of the sun and tropic token-pieces. Here palms, alpacas, and volcanoes; sun’s disks and stars; ecliptics, horns-of-plenty, and rich banners waving, are in luxuriant profusion stamped; so that the precious gold seems almost to derive an added preciousness by passing through those fancy mints, so Spanishly poetic. The equatorial doubloon from Quito—REPUBLICA DEL ECUADOR: QUITO—its three Andean peaks (flame, tower, cock), and the arched zodiac, the sun entering Libra.

“There’s something ever egotistical in mountain-tops and towers,” he murmured. “The firm tower, that is Ahab; the volcano, that is Ahab; the courageous fowl, that, too, is Ahab; all are Ahab; and this round gold is but the image of the rounder globe, which, like a magician’s glass, mirrors back each man’s mysterious self. Great pains, small gains for those who ask the world to solve them; it cannot solve itself. See—he enters the sign of storms, the equinox! From storm to storm! Born in throes, ’tis fit that man should live in pains and die in pangs! So be it, then!”

“No fairy fingers can have pressed the gold, but devil’s claws must have left their mouldings there since yesterday,” murmured Starbuck to himself. “The old man seems to read Belshazzar’s awful writing.” Starbuck, leaning by, read otherwise: a dark vale between three mighty peaks, almost the Trinity in faint earthly symbol. In this vale of Death God girds us round; and over all our gloom the sun of Righteousness shines a beacon. “This coin speaks wisely, mildly, truly—but sadly. I will quit it, lest Truth shake me falsely.”

Stubb by the try-works: “Faces nine fathoms long from a piece of gold I’d spend quick on Negro Hill. What’s in this doubloon? Signs and wonders! The zodiac! Book! you lie there; the fact is, you books must know your places. You’ll do to give us the bare words and facts, but we come in to supply the thoughts. Look you, Doubloon—your zodiac is man’s life: Aries begets us; Taurus bumps us first; Gemini, Virtue and Vice; then Cancer drags us back; Leo bites and paws; we escape and hail Virgo—our first love; we marry; then Libra—happiness weighed and found wanting; while we’re sad, Scorpio stings our rear; we pluck the shafts, when Sagittarius showers arrows; stand aside! Capricorn, the battering-ram, tosses us; Aquarius drowns us; Pisces—we sleep. The sun goes through it jollily each year, and so does jolly Stubb. Adieu, Doubloon!”

Flask: “A round thing of gold; whoever raises a certain whale gets it. Worth sixteen dollars—nine hundred and sixty cigars.”

The old Manxman shuffled round the mast, muttering: “In a month and a day, when the sun stands in one of these signs. The horse-shoe opposite—Leo, the roaring and devouring lion. Ship, old ship! my old head shakes to think of thee.”

“There’s another rendering now; but still one text. All sorts of men in one kind of world, you see.”

Queequeg, tattooed like the zodiac itself, compared the coin to his own thigh-bone—found Sagittarius, or some king’s old button; shook his head.

Fedallah came silently, made a sign to the sign, and bowed; a fire-worshipper acknowledging the sun.

“Ho! more and more. This way comes Pip—poor boy! would he had died, or I; he’s half horrible to me.”

Then Pip—poor boy!—with that unearthly idiot face, came to read: “I look, you look, he looks; we look, ye look, they look.” He said it again—getting it by heart. Then, with sudden cawing rapture: “And I, you, and he; and we, ye, and they, are all bats; and I’m a crow, especially when I stand a’top of this pine tree here. Caw! caw! Where’s the scare-crow? There he stands; two bones stuck into a pair of old trowsers, and two more poked into the sleeves of an old jacket.”

“Here’s the ship’s navel,” he ran on, “and they are all on fire to unscrew it. Unscrew your navel—what then? But if it stays nailed—ugly, too; when aught’s nailed to the mast, things grow desperate. Ha, ha! old Ahab! the White Whale; he’ll nail ye!” Then, wandering to Tolland memories: a pine cut down with a silver ring grown over; so, in the resurrection, men shall fish up this old mast and find a doubloon lodged with bedded oysters for bark. “Oh, the gold! the precious, precious, gold! Hish! hish! God goes ’mong the worlds blackberrying. Cook! ho, cook! and cook us! Jenny! hey, hey, get your hoe-cake done!” And Stubb, over-witty for his own sanity, slipped away from Pip’s too-crazy wit, leaving him muttering about the nailed coin and nailed fates.
