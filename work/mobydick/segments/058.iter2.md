Ere long I shall paint to you, as well as one may without canvas, something like the true form of the whale when his absolute body is moored alongside, and you may fairly step upon him. But first, let us advert to those curious imaginary portraits which even to-day confidently challenge the landsman’s faith. It is time to set the world right by proving such pictures all wrong.

From the primal Hindoo, Egyptian, and Grecian sculptures, down to the Christian painters, license has prevailed. In the famous cavern-pagoda of Elephanta, the Matse Avatar—half man, half whale—gives a tail more like an anaconda’s taper than the broad palms of the true flukes. Guido’s Perseus and Hogarth’s “Perseus Descending” do no better: a howdah on its back, and a tusked mouth taken for Traitors’ Gate. The book-binder’s dolphin-whale, winding like a vine-stalk round an anchor—picturesque but fabulous—lingers from old devices.

Go to old voyagers and “scientific” delineators. In Harris’s plates from a Dutch Spitzbergen voyage (1671), white bears run over raft-like whales; elsewhere, the prodigious blunder of perpendicular flukes. Captain Colnett’s “Physeter,” “drawn by scale” from one hoisted on deck, has an eye which, by its own scale applied to a full-grown sperm whale, would make that eye a bow-window five feet long—ah, my gallant captain, why did ye not give us Jonah looking out of it! Nor are the most conscientious compilations for the young and tender free from the same heinousness. Even Goldsmith’s Animated Nature (1807) gives a whale much like an amputated sow, and a “narwhale” that is a hippogriff palmed for genuine upon schoolboys. Lacépède errs; even Scoresby denies his Mysticetus any counterpart in nature. But the cap-sheaf was reserved for Frederick Cuvier (1836), who gives what he calls a picture of the Sperm Whale—not a Sperm Whale, but a squash; perhaps, like Desmarest, from a Chinese drawing—and what lively lads with the pencil those Chinese are, many queer cups and saucers inform us.

As for the sign-painters’ whales seen in the streets hanging over oil-shops, what shall be said of them? They are generally Richard III. whales, with dromedary humps, and very savage; breakfasting on three or four sailor tarts, that is whaleboats full of mariners: their deformities floundering in seas of blood and blue paint. Yet are these manifold mistakes not so surprising. Most drawings are from the stranded fish: as a wrecked ship with broken back ill-images her proud hull at sea, so the stranded whale falsifies his living swells and undulations. Though elephants have stood for their full-lengths, the living Leviathan has never yet fairly floated himself for his portrait; afloat the vast bulk of him is out of sight, like a launched line-of-battle ship; and out of that element it is a thing eternally impossible for mortal man to hoist him bodily into the air, so as to preserve all his mighty swells and undulations. And, not to speak of the highly presumable difference of contour between a young sucking whale and a full-grown Platonian Leviathan; yet, even in the case of one of those young sucking whales hoisted to a ship’s deck, such is then the outlandish, eel-like, limbered, varying shape of him, that his precise expression the devil himself could not catch.

Nor does the skeleton yield his contour. Though Jeremy Bentham’s skeleton, hung for candelabra in an executor’s library, correctly conveys the idea of a burly-browed utilitarian old gentleman; yet nothing of this kind could be inferred from any leviathan’s articulated bones. As the great Hunter says, the skeleton bears the same relation to the fully invested animal as the insect to the chrysalis that roundly envelopes it. Mark, too, the side fin: its bones almost exactly answer to the human hand, minus only the thumb—four fingers—yet all forever mittened in flesh. “However recklessly the whale may sometimes serve us,” said humorous Stubb, “he can never be truly said to handle us without mittens.”

So conclude: the great Leviathan must remain unpainted to the last. One portrait may hit nearer than another; none can hit with any considerable exactness. The only tolerable idea of his living contour is by going a-whaling yourself; but in so doing, you run no small risk of being eternally stove and sunk. Wherefore, be not too fastidious in your curiosity touching this Leviathan.
