“Hist! did you hear that noise, Cabaco?” Middle-watch; fair moon; the men, in a cordon from butt to scuttle-butt, passed the buckets on the hallowed quarter-deck, careful not to speak or rustle. It was in the midst of this repose, that Archy, one of the cordon, whose post was near the after-hatches, whispered to his neighbor, a Cholo, the words above.

“There it is again—under the hatches—don’t you hear it—a cough—it sounded like a cough.”

“Cough be damned! Pass along that return bucket.”

“There again—there it is!—it sounds like two or three sleepers turning over, now!”

“Caramba! it’s the three soaked biscuits ye ate for supper turning over inside of ye—look to the bucket!”

“I’ve sharp ears.”

“Aye, you’re the chap that heard the old Quakeress’s knitting-needles fifty miles from Nantucket—the chap.”

“Grin away; Hark ye, Cabaco, there is somebody down in the after-hold that has not yet been seen on deck; and I suspect our old Mogul knows something of it too. I heard Stubb tell Flask, one morning watch, that there was something of that sort in the wind.”

“Tish! the bucket!”
