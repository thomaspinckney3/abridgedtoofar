At daybreak. “D’ye see him?”—“See nothing, sir.”—“Turn up all hands and make sail!—the top-gallant sails!—’tis but resting for the rush.”

Such pertinacious pursuit—through day into night, and night into day—was no marvel to the South Sea fishery; by compass and experience a sagacious commander reads a hidden wake as a pilot reads a coast, till for a time a thing writ in water is as sure as land.

The ship tore on, leaving such a furrow as when a cannon-ball, missent, becomes a plough-share. “By salt and hemp!” cried Stubb, “this swift motion of the deck creeps up one’s legs and tingles at the heart. Some one launch me, spine-wise—by live-oaks! my spine’s a keel. Ha! we go the gait that leaves no dust behind!” “There she blows—right ahead!”—“Aye, aye!” cried Stubb, “I knew it—ye can’t escape—blow on and split your spout, O whale! the mad fiend himself is after ye! blow your trump—blister your lungs!—Ahab will dam off your blood, as a miller shuts his watergate upon the stream!” And so, bubblingly wrought up by the frenzies of the chase, whatever pale fears they had were routed, as timid prairie hares scatter before the bounding bison. The hand of Fate had snatched all their souls; the wind that made great bellies of their sails, and rushed the vessel on by arms invisible as irresistible—this seemed the symbol of that unseen agency which so enslaved them to the race. They were one man, not thirty. For as the one ship that held them all; though it was put together of all contrasting things—oak, and maple, and pine wood; iron, and pitch, and hemp—yet all these ran into each other in the one concrete hull, balanced and directed by the long central keel; even so, all the individualities of the crew, this man’s valor, that man’s fear; guilt and guiltiness, all varieties were welded into oneness, and were all directed to that fatal goal which Ahab their one lord and keel did point to. The rigging lived. The mast-heads, like the tops of tall palms, were outspreadingly tufted with arms and legs... Ah! how they still strove through that infinite blueness to seek out the thing that might destroy them! “Why sing ye not out for him, if ye see him?” cried Ahab; “ye have been deceived; not Moby Dick casts one odd jet that way, and then disappears.” Hardly had he reached his perch, when—much nearer than the place of the imaginary jet—Moby Dick bodily burst into view, breaching; rising with his utmost velocity from the furthest depths, booming his entire bulk into the pure air, piling up a mountain of dazzling foam. In those moments, the torn, enraged waves he shakes off, seem his mane; in some cases, this breaching is his act of defiance. “There she breaches! there she breaches!”—spray intolerably glittering, then fading to the dim mist of an advancing shower.

“Aye, breach thy last to the sun, Moby Dick!” cried Ahab. “Down all but one man at the fore. The boats!—stand by!” Men slid like shooting stars by backstays and halyards. “Lower away. Mr. Starbuck, the ship is thine—keep away from the boats, yet keep near them. Lower, all!”

Moby Dick turned, coming for the three crews. Ahab, central, cheered to take him head-and-head; but ere that close limit was gained, the White Whale, churning himself into furious speed, rushed among them with open jaws and a lashing tail, appalling on every side; heedless of irons, intent on annihilating every plank. Incessantly wheeling, like trained chargers, the boats for a while eluded him, though at times by a plank’s breadth; while all the time Ahab’s unearthly slogan tore every other cry but his to shreds. At last, in his untraceable evolutions, the slack of the three lines now fast to him so crossed and recrossed that they foreshortened, and of themselves warped the boats towards the planted irons. Seizing a moment as the whale drew a little aside, Ahab paid out, then hauled to clear the snarls—when up, corkscrewed in the mazes of line, came loose harpoons and lances, all their barbs flashing to his very chocks. He reached within—through—and without—the rays of steel; twice sundered the rope, dropped the intercepted fagot of iron to the sea—fast again. That instant the White Whale rushed among the remaining tangles, dragged the more-involved boats of Stubb and Flask together like two rolling husks on a surf-beaten beach, and then, diving, disappeared in a boiling maelstrom, in which, for a space, the odorous cedar chips of the wrecks danced round and round, like the grated nutmeg in a swiftly stirred bowl of punch. While the two crews were yet circling in the waters, reaching for revolving tubs, oars, and floating furniture—while aslope little Flask bobbed up and down like an empty vial, twitching his legs upwards to escape the dreaded jaws of sharks, and Stubb was lustily singing out for some one to ladle him up—and while the old man’s line, now parting, admitted of his pulling into the creamy pool to rescue whom he could—arrow-like, shooting perpendicularly from the sea, the White Whale dashed his broad forehead against Ahab’s bottom, and sent it turning over and over till it fell gunwale down; the men struggled out like seals.

His first uprising involuntarily launched him a little away; feeling with his flukes from side to side, he smote at every chip that touched him; then, as if satisfied, pushed his pleated forehead on, trailing intertangled lines, and continued his leeward way at a traveller’s methodic pace.

As before, the ship bore down to the rescue; a boat was dropped; mariners, tubs, oars, and whatever else could be caught at were picked up and landed on deck. Sprained shoulders, wrists, and ankles; livid contusions; wrenched irons; intricacies of rope; shattered oars and planks—but no fatal ill. As with Fedallah the day before, so Ahab now grimly clung to his boat’s broken half.

Helped to the deck, he still half-hung on Starbuck’s shoulder. His ivory leg was snapped—one short sharp splinter left. “’Tis sweet to lean sometimes,” said Ahab, “be the leaner who he will.” “The ferrule has not stood, sir,” quoth the carpenter. “Aye! and all splintered to pieces, Stubb!—d’ye see it. But even with a broken bone, old Ahab is untouched; nor can white whale, nor man, nor fiend, so much as graze old Ahab in his own proper and inaccessible being. Aloft there! which way?”—“Dead to leeward, sir.”—“Up helm, then; pile on sail again, ship-keepers! Down the rest of the spare boats and rig them—Mr. Starbuck, away, and muster the crews.”

“Oh, how this splinter gores me now! Accursed fate!—that the unconquerable captain in the soul should have such a craven mate! Give me something for a cane—there, that shivered lance will do. Muster the men.” The Parsee was not there. “Caught among the tangles of your line,” Stubb muttered; “I thought I saw him dragging under.” “My line! my line? Gone?—gone? What means that little word?—What death-knell rings in it, that old Ahab shakes as if he were the belfry! The harpoon, too!—the forged iron—’tis in the fish!—Aloft there! Keep him nailed! Quick!—all hands!—I’ll ten times girdle the unmeasured globe; yea and dive straight through it, but I’ll slay him yet!”

“Great God! but for one single instant show thyself,” cried Starbuck. “Never, never wilt thou capture him, old man. In Jesus’ name no more of this! Two days chased; twice stove to splinters; thy very leg once more snatched; thy evil shadow gone; all good angels mobbing thee with warnings—what more wouldst thou have? Shall we be dragged by him to the bottom? towed to the infernal world? Oh, impiety and blasphemy to hunt him more!”

“Starbuck,” said Ahab, “of late I’ve felt strangely moved to thee; but in this matter be thy face to me a lipless blank. Ahab is for ever Ahab, man. This whole act’s immutably decreed. ’Twas rehearsed by thee and me a billion years before this ocean rolled. Fool! I am the Fates’ lieutenant; I act under orders. Look thou, underling! that thou obeyest mine.—Stand round me, men. Ye see an old man cut to the stump; propped on a lonely foot. ’Tis Ahab—his body’s part; but Ahab’s soul’s a centipede, that moves upon a hundred legs. I feel strained; but ere I break, ye’ll hear me crack; and till ye hear that, know Ahab’s hawser tows his purpose yet. Believe ye in omens? Laugh aloud, and cry encore! For ere they drown, drowning things will twice rise; then rise again, to sink for evermore. So with Moby Dick—two days he’s floated—tomorrow will be the third. Aye, men, he’ll rise once more,—but only to spout his last! D’ye feel brave?” “As fearless fire,” cried Stubb. “And as mechanical,” muttered Ahab. Then as the men went forward, he muttered on: “The things called omens! And yesterday I talked the same to Starbuck there, concerning my broken boat. Oh! how valiantly I seek to drive out of others’ hearts what’s clinched so fast in mine!—The Parsee—the Parsee!—gone, gone? and he was to go before:—but still was to be seen again ere I could perish—How’s that?—There’s a riddle now might baffle all the lawyers backed by the ghosts of the whole line of judges:—like a hawk’s beak it pecks my brain. I’ll, I’ll solve it, though!”

Dusk: the whale still to leeward; the sound of hammers and the hum of the grindstone till near daylight; new boats rigged, fresh steel sharpened. Of the broken keel of Ahab’s wrecked craft the carpenter made him another leg; and Ahab, slouched within his scuttle, his hid, heliotrope glance sat due eastward for the earliest sun.
