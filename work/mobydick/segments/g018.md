## Chapter 1

In the mid-watch Ahab thrust his face from the scuttle, snuffed the air like a sagacious ship’s dog, and swore a whale was near. Soon that peculiar odor of the living sperm-whale was plain to all. After taking compass and dog-vane, and matching the wind to the wafted scent, he shifted the ship a trifle, shortened sail—an acute policy soon vindicated by daybreak’s sight: a long, oily sleek, lengthwise ahead, with pleated wrinkles like the polished marks of some swift tide-rip.

“Man the mast-heads! Call all hands!”

Daggoo’s clubbed handspikes thundered; sleepers seemed to exhale from the scuttle, clothes in hand. Ahab cast loose the life-line, and was swayed aloft; while yet but two-thirds up, peering between main-top-sail and top-gallant-sail, he cried like a gull, “There she blows!—there she blows! A hump like a snow-hill! It is Moby Dick!”

The men rushed rigging-ward. Ahab gained his final perch above the other look-outs; Tashtego, just beneath, said he too had seen. “Not the same instant; not the same—no, the doubloon is mine,” Ahab answered; and then, lingering, methodic, “There she blows!—there she blows!—there she blows! There again!—there again!” Orders flew: “He’s going to sound! In stunsails! Down top-gallant-sails! Stand by three boats. Mr. Starbuck, stay on board and keep the ship! Helm there! Luff!—steady!”

Boats dropped; all but Starbuck’s spread small sails; paddles plied; Ahab headed the onset. Fedallah’s eyes burned with a pale death-glimmer. The ocean smoothed to a noon-meadow; the White Whale glided on, his dazzling hump ever and anon revealed, his broad, milky forehead casting a glistening white shadow before him, musical ripples playing to that shade; bright bubbles danced beside; a shattered lance-pole stuck like a flag-staff from his back, where soft-toed sea-fowl rocked, their tail-feathers streaming like pennons. A gentle joyousness—a mighty mildness of repose in swiftness—invested him; calm, enticing calm—the vesture of tornadoes.

Without showing the full terrors of his jaw, he rose, arched high—like Virginia’s Natural Bridge—bannered flukes waving, and sounded. The three boats floated stilly, oars apeak, sheets adrift, awaiting his reappearance. “The birds!—the birds!” cried Tashtego, as the white fowl came fluttering to Ahab’s boat, wheeling and crying. Ahab peered down; saw a white living spot uprising, magnifying as it rose—two long, crooked rows of glistening teeth, scrolled jaw, his vast bulk half-blending with the blue. The mouth yawned under the boat like an open-doored marble tomb. With a sidelong sweep of the steering oar Ahab whirled clear; then sprang to the bows, seized Perth’s harpoon, and bade his men stand by to stern.

Perceiving the stratagem, Moby Dick, with malicious intelligence, shot his pleated head lengthwise beneath the boat. Lying obliquely on his back, shark-like, he slowly, feelingly took the bows between his jaws; the scrolled lower jaw curled into the air, a tooth caught in a row-lock; the bluish pearl-white within was within six inches of Ahab’s head. The White Whale shook the slight cedar as a mildly cruel cat her mouse; Fedallah crossed his arms; the tiger-yellow crew tumbled aft. The gunwales sprang; then both jaws, like enormous shears, slid aft and bit the craft in twain; the halves drifted, the crew clung, striving to lash oars across.

Ahab, first to note the crafty upraising of the head, had leaned to push the boat out of the bite; it tilted, shook him off; he fell flat upon the sea. Moby Dick ripplingly withdrew, pitched up and down—pitchpoling—then resumed his horizontal attitude, and swam round and round the wrecked crew, churning the water in contracting circles. Ahab, half-smothered in the foam of the whale’s tail, too much of a cripple to swim yet afloat, bobbed like a tossed bubble. The other boats hung without, fearing to strike lest they doom the jeopardized castaways.

All this the ship had descried. Squaring her yards, she bore down; the old man, in the water, hailed her—“Sail on the”—a breaking sea whelmed him. Rising on a crest: “Sail on the whale!—Drive him off!” The Pequod broke the charmed circle; the whale sullenly swam off; the boats rescued.

Dragged into Stubb’s boat, brine crusting his wrinkles, Ahab’s long tension cracked; he lay as trodden by herds. But great hearts condense to one deep pang and rise. “The harpoon,” he gasped, leaning on one arm—“is it safe?” “Aye, sir; it was not darted.” “Any missing men?” “Five oars; five men.” “That’s good.—Help me. So—I see him! there! leeward still. Hands off! The eternal sap runs up in Ahab’s bones again! Set the sail; out oars; the helm!”

Double-banked now, they strained; but the whale seemed treble-banked in every fin. So the boats made for the ship; were swayed up; canvas stacked high, stun-sails outspread like an albatross’s double-jointed wings; the Pequod bore down in his wake. At methodic intervals the glittering spout was cried; at each report of “just gone down” Ahab took the time; when the hour expired: “Whose is the doubloon now? D’ye see him?”—then hoisted to his perch. The day wore on—Ahab now aloft and motionless; anon pacing, binnacle-watch in hand.

Passing, at every turn, his own wrecked boat dropped on the quarter-deck, he paused. Stubb, brave and mechanical, ventured a jest: “The thistle the ass refused; it pricked his mouth too keenly, sir; ha! ha!” “What soulless thing is this that laughs before a wreck? Groan nor laugh should be heard before a wreck.” “’Tis a solemn sight,” said Starbuck, “an omen, and an ill one.” “Omen?—the dictionary! If the gods think to speak outright to man, they will honorably speak outright; not shake their heads, and give an old wives’ darkling hint.—Begone! Ye two are the opposite poles of one thing; Starbuck is Stubb reversed, and Stubb is Starbuck; and ye two are all mankind; and Ahab stands alone among the millions of the peopled earth, nor gods nor men his neighbors!”

Dusk fell. “How heading when last seen?”—“Straight to leeward.”—“Good! he will travel slower now ’tis night. Down royals and top-gallant stun-sails. We must not run over him before morning.” Then to the doubloon: “Men, this gold is mine, for I earned it; but I shall let it abide here till the White Whale is dead; and then, whosoever of ye first raises him, upon the day he shall be killed, this gold is that man’s; and if on that day I shall again raise him, then, ten times its sum shall be divided among all of ye! Away now!—the deck is thine, sir!” Slouched in the scuttle, he stood till dawn, rousing at intervals to mark the night.


## Chapter 2

Daybreak. “D’ye see him?”—“See nothing, sir.”—“Turn up all hands and make sail!—the top-gallant sails!—’tis but resting for the rush.”

Such pertinacious pursuit—through day into night, night into day—was no marvel to South Sea fishery. From one day’s marked chase, by compass-bearing and experience, the sagacious commander reads a hidden wake as a pilot reads a coast gone from sight; the wind and sea his allies, a thing writ in water becomes, for a time, as sure as land.

The ship tore on, furrowing the sea like a missent cannon-ball become ploughshare. “By salt and hemp!” cried Stubb, “this swift deck creeps up one’s legs and tingles at the heart. Launch me spine-wise; my spine’s a keel. Ha! we go the gait that leaves no dust behind!” A cry: “There she blows—right ahead!”—then doubt—then denial from Ahab. Moments after, nearer than the first mistaken jet, Moby Dick burst bodily into view, breaching—his entire bulk booming into the air, piling a mountain of dazzling foam. “There she breaches! there she breaches!” The intolerable glitter faded to the dim mist of a coming shower.

“Aye, breach thy last to the sun, Moby Dick!” cried Ahab. “Down all but one man at the fore. The boats!—stand by!” Men slid like shooting stars by stays and halyards. “Lower away. Mr. Starbuck, the ship is thine—keep away from the boats, yet keep near them. Lower, all!”

Moby Dick turned, coming for the three crews. Ahab, central, cheered to take him head-and-head; but, while all three boats were yet plain as the ship’s three masts to his eye, the whale, with open jaws and lashing tail, offered appalling battle on every side; heedless of irons, intent on annihilating each plank. Skilfully manœuvred, the boats wheeled like trained chargers; yet, in his untraceable evolutions, the White Whale so crossed and recrossed that the three fast lines tangled and foreshortened, warping the boats toward the planted irons. Ahab paid out, then hauled to clear snarls—when up, corkscrewed in the mazes of line, came loose harpoons and lances, all their barbs flashing to his very chocks. He reached within, dragged the line beyond, twice sundered it, and dropped the intercepted fagot of steel to the sea—fast again. That instant the whale rushed among the other tangles; Stubb’s and Flask’s more-involved boats were irresistibly dragged together, dashed like rolling husks on a surf-beaten beach; then the whale, shooting perpendicularly, dashed his broad forehead against Ahab’s bottom, sent it turning over and over, till it fell gunwale down; the men struggled out like seals.

His first uprising involuntarily launched him a little away; feeling with his flukes, he smote at every chip that touched him; then, as if satisfied, pushed his pleated forehead on, trailing intertangled lines. The ship, having borne down to the rescue, dropped a boat, picked up mariners, tubs, oars, all floating furniture. Sprains and contusions; wrenched irons; intricacies of rope; but no fatal ill. As with Fedallah the day before, so Ahab now grimly clung to a broken half.

Helped to the deck, he yet half-hung on Starbuck’s shoulder. His ivory leg was snapped—one sharp splinter left. “’Tis sweet to lean sometimes,” said Ahab, “be the leaner who he will.” “The ferrule has not stood, sir,” quoth the carpenter. “But no bones broken, sir, I hope,” said Stubb. “Aye! and all splintered to pieces, Stubb!—d’ye see it. But even with a broken bone, old Ahab is untouched; nor can white whale, nor man, nor fiend, so much as graze old Ahab in his own proper and inaccessible being. Aloft there! which way?”—“Dead to leeward, sir.”—“Up helm, then; pile on sail again, ship-keepers! Down the rest of the spare boats and rig them—Mr. Starbuck, away, and muster the crews.”

A twinge: “Oh, how this splinter gores me now! Accursed fate!—that the unconquerable captain in the soul should have such a craven mate!” He took a shivered lance for a cane. Muster called: the Parsee was not there. “Caught among the tangles of your line,” Stubb muttered; “I thought I saw him dragging under.” “My line! my line? Gone?—gone? What means that little word?—What death-knell rings in it, that old Ahab shakes as if he were the belfry! The harpoon, too!—the forged iron—’tis in the fish!—Aloft there! Keep him nailed! Quick!—all hands!—I’ll ten times girdle the unmeasured globe; yea and dive straight through it, but I’ll slay him yet!”

“Great God! but for one single instant show thyself,” cried Starbuck. “Never, never wilt thou capture him, old man. In Jesus’ name no more of this! Two days chased; twice stove to splinters; thy very leg once more snatched; thy evil shadow gone; all good angels mobbing thee with warnings—what more wouldst thou have? Shall we be dragged by him to the bottom? towed to the infernal world? Oh, impiety and blasphemy to hunt him more!”

“Starbuck,” said Ahab, “of late I’ve felt strangely moved to thee; but in this matter be thy face to me a lipless blank. Ahab is for ever Ahab, man. This whole act’s immutably decreed. Fool! I am the Fates’ lieutenant; I act under orders. Stand round me, men. Ye see an old man cut to the stump; propped on a lonely foot. ’Tis Ahab—his body’s part; but Ahab’s soul’s a centipede, that moves upon a hundred legs. I feel strained; but ere I break, ye’ll hear me crack; and till ye hear that, know Ahab’s hawser tows his purpose yet. Believe ye in omens? Laugh aloud, and cry encore! For ere they drown, drowning things will twice rise; then rise again, to sink for evermore. So with Moby Dick—two days he’s floated—tomorrow will be the third. Aye, men, he’ll rise once more,—but only to spout his last! D’ye feel brave?” “As fearless fire,” cried Stubb. “And as mechanical,” muttered Ahab.

Dusk: the whale still to leeward; the grindstone hummed till near daylight; new boats rigged, fresh steel sharpened. From Ahab’s broken keel the carpenter made another leg; and Ahab, slouched within his scuttle, his hid, heliotrope glance set due eastward for the earliest sun, stood fixed as before.


## Chapter 3

Fair and fresh the third day dawned. Look-outs dotted every mast. “D’ye see him?”—“Not yet.”—“In his infallible wake, though; follow that wake,” said Ahab. He felt the day’s loveliness like a taunt. “Here’s food for thought, had Ahab time to think; but Ahab never thinks; he only feels—feels—feels. Thinking is God’s coolness; our poor hearts throb too much for that. And yet—the wind! Noble and heroic; who ever conquered it? These warm Trades blow straight on, in steadfast, vigorous mildness; and by the eternal Poles! these same Trades, or something like them, so unchangeable and strong, blow my keeled soul along!” He started. “Nothing! and noon at hand! I’ve oversailed him—got the start! About! about! Man the braces!”

Reversed, she sailed hard upon the breeze, rechurning her own white wake. Starbuck coiled the main-brace, bones damp within him. “God keep us,” he murmured, “lest I disobey my God in obeying him.” “Stand by to sway me up!” cried Ahab; and once more he swung on high. An hour—gold beaten to ages. At last, three points off the weather bow, a spout; three shrieks up from three mast-heads. “Forehead to forehead I meet thee, this third time, Moby Dick! Brace sharper up—crowd her into the wind’s eye. Too far yet to lower; but let me have one more round look. The same sea I first saw, a boy, from Nantucket sand-hills—the same to Noah as to me. Green moss on these mast cracks; none on Ahab’s head!—we both grow old, old mast, yet thou outlast’st me. ‘He should still go before me, my pilot; and yet be seen again’—where? Have I eyes at the bottom? Aye, Parsee, thou told’st direful truth of thyself; there thy shot fell short. Good-bye, mast-head—keep a good eye while I’m gone.” He gave the word and was lowered.

As he hovered in his stern, he waved to Starbuck to pause. “For the third time my soul’s ship starts upon this voyage, Starbuck.” “Aye, sir.” “Some ships sail and are missing ever after.” “Truth, sir: saddest truth.” “I feel like a billow all one crested comb. I am old;—shake hands with me, man.” Their hands met; eyes fastened; Starbuck’s tears the glue. “Oh, my captain, my captain!—noble heart—go not—go not!” “Lower away!” cried Ahab, tossing the mate’s arm; and the boat leaped on.

“The sharks! the sharks!” cried a voice; but Ahab’s own voice rode higher, and he heard not. Sharks snapped the blades at every dip, following like vultures the banners of eastern regiments. “Heart of wrought steel!” murmured Starbuck, gazing after—“lowering thy keel among ravening sharks; this the critical third day. For when three days flow as one continuous pursuit, the first the morning, the second noon, the third the evening—and the end.”

A downward pointed arm from aloft—Moby Dick had sounded. Ahab held on a little sideways from the ship, silent as the head-beat waves hammered the bow. “Drive in your nails, O ye waves! ye but strike a thing without a lid; no coffin and no hearse can be mine:—and hemp only can kill me! Ha! ha!” The waters slowly swelled in broad circles; upheaved; a subterraneous hum; then, bedraggled with ropes and irons, a vast form shot obliquely from the sea, hovered in a thin veil of mist rainbowed in the air, then fell swamping back; the circling surface creamed like new milk round the marble trunk.

“Give way!” The boats darted. Maddened by fresh irons corroding in him, Moby Dick came head-on, flailing the mates’ boats apart, leaving Ahab’s almost without a scar. As Daggoo and Queequeg stopped strained planks, the whale turned and showed one whole flank. A cry: lashed round and round upon his back, pinioned in the involutions wound during the night, the half-torn body of the Parsee stared—distended eyes full on Ahab. The harpoon dropped from his hand. “Befooled!—Aye, Parsee! I see thee again. And thou goest before; and this—this then is the hearse thou didst promise. But the last letter—where the second hearse? Away, mates, to the ship! those boats are useless; repair them if ye can and return; if not, Ahab is enough to die—Down, men! The first thing that but offers to jump from this boat I stand in, that thing I harpoon. Ye are not other men, but my arms and my legs. Where’s the whale? gone down again?”

Not seeking them, as Starbuck cried—“Oh! Ahab, not too late even now to desist. See! Moby Dick seeks thee not. It is thou that madly seekest him!”—the whale swam steadily forward, almost past the ship, intent on his own straight path. Ahab, sliding by the vessel, hailed to have her follow not too swiftly, at a judicious interval. He saw Tashtego, Queequeg, Daggoo run to the mast-heads; men hammering in the staved boats; Stubb and Flask bundling new irons. As he saw and heard those hammers, far other hammers seemed driving a nail into his heart. He rallied; noted the vane gone from the main; shouted Tashtego down for another flag and hammer, to nail it fast.

Whether fagged by three days’ chase and the hamper he bore, or moved by latent malice, the White Whale’s way abated as Ahab once more neared him; but the sharks pertinaciously stuck to the boat, biting till the oar-blades grew jagged. “Heed them not! those teeth but give new rowlocks to your oars. Pull on! ’tis the better rest, the shark’s jaw than the yielding water.” “At every bite, sir, the blades grow smaller!” “They will last long enough!—all alive! we near him. The helm!—let me pass.” Helped to the bows, Ahab, within the smoky mountain mist of the whale’s spout curled round his Monadnock hump, arched back, arms high to the poise, darted his fierce iron—and his far fiercer curse. As steel and curse sank, Moby Dick writhed sideways, rolled his nigh flank against the bow, suddenly canted the boat; three oarsmen flew; two clutched and hurled themselves inboard again; the third dropped astern, still afloat. Ahab shouted to take new turns with the line, and tow up to the mark; the double strain snapped it in the empty air. “What breaks in me? Some sinew cracks!—’tis whole again. Oars! Burst in upon him!”

Hearing the sea-crashing rush, the whale wheeled to present his blank forehead at bay; but catching sight of the nearing black hull—seemingly its larger and nobler foe—he bore down upon the advancing prow, jaws smiting amid fiery foam. “I grow blind,” gasped Ahab. “Hands!—is’t night?—The whale! the ship!” The crew forced the boat through sledge-hammering seas; but two bow-planks, whale-smitten before, burst in; the boat lay almost level with the waves; men strove to stop the gap and bale.

For one beholding instant, Tashtego’s hammer hung suspended at the main-truck; the red flag half-wrapping him streamed straight, as his own forward-flowing heart; Starbuck and Stubb, on the bowsprit beneath, caught sight of the down-coming monster. “The whale, the whale! Up helm! Oh, all ye sweet powers of air, now hug me close!” cried Starbuck. “Stand not by me, but stand under me, whoever will help Stubb; for Stubb, too, sticks here. I grin at thee, thou grinning whale!” bawled Stubb; Flask groaned for his mother’s part-pay.

Retribution, swift vengeance, eternal malice were in the whale’s whole aspect; his solid white buttress smote the starboard bow; timbers reeled; waters poured through like torrents down a flume. “The ship! The hearse!—the second hearse!” cried Ahab from the boat; “its wood could only be American!”

The whale dived beneath the settling ship, quivered along her keel, shot to the surface far off the other bow, near Ahab’s boat, and lay a moment quiescent. Ahab turned from the sun. “Oh! ye three unsurrendered spires; thou uncracked keel; firm deck, haughty helm, Pole-pointed prow—death-glorious ship! must ye perish, and without me? Oh, lonely death on lonely life! Now I feel my topmost greatness lies in my topmost grief. Pour in, bold billows of my foregone life, and top this piled comber of my death! Towards thee I roll, thou all-destroying but unconquering whale; to the last I grapple with thee; from hell’s heart I stab at thee; for hate’s sake I spit my last breath at thee. Sink all coffins and all hearses to one common pool! and since neither can be mine, let me then tow to pieces, while still chasing thee, though tied to thee, thou damned whale! Thus, I give up the spear!”

He darted. The line ran with igniting velocity;—ran foul. Ahab stooped; cleared it; but the flying turn caught him round the neck, and, voiceless as Turkish mutes bowstring their victim, shot him from the boat. The eye-splice at the rope’s end flew out, smote the sea, and vanished.

The tranced crew turned. “The ship?—where?” Through dim, bewildering mediums they saw her sidelong fading phantom; only the upper masts out of water; the pagan harpooneers still, fixed by fate, on their sinking look-outs. Concentric circles seized boat and men and every lone chip; spinning, all went in one vortex out of sight.

As the last whelmings poured over the sunken head at the mainmast, a few inches of the spar yet showed; long streaming yards of the flag undulated with ironical coincidings over the destroying billows; and, at that instant, a red arm and a hammer hovered uplifted, nailing the flag faster to the subsiding spar. A sky-hawk, that had tauntingly followed the truck, pecking and incommoding Tashtego, chanced to intercept its broad wing between hammer and wood; feeling the aetherial thrill, the submerged savage, in his death-gasp, kept his hammer frozen; and the bird of heaven, with archangelic shrieks, imperial beak thrust upward, folded in Ahab’s flag, went down with his ship, which, like Satan, would not sink till she had dragged a living part of heaven, and helmeted herself with it. Small fowls screamed over the yet yawning gulf; a sullen white surf beat its sides; then all collapsed, and the great shroud of the sea rolled on as it rolled five thousand years ago.


## Chapter 4

“AND I ONLY AM ESCAPED ALONE TO TELL THEE” Job.

The drama’s done. Why then here does any one step forth?—Because one did survive the wreck.

After the Parsee’s disappearance, I was he whom Fate ordained to take the bowsman’s place; and I it was who, when on the last day the three were tossed from the rocking boat, was dropped astern. Floating on the margin of the ensuing scene, and in full sight, when the half-spent suction of the sunk ship reached me, I was slowly drawn toward the closing vortex. At last the creamy pool whirled me to its button-black centre; the bubble burst; and, liberated by its cunning spring and buoyant by nature, the coffin life-buoy shot lengthwise from the sea, fell over, and floated by my side. Buoyed by that coffin for almost a day and night, I drifted on a soft, dirge-like main. The sharks glided by with padlocks on their mouths; the sea-hawks sailed with sheathed beaks. On the second day a sail drew near, nearer, and picked me up at last—the devious-cruising Rachel; retracing in search of her missing children, she only found another orphan.
