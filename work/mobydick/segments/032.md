Some days elapsed, ice and icebergs all astern, the Pequod went rolling through the bright Quito spring, which, at sea, almost perpetually reigns on the threshold of the eternal August of the Tropic. The clear, ringing days were as crystal goblets of Persian sherbet, heaped up with rose-water snow; the starred and stately nights seemed haughty dames in jewelled velvets, nursing in lonely pride the memory of their absent conquering Earls. And all these subtle agencies wrought on Ahab's texture.

Old age is always wakeful; as if, the longer linked with life, the less man has to do with aught that looks like death. So with Ahab: of late he seemed to live in the open air. "It feels like going down into one's tomb," he would mutter, "for an old captain like me to be descending this narrow scuttle, to my grave-dug berth."

Almost every twenty-four hours the silent steersman would watch the cabin-scuttle, and ere long the old man would emerge, gripping the iron banister to help his crippled way. He usually abstained from patrolling the quarter-deck, lest the crack of his bony step disturb his mates' dreams. But once the mood was on him too deep for common regardings; and as he measured the ship with heavy, lumber-like pace, Stubb came up and hinted that if the Captain pleased to walk the planks, no one could say nay; but there might be some way of muffling the noise — something about a globe of tow, and the insertion of the ivory heel.

"Am I a cannon-ball, Stubb," said Ahab, "that thou wouldst wad me that fashion? But go thy ways; I had forgot. Below to thy nightly grave; where such as ye sleep between shrouds, to use ye to the filling one at last. — Down, dog, and kennel!"

Stubb was speechless a moment; then said excitedly, "I am not used to be spoken to that way, sir; I do but less than half like it."

"Avast!" gritted Ahab, moving violently away as if to avoid some passionate temptation.

"No, sir; not yet," said Stubb, emboldened, "I will not tamely be called a dog, sir."

"Then be called ten times a donkey, and a mule, and an ass, and begone, or I'll clear the world of thee!"

As he said this, Ahab advanced upon him with such overbearing terrors in his aspect that Stubb involuntarily retreated.

"I was never served so before without giving a hard blow for it," muttered Stubb, descending the cabin-scuttle. "Somehow, I don't well know whether to go back and strike him, or — down here on my knees and pray for him? It would be the first time I ever _did_ pray. He's the queerest old man Stubb ever sailed with. How he flashed at me! — his eyes like powder-pans! There's something on his mind. He aint in his bed three hours out of the twenty-four; the steward says his pillow's always frightful hot, as though a baked brick had been on it. A hot old man! I guess he's got what some folks ashore call a conscience; a kind of Tic-Dolly-row they say — worse nor a toothache. What does he go into the after hold for, every night? Who's made appointments with him there? Think not, is my eleventh commandment; and sleep when you can, is my twelfth. But he called me ten times a donkey, and piled a lot of jackasses on top of _that!_ That old man has turned me wrong side out. The only way's to stash it; in the morning, I'll see how this plaguey juggling thinks over by daylight."
