*The Lee Shore.*

When the Pequod thrust her vindictive bows into the cold malicious waves, who should I see at her helm but that tall, newlanded mariner I had encountered in New Bedford. The land seemed scorching to his feet—mid-winter just landed from a four years' dangerous voyage, and pushing off again for still another tempestuous term.

It fared with him as with the storm-tossed ship that drives along the leeward land. The port would fain give succor; in the port is safety, comfort, hearthstone, friends. But in that gale, the port is that ship's direst jeopardy; she must fly all hospitality; one touch of land would make her shudder through and through. With all her might she crowds all sail off shore; for refuge's sake forlornly rushing into peril; her only friend her bitterest foe.

Glimpses do ye seem to see of that mortally intolerable truth; that all deep, earnest thinking is but the intrepid effort of the soul to keep the open independence of her sea, while the wildest winds of heaven and earth conspire to cast her on the treacherous, slavish shore?

But as in landlessness alone resides highest truth, shoreless, indefinite as God—so, better is it to perish in that howling infinite, than be ingloriously dashed upon the lee. Bear thee grimly, demigod! Up from the spray of thy ocean-perishing—straight up, leaps thy apotheosis!
