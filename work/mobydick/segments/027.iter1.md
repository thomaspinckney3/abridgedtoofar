We are now fairly embarked in whaling; and since landsmen count it unpoetical, butcherly, unclean and disreputable, I am all anxiety to right us.

Butchers we are—true; but butchers also—of the bloodiest badge—are all Martial Commanders whom the world delights to honor. As for uncleanliness, ye shall be initiated ere long into certain facts; but even granting the charge—what are slippery decks to the carrion of the battle-field, from which so many soldiers return to drink in all ladies’ plaudits? And if peril ennobles the soldier, let me assure ye many a veteran who has marched up to a battery would quickly recoil at the apparition of the sperm whale’s vast tail fanning into eddies the air over his head. For what are the comprehensible terrors of man to the interlinked terrors and wonders of God!

Though the world scouts us, yet unwittingly it pays us profound homage: almost all the tapers, lamps, and candles that burn round the globe, burn—as before so many shrines—to our glory!

Look in other lights. Why admirals of whaling fleets in De Witt’s day? Why Louis XVI fitting out ships from Dunkirk, inviting Nantucket families? Why Britain paying bounties by the million? How comes it that we Americans outnumber all others; seven hundred vessels; eighteen thousand men; millions of dollars afloat—if there be not something puissant in whaling?

But this is not the half. I freely assert no single peaceful influence in threescore years has more operated upon the whole broad world than the high and mighty business of whaling; one way and another, it has begotten events so continuously momentous, that whaling may well be regarded as that Egyptian mother, who bore offspring themselves pregnant from her womb. Let a handful suffice. For many years past the whale-ship has been the pioneer to remotest seas; she explored archipelagoes where no Cook or Vancouver sailed; first interpreted between men-of-war and savages; showed them the way to once savage harbors. Your Cooks and Krusensterns are trumpeted; I say anonymous captains out of Nantucket were as great, and greater. For in their succourless empty-handedness, they, in the heathenish sharked waters, and by the beaches of unrecorded, javelin islands, battled with virgin wonders and terrors. What is made such a flourish of in the old South Sea Voyages was but the lifetime commonplaces of our heroic Nantucketers; adventures Vancouver gives three chapters to, these men accounted unworthy of the ship’s common log. Ah, the world! Oh, the world! Until the fishery rounded Cape Horn, scarce any but colonial commerce touched the opulent Spanish Pacific; the whaleman broke the jealous policy of the crown, and helped eventuate the liberation of Peru, Chili, and Bolivia. That great America on the other side, Australia—blunder-born by a Dutchman—other ships shunned; the whale-ship touched there, mothering that mighty colony, yea, once feeding its infancy with her benevolent biscuit. Polynesia confesses the same. And if that double-bolted land, Japan, is ever to become hospitable, it is the whale-ship alone to whom the credit will be due; for already she is on the threshold.

But if, in the face of all this, you still declare that whaling has no æsthetically noble associations connected with it, then am I ready to shiver fifty lances with you there, and unhorse you with a split helmet every time. “No æsthetic dignity,” you say? Who first wrote of Leviathan but mighty Job? Who first chronicled a whaling voyage but Alfred the Great, taking down the words of Other the Norwegian? Who pronounced our eulogy in Parliament but Edmund Burke?

“No good blood in our veins?” Better than royal there. Benjamin Franklin’s grandmother was Mary Folger of Nantucket—kith and kin to harpooneers—noble Benjamin’s own stock darting the barbed iron round the world.

“Not respectable?” Whaling is imperial. By old English law the whale is “a royal fish.” “Only nominal?” In a Roman triumph, the bones of a whale, brought from the Syrian coast, were the most conspicuous object in the cymballed procession. “No dignity?” The heavens attest it: Cetus is a constellation in the South! No more! Drive down your hat in presence of the Czar, and take it off to Queequeg!

And, as for me, if, by any possibility, there be any as yet undiscovered prime thing in me; if I shall ever deserve any real repute in that small but high hushed world which I might not be unreasonably ambitious of; if hereafter I shall do anything that, upon the whole, a man might rather have done than to have left undone; if, at my death, my executors, or more properly my creditors, find any precious MSS. in my desk, then here I prospectively ascribe all the honor and the glory to whaling; for a whale-ship was my Yale College and my Harvard.
