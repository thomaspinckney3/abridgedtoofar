## Chapter 1

All this while the prodigious head of a Sperm Whale hung to starboard; we must let it hang, praying the tackles hold, for other matters press. Drifting into yellow brit water—token of Right Whales—Stubb’s and Flask’s boats gave chase to tall spouts to leeward. Fast to a whale and towed perilously near the hull—“Cut, cut!”—they paid out line, pulled ahead, and, winning but a few feet, felt the line rasp lightning-like under the keel and leap quivering to the bows, the drops flinging off like bits of broken glass. The fagged whale altered and went round the stern, towing both boats in a full circuit, while sharks quit the Sperm head for the new blood and drank like Israelites at the smitten rock. Thickening spout; a frightful roll; he turned upon his back a corpse.

Making fast to the flukes and preparing to tow, the headsmen talked. Stubb, disgusted with “so ignoble a leviathan,” wondered what Ahab wanted with the lump. Flask told the charm: a ship with a Sperm head starboard and a Right head larboard can never capsize. “I don’t know why,” he added, “but Fedallah says so; and I don’t half like that chap.” “Sink him! I never look at him,” cried Stubb; “Flask, I take that Fedallah to be the devil in disguise. He tucks up his tail, coiled away in his pocket, I guess; he’s always wanting oakum for the toes of his boots.” They bandied sailor’s theology—duck the devil, and if he ducks you, give him two black eyes; the governor won’t clap him in double-darbies, having signed a bond to roast the kidnappees he brings. “Do you suppose Fedallah wants to kidnap Captain Ahab?” “You’ll know it before long, Flask. I’ll keep a sharp look-out; if he tries it, I’ll grab his tail, take it to the capstan, and heave it short off at the stump.”

With the boats returning, they were hailed to tow the Right Whale on the larboard side where fluke-chains waited. “Didn’t I tell you so?” cried Flask. In good time it proved true; the Pequod, steeply leaned over by the first head, now, by the counterpoise of both, regained an even keel—sorely strained, though. “So, when on one side you hoist in Locke’s head, you go over that way; but now, on the other side, hoist in Kant’s and you come back again; but in very poor plight. Thus, some minds for ever keep trimming boat. Oh, ye foolish! throw all these thunder-heads overboard, and then you will float light and right.”

Unlike the customary Right Whale handling, neither lips, tongue, nor bone-blinds were yet removed; both carcases had dropped astern, and the head-laden ship looked a mule with overburdening panniers. Fedallah, calmly eyeing the Right Whale’s head, glanced from its deep wrinkles to the lines in his own hand; and Ahab so chanced to stand that the Parsee occupied his shadow; while, if the Parsee’s shadow was there at all it seemed only to blend with, and lengthen Ahab’s. Laplandish speculations flew among the toilers at these passing things.


## Chapter 2

“Here, now, are two great whales, laying their heads together; let us join them, and lay together our own.” The Sperm and the Right—folio leviathans, the only whales regularly hunted—present, in their heads, extremes. The Sperm’s shows a certain mathematical symmetry and pervading dignity—grey-headed at the summit, tokening age and experience.

Note first what is least dissimilar: eye and ear. Far back and low near the jaw’s angle, a lashless eye, colt-small for such magnitude. From its sideway set, the whale can never see object exactly ahead or astern; he has two side fronts and two backs; with many cubic feet of head like a mountain between, each eye must impart a wholly separate picture—profound darkness and nothingness all between. Bear this in mind in the fishery—and in scenes to come. A puzzling question lingers: though both eyes act, can his brain attentively examine two distinct prospects at the same instant, as if a man solved two Euclid problems at once?

The ear is as curious. No external leaf; a minute hole a quill can hardly enter, lodged a little behind the eye. In the Sperm an external opening; in the Right, evenly covered by a membrane—imperceptible without. Is it not curious, that so vast a being should see the world through so small an eye, and hear the thunder through an ear smaller than a hare’s? But if his eyes were broad as Herschel’s lens, his ears cathedral-porched, would that make him longer of sight, sharper of hearing? Not at all.—Why then do you try to “enlarge” your mind? Subtilize it.

Cant over the Sperm’s head and peer down the mouth—its glistening membrane glossy as bridal satin. Then behold the portentous lower jaw, lid of an immense snuff-box; pried overhead, its teeth make a terrific portcullis; fathoms down, a dispirited whale will hang the jaw straight like a jib-boom. Often this jaw is unhinged and hoisted for teeth and hard white whalebone, from which fishermen fashion canes, umbrella-stocks, riding-whip handles. With weary hoist it is dragged aboard; and, after days, Queequeg, Daggoo, and Tashtego—accomplished dentists—set to drawing teeth: Queequeg lances the gums; the jaw is lashed to ringbolts; a tackle rigged aloft drags them out as Michigan oxen stump old oaks. In old whales forty-two teeth, much worn, not decayed; the jaw sawn to slabs and piled away like house-joists.


## Chapter 3

Cross the deck to the Right Whale’s head. If the Sperm’s front is a rounded Roman chariot, the Right’s bears an inelegant resemblance to a gigantic galliot-toed shoe; old Dutch voyagers likened it to a shoemaker’s last—ample enough to lodge that nursery old woman and all her brood. According to your point of view, the head shifts aspect: stand atop; the two F-shaped spoutholes make it an enormous bass-viol; fix on the green barnacled “crown” or “bonnet,” with live crabs nesting, and it is a diademed king of the sea—though a sulky one. Look at that hanging lower lip—what a huge sulk and pout! twenty feet long, five deep; a sulk to yield some five hundred gallons of oil.

Hare-lipped, we slide over the slippery threshold into the mouth—“Good Lord! is this the road that Jonah went?” Twelve feet high at the roof, a sharp ridge-pole; ribbed, arched, hairy sides present those wondrous, half-vertical, scimetar-shaped slats—say three hundred on a side—depending from the crown-bone: Venetian blinds whose hairy fringes strain the brit-seas. Marks and hollows in the central blinds serve some to count age, as oaks by rings; as likely as any analogy. Old travelers called them whiskers, hogs’ bristles, fins; and, indeed, the ladies’ busks and umbrellas draw their stiffening from the same bone—glories of Queen Anne’s farthingale now on the decline.

Forget blinds and whiskers; stand within among colonnades of bone and fancy the great Haarlem organ; for carpet, a soft Turkey rug—the tongue—fat and tender, apt to tear in hoisting, a six-barreler by the look. To sum up: no great well of sperm here; no ivory teeth; no long slender mandible; while in the Sperm there are none of these blinds; no huge lower lip; and scarcely any tongue. Two external spout-holes to the Right; one only to the Sperm. Look your last while they lie together: one will soon sink unrecorded. The Sperm’s broad brow wears prairie-like placidity, speculative indifference as to death; the Right’s amazing lip, pressed to the vessel’s side, speaks of enormous practical resolution in facing death. This Right Whale I take for a Stoic; the Sperm, a Platonian, who might have taken up Spinoza in his latter years.


## Chapter 4

Regard now, as a sensible physiologist, the Sperm Whale’s front in its compacted collectedness; form an unexaggerated estimate of whatever battering-ram power is lodged there. In ordinary swimming the front presents an almost wholly vertical plane; mouth entirely under the head; no external nose—the spout-hole on top; eyes and ears set far back at the sides. The whole front is a dead, blind wall, without organ or tender prominence. Bone does not begin till near twenty feet from the forehead; all before is an enormous boneless wad. About the head the blubber-envelope, though not so thick, is of a boneless toughness; the severest harpoon, the sharpest lance, impotently rebounds from it—as though the forehead were paved with horses’ hoofs.

Think of sailors fending two crowding Indiamen: not iron or wood, but a great wad of tow and cork in ox-hide bravely takes the jam. Supplement this by a conceit: the Sperm Whale has no swimming-bladder; yet now depresses his head wholly, anon elevates it high. Considering the honeycombed interior, may those mystical lung-celled honeycombs be susceptible to atmospheric distension and contraction? If so, fancy the irresistibleness of a dead, impregnable wall impelled by buoyant might within, and behind all a corded mass of tremendous life obedient to one volition.

When I shall detail his braining feats, be ready to abide by this: though the Sperm Whale stove a passage through the Isthmus of Darien, and mixed the Atlantic with the Pacific, you would not elevate one hair of your eyebrow. But clear Truth is for salamander giants only to encounter.


## Chapter 5

Now comes the Baling of the Case. Regard the Sperm’s head as a solid oblong; sideways divide into two quoins: the lower, bone—cranium and jaws; the upper, an unctuous mass, wholly free of bone, whose broad forward end forms the apparent vertical forehead. Horizontally subdivide that upper quoin at mid-forehead: the lower subpart is the junk—an immense honeycomb of oil, ten thousand infiltrated cells crossing and recrossing with tough elastic white fibres; the upper subpart is the Case—“the great Heidelburgh Tun of the Sperm Whale.” As that famous tierce is mystically carved, so the whale’s vast plaited forehead adorns his wondrous tun; and as Heidelburgh brimmed with Rhenish wines, so this tun holds the most precious of his oily vintages—spermaceti in absolutely pure, limpid, odoriferous state. In life perfectly fluid, after death it concretes, shooting delicate crystals like first ice. A large Case yields about five hundred gallons; much is spilled and lost in the ticklish business of securing what you can.

Within, a silken pearl-coloured membrane lines it—richer than any tun’s coating. The Case embraces the entire length of the head’s top; if a whale be eighty feet, then more than twenty-six feet is the tun’s depth when hoisted lengthwise against a ship’s side. In decapitating, the cutting-tool works close to where entrance must afterwards be forced into the magazine; heed is needful, lest a careless stroke invade the sanctuary and wastingly let out the treasure. It is this decapitated end that is at last elevated out of water and retained there by enormous cutting tackles—a wilderness of hempen combinations. Attend now to that marvellous—and here almost fatal—operation whereby the great Heidelburgh Tun is tapped.


## Chapter 6

Nimble as a cat, Tashtego ran out upon the overhanging mainyard-arm above the hoisted Tun, rigged a light whip-block, swung an end down to deck, and hand-over-hand dropped to the summit of the head. Spade in hand, like a treasure-hunter sounding for masoned gold, he sought the place to break in. A stout, iron-bound bucket was bent to the whip; with a very long pole he guided it down into the Case. Up it came, bubbling like a dairy-maid’s pail of new milk; lowered, emptied to a great tub; remounted; and so round and round till the deep cistern would yield no more—twenty feet of pole going in at the end.

Then, all at once, a queer accident. Whether Tashtego in heedlessness loosed his one-handed hold on the great cabled tackles; or the footing was oozy; or the Evil One so willed; as the eightieth or ninetieth bucket came suckingly up—my God! poor Tashtego—like a twin reciprocating bucket dropped head-foremost down into the great Tun, and, with a horrible oily gurgling, went clean out of sight! “Man overboard!” cried Daggoo, first to his senses. Foot in the bucket, he shot aloft to the head. The before lifeless head now heaved below the surface, throbbing as if seized with a momentous idea; it was only the poor Indian’s struggles revealing the perilous depth. A sharp crack—the unspeakable horror: one of the two enormous hooks tore out; the mass swung; the ship reeled like smitten by an iceberg; the one remaining hook strained to part; the head’s violent motions worsened all.

“Come down!” they yelled to Daggoo; but clinging to the heavy tackles, having cleared the foul, he rammed down the bucket to the collapsed well that Tash might grasp it. “Avast! How will that help him?” cried Stubb. Then a voice like a rocket: “Stand clear of the tackle!” Thunder-boom—the enormous mass dropped like Niagara’s Table-Rock; the suddenly relieved hull rolled far to her copper; Daggoo swung through spray; and Tashtego sank utterly. A naked figure with a boarding-sword flashed over the bulwarks—Queequeg dived.

Moments, ripples, no sign. A boat shoved off. “Ha! ha!” cried Daggoo from aloft; an arm thrust upright from the blue, as from grass over a grave. “Both! both!” Soon Queequeg struck out with one hand, the other clutching the Indian’s long hair. Drawn in, Tashtego was long in coming to; Queequeg looked not brisk. The rescue? Diving after the descending head, Queequeg side-lunged with his keen sword near its bottom, scuttling a large hole; thrusting his long arm inwards and upwards, he hauled out Tash by the head. He averred a leg first offered; knowing that was not as it ought to be, and might occasion trouble, he thrust it back, and by a dexterous toss wrought a somerset, so the next trial came forth the good old way—head foremost. The head itself did as well as could be expected.

Thus, through the courage and great skill in obstetrics of Queequeg, the delivery of Tashtego was accomplished—“a running delivery,” as you may say. Midwifery should be taught with fencing and boxing, riding and rowing. Had Tash perished in that head, ’twere a precious perishing—smothered in the whitest and daintiest spermaceti; coffined and hearsed in the sanctum sanctorum of the whale. Only one sweeter end readily recurs: the Ohio honey-hunter sucked into such exceeding store that he died embalmed. How many, think ye, have likewise fallen into Plato’s honey head, and sweetly perished there?


## Chapter 7

Physiognomy and phrenology have not scanned this Leviathan’s lines; yet, trying all, I achieve what I can. The Sperm Whale has no proper nose; and since the nose is central and most modifies a face, its absence should mar the countenance. But magnitude redeems defect; a nose to the whale were impertinent—no pestilent conceit of a thing to be pulled intrudes upon your noble conceptions as you sail round his vast head.

Perhaps the most imposing view is the full front. In man a fine brow is like the East troubled with morning; in bull or elephant, grand; in decrees, the golden imperial seal. But most are mere alpine strips along the snow-line. In the great Sperm Whale this god-like dignity of brow is so immensely amplified that, gazing full-front, you feel the Deity and dread powers more forcibly than in any living object. You see no one point precisely; no nose, eyes, ears, or mouth; “nothing but that one broad firmament of a forehead, pleated with riddles; dumbly lowering with the doom of boats, and ships, and men.” In profile, the grandeur softens but does not diminish; there you plainly perceive the semi-crescentic depression—Lavater’s mark of genius.

Genius? He has written no book, spoken no speech. It is declared in his doing nothing particular to prove it; declared in his pyramidical silence. Had the young Orient deified the tongueless crocodile, much more had it enthroned this tongueless lord. If ever a poetical nation lures back May-day gods to an unhaunted hill and egotistical sky, be sure, exalted to Jove’s high seat, the great Sperm Whale shall lord it. I but put that brow before you. Read it if you can.


## Chapter 8

If physiognomically the Sperm Whale be a Sphinx, to the phrenologist his brain is that circle impossible to square. Unhinge the jaw; the skull’s side shows an inclined plane; in life it is almost squared by the enormous superincumbent junk and sperm. At the high end the skull forms a crater to bed part of that mass; beneath its long floor—“in another cavity seldom exceeding ten inches”—reposes the mere handful of brain, twenty feet from the apparent forehead, hidden behind vast outworks like a citadel within Quebec’s fortifications. Many a whaleman denies any other brain than the palpable semblance—cubic yards of sperm. Phrenologically, then, the living head is an entire delusion; the whale, like all mighty things, wears a false brow to the common world.

Unload the sperm and take a rear view of the high end and you will be struck with its resemblance to a human skull reversed; scaled to man’s size, you might confound it on a plate; remarking summit-depressions, you’d say—no self-esteem, no veneration—and, by those negations with his bulk and power, conceive the truest, if not the most exhilarating, notion of exalted potency.

Consider the spine. Vertebræ are like a strung necklace of dwarfed skulls; Germans call them undeveloped skulls. Much of a man’s character, I think, is betokened in his backbone. “I would rather feel your spine than your skull, whoever you are.” Apply it to the whale: the cranial cavity is continuous with the first vertebra; the spinal canal at first measures like the brain, and for many feet remains of nearly equal girth; filled with the same strangely fibrous substance, directly communicating with the brain. Thus, the comparative smallness of brain proper is compensated by the magnitude of spinal cord. Assume, then, a spinal phrenology: the august hump rises over one of the larger vertebræ, its outer convex mould. From that relative site, call it the organ of firmness or indomitableness. And that he is indomitable, you will yet have reason to know.
