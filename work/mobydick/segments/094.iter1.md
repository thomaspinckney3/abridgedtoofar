“In vain it was to rake for Ambergriese in the paunch of this Leviathan, insufferable fetor denying not inquiry.”—Sir T. Browne. A week or two after the last lowering, as we sailed over a sleepy, vapory, mid-day sea, the many noses on the Pequod’s deck proved more vigilant than the three pairs of eyes aloft. A peculiar, not very pleasant smell was smelt. “Some of those drugged whales we tickled the other day,” said Stubb; “I thought they would keel up.”

The vapors slid aside; a ship lay becalmed; and by the eddying vulture sea-fowl, it was plain the whale alongside was blasted—died and floated unappropriated. It may well be conceived what an unsavory odor such a mass must exhale; worse than an Assyrian city in the plague, when the living are incompetent to bury the departed. So intolerable indeed is it regarded by some, that no cupidity could persuade them to moor alongside of it. Coming nearer, the Frenchman had a second fish—one of those dyspeptic whales that die prodigiously indigested; almost bankrupt of oil, yet not to be turned up by a knowing fisherman.

Stubb vowed he recognised his cutting spade-pole entangled round one whale’s tail. “There’s a Crappo content with our leavings—the drugged whale—and scraping dry bones of the other. Poor devil! Pass a hat; make him a present of a little oil. For what oil he’ll get from that drugged whale there, wouldn’t be fit to burn in a jail; no, not in a condemned cell. And as for the other whale, why, I’ll agree to get more oil by chopping up and trying out these three masts of ours, than he’ll get from that bundle of bones. Though—” and he sniffed—“maybe there’s ambergris. I wonder if our old man has thought of that. Yes, I’m for it.”

Stubb called his crew and pulled for the stranger. Her stem was carved and painted like a green thorned stalk, the whole terminating in a symmetrical folded bulb of a bright red colour. Upon her head boards, in large gilt letters, he read “Bouton de Rose,”—Rose-button, or Rose-bud; and this was the romantic name of this aromatic ship. “A wooden rose-bud, eh?” cried Stubb, hand to nose; “how like all creation it smells!”

He must pull to starboard, close to the blasted whale, to hail. “Bouton-de-Rose, ahoy! any of you that speak English?” “Yes,” rejoined a Guernsey-man—the chief-mate. “Have ye seen the White Whale—Moby Dick?” “Never heard of such a whale. Cachalot Blanche?—no.” Stubb trumpeted back to Ahab—“No, Sir! No!”—and returned.

The Guernsey-man, in the chains with a cutting-spade, had slung his nose in a bag. “Broke it?” said Stubb. “I wish it was broken, or I had none! What are you holding yours for?” “It’s a wax nose; I have to hold it on. Air rather gardenny; throw us a bunch of posies, Bouton-de-Rose.” The sailors in tasselled red caps worked slow, talked fast, with noses like jib-booms; some ran aloft for air; others puffed short-stemmed pipes or held oakum dipped in coal-tar to their nostrils. From the round-house a fiery face thrust out—the tormented surgeon, yelling remonstrances, self-immured to avoid the pest.

The Guernsey-man detested his captain, a delicate, moustached gentleman in red velvet vest with watch-seals; a Cologne manufacturer on his first voyage; a conceited ignoramus, said the mate. Sounding him, Stubb perceived no suspicion of ambergris; so they concocted a plan—Guernsey, under cover of interpreter, to tell the Captain what he pleased as from Stubb; Stubb to utter any nonsense uppermost.

Introduced, Stubb eyed the vest and seals. “Tell him he looks babyish to me.” The Guernsey-man, in French, said instead that only yesterday a ship’s captain, chief-mate, and six sailors had died of a fever from a blasted whale. The Captain started, eager. “Now,” whispered the mate. “Tell him,” said Stubb, “he’s no more fit to command a whale-ship than a St. Jago monkey. In fact, tell him from me he’s a baboon.” The interpreter said: “The other whale, the dried one, is far more deadly; as we value our lives, cut loose.” Instantly the Captain ran forward and ordered his crew to cast loose all cables and chains.

“What now?”—“Tell him I’ve diddled him.” The interpreter: “He is happy to have been of any service.” The Captain, grateful, invited Stubb below to a bottle of Bordeaux. “Thank him; tell him it’s against my principles to drink with the man I’ve diddled. If Monsieur would live another day to drink, best drop all four boats and pull the ship away; it’s so calm they won’t drift.” Stubb over the side again, hailed that he had a long tow-line and would help by towing the lighter whale off. While the French boats towed their ship one way, Stubb benevolently towed the whale the other, slacking an unusually long line. A breeze sprang; he feigned to cast off; the Frenchman increased his distance; the Pequod slid between him and Stubb’s whale. Stubb pulled to the floating body, hailed his ship, and set to reaping the fruit of his cunning.

With sharp boat-spade he excavated behind the side fin, as if digging a sea-cellar; ribs rang like Roman tiles in fat loam; the crew in high excitement, as gold-hunters; fowls diving and screaming. The horrible nosegay increased—when suddenly, from the plague’s heart, there stole a faint perfume, flowing through the bad smells without blending, as one river within another. “I have it, I have it,” cried Stubb, striking—“a purse! a purse!” He thrust both hands and drew out handfuls like ripe Windsor soap, or rich mottled old cheese; unctuous; easily dented; a hue between yellow and ash. Ambergris—worth a guinea an ounce. Some six handfuls were obtained; more lost to sea—when impatient Ahab’s loud command bade Stubb desist and come on board, else the ship would bid them good-bye.
