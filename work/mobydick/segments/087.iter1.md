To make the boat slide bravely, some whalers grease their bottoms; Queequeg believed in such anointings, and one morning crawled under the hanging craft, rubbing in the unctuousness as if to raise a crop of hair from her bald keel. His presentiment was warranted by the event.

Towards noon whales were raised; but as the ship sailed down, they fled with the disordered flight of Cleopatra’s barges. Nevertheless, the boats pursued, and Stubb’s was foremost. Tashtego planted one iron; but the stricken whale, without sounding, ran the faster; the lance must reach him or he be lost—yet to haul up to his flank was impossible.

Of all the wondrous devices and dexterities, the sleights of hand and countless subtleties, to which the veteran whaleman is so often forced, none exceed that fine manœuvre with the lance called pitchpoling. Its grand fact and feature is the wonderful distance to which the long lance is accurately darted from a violently rocking bow; steel and wood included, the spear is some ten or twelve feet in length, slighter than the harpoon, of light pine, and furnished with a warp by which it is hauled back. Look now at Stubb; a man who from his humorous, deliberate coolness and equanimity in the direst emergencies, was specially qualified to excel in pitchpoling. Look at him; he stands upright in the tossed bow of the flying boat; wrapt in fleecy foam, the towing whale is forty feet ahead. Handling the long lance lightly, glancing twice or thrice along its length to see if it be exactly straight, Stubb whistlingly gathers up the coil of the warp in one hand, so as to secure its free end in his grasp, leaving the rest unobstructed. Then holding the lance full before his waistband’s middle, he levels it at the whale; when, covering him with it, he steadily depresses the butt-end in his hand, thereby elevating the point till the weapon stands fairly balanced upon his palm, fifteen feet in the air. He minds you somewhat of a juggler, balancing a long staff on his chin. Next moment with a rapid, nameless impulse, in a superb lofty arch the bright steel spans the foaming distance, and quivers in the life spot of the whale. Instead of sparkling water, he now spouts red blood.

“That drove the spigot out of him!” cried Stubb. “’Tis July’s immortal Fourth; all fountains must run wine today! Would now, it were old Orleans whiskey, or old Ohio, or unspeakable old Monongahela! Then, Tashtego, lad, I’d have ye hold a canakin to the jet, and we’d drink round it! Yea, verily, hearts alive, we’d brew choice punch in the spread of his spout-hole there, and from that live punch-bowl quaff the living stuff.”

Again and again to such gamesome talk, the dexterous dart is repeated; the spear returning like a greyhound in skilful leash. The agonized whale goes into his flurry; the tow-line slackens; the pitchpoler drops astern, folds his hands, and mutely watches the monster die.
