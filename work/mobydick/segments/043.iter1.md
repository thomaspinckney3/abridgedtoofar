Midnight; the foresail rises and discovers the watch, lounging, leaning, singing in chorus:

“Farewell and adieu to you, Spanish ladies!
Farewell and adieu to you, ladies of Spain!”

A Nantucket sailor calls for a tonic song, and all follow:

“Our captain stood upon the deck,
A spy-glass in his hand,
… So, be cheery, my lads! may your hearts never fail!
While the bold harpooner is striking the whale!”

“Eight bells there, forward!” pipes the mate from aft. The bell-boy, Pip—black, slight, and sleepy—is bullied up. Dutch and French, Maltese and Sicilian, Iceland and Long-Island, Azore and China sailors—each flings his national quip or caper. “Pip! little Pip! hurrah with your tambourine!” “Don’t know where it is.” “Beat thy belly, then, and wag thy ears.” The half of them dance; some drift below; some coil among rigging and oaths. “Go it, Pip! Bang it, bell-boy! Make fire-flies!” “There goes another jingler dropped off; I pound it so,” says Pip.

Tashtego, quiet, smokes. The Old Manx Sailor: “I wonder whether those jolly lads bethink them of what they are dancing over. I’ll dance over your grave, I will—that’s the bitterest threat of your night-women, that beat head-winds round corners. O Christ! to think of the green navies and the green-skulled crews! Well, well; belike the whole world’s a ball; dance on, lads, you’re young; I was once.” Clusters form; the sky darkens; a wind rises. “By Brahma!” cries a Lascar; “the sky-born Ganges turns to wind!” The Maltese shakes his tasselled cap: “Now would all the waves were women; I’d drown and chassee forever!” The Sicilian reclines, pagan. A Tahitian starts from his mat, hailing holy nakedness and Pirohitee’s torrents—then, “The blast! the blast! Up, spine, and meet it!”

“Stand by for reefing, hearties!” says a Portuguese. “Crack, old ship!” says the Dane. “Our captain has his birthmark,” mutters the Manxman; “and there’s another in the sky—lurid-like, all else pitch black.” “What of that?” growls Daggoo; “who’s afraid of black’s afraid of me!” A Spanish sailor, touchy, pricks him; knives glitter; “A row! a row!” Tashtego puffs: “Gods and men—both brawlers! Humph!” The Old Manx Sailor: “Ready formed. There! the ringed horizon. In that ring Cain struck Abel. Sweet work, right work! No? Why then, God, mad’st thou the ring?”

“Hands by the halyards! in top-gallant sails! Stand by to reef topsails!” comes the cry from the quarter-deck. They scatter. Pip cowers under the windlass: “Jollies? Lord help such jollies! Crish, crash! there goes the jib-stay! Blang-whang! Duck lower, Pip—here comes the royal yard! Worse than the whirled woods the last day of the year! Fine prospects to ’em; they’re on the road to heaven. Hold on hard! Jimmini, what a squall! But those chaps there are worse yet—they are your white squalls, they. White squalls? white whale—shirr! shirr! Here have I heard all their chat just now, and the white whale—shirr! shirr!—but spoken of once! and only this evening—it makes me jingle all over like my tambourine—that anaconda of an old man swore ’em in to hunt him! Oh, thou big white God aloft there somewhere in yon darkness, have mercy on this small black boy down here; preserve him from all men that have no bowels to feel fear!”
