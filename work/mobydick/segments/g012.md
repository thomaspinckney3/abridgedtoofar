## Chapter 1

The predestinated day arrived, and we duly met the ship Jungfrau, Derick De Deer, master, of Bremen; once the Dutch and Germans were the greatest whalers, now seldom seen; yet here, in the wide Pacific, their flag fluttered. For some reason the Jungfrau was eager to pay her respects. While yet a distance off, she rounded to, dropped a boat, and her captain came bow-forward, impatient as a man who would be at once both host and petitioner.

“What has he in his hand there?” cried Starbuck. “Impossible!—a lamp-feeder!”

“Not that,” said Stubb; “no, no, it’s a coffee-pot, Mr. Starbuck; he’s coming off to make us our coffee, is the Yarman.”

“Go along with you,” cried Flask, “it’s a lamp-feeder and an oil-can. He’s out of oil, and has come a-begging.”

However curious to see an oil-ship borrowing oil at sea, yet such things happen; and Derick did indubitably flourish a lamp-feeder. Clambering aboard, he proved himself wholly ignorant of the White Whale, turned instantly to his dark nights and empty Bremen can, and hinted his ship was what we call clean—well named Jungfrau, the Virgin. His necessities supplied, he pushed off—had not gained his ship’s side when whales were raised from both mast-heads. So eager was he that, without putting aboard his very oil-can, he slewed round and made after the leviathan lamp-feeders.

The game rose to leeward; the Jungfrau’s four boats had the start of ours. Eight whales, abreast, went before the wind; in their wide wake, many fathoms astern, swam a huge humped old bull, yellowed as by jaundice, his spout short and choking, his wake dashed where back waters met; now and then he rolled and exposed the unnatural stump of his starboard fin.

“Who’s got some paregoric?” said Stubb. “Lord, think of having half an acre of stomach-ache! did ever whale yaw so before?”

“Only wait a bit, old chap, and I’ll give ye a sling for that wounded arm,” cried cruel Flask to the whale-line.

“Mind he don’t sling thee with it,” cried Starbuck. “Give way, or the German will have him.”

Derick still led; he even shook his lamp-feeder in derision. “The ungracious dog!” cried Starbuck; then, in his old intense whisper—“Give way, greyhounds! Dog to it!”

“It’s against my religion to get mad,” shouted Stubb, “but I’d like to eat that villainous Yarman—Pull—won’t ye? A hogshead of brandy to the best man! Who’s that been dropping an anchor?—we’re becalmed. Halloo, here’s grass growing in the boat’s bottom!”

“Oh! see the suds he makes!” danced Flask. “Oh, do pile on the beef—he’s a hundred barreller—the bank of England!—Oh, do, do, do!”

Derick, in unmannerly spite, pitched overboard his lamp-feeder and oil-can to foul us and spur himself. “Pull now, men, like fifty thousand line-of-battle-ship loads of red-haired devils,” cried Stubb. “What d’ye say, Tashtego?”

“I say, pull like god-dam,” cried the Indian.

Our three boats ranged abreast and neared him; but Derick’s start would yet have served him, had not a crab caught his midship oarsman’s blade. While Derick stormed and his boat nearly capsized, Starbuck, Stubb, and Flask slanted up on his quarter; an instant more and all four boats lay diagonically in the whale’s immediate wake. It was pitiable and maddening—the vast dumb brute going head out, one poor fin beating his side in an agony, yawing this way and that, sinking at each billow. He had no voice save that choking respiration through his spiracle.

Rather than be foiled, Derick risked a long dart; but no sooner did his harpooneer rise than Queequeg, Tashtego, and Daggoo sprang to their feet, and in a diagonal row simultaneously pointed their barbs; “Blinding vapors of foam and white-fire!”—their three Nantucket irons entered the whale, the three boats bumped the German’s aside; Derick and his baffled harpooneer were spilled and sailed over.

“Don’t be afraid, my butter-boxes,” cried Stubb as he shot by; “ye’ll be picked up presently—I saw some sharks astern—St. Bernard’s dogs, you know. Hurrah! Every keel a sunbeam!—Here we go like three tin kettles at the tail of a mad cougar! This whale carries the everlasting mail!”

The monster’s rush was brief; he sounded tumultuously. The three lines flew round the loggerheads, gouging deep grooves; the bows were almost awash, the sterns tilting high. Holding on thus sometimes torments Leviathan into rising to meet the lance; yet the longer he stays below, the more he is exhausted. Consider the pressure on his two-thousand-square-foot surface—at least equal to fifty atmospheres. Suspended by three such thin threads to three bits of board!—“Canst thou fill his skin with barbed irons? … he esteemeth iron as straw.” With the strength of a thousand thighs in his tail, he had run his head under the mountains of the sea, to hide him from the Pequod’s fish-spears.

“Stand by, men; he stirs,” cried Starbuck, as the lines vibrated. “Haul in! Haul in!” The coils came in hand over hand, and soon the whale broke water within two ship’s lengths, his motions denoting extreme exhaustion. The whale’s non-valvular blood-vessels make a deadly drain at a mere puncture; yet so vast is the quantity, he will bleed and bleed, like a river in drought whose source is far-off hills. Drawing close, we beheld where his eyes had been—blind bulbs protruded; pity there was none. Rolling in his blood, he disclosed a strangely discolored bunch low on the flank.

“A nice spot,” cried Flask, “just let me prick him there once.”

“Avast!” cried Starbuck; “there’s no need of that!”—but humane Starbuck was too late. An ulcerous jet shot from the cruel wound; goaded beyond sufferance, spouting thick blood, he blindly darted at the craft, bespattering crews with gore, capsizing Flask’s boat, marring bows—his death stroke. Spent by loss of blood, he rolled away; impotently flapped with his stumped fin; over and over slowly revolved like a waning world; turned up the white secrets of his belly; lay like a log, and died. “As when by unseen hands the water is gradually drawn off from some mighty fountain,” so sank his last long dying spout.

The body showed signs of sinking with all its treasures unrifled. By Starbuck’s orders, lines were secured at different points, each boat a buoy, till the ship came up. He was fluke-chained fast; cutting in, we found the corroded length of a buried harpoon in the bunch, and, stranger yet, a lance-head of stone. What other marvels that cabinet held none can say; for suddenly the ship was dragged sideways—the body’s tendency to sink immensely increasing. Starbuck hung on till the Pequod herself would have capsized if still locking arms with the corpse; but when the command came to break clear, the strain on the timber-heads made it impossible to cast off. Everything was aslant; the ship groaned; ivory inlayings started. Handspikes and crows were vain.

“Hold on, won’t ye?” cried Stubb to the body. “Run one of ye for a prayer book and a pen-knife, and cut the big chains.”

“Knife? Aye, aye,” cried Queequeg; seizing the carpenter’s heavy hatchet, he leaned out a porthole and slashed at the fluke-chains. A few strokes full of sparks—and with a terrific snap every fastening went adrift; the ship righted; the carcase sank.

This occasional sinking of the newly-killed Sperm Whale is curious. Not the meagre old only go down; brawny, buoyant heroes sometimes sink; though the Sperm Whale far less than the Right Whale, whose Venetian blinds alone weigh more than a ton. After hours or days, gases generate; he swells and rises—a sort of animal balloon; a line-of-battle ship could hardly keep him under then. On New Zealand grounds, when a Right Whale gives token of sinking, they buoy him for the resurrection.

Not long after the sinking, a cry from our mast-heads announced the Jungfrau again lowering; the only spout in sight was a Fin-Back’s—uncapturable for his incredible speed, yet whose spout the unskilful mistake for the Sperm’s. Derick and all his host went valiantly after that unnearable brute; the Virgin crowding all sail, vanished to leeward in hopeful chase. Oh! many are the Fin-Backs, and many are the Dericks, my friend.


## Chapter 2

There are some enterprises in which a careful disorderliness is the true method. The deeper I dive into whaling’s spring-heads, the more I am impressed with its honorableness and antiquity; demi-gods and prophets have shed distinction upon it, and I am transported to belong, though subordinately, to so emblazoned a fraternity.

The gallant Perseus, a son of Jupiter, was the first whaleman; and to our calling’s eternal honor, the first whale attacked was struck not for sordid lamp-feeders, but to succor the distressed. Every one knows Andromeda—tied to a rock, and as Leviathan was bearing her off, Perseus harpooned the monster and married the maid; slain, too, at the very first dart. Doubt not the Arkite story; in ancient Joppa, a vast whale skeleton stood for ages, held to be the identical bones of Perseus’ fish; carried to Italy in triumph by the Romans. Mark, too—Jonah sailed from Joppa.

Akin is St. George and the Dragon. I maintain the dragon was a whale; whales and dragons are jumbled in old chronicles; “Thou art as a lion of the waters, and as a dragon of the sea,” saith Ezekiel—plainly meaning a whale. Modern painters mislead; the griffin-shape and land battle are but the ignorance of artists; the beast might have wallowed on the beach; the saint’s steed been a sea-horse. Placed before strict truth, Dagon fares as he did before the ark—horse’s head and palms off, the fishy stump remains. Thus one of our noble stamp, even a whaleman, is England’s tutelary guardian; and by rights we Nantucketers should be of the most noble order of St. George. Let not those knights disdain a tarred trowser; we are better entitled to their decoration than they.

Hercules? Swallowed and thrown up by a whale, he is at least an involuntary whaleman; the whale caught him, if not he the whale. By best contradictory authorities, the Grecian story is derived from the Hebrew Jonah; if I claim the demi-god, why not the prophet?

Nor heroes and saints alone. Our grand master is the dread Vishnoo, who, when the Vedas lay at the bottom of the waters, became a whale and, sounding to the uttermost depths, rescued the sacred volumes. Was not this Vishnoo a whaleman, then?

Perseus, St. George, Hercules, Jonah, and Vishnoo! there’s a member-roll for you! What club but the whaleman’s can head off like that?


## Chapter 3

Some Nantucketers distrust Jonah’s story; but sceptical Greeks doubted Hercules and the whale, and Arion and the dolphin; their doubtings made the traditions no whit the less facts.

One old Sag-Harbor whaleman argued from his quaint Bible plates that Jonah’s whale had two spouts—a Right Whale trait, whose swallow is so small “a penny roll would choke him.” Bishop Jebb answers beforehand: Jonah need not be tombed in the belly, but lodged in some part of the mouth; and the Right Whale’s mouth would accommodate a couple of whist-tables, all the players—though, being toothless, he offers no hollow tooth to ensconce in.

Sag-Harbor urged gastric juices; but a German exegetist supposes Jonah took refuge in the floating body of a dead whale—even as French soldiers tented in dead horses. Others divine he swam to another vessel near by with a whale for figure-head—possibly called “The Whale.” Learned heads have opined the “whale” meant a life-preserver—an inflated bag of wind. Poor Sag-Harbor is worsted.

Yet he had one more reason: swallowed in the Mediterranean, vomited within three days’ journey of Nineveh on the Tigris—more than three days’ march from the nearest Mediterranean point. Could the whale carry him by the Cape of Good Hope? Such a supposition involves the circumnavigation of Africa in three days, and the Tigris is too shallow for whales. But a Portuguese priest advanced this very Cape route as magnifying the miracle; and so it does. Besides, to this day the highly enlightened Turks devoutly believe; and an old English traveller speaks of a Turkish Mosque built in Jonah’s honor, with a miraculous lamp that burnt without any oil.


## Chapter 4

To make boats slide bravely, some whalers grease their bottoms; Queequeg believed in such anointings, and one morning crawled under the hanging craft, rubbing in the unctuousness as if to raise a crop of hair from her bald keel. His presentiment was warranted by the event.

Towards noon whales were raised; but as the ship sailed down, they fled with the disordered flight of Cleopatra’s barges. The boats pursued; Stubb’s was foremost. Tashtego planted one iron, yet the whale, without sounding, ran the faster; the lance must reach him or he be lost—but to haul up to his flank was impossible.

Then came that wondrous manœuvre called pitchpoling: the long, light pine lance, ten or twelve feet, handled by a warp, darted a wonderful distance from a violently rocking bow. Look at Stubb—cool, humorous in danger—balancing the shaft fifteen feet in air as a juggler a staff on his chin; then, with a rapid, nameless impulse, the bright steel spans the foaming space and quivers in the life-spot. Instead of sparkling water, he now spouts red blood.

“That drove the spigot out of him!” cried Stubb. “’Tis July’s immortal Fourth; all fountains must run wine today! Would now, it were old Orleans whiskey! Then, Tashtego, lad, we’d brew choice punch in the spread of his spout-hole.”

Again and again the dexterous dart; the spear returns like a greyhound in leash; the agonized whale goes into his flurry; the tow-line slackens; the pitchpoler drops astern, folds his hands, and mutely watches the monster die.


## Chapter 5

That for six thousand years—and no one knows how many ages before—the great whales should have been spouting all over the sea; and that for centuries thousands of hunters have watched at the very fountain; and yet, down to this blessed minute (fifteen and a quarter minutes past one o’clock P.M., December 16th, 1851), it should remain a problem whether these spoutings are water or vapor—this is noteworthy.

Whales have regular lungs; they cannot breathe through the mouth; the Sperm’s windpipe has no connexion with it; he breathes through his spiracle atop the head. Breathing, indispensable to vitality, aerates the blood; if all blood were aerated at once, a man might live awhile without another breath. Anomalous as it seems, the whale does thus live by intervals, his full hour and more at the bottom, without drawing a breath. Between ribs and spine he carries a Cretan labyrinth of vermicelli-like vessels, distended with oxygenated blood when he quits the surface—his surplus stock of vitality, as a camel’s supplementary stomachs carry drink.

Hence that obstinacy in having his spoutings out. Unmolested, if he stays eleven minutes and jets seventy times, whenever he rises again he will have his seventy breaths to a minute. Alarm him after a few breaths, he will be dodging up to make good his allowance; not till they are told will he go down for his term. Thus necessity, not thy skill, O hunter, strikes victory to thee; for a thousand fathoms below, no hook or net could catch him. In man breathing is incessant; but the Sperm Whale only breathes about one seventh or Sunday of his time.

As the spouting canal is locked against water, and opens to the windpipe alone, the whale has no voice—unless you say that when he strangely rumbles, he talks through his nose. But then again, what has the whale to say? Seldom have I known any profound being that had anything to say to this world, unless forced to stammer for a living. Oh! happy that the world is such an excellent listener!

Is the spout water or vapor? You might almost stand in it, and yet be undecided. The central body is hidden in snowy mist; close by, the sea is all commotion, cascading; the drops you think you see may be mere condensation, or those few always lodged in the countersunk fissure of the blow-hole, for even in calm the whale carries a small basin of water on his head, as a rock-cavity holds rain. Nor is it prudent to be over-curious. Among whalemen, the spout is deemed poisonous; they try to evade it. I know one who, coming into closer contact, had the skin peel from cheek and arm; and if spouted fairly into your eyes, it will blind you. Best let this deadly fountain alone.

Still we may hypothesize. Mine is this: the spout is nothing but mist. And I am impelled to it by considerations touching the whale’s inherent dignity and sublimity—never found on soundings, nor near shores; both ponderous and profound. From the heads of all ponderous profound beings—Plato, Pyrrho, the Devil, Jupiter, Dante—there goes up a certain semi-visible steam while thinking deep thoughts. While composing a treatise on Eternity, I placed a mirror before me and saw a curious worming and undulation in the atmosphere over my head; the invariable moisture of my hair, after six cups of hot tea in my thin-shingled attic, of an August noon, seems further argument.

How nobly it raises our conceit of the mighty, misty monster to behold him solemnly sailing a calm tropical sea, his vast mild head overhung by a canopy of vapor—sometimes glorified by a rainbow, as if Heaven had put its seal upon his thoughts. For rainbows do not visit clear air; they irradiate vapor. And through all the thick mists of my doubts, divine intuitions now and then shoot, enkindling my fog with a heavenly ray. For this I thank God; for all have doubts; many deny; but doubts or denials, few along with them have intuitions. Doubts of all things earthly, and intuitions of some things heavenly; this combination makes neither believer nor infidel, but makes a man who regards them both with equal eye.


## Chapter 6

Other poets have warbled the antelope’s eye and the bird that never alights; less celestial, I celebrate a tail. Reckon the largest Sperm Whale’s tail from where the trunk tapers to a man’s girth; its upper surface alone covers fifty square feet. The compact round root expands into two broad, firm, flat palms or flukes, shoaling to less than an inch; at the crotch they slightly overlap, then recede like wings, leaving a wide vacancy. In no living thing are lines of beauty more exquisitely defined than in their crescentic borders; at full expansion the tail will exceed twenty feet across.

Seemingly a dense web of welded sinews, it is composed of three strata: upper and lower fibres long and horizontal; the middle very short, running crosswise, like the thin Roman tile-courses that give masonry its strength. Moreover, the whole bulk is knit with warp and woof of muscular fibres running down and blending into the flukes, so that in the tail the measureless force of the whale is concentrated to a point. Could annihilation be to matter, this were the thing to do it.

Nor does strength cripple grace; rather it bestows it. Take away the tied tendons bursting from the marble Hercules, and the charm is gone. When Angelo paints even God the Father, mark what robustness; while those soft Italian Sons hint only the negative power of submission. Such the organ’s elasticity, that whether in sport, earnest, or anger, its flexions are invariably graceful. Therein no fairy’s arm can transcend it.

Five great motions are peculiar to it. First, propulsion: being horizontal, it never wriggles—wriggling is a sign of inferiority; scroll-wise coiled beneath, then sprung back, it gives that darting leap; side-fins only steer. Second, as a mace in battle: in striking at a boat he curves away, the blow inflicted by recoil; in unobstructed air the stroke is irresistible—your only salvation, elude it; through water it cracks a rib, dashes a plank or two—mere child’s play in the fishery. Third, sweeping: there seems a delicacy of touch concentrated here, a maidenly gentleness, the immense palms softly moving side to side; feel but a sailor’s whisker—woe to whiskers and all. Fourth, lobtailing: unbent from dignity, kitten-like he plays; broad palms flirt high, then smite—the concussion thunders for miles, as if a great gun were discharged. Fifth, peaking flukes: about to plunge, his entire flukes with thirty feet of body are tossed erect, vibrating a moment, then shoot from sight. Excepting the sublime breach, this is perhaps the grandest sight in nature—Out of the bottomless profundities the gigantic tail seems spasmodically snatching at the highest heaven. Standing once at sunrise, I saw a herd all heading east, flukes peaked and for a moment vibrating in concert—never in Persia, home of fire-worshippers, was such an embodiment of adoration. As Ptolemy testified of the elephant, I then testified of the whale, pronouncing him the most devout of beings.

Let no chance comparison place tail and elephant’s trunk on equality. As the mightiest elephant is but a terrier to Leviathan, so his trunk is but the stalk of a lily to these flukes; the direst trunk-blow is a fan’s tap beside the crush of the ponderous flukes, which have hurled entire boats and crews into the air.

The more I consider this mighty tail, the more I deplore my inability to express it. At times its gestures, which would grace a human hand, remain inexplicable. In herds, so remarkable are these mystic motions, hunters have declared them akin to Free-Mason signs; that the whale, by these methods, intelligently conversed. Dissect him how I may, I but go skin-deep; I know him not, and never will. If I know not even the tail, how understand his head?—much more, how comprehend his face, when face he has none? Thou shalt see my back parts, my tail, he seems to say, but my face shall not be seen. But I cannot make out his back parts; and hint what he will about his face, I say again he has no face.
