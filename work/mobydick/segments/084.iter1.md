The predestinated day arrived, and we duly met the ship Jungfrau, Derick De Deer, master, of Bremen; once the greatest whaling people, the Dutch and Germans are now among the least; yet here, at wide intervals, you still occasionally meet their flag in the Pacific. For some reason the Jungfrau was eager to pay her respects. While yet a distance off, she rounded to, dropped a boat, and her captain came bow-forward, impatient as a man who would be at once both host and petitioner.

“What has he in his hand there?” cried Starbuck. “Impossible!—a lamp-feeder!”

“Not that,” said Stubb; “no, no, it’s a coffee-pot, Mr. Starbuck; he’s coming off to make us our coffee, is the Yarman; don’t you see that big tin can there alongside of him?—that’s his boiling water. Oh! he’s all right, is the Yarman.”

“Go along with you,” cried Flask, “it’s a lamp-feeder and an oil-can. He’s out of oil, and has come a-begging.”

However curious to see an oil-ship borrowing oil on the whale-ground, yet such things happen; and in the present case Captain Derick De Deer did indubitably flourish a lamp-feeder. As he mounted the deck, Ahab abruptly accosted him, but in his broken lingo the German soon evinced his complete ignorance of the White Whale; immediately turned the talk to his lamp-feeder and oil can, to his dark hammocks, his last drop of Bremen oil being gone, and not a single flying-fish yet captured; concluding by hinting that his ship was what we call clean—well named Jungfrau, the Virgin. His necessities supplied, he departed; but had not gained his ship’s side when whales were raised from the mast-heads of both vessels; and so eager was he that, without pausing to put aboard even his oil-can and lamp-feeder, he slewed round and made after the leviathan lamp-feeders.

The game rose to leeward; the Jungfrau’s four boats had the start of ours. There were eight whales, abreast, going before the wind; in their wide wake, many fathoms astern, swam a huge humped old bull, yellowed as by jaundice, his spout short and choking, his wake dashed where back waters met; now and then he rolled and exposed the unnatural stump of his starboard fin.

“Who’s got some paregoric?” said Stubb. “Lord, think of having half an acre of stomach-ache! did ever whale yaw so before?”

“Only wait a bit, old chap, and I’ll give ye a sling for that wounded arm,” cried cruel Flask to the whale-line.

“Mind he don’t sling thee with it,” cried Starbuck. “Give way, or the German will have him.”

Derick still led; he even shook his lamp-feeder in derision. “The ungracious dog!” cried Starbuck; then, in his old intense whisper—“Give way, greyhounds! Dog to it!”

“I tell ye what it is, men”—cried Stubb to his crew—“it’s against my religion to get mad; but I’d like to eat that villainous Yarman—Pull—won’t ye? Are ye going to let that rascal beat ye? Do ye love brandy? A hogshead of brandy, then, to the best man. Come, why don’t some of ye burst a blood-vessel? Who’s that been dropping an anchor overboard—we don’t budge an inch—we’re becalmed. Halloo, here’s grass growing in the boat’s bottom—and by the Lord, the mast there’s budding. This won’t do, boys. Look at that Yarman! The short and long of it is, men, will ye spit fire or not?”

“Oh! see the suds he makes!” cried Flask, dancing up and down—“What a hump—Oh, do pile on the beef—lays like a log! Oh! my lads, do spring—slap-jacks and quahogs for supper, you know, my lads—baked clams and muffins—oh, do, do, spring,—he’s a hundred barreller—don’t lose him now—don’t oh, don’t!—see that Yarman—Oh, won’t ye pull for your duff, my lads—such a sog! such a sogger! Don’t ye love sperm? There goes three thousand dollars, men!—a bank!—a whole bank! The bank of England!—Oh, do, do, do!—What’s that Yarman about now?”

At this moment Derick, in unmannerly spite, pitched overboard his lamp-feeder and oil-can to foul us and spur himself. “Pull now, men, like fifty thousand line-of-battle-ship loads of red-haired devils,” cried Stubb. “What d’ye say, Tashtego?”

“I say, pull like god-dam,” cried the Indian.

Our three boats ranged abreast and neared him; but Derick’s start would yet have served him, had not a crab caught his midship oarsman’s blade. While Derick stormed and his boat nearly capsized, Starbuck, Stubb, and Flask slanted up on his quarter; an instant more and all four boats lay diagonically in the whale’s immediate wake, the foaming swell stretching from them on both sides. It was a terrific, most pitiable, and maddening sight. The whale was now going head out, his one poor fin beating his side in an agony; now to this hand, now to that, he yawed in his faltering flight; and still at every billow he spasmodically sank, or sideways rolled up his one beating fin. So have I seen a bird with clipped wing making affrighted broken circles in the air, vainly striving to escape the piratical hawks. But the bird has a voice, and with plaintive cries will make known her fear; but the fear of this vast dumb brute of the sea, was chained up and enchanted in him; he had no voice, save that choking respiration through his spiracle, and this made the sight of him unspeakably pitiable; while still, in his amazing bulk, portcullis jaw, and omnipotent tail, there was enough to appal the stoutest man who so pitied.

Rather than be foiled, Derick risked a long dart; but no sooner did his harpooneer rise than Queequeg, Tashtego, and Daggoo sprang to their feet, and in a diagonal row simultaneously pointed their barbs; “Blinding vapors of foam and white-fire!”—their three Nantucket irons entered the whale, the three boats bumped the German’s aside; Derick and his baffled harpooneer were spilled and sailed over.

“Don’t be afraid, my butter-boxes,” cried Stubb as he shot by; “ye’ll be picked up presently—I saw some sharks astern—St. Bernard’s dogs, you know. Hurrah! Every keel a sunbeam!—Here we go like three tin kettles at the tail of a mad cougar!—this whale carries the everlasting mail!”

The monster’s rush was brief; he sounded tumultuously. The three lines flew round the loggerheads, gouging deep grooves; the bows were almost awash, the sterns tilting high. Holding on thus sometimes torments Leviathan into rising to meet the lance; yet the longer he stays below, the more he is exhausted. Consider the pressure on his two-thousand-square-foot surface—at least equal to fifty atmospheres. As the three boats lay there on that gently rolling sea, gazing down into its eternal blue noon, not eight inches of perpendicular rope were visible at the bows. Seems it credible that by three such thin threads the great Leviathan was suspended like the big weight to an eight day clock. Suspended? and to what? To three bits of board. “Canst thou fill his skin with barbed irons? … he esteemeth iron as straw.” With the strength of a thousand thighs in his tail, he had run his head under the mountains of the sea, to hide him from the Pequod’s fish-spears.

In that sloping afternoon sunlight, the shadows that the three boats sent down beneath the surface, must have been long enough and broad enough to shade half Xerxes’ army. Who can tell how appalling to the wounded whale must have been such huge phantoms flitting over his head!

“Stand by, men; he stirs,” cried Starbuck, as the lines vibrated, conducting up, as by wires, the life and death throbs of the whale. The next moment, relieved in great part from the downward strain, the boats gave a sudden bounce upwards.

“Haul in! Haul in!” cried Starbuck again; “he’s rising.” The coils came in hand over hand, and soon the whale broke water within two ship’s lengths, his motions denoting extreme exhaustion. In land beasts, certain valves in the veins shut off the blood at a wound; not so with the whale, whose non-valvular blood-vessels make a deadly drain at a mere puncture; yet so vast is the quantity, he will bleed and bleed, like a river in drought whose source is far-off hills.

As the boats now more closely surrounded him, the whole upper part of his form, with much ordinarily submerged, was revealed. His eyes, or rather the places where his eyes had been, were beheld. As strange misgrown masses gather in the knot-holes of the noblest oaks when prostrate, even so from the points which the whale’s eyes had once occupied, now protruded blind bulbs, horribly pitiable to see. But pity there was none. For all his old age, and his one arm, and his blind eyes, he must die the death and be murdered, in order to light the gay bridals and other merry-makings of men, and also to illuminate the solemn churches that preach unconditional inoffensiveness by all to all. Still rolling in his blood, at last he partially disclosed a strangely discolored bunch low on the flank.

“A nice spot,” cried Flask, “just let me prick him there once.”

“Avast!” cried Starbuck; “there’s no need of that!”—but humane Starbuck was too late. An ulcerous jet shot from the cruel wound; goaded beyond sufferance, spouting thick blood, he blindly darted at the craft, bespattering crews with gore, capsizing Flask’s boat, marring bows—his death stroke. Spent by loss of blood, he rolled away; impotently flapped with his stumped fin; over and over slowly revolved like a waning world; turned up the white secrets of his belly; lay like a log, and died. “As when by unseen hands the water is gradually drawn off from some mighty fountain,” so sank the last long dying spout.

The body showed signs of sinking with all its treasures unrifled. By Starbuck’s orders, lines were secured at different points, each boat a buoy, till the ship came up. He was fluke-chained fast; cutting in, we found the corroded length of a buried harpoon in the bunch, and, stranger yet, a lance-head of stone. What other marvels that cabinet held none can say; for suddenly the ship was dragged sideways—the body’s tendency to sink immensely increasing. Starbuck hung on till the Pequod herself would have capsized if still locking arms with the corpse; but when the command came to break clear, the strain on the timber-heads made it impossible to cast off. Everything was aslant; the ship groaned; ivory inlayings started. Handspikes and crows were vain.

“Hold on, won’t ye?” cried Stubb to the body. “Run one of ye for a prayer book and a pen-knife, and cut the big chains.”

“Knife? Aye, aye,” cried Queequeg; seizing the carpenter’s heavy hatchet, he leaned out a porthole and slashed at the fluke-chains. A few strokes full of sparks—and with a terrific snap every fastening went adrift; the ship righted; the carcase sank.

This occasional sinking of the newly-killed Sperm Whale is curious. Not the meagre old only go down; brawny, buoyant heroes sometimes sink; though the Sperm Whale far less than the Right Whale, whose Venetian blinds alone weigh more than a ton. After hours or days, gases generate; he swells and rises—a sort of animal balloon. On New Zealand grounds, when a Right Whale gives token of sinking, they buoy him for the resurrection.

Not long after the sinking, a cry from our mast-heads announced the Jungfrau again lowering; the only spout in sight was a Fin-Back’s—uncapturable for his incredible speed, yet whose spout the unskilful mistake for the Sperm’s. Derick and all his host went valiantly after that unnearable brute; the Virgin crowding all sail, vanished to leeward in hopeful chase. Oh! many are the Fin-Backs, and many are the Dericks, my friend.
