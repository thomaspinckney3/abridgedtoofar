Though consumed with the hot fire of his purpose, Ahab might be far too wedded by nature and long habit to a fiery whaleman’s ways wholly to abandon the collateral prosecution of the voyage. It would be refining too much to say his vindictiveness towards the White Whale extended to all sperm whales, multiplying chances by multiplying slain; but other considerations swayed him.

To accomplish his object Ahab must use tools; and of all tools used in the shadow of the moon, men are most apt to get out of order. He knew that however magnetic his ascendency over Starbuck, it did not cover the complete spiritual man any more than corporeal superiority involves intellectual mastership. Starbuck’s body and coerced will were Ahab’s while the magnet lay at his brain; yet in his soul he abhorred the quest and would frustrate it. It might be a long interval ere the White Whale was seen; during it Starbuck would relapse into open rebellion unless common prudential influences bore on him. Not only that, but the subtle insanity of Ahab respecting Moby Dick was noways more significantly manifested than in his superlative sense and shrewdness in foreseeing that, for the present, the hunt should in some way be stripped of that strange imaginative impiousness which naturally invested it; that the full terror of the voyage must be kept withdrawn into the obscure background (for few men’s courage is proof against protracted meditation unrelieved by action); that when they stood their long night watches, his officers and men must have some nearer things to think of than Moby Dick. For however eagerly they had hailed the quest, all sailors of all sorts are more or less capricious and unreliable—they live in the varying outer weather, and they inhale its fickleness—and so temporary interests and employments must intervene and hold them healthily suspended for the final dash.

Nor was Ahab unmindful of another thing. In times of strong emotion mankind disdain all base considerations; but such times are evanescent. The permanent constitutional condition of the manufactured man, thought Ahab, is sordidness. Grant that the White Whale breeds a certain generous knight-errantism in them; still, while for love they give chase, they must have food for their more common appetites. Even the high lifted and chivalric Crusaders were not content to traverse two thousand miles without burglaries and pious perquisites by the way. Had they been held strictly to one romantic end, too many had turned in disgust. I will not strip these men, thought Ahab, of all hopes of cash—aye, cash. They may scorn cash now; but let some months go by, and no perspective promise of it to them, and then this same quiescent cash all at once mutinying in them, this same cash would soon cashier Ahab.

He had, too, by prematurely revealing the private purpose of the Pequod’s voyage, laid himself open to the charge of usurpation; with impunity the crew might refuse obedience, even wrest command. His only protection lay in predominating brain, heart, and hand, backed by a heedful, calculating attention to every minute atmospheric influence affecting his men.

For all these reasons he saw he must still, in a good degree, continue true to the nominal purpose: observe usages, and even evince passionate interest in the general pursuit. His voice now often hailed the mast-heads; let no porpoise go unreported. Vigilance was not long without reward.
