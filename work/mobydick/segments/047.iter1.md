Follow Ahab to his cabin after the oath-bound night, and you would see him draw forth wrinkled rolls of yellow sea-charts, spread them on the screwed-down table, and, beneath a rocking pewter lamp, trace courses across blank spaces. Piles of old log-books lay at his hand—seasons and places of former captures collated—and as he marked the charts, invisible pencils seemed to chase and furrow his wrinkled brow.

This was his nightly work. With all four oceans before him, he threaded a maze of currents and eddies toward the certainty of that monomaniac thought. To the unacquainted, such a quest might seem absurd—one solitary creature in unhooped oceans. Not so to Ahab, who knew the sets of tides and the drifts of the sperm whale’s food, the regular seasons and latitudes; from these he derived surmises approaching certainties, the timeliest day to be upon this or that ground. So assured is the whale’s periodicalness that some would chart his migrations like swallows.

Moreover, in passages between grounds, sperm whales, guided by some infallible instinct—say, rather, secret intelligence from the Deity—mostly swim in veins, mysterious ocean-lines, straight as surveyors’ parallels, yet a few miles broad; within that magic zone, at set seasons, they may with confidence be looked for. Hence Ahab could hope for encounters not only on separate feeding-grounds, but even as he crossed between them.

Yet herds shift; solitaries shift; and Moby Dick’s known resorts were but ocean-inns, not abodes. The crowning datum was the Season-on-the-Line—years of periodic lingerings where most deadly encounters had occurred, where the waves were storied with his deeds, and where the monomaniac old man found the awful motive to his vengeance. But he would not rest his all upon one flattering fact; nor lull his vow’s sleeplessness with any postponement.

The Pequod sailed at the very beginning of that season; no doubling Cape Horn and reaching the equatorial Pacific in time. He must wait the ensuing year; and perhaps he chose thus, to spend the interval in miscellaneous hunt, should the White Whale turn up in Persian Gulf, Bengal Bay, China Seas, or any waters haunted by his race. Monsoons or Trades might blow him into the zig-zag of the Pequod’s wake.

“Mad,” say you, to recognize a single whale again? No. Moby Dick’s snow-white brow and hump were unmistakable. And have I not tallied the whale, Ahab would mutter to himself, as after poring over his charts till long after midnight he would throw himself back in reveries—tallied him, and shall he escape? His broad fins are bored, and scalloped out like a lost sheep’s ear! Then his mad mind would run in a breathless race till faintness came, and in the open air of the deck he would seek to recover his strength. Ah, God! what trances of torments does that man endure who is consumed with one unachieved revengeful desire. He sleeps with clenched hands; and wakes with his own bloody nails in his palms.

At times intolerable dreams resumed the day’s intense thoughts, carried them on amid a clashing of phrensies, and whirled them round and round and round in his blazing brain, till the very throbbing of his life-spot became insufferable anguish; and when, as was sometimes the case, these spiritual throes heaved his being from its base, a chasm seemed opening in him, from which forked flames and lightnings shot up, and accursed fiends beckoned him to leap down among them; when this hell in himself yawned beneath him, a wild cry would be heard through the ship; and with glaring eyes Ahab would burst from his state room, as though escaping from a bed that was on fire. Yet these, instead of symptoms of some latent weakness or fright at his own resolve, were tokens of its intensity. For the Ahab that had gone to his hammock—the scheming, unappeasedly steadfast hunter of the white whale—was not the agent that so caused him to burst from it in horror again. The latter was the eternal, living principle or soul in him; and in sleep, being for the time dissociated from the characterizing mind that at other times employed it, it sought escape from the scorching contiguity of the frantic thing, of which, for the time, it was no longer an integral. But as the mind does not exist unless leagued with the soul, therefore it must have been that, in Ahab’s case, yielding up all his thoughts and fancies to his one supreme purpose; that purpose, by its own sheer inveteracy of will, forced itself against gods and devils into a kind of self-assumed, independent being of its own. Nay, could grimly live and burn, while the common vitality to which it was conjoined, fled horror-stricken from the unbidden and unfathered birth. Therefore the tormented spirit that glared out of bodily eyes, when what seemed Ahab rushed from his room, was for the time but a vacated thing, a formless somnambulistic being, a ray of living light, to be sure, but without an object to colour, and therefore a blankness in itself. God help thee, old man, thy thoughts have created a creature in thee; and he whose intense thinking thus makes him a Prometheus; a vulture feeds upon that heart for ever; that vulture the very creature he creates.
