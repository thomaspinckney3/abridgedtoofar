Midnight, Forecastle.

Foresail rises and discovers the watch, standing, lounging, leaning, and singing in chorus:

“Farewell and adieu to you, Spanish ladies!
Farewell and adieu to you, ladies of Spain!”

A Nantucket sailor: “Oh, boys, don’t be sentimental; it’s bad for the digestion! Take a tonic, follow me!”

“Our captain stood upon the deck,
A spy-glass in his hand,
… So, be cheery, my lads! may your hearts never fail!
While the bold harpooner is striking the whale!”

“Eight bells there, forward!” pipes the mate from aft. The bell-boy, Pip—black, slight, and sleepy—is bullied up.

Dutch Sailor: “Grand snoozing to-night, maty; fat night for that. We sing; they sleep—aye, lie down there, like ground-tier butts. Tell ’em it’s the resurrection; they must kiss their last, and come to judgment. That’s the way—that’s it; thy throat ain’t spoiled with eating Amsterdam butter.”

French Sailor: “Hist, boys! let’s have a jig or two before we ride to anchor in Blanket Bay. Pip! little Pip! hurrah with your tambourine!”
Pip (sulky): “Don’t know where it is.”
French Sailor: “Beat thy belly, then, and wag thy ears. Legs! legs!”

“Go it, Pip! Bang it, bell-boy! Make fire-flies!” cries an Azore; “there goes another jingler dropped off; I pound it so,” says Pip. Tashtego, quiet, smokes.

Old Manx Sailor: “I wonder whether those jolly lads bethink them of what they are dancing over. I’ll dance over your grave, I will—that’s the bitterest threat of your night-women, that beat head-winds round corners. O Christ! to think of the green navies and the green-skulled crews! Well, well; belike the whole world’s a ball; dance on, lads, you’re young; I was once.”

They cluster; the wind rises. “By Brahma!” cries a Lascar; “the sky-born Ganges turns to wind!”

Maltese Sailor (reclining and shaking his cap): “It’s the waves—the snow’s caps turn to jig it now. They’ll shake their tassels soon. Now would all the waves were women, then I’d go drown, and chassee with them evermore! There’s naught so sweet on earth—heaven may not match it!—as those swift glances of warm, wild bosoms in the dance.”

The Sicilian reclines. A Tahitian starts from his mat, hailing holy nakedness and Pirohitee’s torrents—then, “The blast! the blast! Up, spine, and meet it!”

“Stand by for reefing, hearties!” says a Portuguese. “Crack, old ship!” says the Dane.

“Our captain has his birthmark,” mutters the Manxman; “and there’s another in the sky—lurid-like, all else pitch black.”
“What of that?” growls Daggoo; “who’s afraid of black’s afraid of me!”
A Spanish sailor advances: “Aye, harpooneer, thy race is the undeniable dark side of mankind—devilish dark at that. No offence.”
Daggoo (grimly). “None.”

Knives glitter—“A row! a row!” Tashtego puffs: “Gods and men—both brawlers! Humph!” Old Manx Sailor: “Ready formed. There! the ringed horizon. In that ring Cain struck Abel. Sweet work, right work! No? Why then, God, mad’st thou the ring?”

“Hands by the halyards! in top-gallant sails! Stand by to reef topsails!” comes the cry from the quarter-deck. They scatter.

Pip cowers under the windlass: “Jollies? Lord help such jollies! Crish, crash! there goes the jib-stay! Blang-whang! Duck lower, Pip—here comes the royal yard! Worse than the whirled woods the last day of the year! Fine prospects to ’em; they’re on the road to heaven. Hold on hard! Jimmini, what a squall! But those chaps there are worse yet—they are your white squalls, they. White squalls? white whale—shirr! shirr! Here have I heard all their chat just now, and the white whale—shirr! shirr!—but spoken of once! and only this evening—it makes me jingle all over like my tambourine—that anaconda of an old man swore ’em in to hunt him! Oh, thou big white God aloft there somewhere in yon darkness, have mercy on this small black boy down here; preserve him from all men that have no bowels to feel fear!”
