## Chapter 1

Ice and icebergs astern, the Pequod rolled into the bright Quito spring—the warmly cool, clear, ringing, perfumed, overflowing days; the starred and stately nights like haughty dames. But all that witchery turned inward, and more and more it wrought upon Ahab’s texture. Old age is wakeful; and Ahab, linked long with life, haunted the open air, muttering that to descend the scuttle to his grave-dug berth “feels like going down into one’s tomb.”

Nightly, when quietude fell, he would emerge, gripping the iron banister, often abstaining from patrolling the quarter-deck for his mates’ sleep’s sake—the crack of the ivory heel would have set dreams on the crunching teeth of sharks. But once the mood was on him too deep; he lumbered from taffrail to mainmast till Stubb came up, deprecatingly humorous, hinting at muffling the noise with a tow-stuffed globe for that ivory heel.

“Am I a cannon-ball, Stubb,” said Ahab, “that thou wouldst wad me that fashion? But go thy ways; I had forgot. Below to thy nightly grave… Down, dog, and kennel!”

Stubb started, then flared: he was not used to be spoken to so. “Avast!” gritted Ahab; “be called ten times a donkey, and a mule, and an ass, and begone, or I’ll clear the world of thee!” He advanced with such overbearing terrors that Stubb involuntarily retreated down the scuttle.

There he muttered, half for a blow, half for—what?—“down here on my knees and pray for him?” His eyes had flashed like powder-pans. Was he mad? A hot old man, with a conscience—“a kind of Tic-Dolly-row—worse nor a toothache.” Dough-Boy found his hammock sheets knotted, pillow hot as if a baked brick had lain there. What did he in the after hold nightly? Who had appointments with him? Queer, all queer; “but that’s against my principles. Think not, is my eleventh commandment; and sleep when you can, is my twelfth.” Yet even as he slung to hammock, he started: “Didn’t he call me a dog?—ten times a donkey—and pile jackasses on top of that!” His brow had flashed like a bleached bone. “By the Lord, I must have been dreaming—How? how? how?—the only way’s to stash it.” And in the morning, he would see how the plaguey juggling looked by daylight.


## Chapter 2

When Stubb had gone, Ahab leaned over the bulwarks; then called for his ivory stool and his pipe. Lighting it at the binnacle, he sat smoking on that tripod of bones—like a Khan of the plank, a king of the sea.

Thick vapor blew back into his face. “How now,” he soliloquized, withdrawing the tube, “this smoking no longer soothes. Oh, my pipe! hard must it go with me if thy charm be gone! Here have I been unconsciously toiling, not pleasuring—aye, and ignorantly smoking to windward all the while… What business have I with this pipe? This thing that is meant for sereneness… not among torn iron-grey locks like mine. I’ll smoke no more—”

He tossed the lighted pipe into the sea. It hissed; the ship shot by the sinking bubble. With slouched hat, Ahab lurchingly paced the planks.


## Chapter 3

Next morning Stubb told Flask his dream: that Ahab’s ivory leg kicked him; that kicking back, he kicked his own leg off; that Ahab towered into a pyramid he kept battering at. Yet through all his rage, he thought the insult less than it seemed—“Why, it’s not a real leg, only a false leg. There’s a mighty difference between a living thump and a dead thump.” A cane’s blow is not a hand’s; “it was only a playful cudgelling—a whaleboning—not a base kick.” The small pointed foot, too—“whittled down to a point.”

Then a badger-haired old merman with a hump slewed him round, forever muttering “wise Stubb, wise Stubb,” and arguing the insult. Kicked with an ivory leg, and by a great man—“It’s an honor; I consider it an honor. Be kicked by him; account his kicks honors; and on no account kick back; for you can’t help yourself, wise Stubb. Don’t you see that pyramid?”

Stubb woke in his hammock. “What do you think of that, Flask?” Flask thought it foolish. “May be,” said Stubb, “but it’s made a wise man of me. D’ye see Ahab yonder? Best let the old man alone; never speak to him, whatever he says.—Hark!”

“Mast-head, there! Look sharp, all of ye! There are whales hereabouts! If ye see a white one, split your lungs for him!”

“A white whale—did ye mark that, man?” whispered Stubb. “There’s something special in the wind. Ahab has that that’s bloody on his mind. But, mum; he comes this way.”


## Chapter 4

Already boldly launched, we shall soon be lost in the deep’s unshored, harbourless immensities; ere that, attend to a matter indispensable to apprehending the leviathanic allusions to follow—a systematized exhibition of the whale in his broad genera. It is a fearful thing to classify the constituents of a chaos. “No branch of Zoology is so much involved as that which is entitled Cetology,” says Scoresby (A.D. 1820). “Utter confusion exists,” adds Surgeon Beale (A.D. 1839). “Impenetrable veil,” “Unfitness,” “field strewn with thorns,” cry Cuvier, Hunter, Lesson.

Yet though of real knowledge there be little, of books there are a plenty: from the Authors of the Bible and Aristotle, down to Scoresby, Beale, and Bennett. But the usurpation of the Greenland or right-whale as monarch has been complete—poets crowned him so—through ignorance of the great sperm whale. Now hear a new proclamation. This is Charing Cross; hear ye! good people all,—the Greenland whale is deposed,—the great sperm whale now reigneth!

Only Beale and Bennett, surgeons to South-Sea whale-ships, at all put the living sperm whale before you. But as yet, the sperm whale, scientific or poetic, lives not complete in any literature. Far above all hunted whales, his is an unwritten life.

As no better man advances, I offer my own poor draught: not minute anatomy, but an outline, to be hereafter filled by abler builders. I am the architect, not the mason.

First, preliminaries. It remains, in some quarters, a moot point whether a whale be a fish. Linnæus (A.D. 1776) separates whales from fish—warm bilocular heart, lungs, movable eyelids, hollow ears, mammæ, and so on. But waiving argument, I take the good old fashioned ground that the whale is a fish, and call upon holy Jonah to back me. Settle then the external definition: to be short, a whale is a spouting fish with a horizontal tail. There you have him.

By this, I exclude no spouting, horizontal-tailed sea creature Nantucketers call whale; nor include any alien. I deny, however, the Lamatins and Dugongs their credentials—they do not spout. Now, for method: to seize whales bodily, in their entire liberal volume, and sort them bibliographically by magnitude—like books. Hence three primary BOOKS: I. THE FOLIO; II. the OCTAVO; III. the DUODECIMO. As types: Sperm Whale (Folio); Grampus (Octavo); Porpoise (Duodecimo).

BOOK I. Folio. Chapters: I. Sperm Whale; II. Right Whale; III. Fin-Back; IV. Hump-backed; V. Razor Back; VI. Sulphur Bottom.

CH. I. Sperm Whale.—Trumpa, Physeter, Anvil-Headed, Cachalot, Pottsfich, Macrocephalus: largest inhabitant of the globe, most formidable to encounter, most majestic, and most valuable—for from him alone is spermaceti obtained. The name is a philological absurdity, derived from ancient confusions when his oil was accidental and scarce—retained to enhance value—and at last bestowed upon the whale whence the true spermaceti came.

CH. II. Right Whale.—Most venerable, first regularly hunted; yields baleen (“whalebone”) and “whale oil.” The Great Mysticetus; the Greenland Whale; variously baptised—the Whale; the Black Whale; the True Whale. Differences claimed between English Greenland and American Right are inconclusive. Elsewhere I treat him at some length, to elucidate the sperm.

CH. III. Fin-Back.—Fin-Back, Tall-Spout, Long-John: seen in almost every sea, his angular back-fin a conspicuous gnomon casting wavy hour-lines on the dialled sea. Less portly than the Right; olive-coloured; solitary, shy; his lofty jet a misanthropic spear; a Cain among whales. Because he has baleen, sometimes grouped among “Whalebone whales”; but be warned: baleen, hump, fin, teeth—these features are irregularly combined across kinds; on that rock all whale-naturalists have split.

CH. IV. Hump Back.—Often taken on the northern American coast; oil indifferent; baleen present; the most gamesome and light-hearted, making more gay foam than any.

CH. V. Razor Back.—Little known; I have seen him off Cape Horn; his long sharp ridge alone shown. Let him go.

CH. VI. Sulphur Bottom.—A retiring gentleman with a brimstone belly; seldom seen save remotely in southern seas; never chased—he would run away with rope-walks of line. Prodigies are told of him. Adieu!

BOOK II. Octavoes (middling magnitude): I. Grampus; II. Black Fish; III. Narwhale; IV. Thrasher; V. Killer.

CH. I. Grampus.—His loud blowing has furnished a landsman’s proverb. Though not popularly a whale, he possesses the grand distinctive features; fifteen to twenty-five feet; swims in herds; not regularly hunted; his approach sometimes premonitory of the sperm.

CH. II. Black Fish.—Vague name; call him the Hyena Whale. Voracious; Mephistophelean grin from the up-curved inner lips. Hooked dorsal like a Roman nose. Thin blubber, yet some yield above thirty gallons; captured for cheap oil when no better game is up.

CH. III. Narwhale (Nostril whale).—Sixteen feet; horn (a tusk) five to fifteen feet, sinister-sided; what precise purpose it serves, I cannot say—ice-piercer to some, rake to others. A sea-unicorn; in old days its horn, antidote to poison, fetched immense prices; distilled as volatile salts for fainting ladies. Sir Martin Frobisher presented such a prodigious horn to Queen Bess. Picturesque, milk-white dotted with black; fine clear oil; found in circumpolar seas.

CH. IV. Killer.—Savage Feegee fish of a grampus’s bigness; takes Folio whales by the lip, hangs like a leech till they are worried to death. Never hunted.

CH. V. Thrasher.—Famous for his tail; mounts the Folio’s back and works his passage by flogging him; still less is known than of the Killer. Outlaws both.

BOOK III. Duodecimoes (smaller): I. Huzza Porpoise; II. Algerine Porpoise; III. Mealy-mouthed Porpoise. Though but four or five feet, by my definition they are whales—spouting fish with horizontal tails.

CH. I. Huzza Porpoise.—Name of my bestowal; swims in hilarious shoals, tossing like caps on the Fourth-of-July; lucky omens, lads that live before the wind. One gallon of good oil; delicate jaw-fluid prized by jewellers and watchmakers; on your hones, sailors! Watch his tiny spout—you shall see the great sperm himself in miniature.

CH. II. Algerine Porpoise.—A pirate; very savage; Pacific; somewhat larger than the Huzza; buckles to a shark.

CH. III. Mealy-mouthed Porpoise.—Largest porpoise; Pacific; called the Right-Whale Porpoise for his hauntings. Neater figure; no back-fin; lovely tail; hazel, sentimental Indian eyes; but his mealy mouth—white below, black above, a bright waist dividing—makes him look meal-bag felonious. Oil like the common porpoise’s.

Beyond the Duodecimo, I do not proceed; the Porpoise is the smallest of whales. But there is a rabble—Bottle-Nose, Junk, Pudding-Headed, Cape, Leading, Cannon, Scragg, Coppered, Elephant, Iceberg, Quog, Blue, etc.—uncertain, fugitive, half-fabulous. Let future investigators mark and incorporate them by Folio, Octavo, or Duodecimo, as may be.

Finally: this system is left unfinished, as Cologne’s cathedral with the crane upon the incomplete tower. For small erections may be finished by their first architects; grand ones, true ones, ever leave the copestone to posterity. God keep me from ever completing anything. This whole book is but a draught—nay, but the draught of a draught. Oh, Time, Strength, Cash, and Patience!


## Chapter 5

Because the whale-fleet knows a class of officers unknown elsewhere—the harpooneers—here fits a domestic peculiarity. In the old Dutch Fishery, command was divided between the captain and an officer called the Specksnyder—literally, Fat-Cutter; usage made it Chief Harpooneer. Navigation and general management were the captain’s; all whale-hunting concerns the Specksnyder’s. In the British Greenland Fishery, under the corrupted title Specksioneer, he lingers, sadly abridged; now but senior harpooneer, one of the captain’s subalterns. Yet upon the harpooneers’ good conduct the voyage largely depends; and in the American Fishery, by night on a whaling ground, the deck’s command may be his. Therefore the sea’s grand maxim demands he live nominally apart from the men forward and be marked their professional superior—though familiarly their social equal.

At sea the grand distinction is this: officer aft, man forward. Hence mates quarter with the captain; and, in most American whalers, the harpooneers too take their meals in the captain’s cabin and sleep near it.

A Southern whaling voyage is the longest made by man; its perils and common venture breed some family-likeness; yet the punctilious externals of the quarter-deck seldom relax. Many a Nantucket skipper parades with elated grandeur; extorts outward homage like imperial purple, not pilot-cloth. Of all men, moody Ahab least affected shallow assumption; the only homage he exacted was instantaneous obedience. He required no man to unshoe upon the quarter-deck; and there were times, from peculiar circumstances hereafter told, when he addressed them with strange condescensions or in terrorem. Yet even Ahab was not unobservant of the paramount forms and usages of the sea.

Nor will it fail to be perceived that behind those forms he sometimes masked himself; making use of them for private ends. That certain sultanism of his brain, else in good degree unmanifested, through those forms became incarnate in irresistible dictatorship. For intellectual superiority, to assume practical supremacy, needs external arts and entrenchments—paltry and base in themselves—that invest mortal rule. Thus God’s true princes keep from the hustings; thus baubles magnify even idiot imbecility; and when, as with Nicholas the Czar, the ringed crown encircles an imperial brain, plebeian herds crouch before the tremendous centralization. Let the tragic dramatist who would depict indomitableness remember this hint.

But Ahab, my Captain, still moves before me in all his Nantucket grimness; I have only to do with a poor old whale-hunter. Oh, Ahab! what shall be grand in thee must be plucked at from the skies, dived for in the deep, and featured in the unbodied air!


## Chapter 6

Noon. Dough-Boy’s pale loaf-of-bread face lifts from the scuttle: “Dinner.” Ahab, who has just taken the sun and reckoned latitude on the medallion tablet atop his ivory leg, swings by the mizen shrouds and, with an even, unexhilarated “Dinner, Mr. Starbuck,” disappears.

When the last echo of the sultan’s step has died, Starbuck takes a few grave turns, peeps into the binnacle, then, with some touch of pleasantness, “Dinner, Mr. Stubb,” and descends. The second Emir lounges a while, shakes the main brace, then rapidly, “Dinner, Mr. Flask,” follows. Left alone, Flask tips knowing winks, kicks off his shoes, cuts a silent hornpipe over the Grand Turk’s head, slings his cap to the mizentop; but ere he crosses the threshold below, he ships a new face; independent, hilarious Flask enters King Ahab’s presence transformed to Abjectus, or the Slave.

So works the intense artificialness of sea-usages. Officers bold enough on deck, at the captain’s table are inoffensive, deprecatory, humble. Over his ivory-inlaid board Ahab presided like a mute, maned sea-lion on a white coral beach, surrounded by warlike but deferential cubs. In turn each waited to be served. Their eyes fastened on the old man’s knife as he carved; silence, solemn as Frankfort’s coronation banquet. Ahab forbade not talk; only he was dumb. When, between knife and fork, he motioned Starbuck’s plate, the mate received his meat like alms; cut tenderly; started if steel grazed earthenware; chewed noiselessly. Poor little Flask—the youngest son—had the shinbones; his would have been the drumsticks. To help himself had seemed larceny. Ahab would scarce have noticed; yet Flask never presumed. Least of all did he presume to butter. Whether the owners denied him for fear of blotching his sunny cheek, or butter in these marketless waters stood at a premium—however it was, Flask, alas! was a butterless man!

Another thing. He was last down and first up. Starbuck and Stubb had the start, and the privilege of lingering. If Stubb, with small appetite, showed symptoms of concluding, Flask must bestir himself or get scarce three mouthfuls; for it is against holy usage that Stubb precede Flask to the deck. So Flask owned that since promotion he had never known aught but hunger more or less. Peace and satisfaction had departed from his stomach. “There’s the fruits of promotion now; the vanity of glory; the insanity of life!” Besides, if any mere sailor had a grudge against him, ample vengeance was a peep through the sky-light: Flask sitting silly and dumfoundered before awful Ahab.

Ahab and his three mates formed the first table. After their inverted departure, the pallid steward restored some hurried order; then the three harpooneers were bidden—the residuary legatees—turning the high and mighty cabin into a servants’ hall. In fierce contrast to the intolerable constraint and nameless domineerings above, the harpooneers’ board was frantic democracy. While mates feared the hinges of their jaws, the harpooneers chewed with a report; they dined like lords, filled like Indian ships. Queequeg and Tashtego’s appetites were portentous; Dough-Boy, if not nimble hop-skip-and-jump with great barons of salt-junk, felt Tashtego’s harpoon-wise fork prick him forward. Once Daggoo, in sudden humor, snatched him up bodily, thrust his head into a trencher, while Tashtego, knife in hand, traced the circle preliminary to scalping. Dough-Boy—progeny of a bankrupt baker and a hospital nurse—was a nervous little fellow; the standing spectacle of black terrific Ahab, and these periodical visitations, made his life one continual lip-quiver. Commonly, having furnished all demanded things, he escaped to his pantry and fearfully peeped through the blinds till the tempest passed.

It was a sight: Queequeg’s filed teeth opposite Tashtego’s; Daggoo, seated on the floor (a bench would have lifted his hearse-plumed head into the carlines), at every motion making the low frame shake like a ship bearing an African elephant. Yet the great negro was abstemious, dainty even—small mouthfuls maintaining so broad and superb a person; doubtless he fed strong on the abounding element of air. Not by beef or bread are giants made. But Queequeg’s mortal, barbaric smack of the lip—ugly enough—made Dough-Boy glance at his own lean arms for teeth-marks. When Tashtego sang out for him to produce himself that his bones might be picked, the crockery rattled with Dough-Boy’s palsy. Nor did the whetstones help—the harpooneers, carrying them in their pockets, ostentatiously sharpened their knives at dinner; that grating sound tranquillized him not at all. Alas! hard fares the white waiter who waits upon cannibals. Not a napkin should he carry on his arm, but a buckler.

Though these barbarians dined in the cabin and nominally lived there, they were scarcely in it save at meals and before sleep, passing through to their own quarters. In this, Ahab was no exception to American whale-captains who deem the cabin theirs by right, others being there by courtesy. In real truth, mates and harpooneers lived out of the cabin more than in; for in the cabin was no companionship; socially, Ahab was inaccessible. He lived in the world as the last of the Grisly Bears in settled Missouri. And as, when Spring and Summer depart, that wild Logan, burying in a tree’s hollow, lives out the winter there, sucking his own paws; so, in his inclement, howling old age, Ahab’s soul, shut in the caved trunk of his body, fed upon the sullen paws of its gloom.
