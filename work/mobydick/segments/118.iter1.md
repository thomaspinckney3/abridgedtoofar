Jolly sights and sounds bore down before the wind: the Nantucket Bachelor, her last cask wedged and her bursting hatches bolted, now, in glad holiday apparel, sailed somewhat vain-gloriously among the ships ere pointing home. Red bunting streamed from hats; a whale-boat hung bottom down; the long lower jaw of their last whale swung from her bowsprit; barrels of sperm were lashed in the tops and cross-trees; a lamp at her main truck.

So full her luck that beef and bread had been given away for sperm; supplemental casks bartered and stowed even in state-rooms; the cabin table knocked into kindling, the mess dining off an oil-butt head. In fo’c’sle and galley alike they jested that the cook had headed his boiler and filled it; the steward plugged his coffee-pot and filled it; the harpooneers headed the sockets of their irons and filled them; everything was filled with sperm except the captain’s pantaloons pockets, reserved to thrust his hands into in complacency.

As this glad ship of good luck bore down upon the moody Pequod, the barbarian sound of enormous drums came from her forecastle; the mates and harpooneers danced with the olive-hued girls eloped from the Polynesian Isles; while aloft, in an ornamented boat, three Long Island negroes, with glittering fiddle-bows of whale ivory, were presiding over the hilarious jig. Meanwhile others were tumultuously busy at the masonry of the try-works, hurling the now useless brick and mortar into the sea. You would have almost thought they were pulling down the cursed Bastille, such wild cries they raised. Lord and master over all this scene, the captain stood erect on the elevated quarter-deck, as if the whole rejoicing drama were contrived for his diversion.

And Ahab, he too stood on his quarter-deck, shaggy and black, with a stubborn gloom; and as the two ships crossed each other’s wakes—one all jubilations for things passed, the other all forebodings as to things to come—their two captains in themselves impersonated the whole striking contrast of the scene.

“Come aboard, come aboard!” cried the gay commander, lifting glass and bottle.

“Hast seen the White Whale?” gritted Ahab.

“No; only heard of him; but don’t believe in him at all. Come aboard!”

“Thou art too damned jolly. Sail on. Hast lost any men?”

“Not enough to speak of—two islanders;—but come aboard, old hearty; merry’s the play; a full ship and homeward-bound.”

“How wondrous familiar is a fool!” muttered Ahab; then aloud, “Thou art a full ship and homeward bound, thou sayst; well, then, call me an empty ship, and outward-bound. So go thy ways, and I will mine. Forward there! Set all sail, and keep her to the wind!”

Thus one went cheerily before the breeze; the other stubbornly fought against it. Ahab, leaning over the taffrail, eyed the homeward craft, took from his pocket a small vial of sand, and, looking from ship to vial, seemed thereby bringing two remote associations together; for that vial was filled with Nantucket soundings.
