All this while the prodigious head of a Sperm Whale hung to starboard; we must let it hang, praying the tackles hold, for other matters press. Drifting into yellow brit water—token of Right Whales—Stubb’s and Flask’s boats gave chase to tall spouts to leeward. Fast to a whale and towed perilously near the hull—“Cut, cut!”—they paid out line, pulled ahead, and, winning but a few feet, felt the line rasp lightning-like under the keel and leap quivering to the bows, the drops flinging off like bits of broken glass. The fagged whale altered and went round the stern, towing both boats in a full circuit, while sharks quit the Sperm head for the new blood and drank like Israelites at the smitten rock. Thickening spout; a frightful roll; he turned upon his back a corpse.

Making fast to the flukes and preparing to tow, the headsmen talked. Stubb, disgusted with “so ignoble a leviathan,” wondered what Ahab wanted with the lump. Flask told the charm: a ship with a Sperm head starboard and a Right head larboard can never capsize. “I don’t know,” he added, “but I heard that gamboge ghost of a Fedallah saying so, and he seems to know all about ships’ charms. But I sometimes think he’ll charm the ship to no good at last. I don’t half like that chap, Stubb.” “Sink him! I never look at him,” cried Stubb; “Flask, I take that Fedallah to be the devil in disguise. The reason why you don’t see his tail is because he tucks it up out of sight; he carries it coiled away in his pocket, I guess; blast him! he’s always wanting oakum to stuff into the toes of his boots.” “What’s the old man have so much to do with him for?” “Striking up a swap or a bargain, I suppose.” “Bargain?—about what?” “Why, do ye see, the old man is hard bent after that White Whale, and the devil there is trying to come round him, and get him to swap away his silver watch, or his soul, or something of that sort, and then he’ll surrender Moby Dick.” “Pooh! Stubb, you are skylarking; how can Fedallah do that?” “I don’t know, Flask, but the devil is a curious chap, and a wicked one, I tell ye. Why, they say as how he went a sauntering into the old flag-ship once, switching his tail about devilish easy and gentlemanlike. The old governor was at home, and asked the devil what he wanted. The devil, switching his hoofs, up and says, ‘I want John.’ ‘What for?’ says the old governor. ‘What business is that of yours,’ says the devil, getting mad,—‘I want to use him.’ ‘Take him,’ says the governor—and by the Lord, Flask, if the devil didn’t give John the Asiatic cholera before he got through with him, I’ll eat this whale in one mouthful.” “How old do you suppose Fedallah is, Stubb?” “Do you see that mainmast there?”—pointing to the ship—“well, that’s the figure one; now take all the hoops in the Pequod’s hold, and string along in a row with that mast, for oughts, do you see; well, that wouldn’t begin to be Fedallah’s age.” They bandied sailor’s theology—duck the devil, and if he ducks you, give him two black eyes; the governor won’t clap him in double-darbies, having signed a bond to roast the kidnappees he brings. “Do you suppose Fedallah wants to kidnap Captain Ahab?” “You’ll know it before long, Flask. I’ll keep a sharp look-out; if he tries it, I’ll grab his tail, take it to the capstan, and heave it short off at the stump.”

With the boats returning, they were hailed to tow the Right Whale on the larboard side where fluke-chains waited. “Didn’t I tell you so?” cried Flask. In good time it proved true; the Pequod, steeply leaned over by the first head, now, by the counterpoise of both, regained an even keel—sorely strained, though. “So, when on one side you hoist in Locke’s head, you go over that way; but now, on the other side, hoist in Kant’s and you come back again; but in very poor plight. Thus, some minds for ever keep trimming boat. Oh, ye foolish! throw all these thunder-heads overboard, and then you will float light and right.”

No customary stripping had yet been done; the carcases of both whales had dropped astern, and the head-laden ship looked a mule with overburdening panniers. Fedallah, calmly eyeing the Right Whale’s head, glanced from its deep wrinkles to the lines in his own hand; and Ahab so chanced to stand that the Parsee occupied his shadow; while, if the Parsee’s shadow was there at all it seemed only to blend with, and lengthen Ahab’s. Laplandish speculations flew among the toilers at these passing things.
