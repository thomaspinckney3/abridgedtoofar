## Chapter 1

It was in the more pleasant weather, that in due rotation with the other seamen my first mast-head came round. In most American whalemen the mast-heads are manned almost as the anchor trips; and if, after years, a ship draws nigh home with anything empty in her, then her mast-heads are kept manned to the last—hope not struck till the spires of port are under the skysail-poles.

As the business is an ancient one, expatiate we a little. The earliest standers of mast-heads I take to have been the old Egyptians; their stair-like pyramids furnished those long-legged astronomers a mounting place to “sing out” for new stars, even as our look-outs sing for sails or whales. Saint Stylites on his pillar was a dauntless stander, who literally died at his post. Of modern standers we have a lifeless set—Napoleon aloft on the Vendôme column; great Washington on his main-mast in Baltimore; Nelson in Trafalgar Square—well able to face a gale, but answering no hail from below.

Nor is it unwarrantable to couple shore-standers with sea-standers; Obed Macy tells how Nantucketers, ere regular whale-ships, nailed cleats up coast spars for look-outs; New Zealand Bay-men, too, cried whales from beach-spires. But turn we to the one proper mast-head, that of a whaleship at sea. From sun-rise to sun-set the three mast-heads are manned; two-hour turns as at the helm. In the serene tropics it is delightful: you stand a hundred feet above the silent decks, the masts like stilts, the ship indolently rolling; a sublime uneventfulness invests you; no news, no stocks, your dinners stowed in casks, your bill of fare immutable.

Yet months of your voyage are spent up there, and the place is destitute of cosy inhabitiveness. Your perch is the head of the t’gallant-mast, upon two thin cross-trees—cosy as a bull’s horns. A watch-coat is but an extra skin; you cannot fit a shelf into it. Much to be deplored, too, that our southern mast-heads lack those enviable crow’s-nests of the Greenlandmen. Captain Sleet, in his fireside narrative, describes his Sleet’s crow’s-nest—seat, locker, racks for trumpet and glass—nay, even a rifle to pop stray narwhales. He enlarges learnedly upon binnacle deviations; but for all his azimuths, he knew that case-bottle nicely tucked within reach was a faithful comforter. Brave, honest, learned Captain; only why ignore that bottle?

But if we South fishers lack such snuggeries, our seas are serenity itself. I used to lounge up the rigging, chat a bit in the top, then throw a lazy leg over the topsail-yard for a preliminary survey, and at last mount to my destination. Let me make a clean breast: I kept but sorry guard. With the problem of the universe revolving in me, how could I, being left to myself at that thought-engendering altitude, strictly obey, “Keep your weather eye open, and sing out every time”?

Ship-owners of Nantucket, beware of enlisting any lad with lean brow and hollow eye, who ships with the Phaedon instead of Bowditch in his head. Your whales must be seen before killed; this sunken-eyed Platonist will tow you ten wakes and never make you one pint of sperm the richer. Childe Harold himself often perches at the mast-head and ejaculates:

“Roll on, thou deep and dark blue ocean, roll! Ten thousand
blubber-hunters sweep over thee in vain.”

Very often captains upbraid such absent-minded youths with not feeling interest in the voyage. All in vain: lulled by the blending cadence of waves with thoughts, they lose identity; the mystic ocean below becomes the visible image of the deep, blue, bottomless soul; every dim fin an embodiment of elusive thoughts. In that enchanted mood your spirit ebbs away; there is no life in you now except that rocking life borrowed from the sea; but stir an inch—slip your hold—and horror darts back your identity. Over Descartian vortices you hover; and perhaps at noon, with one half-throttled shriek, you drop into the summer sea, to rise no more. Heed it well, ye Pantheists!


## Chapter 2

It was not long after the affair of the pipe, that Ahab, as was his wont, paced the quarter-deck; his ivory stride scored deeper dents that day, as if the one thought within paced as he paced. Near sunset he halted by the bulwarks, fixed his bone leg in the auger-hole, grasped a shroud, and ordered Starbuck to send everybody aft. “Mast-heads, there! come down!”

The ship’s company gathered, eyeing him as men watch a storm coming up the horizon. He paced again; then suddenly: “What do ye do when ye see a whale, men?” “Sing out for him!” “And next?” “Lower away, and after him!” “And what tune is it ye pull to?” “A dead whale or a stove boat!” At each shout his countenance strangely and fiercely glowed.

Half-revolving in his pivot-hole, he reached high a shroud, and cried, holding up a bright coin: “D’ye see this Spanish ounce of gold? it is a sixteen dollar piece. Whosoever of ye raises me a white-headed whale with a wrinkled brow and a crooked jaw—aye, with three holes in his starboard fluke—he shall have this gold ounce, my boys!” The top-maul rang; the coin was nailed to the main-mast. “Look sharp for white water; if ye see but a bubble, sing out.”

Tashtego, Daggoo, and Queequeg started at the traits. “Captain Ahab,” said Tashtego, “that white whale must be the same that some call Moby Dick.” “Moby Dick?” shouted Ahab. They told of his fan-tailing, his bushy spout, the twisted irons in him. “Corkscrew!” cried Ahab. “Aye, the harpoons lie all twisted and wrenched in him; his spout is a big one, white as Nantucket wool; he fan-tails like a split jib. Death and devils! men, it is Moby Dick ye have seen—Moby Dick!”

Starbuck at last: “Captain Ahab, I have heard of Moby Dick—but it was not Moby Dick that took off thy leg?” “Aye, Starbuck; it was Moby Dick that dismasted me; made a poor pegging lubber of me!” Then, with a terrific sob: “Aye, aye! and I’ll chase him round Good Hope, and round the Horn, and round the Norway Maelstrom, and round perdition’s flames before I give him up. And this is what ye have shipped for, men! till he spouts black blood and rolls fin out. What say ye—will ye splice hands on it now?” Harpooneers and seamen shouted: “A sharp eye for the white whale; a sharp lance for Moby Dick!”

But Starbuck: “I am game for his crooked jaw, and for the jaws of Death, if it fairly comes in the way of the business; but I came here to hunt whales, not my commander’s vengeance. How many barrels will thy vengeance yield thee? it will not fetch thee much in our Nantucket market.” Ahab: “If money’s the measurer, then let me tell thee, my vengeance will fetch a great premium here!” He smote his chest.

“Vengeance on a dumb brute!” cried Starbuck. Then Ahab, the little lower layer: “All visible objects, man, are but as pasteboard masks. If man will strike, strike through the mask! To me, the white whale is that wall, shoved near to me. He tasks me; he heaps me; I see in him outrageous strength, with an inscrutable malice sinewing it. Talk not to me of blasphemy; I’d strike the sun if it insulted me.”

The measure came. He ranged mates and harpooneers at the capstan; made the mates cup-bearers to his pagan kinsmen; brimmed the harpoon sockets with fiery waters. “Drink and swear, ye men that man the deathful whaleboat’s bow—Death to Moby Dick! God hunt us all, if we do not hunt Moby Dick to his death!” The steel goblets hissed dry; the crew dispersed; and Ahab retired to his cabin.


## Chapter 3

By the stern windows Ahab sat alone, gazing out. “I leave a white and turbid wake; pale waters, paler cheeks. The diver sun goes down; my soul mounts up! Is, then, the crown too heavy that I wear? this Iron Crown of Lombardy. ’Tis iron—that I know—not gold. ’Tis split, too—the jagged edge galls me so, my brain seems to beat against the solid metal.”

“All loveliness is anguish to me; gifted with the high perception, I lack the low, enjoying power; damned most malignantly—damned in the midst of Paradise!” He waves from the window. “They think me mad—Starbuck does; but I’m demoniac, I am madness maddened! The prophecy was that I should be dismembered; and—aye! I lost this leg. I now prophesy that I will dismember my dismemberer.”

He flung his challenge upward. “Come forth! Swerve me? ye cannot swerve me, else ye swerve yourselves! The path to my fixed purpose is laid with iron rails, whereon my soul is grooved to run. Over unsounded gorges, through the rifled hearts of mountains, under torrents’ beds, unerringly I rush! Naught’s an obstacle, naught’s an angle to the iron way!”


## Chapter 4

By the mainmast, Starbuck leaned. “My soul is more than matched; she’s overmanned; and by a madman! He drilled deep down and blasted all my reason out of me. I see his impious end, yet feel that I must help him to it. Will I, nill I, the ineffable thing has tied me to him; tows me with a cable I have no knife to cut. Yet is there hope. The hated whale has the round watery world; God may wedge aside his heaven-insulting purpose.”

A burst of revelry from the forecastle. “Hark the infernal orgies! Foremost the gay bow shoots, but only to drag dark Ahab after it, brooding over the dead water of the wake. Peace! set the watch! Oh, life—now do I feel the latent horror in thee; yet it is not me! With what soft human feeling is left, I’ll try to fight ye, ye grim, phantom futures!”


## Chapter 5

Fore-top; Stubb, mending a brace. “Ha! ha! ha!—I’ve been thinking over it, and that ha, ha’s the final consequence. Why so? Because a laugh’s the wisest, easiest answer to all that’s queer; and come what will, one comfort’s left—it’s all predestinated. I heard not all his talk with Starbuck; but be sure the old Mogul has fixed him, too. Well, Stubb—wise Stubb—what of it? Here’s a carcase. Be it what it will, I’ll go to it laughing. Fa, la! lirra, skirra!”

He trolls a stave:

“We’ll drink to-night with hearts as light,
To love, as gay and fleeting
As bubbles that swim, on the beaker’s brim,
And break on the lips while meeting.”

“Who calls? Mr. Starbuck? Aye, aye, sir—just through with this job—coming.”


## Chapter 6

Midnight; the foresail rises and discovers the watch, lounging, leaning, singing in chorus:

“Farewell and adieu to you, Spanish ladies!
Farewell and adieu to you, ladies of Spain!”

A Nantucket sailor calls for a tonic song, and all follow:

“Our captain stood upon the deck,
A spy-glass in his hand,
… So, be cheery, my lads! may your hearts never fail!
While the bold harpooner is striking the whale!”

“Eight bells there, forward!” pipes the mate from aft. The bell-boy, Pip—black, slight, and sleepy—is bullied up. Dutch and French, Maltese and Sicilian, Iceland and Long-Island, Azore and China sailors—each flings his national quip or caper. “Pip! little Pip! hurrah with your tambourine!” “Don’t know where it is.” “Beat thy belly, then, and wag thy ears.” The half of them dance; some drift below; some coil among rigging and oaths. “Go it, Pip! Bang it, bell-boy! Make fire-flies!” “There goes another jingler dropped off; I pound it so,” says Pip.

Tashtego, quiet, smokes. The old Manx sailor wonders what they dance over—green navies, green-skulled crews. “Belike the whole world’s a ball; dance on, lads, you’re young; I was once.” Clusters form; the sky darkens; a wind rises. “By Brahma!” cries a Lascar; “the sky-born Ganges turns to wind!” The Maltese shakes his tasselled cap: “Now would all the waves were women; I’d drown and chassee forever!” The Sicilian reclines, paganly enumerating the grazings of the dance. A Tahitian starts from his mat, hailing holy nakedness and Pirohitee’s torrents—then, “The blast! the blast! Up, spine, and meet it!”

“Stand by for reefing, hearties!” says a Portuguese. “Crack, old ship!” says the Dane. “Our captain has his birthmark,” mutters the Manxman; “and there’s another in the sky—lurid-like, all else pitch black.” “What of that?” growls Daggoo; “who’s afraid of black’s afraid of me!” A Spanish sailor, touchy, pricks him; knives glitter; “A row! a row!” Tashtego puffs: “Gods and men—both brawlers! Humph!”

“Hands by the halyards! in top-gallant sails! Stand by to reef topsails!” comes the cry from the quarter-deck. They scatter. Pip cowers under the windlass: “Crish, crash! there goes the jib-stay! Blang-whang! Duck lower, Pip—here comes the royal yard! Worse than the whirled woods the last day of the year! Fine prospects to ’em; they’re on the road to heaven. Hold on hard! Jimmini, what a squall! But those chaps there are worse yet—they are your white squalls, they. White squalls? white whale, shirr! shirr! Here have I heard all their chat, and the white whale—but spoken of once! and only this evening that anaconda of an old man swore ’em in to hunt him!” He trembles and prays: “Oh, thou big white God aloft there somewhere in yon darkness, have mercy on this small black boy down here; preserve him from all men that have no bowels to feel fear!”


## Chapter 7

I, Ishmael, was one of that crew; my shouts went up with the rest; my oath was welded with theirs; and stronger I shouted, and more I hammered and clinched my oath, because of the dread in my soul. A wild, mystical, sympathetical feeling was in me; Ahab’s quenchless feud seemed mine. With greedy ears I learned the history of that murderous monster against whom we had sworn.

For some time past, though at intervals only, the unaccompanied, secluded White Whale had haunted those uncivilized seas mostly frequented by Sperm Whale fishermen. Yet not all knew of his existence; few had knowingly seen him; fewer still had actually given him battle. The causes were many: the wide sprinkling of cruisers over the watery circumference; solitary latitudes; year-long voyages without a news-telling sail; irregular sailings from home; and the fishery’s inordinate distances—all these long obstructed the spreading of specific tidings concerning Moby Dick. Vessels at times reported a Sperm Whale of uncommon magnitude and malignity which, after great mischief, escaped; to some it was not unfair to presume the whale was Moby Dick. But of late the fishery had been marked by ferocity and cunning in many monsters; and those who ignorantly gave him battle ascribed their terrors to the general perils of the fishery more than to any individual cause. So, too, had the encounter between Ahab and the whale been popularly regarded.

At first, those who, hearing of the White Whale, by chance caught sight of him, lowered as boldly as for any whale; but calamities—no mere sprains or amputations, but fatal to the last degree—ensued; repeated repulses piled his terrors upon him, till they shook the fortitude of many brave hunters. Wild rumors, too, grew like fungi from the smitten trunk of fact. Sea-life breeds them; whalemen—superstitious beyond other sailors, and most conversant with appalling marvels—nourish them. Ever gathering volume in their world-circling transit, hints and half-formed foetal suggestions of supernatural agency invested Moby Dick with terrors unborrowed from the visible; so that few, who had at least heard of him by rumor, were willing to encounter his jaw.

Practical influences worked likewise. The Sperm Whale’s pre-eminent tremendousness, as fearfully distinct from other leviathans, has never died out among whalemen. Right-whalemen, brave enough in northern warfare, shrank from this apparition of the South. Naturalists of repute have helped awe along—Olassen and Povelson thirsting him for human blood; even Cuvier saying all fish are “struck with the most lively terrors” at his sight. The fishery’s realities amend such reports; but in certain vicissitudes the old belief is revived in the hunters’ minds.

Thus overawed, men recalled the early days when it was hard to induce practised Right-whalemen to embark against the Sperm Whale; to attempt it, they protested, was to be torn into a quick eternity. Nevertheless, some there were who would give chase; and a greater number who, hearing vaguely, without specific calamities or superstitions, would not flee if battle were offered.

One conceit linked at last with the White Whale in credulous minds was that Moby Dick was ubiquitous; encountered in opposite latitudes at the same instant. Faint plausibility there was: the secrets of currents are hidden; the hidden ways of the Sperm Whale beneath the surface remain, in great part, unaccountable; after sounding to great depths, his mystic modes of transporting himself to distant points are not known. Scoresby records whales taken far north in the Pacific with Greenland irons in them; the interval between assaults sometimes few days; hence some inferred the Nor’ West Passage was never a problem to the whale. Such prodigies as Strello’s wreck-lake or Arethusa’s subterranean flight are, in the whalemen’s world, almost matched by realities. Forced into familiarity with these, and knowing the White Whale had escaped after repeated intrepid assaults, men went further: declaring him not only ubiquitous but immortal (for immortality is but ubiquity in time); that though groves of spears were planted in his flanks, he would still swim; that if ever he spouted thick blood, it were a ghastly deception, and his unsullied jet would again be seen hundreds of leagues away.

But strip him of superstitions, there was enough in his earthly make and incontestable character to strike imagination. It was not so much his bulk as his peculiar snow-white wrinkled forehead, and high pyramidical white hump—tokens whereby, in limitless seas, he revealed his identity afar. The rest of him was streaked, spotted, marbled with that shrouded hue; gliding at noon through a dark blue sea, he left a milky-way wake of creamy foam spangled with golden gleamings. Nor his hue, nor his deformed lower jaw, so much invested him with terror, as his unexampled intelligent malignity; and, beyond all, those treacherous retreats when, feigning flight, he suddenly turned, bearing down, staving boats, or driving them in consternation back to their ship.

Fatalities had attended his chase. Though disasters are not uncommon, yet, in his case, such seemed the infernal aforethought that every death was not wholly laid to an unintelligent agent. Judge, then, to what pitches of fury desperate hunters were impelled, when, amid chips of chewed boats and sinking limbs of torn comrades, they swam out of the white curds of his direful wrath into serene, exasperating sunlight that smiled on as at a bridal.

Then one captain—Ahab—seizing a line-knife from his broken prow, dashed at the whale, as a duellist at his foe, blindly seeking with a six-inch blade to reach the fathom-deep life. Sweeping his sickle-shaped lower jaw, Moby Dick reaped away Ahab’s leg as a mower a blade of grass. Small reason to doubt that ever since, Ahab cherished a wild vindictiveness; and in his frantic morbidness he came to identify with the whale not only all bodily woes, but all intellectual and spiritual exasperations. The White Whale swam before him as the monomaniac incarnation of those malicious agencies which deep men feel eating in them. All that most maddens and torments; all truth with malice in it; all that cracks the sinews and cakes the brain; all evil, to crazy Ahab, were personified and made practically assailable in Moby Dick. He piled upon that white hump the sum of his race’s rage from Adam down; then, as if his chest were a mortar, burst his hot heart’s shell upon it.

His monomania did not, perhaps, rise at the instant of dismemberment. Then he but loosed a sudden corporal animosity; felt the bodily laceration and nothing more. But forced homeward, for long months Ahab and anguish lay together in one hammock, rounding in mid-winter the howling Patagonian Cape; then his torn body and gashed soul bled into one and made him mad. At intervals in the passage he was a raving lunatic; they laced him fast as he sailed, raving in his hammock. In more sufferable latitudes, under mild stun’sails, he seemed restored; issued calm orders; mates thanked God the madness had gone; yet in his hidden self he raved on. Human madness is oft a cunning, feline thing; fleeing, it but transmutes into subtler form. Ahab’s full lunacy did not subside; it deepened and contracted, like the unabated Hudson narrowing unfathomably through the Highlands. None of his great natural intellect perished; his living agent became a living instrument; his special lunacy stormed his general sanity and turned its cannon on its own mad mark; so that, far from losing strength, to that one end he possessed a thousand-fold more potency than he had sanely brought to bear on any reasonable object.

He knew, in his heart, something of this: “All my means are sane, my motive and my object mad.” He dissembled to mankind, and in some sort still did; but that dissembling was subject to his perceptibility, not his will. So well did he succeed that, when he stepped ashore on his ivory leg, no Nantucketer thought him other than naturally grieved; his sea-delirium ascribed to grief; his moodiness to the quick of that casualty. Far from distrusting his fitness, the prudent isle deemed him, for those very reasons, edged for the bloody hunt. Gnawed within and scorched without by the fangs of an incurable idea, such an one seemed the very man to dart iron at the most appalling of brutes, or, failing that, to cheer and howl on his underlings to the attack. Be it as it may, certain it is, that with his mad secret bolted within, Ahab purposely sailed with the one, only, all-engrossing object of hunting the White Whale. Had one shore-friend half dreamed what lurked in him, they had wrenched the ship from such a fiendish man. They were bent on profits; he on an audacious, immitigable, supernatural revenge.

Here, then, was this grey-headed, ungodly old man, chasing with curses a Job’s whale round the world, at the head of a crew of mongrel renegades, castaways, and cannibals—morally enfeebled by Starbuck’s unaided virtue, Stubb’s invulnerable jollity, and Flask’s mediocrity. Such a crew, so officered, seemed specially picked by some infernal fatality to help him to his revenge. How they so aboundingly responded—by what evil magic their souls were possessed, till at times his hate seemed theirs; what the White Whale was to them, or how, in some dim, unsuspected way, he might have seemed to their unconscious understandings the gliding demon of the seas of life—all this were to dive deeper than Ishmael can go. The subterranean miner that works in us all—who can tell whither his shaft leads by the shifting, muffled sound of his pick? Who does not feel the irresistible arm drag? What skiff in tow of a seventy-four can stand still? For one, I gave myself up to the abandonment of the time and tide; yet, while all a-rush to encounter the whale, could see naught in that brute but the deadliest ill.
