“Here, now, are two great whales, laying their heads together; let us join them, and lay together our own.” The Sperm and the Right—folio leviathans, the only whales regularly hunted—present, in their heads, extremes. The Sperm’s shows a certain mathematical symmetry and pervading dignity—grey-headed at the summit, tokening age and experience.

Let us now note what is least dissimilar—eye and ear. Far back and low near the jaw’s angle, a lashless eye, colt-small for such magnitude. From its sideway set, the whale can never see an object exactly ahead or astern. “If your bitterest foe were walking straight towards you, with dagger uplifted in broad day, you would not be able to see him, any more than if he were stealing upon you from behind.” In a word, “you would have two backs, so to speak; but, at the same time, also, two fronts (side fronts): for what is it that makes the front of a man—what, indeed, but his eyes?” Effectually divided by many cubic feet of head, each eye imparts a wholly separate picture—profound darkness and nothingness all between. This peculiarity is to be borne in mind in the fishery—and remembered in scenes to come.

A curious and most puzzling question arises here. So long as a man’s eyes are open in the light, seeing is involuntary; yet, though he may take in an undiscriminating sweep, he cannot attentively examine two things at the same instant; separate them and surround each with darkness, and, in bringing your mind to bear on one, “the other will be utterly excluded from your contemporary consciousness.” How is it, then, with the whale? True, both his eyes, in themselves, must simultaneously act; but is his brain so much more comprehensive and combining than man’s, that, at the same moment of time, he can attentively examine two distinct prospects, one on one side of him, and the other in an exactly opposite direction? If he can, then is it as marvellous as “if a man were able simultaneously to go through the demonstrations of two distinct problems in Euclid.”

It may be but an idle whim, but it has always seemed to me that the extraordinary vacillations of movement displayed by some whales when beset by three or four boats—the timidity and liability to queer frights, so common to such whales—all this indirectly proceeds from the helpless perplexity of volition in which their divided and diametrically opposite powers of vision must involve them.

The ear is as curious: no external leaf; a minute hole a quill can hardly enter, lodged a little behind the eye. In the Sperm an external opening; in the Right, evenly covered by a membrane—imperceptible without. Is it not curious, that so vast a being should see the world through so small an eye, and hear the thunder through an ear smaller than a hare’s? But if his eyes were broad as the lens of Herschel’s great telescope, and his ears capacious as the porches of cathedrals, would that make him any longer of sight, or sharper of hearing? Not at all.—Why then do you try to “enlarge” your mind? Subtilize it.

Cant over the sperm whale’s head and peer down the mouth—its really beautiful and chaste-looking floor to ceiling papered with a glistening white membrane, glossy as bridal satins. Then behold the portentous lower jaw, long narrow lid of an immense snuff-box; pried overhead, its teeth make a terrific portcullis; and, fathoms down, some sulky whale floats with his prodigious jaw hanging straight for all the world like a ship’s jib-boom. This whale is not dead; he is only dispirited; out of sorts, perhaps; hypochondriac; and so supine, that the hinges of his jaw have relaxed, leaving him there in that ungainly sort of plight, a reproach to all his tribe, who must, no doubt, imprecate lock-jaws upon him.

In most cases this lower jaw—being easily unhinged by a practised hand—is hoisted on deck to extract the ivory teeth. With a long, weary hoist it is dragged aboard; and, when the time comes, the harpooneers—accomplished dentists—lance the gums, lash the jaw to ringbolts, and with tackle from aloft drag out the teeth, as Michigan oxen stump old oaks. There are generally forty-two; in old whales much worn, but undecayed. The jaw is afterwards sawn into slabs, and piled away like joists.
