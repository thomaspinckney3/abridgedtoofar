Queequeg was a native of Rokovoko, an island far away to the West and South. It is not down in any map; true places never are.

When a new-hatched savage running wild about his native woodlands in a grass clout, followed by the nibbling goats; even then, in Queequeg’s ambitious soul, lurked a strong desire to see something more of Christendom than a specimen whaler or two. His father was a High Chief, a King; his uncle a High Priest; and on the maternal side he boasted aunts who were the wives of unconquerable warriors. There was excellent blood in his veins—royal stuff; though sadly vitiated, I fear, by the cannibal propensity he nourished in his untutored youth.

A Sag Harbor ship visited his father’s bay. Denied a passage, he vowed a vow; alone in his canoe he hid in a mangrove-thicketed strait the ship must pass; as she glided by he darted out, gained her side; with one backward dash capsized his canoe; climbed the chains; grappled a ring-bolt, swearing not to let go though hacked in pieces. Cutlass over his naked wrists availed not; the Captain relented; but this sea Prince of Wales never saw the cabin—put among sailors, they made a whaleman of him. But like Czar Peter content to toil in the shipyards of foreign cities, Queequeg disdained no seeming ignominy, if thereby he might happily gain the power of enlightening his untutored countrymen. For at bottom—so he told me—he was actuated by a profound desire to learn among the Christians, the arts whereby to make his people still happier than they were; and more than that, still better than they were. But, alas! the practices of whalemen soon convinced him that even Christians could be both miserable and wicked; infinitely more so, than all his father’s heathens. Arrived at last in old Sag Harbor; and then going on to Nantucket, and seeing how they spent their wages, poor Queequeg gave it up for lost. Thought he, it’s a wicked world in all meridians; I’ll die a pagan.

Thus an old idolator at heart, he wore Christian clothes and tried to talk their gibberish. He feared Christianity, or rather Christians, had unfitted him for ascending the pure and undefiled throne of thirty pagan Kings; by and by he would return—as soon as he felt himself baptized again; for the nonce, sail about and sow his wild oats in four oceans. They had made a harpooneer of him; that barbed iron was in lieu of a sceptre now.

His immediate purpose: to go to sea again in his old vocation. Whaling was mine too; Nantucket my port. He resolved to accompany me—same vessel, watch, boat, and mess; to share my every hap; with both my hands in his, boldly dip into the Potluck of both worlds. I joyously assented; his tale ended with his pipe’s last dying puff; he embraced me, pressed his forehead to mine; blowing out the light, we rolled from each other this way and that, and very soon were sleeping.
