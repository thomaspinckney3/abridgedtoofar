Stubb and Flask, mounted on the forecastle bulwarks, passed additional lashings over the hanging anchors. Flask pressed him: “Didn’t you once say any ship Ahab sails should pay extra insurance, as though loaded with powder aft and lucifers forward?” “Well, suppose I did? I’ve part changed my flesh—why not my mind? And even if we are powder aft and lucifers forward, how could they get afire in this drenching spray? Why, you couldn’t get afire now. Shake yourself; you’re Aquarius, or the water-bearer, Flask; might fill pitchers at your coat collar. Besides, for extra risks the companies have extra guarantees. Here are hydrants. And what’s the mighty difference between holding a mast’s lightning-rod in a storm, and standing by a mast that hasn’t any? Don’t you see, you timber-head, that no harm can come to the holder of the rod, unless the mast is first struck? Not one ship in a hundred carries rods; we were in no more danger than ten thousand crews. Would you have every man go about with a small lightning-rod up his hat, like a militia officer’s feather? Why don’t ye be sensible, Flask? it’s easy to be sensible; why don’t ye, then? any man with half an eye can be sensible.”

“Seems we lash these anchors as if never to be used again,” said Stubb. “Tying them is like tying a man’s hands; what big generous hands they are—iron fists! I wonder if the world is anchored anywhere; if she is, she swings with an uncommon long cable. Next to touching land, lighting on deck is the most satisfactory. Wring out my jacket skirts, will ye? They laugh at long-togs; but a long-tailed coat in storms carries off the water, d’ye see. Same with cocked hats: cocks form gable-end eave-troughs. No more monkey-jackets and tarpaulins for me; I’ll mount a swallow-tail and drive down a beaver.” A squall took him: “Halloa! whew! there goes my tarpaulin overboard; Lord, Lord, that the winds that come from heaven should be so unmannerly! This is a nasty night, lad.”
