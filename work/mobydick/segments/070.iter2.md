It was a Saturday night, and such a Sabbath as followed! Ex officio professors of Sabbath breaking are all whalemen. The ivory Pequod seemed a shamble; every sailor a butcher. You would have thought we were offering up ten thousand red oxen to the sea gods. The enormous cutting tackles—among other ponderous things comprising a cluster of blocks generally painted green, and which no single man can possibly lift—this vast bunch of grapes was swayed up to the main-top and lashed to the lower mast-head. Their hawser-like fall led to the windlass; the lower block swung over the whale, and the blubber-hook was bent. From stages, Starbuck and Stubb cut a hole above the side-fin, scarved a semicircle, and set the hook. Then, to a wild chorus, all hands heaved. The ship careened; every bolt in her starts like the nail-heads of an old house in frosty weather; she trembles, quivers, and nods her frighted mast-heads to the sky. A swift snap; a great swash; and the triumphant tackle rose, dragging the semicircular end of the first strip.

As the orange’s rind spirals from the fruit, so the blubber peels in one uniform strip along the scarf; the blanket-piece rises till it grazes the main-top and sways blood-dripping as if let down from the sky—and every one present must dodge its swing, else it may box his ears and pitch him headlong overboard. A boarding-sword slices a low hole; the second tackle hooks; then, with a few lunging cuts, the swordsman severs it in twain—short lower part still fast, the long blanket-piece clear for lowering. While one tackle peels and hoists, the other slackens, and down goes the strip through the main hatch to the blubber-room, where nimble hands coil it like a great live mass of plaited serpents. So the work proceeds: tackles alternating; whale and windlass heaving; heavers singing; mates scarfing; ship straining—and all hands swearing occasionally, by way of assuaging the general friction.
