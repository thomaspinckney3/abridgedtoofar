That night in the mid-watch Ahab thrust his face from the scuttle, snuffed the air like a sagacious ship’s dog, and swore a whale was near. Soon that peculiar odor of the living sperm-whale was plain to all. After taking compass and dog-vane, and matching the wind to the wafted scent, he shifted the ship a trifle, shortened sail—an acute policy soon vindicated by daybreak’s sight: a long, oily sleek, lengthwise ahead, with pleated wrinkles like the polished marks of some swift tide-rip.

“Man the mast-heads! Call all hands!”

Daggoo’s clubbed handspikes thundered; sleepers seemed to exhale from the scuttle, clothes in hand. Ahab cast loose the life-line, and was swayed aloft; while yet but two-thirds up, peering between main-top-sail and top-gallant-sail, he cried like a gull, “There she blows!—there she blows! A hump like a snow-hill! It is Moby Dick!”

The men sprang rigging-ward. Ahab gained his final perch above the other look-outs; Tashtego, just beneath, said he too had seen. “Not the same instant; not the same—no, the doubloon is mine, Fate reserved the doubloon for me. _I_ only; none of ye could have raised the White Whale first. There she blows!—there she blows!—there she blows! There again!—there again!” Orders flew: “He’s going to sound! In stunsails! Down top-gallant-sails! Stand by three boats. Mr. Starbuck, stay on board and keep the ship! Helm there! Luff!—steady!”

Soon all the boats but Starbuck’s were dropped; all the boat-sails set; paddles plied; Ahab headed the onset. Like noiseless nautilus shells, their light prows sped through the sea; but only slowly they neared the foe. The ocean smoothed to a noon-meadow; the White Whale glided on, his dazzling hump ever and anon revealed, his broad, milky forehead casting a glistening white shadow before him, musical ripples playing to that shade; a shattered lance-pole stuck like a flag-staff from his back. A gentle joyousness—a mighty mildness of repose in swiftness—invested him. Not the white bull Jupiter swimming away with ravished Europa clinging to his graceful horns; his lovely, leering eyes sideways intent upon the maid; with smooth bewitching fleetness, rippling straight for the nuptial bower in Crete; not Jove, not that great majesty Supreme! did surpass the glorified White Whale as he so divinely swam. On each soft side—coincident with the parted swell that once leaving him then flowed so wide away—on each bright side, the whale shed off enticings. No wonder some, transported by such serenity, had assailed it; and fatally found that quietude but the vesture of tornadoes. Yet calm, enticing calm, oh, whale! thou glidest on, no matter how many in that same way thou may’st have bejuggled and destroyed before.

Without showing the full terrors of his jaw, he rose, arched high—like Virginia’s Natural Bridge—bannered flukes waving, and sounded. The three boats floated stilly, oars apeak, sheets adrift, awaiting his reappearance. “The birds!—the birds!” cried Tashtego, as the white fowl came fluttering to Ahab’s boat, wheeling and crying. Ahab peered down; saw a white living spot uprising, magnifying as it rose—two long, crooked rows of glistening teeth, scrolled jaw, his vast bulk half-blending with the blue. The glittering mouth yawned beneath the boat like an open-doored marble tomb. With a sidelong sweep of the steering oar Ahab whirled clear; then sprang to the bows, seized Perth’s harpoon, and bade his men stand by to stern.

Perceiving the stratagem, Moby Dick, with malicious intelligence, shot his pleated head lengthwise beneath the boat. Lying obliquely on his back, shark-like, he slowly, feelingly took the bows between his jaws; the scrolled lower jaw curled into the air, a tooth caught in a row-lock; the bluish pearl-white within was within six inches of Ahab’s head. The White Whale shook the slight cedar as a mildly cruel cat her mouse; Fedallah, unastonished, crossed his arms; the tiger-yellow crew tumbled aft. The gunwales sprang; then both jaws, like enormous shears, slid aft and bit the craft in twain; the halves drifted, the crew clung, striving to lash oars across.

Ahab, first to note the crafty upraising of the head, had leaned to push the boat out of the bite; it tilted, shook him off; he fell flat upon the sea. Ripplingly withdrawing from his prey, Moby Dick thrust his oblong white head up and down—pitchpoling—dazzling swells shivering to spray upon his brow. So, in a gale, the but half baffled Channel billows only recoil from the base of the Eddystone, triumphantly to overleap its summit with their scud. Then, resuming his horizontal attitude, he swam round and round the wrecked crew, churning the water in contracting circles. Ahab, half-smothered in the foam of the whale’s tail, too much of a cripple to swim yet afloat, bobbed like a tossed bubble. The other boats hovered, fearing to strike lest they doom the jeopardized castaways.

All this the ship had descried. Squaring her yards, she bore down; the old man, in the water, hailed her—“Sail on the”—a breaking sea whelmed him. Rising on a crest: “Sail on the whale!—Drive him off!” The Pequod broke the charmed circle; the whale sullenly swam off; the boats rescued.

Dragged into Stubb’s boat, brine crusting his wrinkles, Ahab’s long tension cracked; he lay as trodden by herds. Far inland, nameless wails came from him. But such intensity the sooner spent itself. In an instant’s compass, great hearts sometimes condense to one deep pang. “The harpoon,” said Ahab, half way rising, and draggingly leaning on one bended arm—“is it safe?” “Aye, sir; it was not darted.” “Any missing men?” “Five oars; five men.” “That’s good.—Help me. So—I see him! there! leeward still. Hands off! The eternal sap runs up in Ahab’s bones again! Set the sail; out oars; the helm!”

Double-banked now, they strained; but the whale seemed treble-banked in every fin. So the boats made for the ship; were swayed up; canvas stacked high, stun-sails outspread like an albatross’s double-jointed wings; the Pequod bore down in his wake. At methodic intervals the glittering spout was cried; at each “just gone down” Ahab took the time; when the hour expired: “Whose is the doubloon now? D’ye see him?”—then hoisted to his perch. The day wore on—Ahab now aloft and motionless; anon pacing, binnacle-watch in hand.

Passing, at every turn, his own wrecked boat dropped on the quarter-deck, he paused. Stubb, brave and mechanical, ventured a jest: “The thistle the ass refused; it pricked his mouth too keenly, sir; ha! ha!” “What soulless thing is this that laughs before a wreck? Man, man! did I not know thee brave as fearless fire (and as mechanical) I could swear thou wert a poltroon. Groan nor laugh should be heard before a wreck.” “’Tis a solemn sight,” said Starbuck, “an omen, and an ill one.” “Omen?—the dictionary! If the gods think to speak outright to man, they will honorably speak outright; not shake their heads, and give an old wives’ darkling hint.—Begone! Ye two are the opposite poles of one thing; Starbuck is Stubb reversed, and Stubb is Starbuck; and ye two are all mankind; and Ahab stands alone among the millions of the peopled earth, nor gods nor men his neighbors! Cold, cold—I shiver!—How now? Aloft there! D’ye see him? Sing out for every spout, though he spout ten times a second!”

The day was nearly done; only the hem of his golden robe was rustling. “Can’t see the spout now, sir;—too dark,” came a voice from the air. “How heading when last seen?”—“Straight to leeward.”—“Good! he will travel slower now ’tis night. Down royals and top-gallant stun-sails. We must not run over him before morning. Helm there! keep her full before the wind!—Aloft! come down!—Mr. Stubb, send a fresh hand to the fore-mast head, and see it manned till morning.” Then to the doubloon: “Men, this gold is mine, for I earned it; but I shall let it abide here till the White Whale is dead; and then, whosoever of ye first raises him, upon the day he shall be killed, this gold is that man’s; and if on that day I shall again raise him, then, ten times its sum shall be divided among all of ye! Away now!—the deck is thine, sir!” Slouched in the scuttle, he stood till dawn, rousing at intervals to see how the night wore on.
