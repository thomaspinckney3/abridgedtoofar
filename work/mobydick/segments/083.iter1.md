If physiognomically the Sperm Whale be a Sphinx, to the phrenologist his brain is that circle impossible to square. Unhinge the jaw; the skull’s side shows a moderately inclined plane; in life it is angularly filled up and almost squared by the enormous superincumbent junk and sperm. At the high end the skull forms a crater to bed part of that mass; beneath its long floor—in another cavity seldom exceeding ten inches—reposes the mere handful of brain, at least twenty feet from the apparent forehead, hidden behind vast outworks like the innermost citadel within the amplified fortifications of Quebec. So like a choice casket is it secreted in him, that I have known some whalemen who peremptorily deny that the Sperm Whale has any other brain than that palpable semblance of one formed by the cubic-yards of his sperm magazine. Lying in strange folds, courses, and convolutions, to their apprehensions, it seems more in keeping with the idea of his general might to regard that mystic part of him as the seat of his intelligence. It is plain, then, that phrenologically the head, in the creature’s living intact state, is an entire delusion; the whale, like all things that are mighty, wears a false brow to the common world.

If you unload his skull of its spermy heaps and take a rear view of the high end, you will be struck with its resemblance to a human skull reversed; scaled to man’s size, you might confound it on a plate; remarking summit-depressions, you’d say—no self-esteem, no veneration—and, with those negations and his prodigious bulk and power, conceive the truest notion of exalted potency.

Consider the spine: vertebræ like a strung necklace of dwarfed skulls—a German conceit of undeveloped skulls. Much of a man’s character is betokened in his backbone. “I would rather feel your spine than your skull, whoever you are.” A thin joist of a spine never yet upheld a full and noble soul. I rejoice in my spine, as in the firm audacious staff of that flag which I fling half out to the world. Apply this spinal branch of phrenology to the Sperm Whale: the cranial cavity is continuous with the first vertebra; the spinal canal, at its origin, is of nearly equal girth with the brain for many feet; it is filled with the same strangely fibrous substance, directly communicating with the brain. Thus the comparative smallness of brain proper is more than compensated by the magnitude of the spinal cord. Assume, then, a spinal phrenology: the august hump rises over one of the larger vertebræ, its outer convex mould. From that relative site, call it the organ of firmness or indomitableness. And that he is indomitable, you will yet have reason to know.
