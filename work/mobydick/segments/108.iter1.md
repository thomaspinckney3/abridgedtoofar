Since Leviathan comes floundering down from the head-waters of the Eternities, inquire we whether, in his generations, he has not degenerated. Upon investigation we find the whales of to-day superior in magnitude to those of the Tertiary. Of all the pre-adamite yet exhumed—the Alabama monster—was less than seventy feet in skeleton; whereas the tape gives seventy-two for a large modern skeleton, and whalemen swear to Sperm Whales near a hundred feet.

“But since Adam’s time?” Pliny and the ancients tell of whales embracing acres, Aldrovandus of eight hundred feet—Rope Walks and Thames Tunnels! Will any whaleman believe it? No. And if ever I go where Pliny is, I, a whaleman (more than he was), will make bold to tell him so. For Egyptian mummies do not outmeasure modern Kentuckians; and the oldest cattle carvings prove Pharaoh’s kine not exceeding Smithfield’s prize beeves: in the face of all this, I will not admit that of all animals the whale alone has degenerated.

Another inquiry remains: the moot point is, whether Leviathan can long endure so wide a chase, and so remorseless a havoc; whether he must not at last be exterminated from the waters, and the last whale, like the last man, smoke his last pipe, and then himself evaporate in the final puff. Comparing the herds of whales with the herds of buffalo, which, not forty years ago, overspread by tens of thousands the prairies, and shook their iron manes and scowled with their thunder-clotted brows upon the sites of populous river-capitals, where now the polite broker sells you land at a dollar an inch—in such a comparison an irresistible argument would seem furnished. But you must look at this matter in every light. Forty men in one ship, hunting Sperm Whales for forty-eight months, think they have done extremely well, and thank God, if they carry home the oil of forty fish; while the moccasined hunters of the old West, the same number for the same months, mounted on horse, not in ships, would have slain not forty, but forty thousand buffalo. Moreover, for safety they now swim in immense caravans; the scattered pods are aggregated into vast but widely separated armies. And the so-called whale-bone whales, driven from promontory to cape, can at last resort to Polar citadels; diving under the ultimate glassy barriers, they come up among icy floes, and, in a charmed circle of everlasting December, bid defiance to pursuit.

But as perhaps fifty of these whale-bone whales are harpooned for one cachalot, some conclude this havoc has seriously thinned their battalions. Yet though some 13,000 have been annually slain on the nor’ west coast by Americans alone, considerations render even this of little account. Natural as it is to doubt the populousness of the enormous, what shall we say to Harto, who tells us that at one hunting the King of Siam took 4,000 elephants; and though hunted for thousands of years, they still survive in great numbers. Much more may the great whale outlast all hunting, since his pasture is precisely twice as large as all Asia, both Americas, Europe, Africa, New Holland, and all the Isles combined.

Moreover, from the presumed great longevity of whales—their probably attaining a century and more—at any one time several distinct adult generations must be contemporary; and what that is, we may soon gain some idea of, by imagining all the grave-yards, cemeteries, and family vaults of creation yielding up the live bodies of all the men, women, and children who were alive seventy-five years ago; and adding this countless host to the present human population of the globe. Wherefore, for all these things, we account the whale immortal in his species, however perishable in his individuality. He swam the seas before the continents broke water; he once swam over the site of the Tuileries, and Windsor Castle, and the Kremlin. In Noah’s flood he despised Noah’s Ark; and if ever the world be flooded again, then the eternal whale will still survive, and, rearing upon the topmost crest of the equatorial flood, spout his frothed defiance to the skies.
