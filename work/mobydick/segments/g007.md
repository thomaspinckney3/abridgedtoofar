## Chapter 1

What the white whale was to Ahab has been hinted; what, at times, he was to me remains unsaid. It was the whiteness of the whale that above all things appalled me. How shall I render it? In many things whiteness refines and enhances; marbles, japonicas, pearls; banners and chargers and ermine and the alb of priests; white belts of wampum, the spotlessness of the divine in Persian flame and in visions of St. John: white robes, a great white throne, the Holy One white like wool. Yet for all these accumulated associations, there lurks an elusive something in the innermost idea of this hue that strikes more of panic to the soul than the redness of blood.

Divorced from kindly associations and coupled with what is terrible, whiteness heightens terror to the furthest bounds. Witness the white bear and the white shark—their ghastly whiteness imparting an abhorrent mildness to their dumb gloating. Not the heraldic tiger so staggers courage as the shrouded ghostliness of those pale marauders. The French, alluding to the white, silent stillness of death, call that shark Requin—from Requiem.

Bethink thee of the albatross: in its wondrous bodily whiteness chiefly lurks the spell; I remember the first I ever saw—regal, unspotted, hooked Roman bill; vast archangel wings as if to embrace some holy ark; its strange eyes peeped me to secrets that took hold of God. “Goney!” cried a sailor; but Coleridge had naught to do with that first awe. The truth was in the bird’s whiteness.

So, too, the White Steed of the Prairies—milk-white, small-headed, bluff-chested, an apparition imperial and archangelical—whose spiritual whiteness clothed him with divineness that enforced a nameless terror. And yet there are other whitenesses that lose strange glory and acquire a repelling chill: the Albino, invested in all-pervading pallor, more hideous than deformity; the White Squall; the White Hoods of Ghent masked in their snowy faction; the marble pallor of the dead, the shroud’s hue, and ghosts rising in a milk-white fog; and even Death, riding his pallid horse.

Name alone can summon a spectralness: White Tower; White Mountains; the White Sea; “the tall pale man” of the Hartz. Lima has taken the white veil—her woe frozen in rigid pallor that admits not the greenness of decay.

To the common apprehension this phenomenon is not prime in terror; yet often the very thing that appals is the whiteness. A mariner, called by night to view his ship sailing through a midnight sea of milky whiteness, feels a silent dread, as if shoals of combed white bears swam around him; the lead assures soundings, but heart and helm go down till blue water is under him again. The backwoodsman views with indifference a prairie sheeted with snow; but not so the sailor in Antarctic scenes, where boundless churchyards grin with icy monuments and splintered crosses.

“White-lead chapter,” sayest thou? “White flag hung out from a craven soul?” Tell me, then, why a strong colt, smelling only the musk of a buffalo robe, starts and snorts with bursting eyes though he never knew the goring bisons of Oregon. Here behold, even in a brute, the instinct of the demonism in the world. So to me the milky sea, the frost-festoons, the windrowed snows—are the shaken robe to the frightened colt.

But not yet the incantation is solved. Why should that which is emblem of the Christian’s Deity be also intensifier of the appalling? Is it that by its indefiniteness it shadows forth the heartless voids and immensities of the universe, stabbing us with annihilation at the sight of the milky way? Or that whiteness is not so much a colour as the visible absence of colour, and the concrete of all colours; hence the dumb blankness, full of meaning, in a wide landscape of snows—a colourless, all-colour of atheism from which we shrink? And when we remember the philosophers who tell us all earthly hues—sunset tinges, butterfly cheeks—are but subtile deceits laid on from without; that Nature paints like the harlot; and that the mystical cosmetic that produces every hue—Light itself—forever remains white; and that, operating unmediated, it would touch even roses with its own blank tinge—pondering all this, the palsied universe lies before us a leper; and like travellers in Lapland refusing coloured glasses, the infidel gazes himself blind at the monumental white shroud that wraps all the prospect round him. And of all these things the Albino whale was the symbol. Wonder ye then at the fiery hunt?

## Chapter 2

“Hist! did you hear that noise, Cabaco?” It was middle-watch; fair moonlight; the men, in a silent cordon from butt to scuttle-butt, passed the buckets. Only the flap of a sail and the hum of the keel.

“There again—under the hatches—a cough,” whispered Archy.

“Cough be damned! Pass along that return bucket.”

“It sounds like sleepers turning over—two or three.”

“Caramba! it’s the three soaked biscuits in your guts. Look to the bucket!”

“Grin away; but there is somebody in the after-hold not yet seen on deck; and I suspect our old Mogul knows it. I heard Stubb tell Flask—”

“Tish! the bucket!”

## Chapter 3

Follow Ahab to his cabin after the oath-bound night, and you would see him draw forth wrinkled rolls of yellow sea-charts, spread them on the screwed-down table, and, beneath a rocking pewter lamp, trace fresh courses across blank spaces. Piles of old log-books lay at his hand—seasons and places of former captures collated—and as he marked the charts, invisible pencils seemed to chase and furrow his wrinkled brow.

This was his nightly work. With all four oceans before him, he threaded a maze of currents and eddies toward the certainty of his monomaniac thought. To the unacquainted, such a quest might seem absurd: one solitary creature in unhooped oceans. Not so to Ahab, who knew the set of tides and the drift of the sperm whale’s food, the assured seasons and latitudes; from these he derived surmises approaching certainties. So assured is the whale’s periodicalness that some would chart his migrations like swallows; indeed, an official circular now divides the ocean into districts and months for recorded days and sightings.

Moreover, in passages between grounds, sperm whales swim in veins—mysterious ocean-lines, straight as surveyors’ parallels, yet a few miles broad—so that within that magic zone, at set seasons, they may with confidence be looked for. Hence Ahab could hope for encounters not only on separate feeding-grounds, but even as he crossed between them, placing and timing himself by art along likely veins.

Yet herds shift; solitaries shift; and Moby Dick’s known resorts were but ocean-inns, not abodes. The crowning datum was the Season-on-the-Line—years of periodic lingerings where most deadly encounters had occurred, where the waves were storied with his deeds, and where Ahab found his awful motive. But he would not rest his all upon one flattering fact; nor lull his vow’s sleeplessness with any postponement.

The Pequod sailed at the very beginning of that season; no doubling Cape Horn and reaching the equatorial Pacific in time. He must wait the ensuing year; and perhaps he chose thus, to spend the interval in miscellaneous hunt, should the White Whale turn up in Persian Gulf or Bengal Bay, or any sea haunted by his race. Monsoons or Trades might blow him into the zig-zag of the Pequod’s wake.

“Mad,” say you, to recognize a single whale again? No. Moby Dick’s snow-white brow and hump were unmistakable; “I have tallied him,” Ahab muttered; “his fins are bored and scalloped like a lost sheep’s ear.” Then his breathless mind would run its race till faintness came, and he would seek the deck’s air. Ah, God! what trances of torments endure they whom one revengeful desire consumes. He sleeps with clenched hands; wakes with bloody nails in his palms.

At times intolerable dreams resumed the day’s intense thoughts and whirled them; flames and fiends beckoned him to leap down the chasm within; then a wild cry, and Ahab burst from his room as from a burning bed. These were not weakness, but tokens of his vow’s intensity. The Ahab who went to his hammock—scheming, steadfast hunter—was not the agent that so drove him out. The eternal, living principle in him, dissociated in sleep, sought escape from the frantic thing that used it by day; the purpose, by sheer inveteracy, had assumed a sort of self-subsistent being. Thus, when what seemed Ahab rushed forth, that glaring spirit in his eyes was for the time a vacated thing, a somnambulistic ray of living light without an object—blankness in itself. God help thee, old man; thy thoughts have created a creature in thee; and he whose intense thinking makes him a Prometheus—upon that heart for ever feeds a vulture; that vulture the very creature he creates.

## Chapter 4

The foregoing matter requires familiar enlargement, to take away incredulity touching the natural verity of the main points. I shall not proceed methodically, but by citations practically known to me as a whaleman.

First: I have personally known three instances in which a harpooned whale escaped, and, after an interval—even three years—was again struck by the same hand and slain; when the two irons, each marked with the same private cypher, were taken from the body. In the three-year instance I myself was in the boat both times, and recognized a huge mole under the whale’s eye. Many other instances have I from veracious men.

Secondly: In the fishery there have been whales popularly cognisable at distant times and places, not solely for bodily peculiarity (for that the try-works soon end), but for their terrible prestige: Timor Tom, New Zealand Jack, Morquan King of Japan, Don Miguel of Chili—names as to Cetacean History what Marius and Sylla are to classics. Some of these, having wrought havoc, were gone in quest of, systematically hunted, chased, and killed by valiant captains, as Captain Butler hunted Annawon through Narragansett woods.

Most landsmen are ignorant of the plainest wonders of the world. Not one disaster in fifty finds record; yet on one Pacific voyage, among the ships we spoke, each had a death by a whale, some more than one; three had each lost a boat’s crew. Economize your lamps: not a gallon you burn but at least one drop of man’s blood was spilled for it.

People ashore admit a whale’s enormousness, but deny specific examples of his power without complimenting the narrator’s facetiousness. I will rest on independent testimony that the sperm whale can, with aforethought, stave in, utterly destroy, and sink a large ship—and has done it.

In 1820 the ship Essex, Captain Pollard, of Nantucket, lowered for a shoal of sperm whales. A very large one, escaping from the boats, bore directly down upon the ship; dashing his forehead against her hull, he so stove her in that in less than ten minutes she settled and fell over; not a plank survives. Owen Chace, the chief mate, wrote a plain narrative. “Every fact seemed to warrant me,” he says, “in concluding that it was anything but chance which directed his operations; he made two several attacks upon the ship, at a short interval, both ahead, thereby combining the speed of the two objects for the shock; to effect which, the exact manoeuvres which he made were necessary. His aspect was most horrible, indicative of resentment and fury.” Again, in the black night after quitting the wreck: “The dark ocean and swelling waters were nothing; … the dismal looking wreck, and the horrid aspect and revenge of the whale, wholly engrossed my reflections.”

In 1807 the ship Union, also of Nantucket, was totally lost off the Azores by a like onset. And Commodore J——, sceptical over a whaling-captains’ dinner in Oahu that any whale could make his stout sloop-of-war leak so much as a thimbleful, was presently met at sea by a portly sperm whale who fetched him such a thwack that, with all pumps going, he ran for the nearest port to heave down. I am not superstitious; but I consider the Commodore’s interview providential. The sperm whale will stand no nonsense.

Take Langsdorff: “An uncommon large whale, the body larger than the ship itself, lay almost at the surface; the ship, in full sail, was almost upon him; it was impossible to prevent striking. The monster, setting up its back, raised the ship three feet at least out of the water. The masts reeled, the sails fell; we thought we had struck a rock; but the creature sailed off solemnly.” Captain D’Wolf, New Englander—my own uncle—substantiates every word.

Lionel Wafer, Dampier’s chum, tells of a terrible shock at sea at four in the morning; guns leaped; men were shaken from hammocks; no ground found with the lead. He imputed it to an earthquake; but I should not wonder if an unseen whale vertically bumped the hull.

As for strength and malice: lines made fast to a running whale have towed a ship in a calm as a horse walks off with a cart; more than once a struck sperm whale, rallying, has pursued boats back to the ship, and then withstood lance-showers from the deck; and upon attack he often opens his mouth and retains it so for minutes, eloquent indication of his character.

Nor are modern marvels new. In the sixth century Procopius, a sober historian, records a great sea-monster captured in the Propontis after destroying vessels at intervals for more than fifty years. He does not name the species; but as he stove ships, and as sperm whales have in isolated instances entered Mediterranean waters by the Dardanelles, and as squid—his food—lurks in those bottoms, I am strongly inclined to think Procopius’s monster a sperm whale. Put these statements together, and, according to all human reasoning, you will perceive the reasonableness of the whole story of the White Whale, catastrophe and all.

## Chapter 5

Though consumed with the hot fire of his purpose, Ahab might be far too wedded by nature and long habit to a fiery whaleman’s ways wholly to abandon the collateral prosecution of the voyage. And if not that, there were motives more influential. It would be refining too much to say his vindictiveness towards the White Whale extended to all sperm whales, multiplying chances by multiplying slain; but other considerations certainly swayed him.

He must use tools; and of all moon-shadow tools, men are most apt to get out of order. His magnetism over Starbuck did not cover the complete spiritual man. Starbuck’s body and coerced will were Ahab’s while the magnet lay at his brain; yet in his soul he abhorred the quest and, could he, would disintegrate or frustrate it. It might be a long interval ere the White Whale was seen; during it Starbuck would relapse into open rebellion unless common prudential influences bore on him. The subtle insanity of Ahab was nowhere more manifest than in his superlative sense that for the present the hunt must be stripped of its imaginative impiety; the full terror kept withdrawn into background; for few men’s courage is proof against protracted meditation unrelieved by action. When they stood long night watches, his officers and men must have nearer things to think of than Moby Dick.

Nor was Ahab unmindful that strong-emotion disdain is evanescent; the permanent constitutional condition of the manufactured man is sordidness. Let the White Whale breed generous knight-errantry if it will; yet while they chase for love, they must sniff the savor of cash. Crusaders robbed by the way to their holy sepulchre; had they been held strictly to one romantic end, too many had turned in disgust. “I will not strip these men,” thought Ahab, “of all hope of cash; otherwise the same cash, mutinying in them, would soon cashier Ahab.”

He had, too, by prematurely revealing the private purpose of the Pequod’s voyage, laid himself open to the unanswerable charge of usurpation; with impunity the crew might refuse obedience, even wrest command. His only protection lay in predominating brain, heart, and hand, backed by heedful attention to every minute atmospheric influence affecting his men.

For all these reasons he saw he must still, in a good degree, continue true to the nominal purpose: observe usages, and even evince passionate interest in the general pursuit. His voice now often hailed the mast-heads; let no porpoise go unreported. Vigilance was not long without reward.

## Chapter 6

A cloudy, sultry afternoon: the men lounged, gazing into lead-coloured waters. Queequeg and I wove a sword-mat for our boat’s lashing. So subdued, so preluding was the scene that each silent sailor seemed resolved into his own invisible self. I, the attendant, passed the woof of marline between the warp with my hand for shuttle; Queequeg, standing sideways, slid his heavy oaken sword between the threads and, idly looking off, drove home every yarn. The dull intermittent sound of the sword ruled the ship and sea; it seemed the Loom of Time, and I the shuttle weaving at the Fates. The warp was necessity; my hand, free will; Queequeg’s easy, indifferent sword—chance. Necessity’s straight threads; free will plying between given lines; chance, though restrained, by turns ruling either, with the last featuring blow at events.

Then a long, musically wild cry dropped like a wing from the clouds. High in the cross-trees that mad Gay-Header, Tashtego, leaned forward, hand stretched like a wand. “There she blows! there! there! there! she blows! she blows!”

“Where-away?”

“On the lee-beam, about two miles! a school!”

Instant commotion. “There go flukes!” cried Tashtego, and the whales disappeared. “Time! time!” called Ahab; Dough-Boy ran, reported the minute. The ship kept away; cranes thrust out; line tubs fixed; mainyard backed; three boats swung like samphire baskets over cliffs; eager crews clung and poised. At that instant a sudden exclamation drew every eye from the whale. Dark Ahab stood surrounded by five dusky phantoms, fresh-formed out of air.
