A day or two passed; all was astir aboard the Pequod. Old sails were mended, new sails coming on board, coils of rigging piled; continual fetching and carrying. Captain Peleg seldom went ashore, roaring from his whalebone den; Bildad, list in hand, marked each arrival.

At the inns, word ran that chests must be on board by night; the ship did not sail for several days. Every one knows what a multitude of things—beds, sauce-pans, knives and forks, shovels and tongs, napkins, nut-crackers, and what not, are indispensable to the business of housekeeping. Whaling is three-years’ housekeeping upon the wide ocean; spare boats, spare spars, spare lines and harpoons—spare everythings, almost, but a spare Captain and duplicate ship.

Chief among the fetchers was Captain Bildad’s sister—Aunt Charity. Never did any woman better deserve her name, which was Charity—Aunt Charity, as everybody called her. And like a sister of charity did this charitable Aunt Charity bustle about hither and thither; now a jar of pickles; now quills for the mate’s log; a third time with a roll of flannel for the small of some rheumatic back; last of all, she came with a long oil-ladle in one hand and a still longer lance in the other.

Often, visiting the craft, I asked after Captain Ahab: “Getting better—expected aboard every day,” they said. If I had been downright honest with myself, I would have seen very plainly in my heart that I did but half fancy being committed this way to so long a voyage, without once laying my eyes on the man who was to be the absolute dictator of it; but when a man suspects any wrong, it sometimes happens that if he be already involved in the matter, he insensibly strives to cover up his suspicions even from himself.

At last it was given out the ship would certainly sail next day. So, at early dawn, Queequeg and I set forth.
