## Chapter 1

The phantoms—so they then seemed—flitted noiseless on the starboard side, casting loose the tackles of the captain’s spare boat. Tall and swart by its bows stood one with a single white tooth wickedly protruding from steel-like lips; a funereal Chinese jacket and wide black trowsers clothed him; but, strangest of all, a glistening white plaited turban crowned the ebonness—his own living hair coiled round and round. Less swart, his companions were tiger-yellow men of the Manillas—fellows whom some honest mariners hold for the devil’s spies afloat.

“All ready there, Fedallah?” cried Ahab.

“Ready,” hissed the white-turbaned old man.

“Lower away then; d’ye hear?—Lower away there, I say!”

With the sheaves whirring, three boats wallowed into the sea; the sailors, goat-like, leaped to them from the rolling hull. Hardly had they shot clear when a fourth keel rounded under the stern—Ahab’s—rowed by the five strangers. He stood erect, hailing, “Spread yourselves!” But, with eyes riveted upon Fedallah and his crew, the other boats stared.

“Never heed yonder yellow boys,” cried Flask, sweeping his great steering-oar. “There!—there again! There she blows right ahead, boys!”

“What think ye of those yellow boys, sir?” bawled Stubb towards Starbuck, whose face, flinty-set, never turned.

“Smuggled aboard,” came the grave quick whisper; “but never mind, Mr. Stubb—(seethe her, my lads!)—duty and profit, hand in hand!”

“Aye, aye; I thought as much,” soliloquized Stubb. “The White Whale’s at the bottom of it. Well then—give way, men! It ain’t the White Whale to-day!”

To soothe his uneasy crew he preached in that queer mingled fun-and-fury which was his: “Pull, pull, my fine hearts-alive; pull, my little ones!—the more the merrier. So—so—long and strong! The devil fetch ye, ye ragamuffins, ye’re asleep! Draw knives and pull with the blades between your teeth! That’s it—my steel-bits—start her, my silver-spoons!”

I set down Stubb’s exordium thus large, for it shows his religion of rowing: fiercest words salted with laughter; fury a mere spice to the joke; the idle indolent yawn of the man bewitching the crew to deadly work.

Meanwhile, ranging most to windward, Ahab’s boat kept foremost; a potent crew pulled him—those tiger-yellow creatures of his rose and fell like five trip-hammers; Fedallah, stripped to the waist, bent at the harpooneer oar; and Ahab, one arm thrown back like a fencer’s, steered as in a thousand lowerings ere the White Whale had torn him. All at once his outstretched arm signaled; the five oars peaked; boat and crew sat motionless. The whales had irregularly settled; from his closer ken, Ahab had seen it.

“Every man look out along his oars!” cried Starbuck. “Thou, Queequeg, stand up!”

The savage sprang to the triangular bow-box and gazed intently. Astern, Starbuck himself, cool and adroit, balanced on the stern platform, silently eyeing the vast blue eye of the sea. Near by, little King-Post, not content with the loggerhead’s palm-breadth, mounted Daggoo’s shoulders; the gigantic negro rolled his noble form to the sea’s rhythm, while flaxen Flask, on his back, looked a snow-flake on basalt.

Stubb, for his part, would solace the lull with his pipe; but even as he struck fire on his palm, Tashtego, star-staring to windward, dropped like light to his thwart and cried, “Down, down all, and give way!—there they are!”

To a landsman nothing were visible then but a troubled bit of greenish-white water and thin puffs of vapor blowing to leeward like scud. Yet under that wavering air, and half beneath a thin sheet of sea, the whales swam; their spoutings their forerunning couriers.

All four boats drove for the dancing whiteness. “Pull, pull, my good boys,” whispered Starbuck with those keen needle-eyes of his dead ahead—his silence cut now and then by a whisper harsh with command or soft with entreaty. Not so Flask: he raved and stamped on his hat, flinging it seaward, roaring to be beached on their black backs. “Look at that chap now,” drawled Stubb between his unlighted pipe. “He’s got fits—that Flask has. Merrily, babes—pull and burst your livers, but take it easy!”

What words Ahab poured into his tiger crew are best omitted here; only infidel sharks might give ear when with tornado brow and foam-glued lips he leaped after his prey.

The sight was full of awe—the omnipotent swells shouldering our low gunwales; the tipped knife-edge pauses and sled-like slides; cries of headsmen and harpooneers; the shuddering gasps of oarsmen; while the ivory Pequod bore down with outstretched sails like a wild hen after her screaming brood. Stranger emotions no raw recruit feels in his first battle than he who first pulls into the charmed, churned circle of the hunted sperm whale.

The dun cloud-shadows deepening made the white waters telling clear. Our sail drew; with the rising wind we rushed so mad that the lee oars scarce could keep from tearing out. Mist swallowed ship and boats. “Give way,” whispered Starbuck, hauling his sheet; “time to kill a fish ere the squall! There’s white water—close! Spring!”

Cries on either beam told the other boats were fast. Hardly heard, when Starbuck’s lightning whisper: “Stand up!” and Queequeg sprang, iron poised. None of us oarsmen, backs turned to peril, saw the life-and-death thing, but reading the mate’s face we felt the instant. “That’s his hump. There—there—give it to him!” A short rushing; the iron leapt. Then, in one welded commotion—an astern push; a forward shock as on a ledge; sail collapsing; a scalding jet; an earthquake underfoot; and we, half-suffocated, were tossed helter-skelter into the white curdling cream. Squall, whale, and harpoon all blended; the whale, but grazed, escaped.

Swamped, yet nearly whole, the boat floated. We swam her round, crossed oars for thwarts, and tumbled in, sitting knee-deep in sea; the craft seemed a coral boat grown from the bottom. The wind howled; bucklers of waves clashed; the squall roared, forked, and crackled like a white prairie-fire in which, unconsumed, we were burning—immortal in these jaws of death! Vain to hail the others. Night thickened with rack and scud; no Pequod. Baling was forbid by the rising sea; oars were life-preservers now. Starbuck, after many failures, got the lantern lit; set it on a waif-pole and gave it Queequeg—the standard-bearer of our forlorn hope. There he sat, holding up that imbecile candle in the heart of that almighty forlornness; the sign and symbol of a man without faith, hopelessly holding up hope in the midst of despair.

Dawn, and still the mist spread. The empty lantern lay crushed. Suddenly Queequeg sprang, hand to ear. We too heard faint creakings of ropes. Nearer; the fog parted to a huge vague form. Affrighted, we leaped for our lives as the ship loomed right upon us. Our abandoned boat gaped beneath her bows like a chip at a cataract’s base; the vast hull rolled over it; it weltered astern; we swam for it, were dashed against, and at last were taken up and landed aboard. Ere the squall’s close the other boats had cut loose and come in. The ship had given us up, but cruised still, if haply she might light on some token of our perishing—an oar or a lance pole.

## Chapter 2

There are queer times when a man takes the whole universe for a vast practical joke, the wit thereof dimly discerned, and suspects the joke is at nobody’s expense but his own. Then nothing dispirits; he bolts down all events and creeds like an ostrich gobbling bullets; small disasters, perils of life and limb, even death itself, seem but sly good-natured punches from the old unaccountable joker. Such a wayward mood comes only in extreme tribulation; it came to me with whaling’s perils; with it I regarded the Pequod’s voyage, and the great White Whale its object.

“Queequeg,” said I, shaken out like a wet dog upon the deck, “does this often happen?” He, as soaked as I, nodded that it did. “Mr. Stubb,” said I to that worthy, smoking in the rain, “our prudent Starbuck—was that his discretion, going plump on a whale with sail set in a foggy squall?” “Certain,” said Stubb. “I’ve lowered from a leaking ship off Cape Horn.” “Mr. Flask,” said I, “is it law for an oarsman to break his back pulling himself back-foremost into death’s jaws?” “Yes,” said King-Post; “and I’d like to see ’em try it face foremost.”

So, considering capsizings common; that in the instant of going-on I must resign my life to a steersman stamping his own craft to scuttles; that our late disaster was chiefly from Starbuck’s drive in a squall—and he most heedful of men; and that I was of his boat; and, lastly, in what a devil’s chase I was implicated touching the White Whale—I thought I might as well go below and rough-draft my will. “Queequeg,” said I, “come along; you shall be my lawyer, executor, and legatee.” When done, my heart felt eased; I survived myself; my death and burial were locked in my chest. Now then, unconsciously rolling up my sleeves, here goes for a cool, collected dive at death and destruction, and the devil fetch the hindmost.

## Chapter 3

“Who would have thought it, Flask!” cried Stubb. “If I had but one leg ye’d not catch me in a boat—unless to stop the plug-hole with my timber toe.” Flask made nothing of it: Ahab had a knee and good part of the other; if off at the hip, that were disabling.

Among whale-wise folk it is argued whether the captain—his life paramount to the voyage—should hazard it in the chase. But with Ahab, the case turned: in a calling where every moment comprises peril, was it wise for a maimed man to enter a whale-boat? The Pequod’s owners must have thought not. Ahab well knew his friends at home might little object to his lowering in certain harmless vicissitudes for nearness and command; but to have a boat apportioned to him as regular headsman, and—above all—to be supplied with five extra men for that same boat—no such conceits had the owners. He therefore solicited no crew; he hinted nothing. Nevertheless he took private measures. After we had fitted the whaleboats, men had spied him bestirring himself—making thole-pins with his own hands for what all thought a spare boat; shaping with solicitous care the small wooden skewers to pin the bow-groove over the running line; ordering an extra sheathing to the bottom—as if to better bear the pointed pressure of his ivory limb; and, most of all, exactly shaping the thigh-board—the clumsy cleat to brace the knee when darting. Oft he stood in that boat, his lone knee fixed in the semicircle, gouging and straightening with the carpenter’s chisel. Folks deemed all this but forethought for the ultimate chase of Moby Dick, whom he had vowed to hunt in person; no one dreamed a separate boat’s crew was assigned to that boat.

With the subordinate phantoms—those yellow men—wonder soon waned; in a whaler wonders soon wane. Outlandish odds and ends of nations ever come up to man these floating outlaws; ships pick queer castaways from planks, wrecks, canoes, blown-off Japanese junks; Beelzebub himself might climb the side and step into the cabin to chat, and no unsubduable excitement shake the forecastle.

But the hair-turbaned Fedallah remained a muffled mystery to the last. Whence he came; by what unaccountable tie he was linked to Ahab’s fortunes; nay, what half-hinted influence—Heaven knows, it might have been authority—he held over him; none knew. Such a creature as temperate, domestic folk but dimly see in dreams; like those who glide among immemorial Oriental isles, unalterable countries where earth’s ghostly aboriginalness lingers; when men, all descendants of the first man, eyed each other as phantoms, and asked sun and moon their ends; when, though Genesis gives angels to the daughters of men, the Rabbins add that devils, too, indulged in mundane amours.

## Chapter 4

Under easy sail we swept four cruising-grounds—the Azores; the Cape de Verdes; the Plate off the Rio de la Plata; and the Carrol Ground southerly from St. Helena—when, one serene moonlight night, with the waves like scrolls of silver and their seethings making a silvery silence, a solitary jet was descried far ahead—plumed and glittering, some god uprising from the sea. Fedallah first saw it; for on such nights he stood mast-head look-out with day’s precision—his turban and the moon, companions in one sky. Silent for many nights, he at last let fall that unearthly cry—“There she blows!” Every man started as if a winged spirit hailed us; it was an unwonted hour, yet the delirium of the cry made all instinctively desire a lowering.

Ahab, pacing with side-lunging strides, commanded t’gallants, royals, stunsails—every rag—to be set; the best man to the helm. With mast-heads manned, the piled craft rolled before the wind; the upbearing taffrail-breeze made the hovering deck feel like air, as though two powers warred in her—one to mount to heaven, one to drive yawingly on. Had you watched Ahab’s face, you had seen two things at strife there too; his live leg made lively echoes; his dead limb struck coffin-taps. But though we shot swiftly, the silvery jet was no more seen that night. Each swore he saw it once, but never twice.

Some days later, again at the same silent hour, again it was announced, again it vanished; so night after night it served us—mysteriously jetted into moon or starlight, disappearing for a day or three, and at each repetition still further in our van—ever alluring us on. With the superstition of their race, and the Pequod’s preternaturalness, some swore that wherever seen—that unnearable spout was cast by one self-same whale; and that whale, Moby Dick. For a time a peculiar dread reigned, as if treacherously beckoning us on that he might turn and rend us in remotest savage seas.

The blue blandness of the weather sharpened this awe; for days we voyaged through seas so lonesomely mild that all space, in repugnance to our vengeful errand, seemed vacating itself of life before our urn-like prow. But turning eastward, the Cape winds howled; the ivory-tusked Pequod bowed to the blast and gored the dark waves till foam like silver chips flew over the bulwarks; the vacuity of life fled, but gave place to sights more dismal. Strange forms darted before our bows; inscrutable sea-ravens thickened our wake; every morning rows perched on our stays, obstinately clinging to the hemp as if deeming us some drifting, uninhabited craft—fit roost for homeless selves. Still unrestingly heaved the black sea, as if its tides were a conscience, the mundane soul in anguish for long sin and suffering.

Cape of Good Hope, do they call ye? Rather Cape Tormentoso, as of yore. Yet calm, snow-white, unvarying, the solitary jet at times would be descried—its feathered fountain still to sky, still beckoning.

In such blackness Ahab took almost continual command of the drenched and dangerous deck; gloomily reserved; seldom addressing his mates. When all is secured aloft, captains and crews become practical fatalists; and there, with ivory leg in its hole and one hand on a shroud, for hours he gazed dead to windward, sleet half congealing his eyelashes. The crew, driven from the forecastle by bursting seas, swung wordless in bowlines along the waist; by night the same mute humanity before the ocean’s shrieks; wordless Ahab stood up to the blast. Once Starbuck, going below to mark the barometer, saw him in his floor-screwed chair, hat and coat still dripping; a chart of tides and currents unrolled; his lantern swinging from his clenched hand; his head thrown back, closed eyes pointed towards the tell-tale’s needle that swung from the beam. Terrible old man! thought Starbuck with a shudder; sleeping in this gale, still thou steadfastly eyest thy purpose.

## Chapter 5

South-east of the Cape, off the Crozetts, a sail loomed—the Goney, or Albatross. From my perch at the fore-mast-head, I beheld a whaler long absent from home—bleached like a stranded walrus’s skeleton; sides channeled with reddened rust; spars and rigging furred like hoar-frost; only her lower sails set; wild long-bearded look-outs torn and bepatched by four years’ cruising swayed in iron hoops, so near we might almost have leaped from one mast-head to the other.

“Ship ahoy! Have ye seen the White Whale?” hailed our quarter-deck. But as the strange captain put his trumpet to his mouth, it fell into the sea; the wind rising, he strove in vain to make himself heard. Our men, in various silent ways, marked the omen at the first mere mention of the name to another ship. Ahab paused; he had almost lowered to board her, but the threatening wind forbade. Taking advantage of the windward, he seized his trumpet and, knowing her for a Nantucketer bound home, roared: “Ahoy there! This is the Pequod, bound round the world! Tell them to address all future letters to the Pacific ocean! and this time three years, if I am not at home, tell them to address them to ——”

The wakes crossed; instantly shoals of small harmless fish, that for days had swum by our side, darted with shuddering fins and ranged themselves by the stranger. “Swim away from me, do ye?” murmured Ahab, gazing down—more sadness in the tone than he had yet evinced. Then, in his old lion voice: “Up helm! Keep her off round the world!”

Round the world! Proud sound; but to what does all circumnavigation conduct? Only through numberless perils to the very point whence we started. Were this world an endless plain, and by sailing eastward we could for ever reach new distances, there were promise; but chasing far mysteries or that demon phantom that swims before all human hearts, over this round globe they either lead us in barren mazes or midway leave us whelmed.

## Chapter 6

The wind and sea then betokened storms—Ahab’s ostensible reason for not boarding the Goney; but, as subsequent conduct showed, had he, by hailing, got a negative to his one absorbing question, he would scarce have cared to consort five minutes with any stranger captain. All this were ill estimated without something said of the peculiar usages of whalers meeting in foreign seas, especially on a common cruising-ground.

If two strangers crossing the Pine Barrens or Salisbury Plain cannot avoid a salute and bit of news, how much more natural, upon the illimitable Barrens and Plains of the sea, that two whalers descrying each other off lone Fanning’s or far King’s Mills should come into close sociable contact—most of all when owned in one seaport, captains and men personally known, with home-letters to exchange. The long-absent outward-bounder may have files a year newer; the other, the latest whaling intelligence—a thing of utmost importance. Nor would difference of country much alter this, so long as one language runs between them; though English whalers, being few, meet seldom; and when they do, there’s apt to be a shyness—your Englishman reserved; your Yankee, he allows that sort of thing to none but himself. The English sometimes affect a metropolitan superiority over the long lean Nantucketer—sea-peasant, they think; wherein that superiority lies, hard saying, seeing the Yankees in one day collectively kill more whales than all the English in ten years; but this foible the islander little minds—knowing he hath a few of his own.

So of all ships separately sailing the sea, whalers have most reason to be sociable—and they are so. Merchantmen oft pass like dandies in Broadway, mutually cutting each other on the high seas; Men-of-War duck ensigns in such a string of silly bowings and scrapings there’s little hearty good-will. Slave-ships hurry past; Pirates, meeting, hail “How many skulls?”—as whalers hail “How many barrels?”—and steer apart, not loving to behold each other’s villanous likeness.

But the honest, hospitable, free-and-easy whaler—what does she when she meets another in decent weather? She has a “Gam,” a thing so unknown to other ships they never heard the name; and if they did, they grin about “spouters” and “blubber-boilers.” Let me learnedly define it.

GAM. NOUN—_A social meeting of two_ (_or more_) _Whaleships, generally on a cruising-ground; when, after exchanging hails, they exchange visits by boats’ crews: the two captains remaining, for the time, on board of one ship, and the two chief mates on the other._

There is another item about Gamming. In a pirate, Man-of-War, or slave-ship, the captain, rowed in his boat, sits in cushioned stern-sheets and often steers with a pretty milliner’s tiller. The whale-boat has no such sofa, no tiller at all. High times indeed, if whaling captains were wheeled about the water on castors like gouty aldermen. As a complete crew must leave the ship, and the boat-steerer is of the number, that subordinate is steersman; the captain, having no place to sit, is pulled off all standing like a pine tree—conscious of all eyes levelled on him from the two ships’ sides; alive to the importance of sustaining his dignity by maintaining his legs. No easy matter: behind him the immense projecting steering-oar hits him in the small of the back; the after-oar raps his knees in front; he is wedged before and behind, and can only expand sideways by settling on his stretched legs; a sudden pitch will near topple him, for length of foundation is naught without breadth. And it would never do, in the world’s riveted gaze, to be seen steadying himself by catching hold with his hands; token of entire buoyant self-command, he carries them in his trowsers’ pockets—perhaps, being very large, heavy hands, for ballast. Nevertheless, there are instances, well authenticated, when, in an uncommonly critical squall, the straddling captain has seized the nearest oarsman’s hair, and held on there like grim death.
