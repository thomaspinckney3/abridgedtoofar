Nimble as a cat, Tashtego ran out upon the overhanging mainyard-arm above the hoisted Tun, swung a light whip’s end to deck, and hand-over-hand dropped to the summit of the head. There—still high elevated above the rest of the company, to whom he cries—he seems some Turkish Muezzin calling the good people to prayers from the top of a tower. Spade in hand, he sought the place to break in. A stout, iron-bound bucket was bent to the whip; with a long pole he guided it down into the Case. Up it came, bubbling like a dairy-maid’s pail of new milk; lowered, emptied to a tub; remounted; and so round till the deep cistern would yield no more—twenty feet of pole gone.

Then, all at once, a queer accident. Whether Tashtego in heedlessness loosed his one-handed hold on the great cabled tackles; or the footing was oozy; or the Evil One so willed, without stating his particular reasons; how it was exactly, there is no telling now; as the eightieth or ninetieth bucket came suckingly up—my God! poor Tashtego—like a twin reciprocating bucket dropped head-foremost down into the great Tun, and, with a horrible oily gurgling, went clean out of sight! “Man overboard!” cried Daggoo. Foot in the bucket, he shot aloft to the head. The before lifeless head now heaved and throbbed, as if that moment seized with some momentous idea; whereas it was only the poor Indian unconsciously revealing by those struggles the perilous depth to which he had sunk. A sharp crack—the unspeakable horror: one of the two enormous hooks tore out; the mass swung; the ship reeled as if smitten by an iceberg; the one remaining hook strained to part.

“Come down!” they yelled to Daggoo; but clinging to the heavy tackles, having cleared the foul, he rammed down the bucket to the collapsed well that Tash might grasp it. “Avast! How will that help him?” cried Stubb. Then a voice like the bursting of a rocket: “Stand clear of the tackle!” Thunder-boom—the enormous mass dropped like Niagara’s Table-Rock; the suddenly relieved hull rolled far to her copper; Daggoo swung through spray; and Tashtego sank. A naked figure with a boarding-sword flashed over the bulwarks—Queequeg dived.

Moments, ripples, no sign. “Ha! ha!” cried Daggoo from aloft; an arm thrust upright from the blue, as from grass over a grave. “Both! both!” Soon Queequeg struck out with one hand, the other clutching the Indian’s long hair. Tashtego was long in coming to; Queequeg looked not brisk.

The rescue? Diving after the descending head, Queequeg side-lunged with his keen sword near its bottom, scuttling a large hole; thrusting his long arm inwards and upwards, he hauled out Tash by the head. He averred a leg first offered; knowing that was not as it ought to be, and might occasion trouble, he thrust it back, and by a dexterous toss wrought a somerset, so the next trial came forth the good old way—head foremost.

And thus, through the courage and great skill in obstetrics of Queequeg, the deliverance, or rather, delivery of Tashtego, was successfully accomplished, in the teeth, too, of the most untoward and apparently hopeless impediments; which is a lesson not to be forgotten. Midwifery should be taught in the same course with fencing and boxing, riding and rowing. I know this queer adventure of the Gay-Header’s will seem incredible to some landsmen, though they may have seen or heard of one’s falling into a cistern ashore. But, peradventure, it may be sagaciously urged, how is this? We thought the tissued, infiltrated head was the lightest and most corky part about him; and yet thou makest it sink. Not at all, but I have ye; for when poor Tash fell in, the case had been nearly emptied of its lighter contents, leaving little but the dense, double-welded wall—much heavier than sea-water—so that a lump of it sinks like lead; the other parts of the head remaining undetached made it go very slowly, affording Queequeg a fair chance for performing his agile obstetrics on the run—yes, a running delivery. Now, had Tashtego perished in that head, it had been a very precious perishing; smothered in the very whitest and daintiest of fragrant spermaceti; coffined, hearsed, and tombed in the secret inner chamber and sanctum sanctorum of the whale. Only one sweeter end can readily be recalled—the delicious death of an Ohio honey-hunter, who, seeking honey in the crotch of a hollow tree, found such exceeding store that leaning too far over, it sucked him in, so that he died embalmed. How many, think ye, have likewise fallen into Plato’s honey head, and sweetly perished there?
