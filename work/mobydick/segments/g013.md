## Chapter 1

South-eastward the long peninsula of Malacca runs, and from it in file stretch Sumatra, Java, Bally, and Timor—a vast green rampart, pierced here and there by sally-ports for ships and whales; conspicuous among them, the Straits of Sunda, dividing Sumatra from Java, buttressed by bold green Java Head like the central gateway to some walled empire rich in spices, silks, jewels, gold, and ivory. No fortresses frown there; the Orientals do not demand lowered topsails, yet they do not renounce more solid tribute. Time out of mind the piratical proas of the Malays have lurked among Sumatra’s coves, demanding black-mail at spear-point.

With a fair wind the Pequod drew nigh these straits; Ahab purposed thence to the Javan sea, then northward by Sperm-Whale waters, inshore by the Philippines, and so to Japan for the great season; and after circling all known grounds, to descend upon the Line, where, foiled elsewhere, he firmly counted upon giving battle to Moby Dick. But in this zoned quest, does he touch no land? Nay. The whale-ship carries no cargo but herself and crew, their weapons and their wants. Years’ water is in her—clear old prime Nantucket water; so that though other ships touch a score of ports, the world-wandering whaler may not sight one grain of soil. Tell them a new flood had come—“Well, boys, here’s the ark!”

Many Sperm Whales had been captured off Java; yet though the palmy cliffs loomed to starboard and the air smelt of fresh cinnamon, not a jet was descried—till at last, hailed from aloft, a spectacle of singular magnificence saluted us. Of late, hunted over all oceans, Sperm Whales now often herd in immense caravans; thus you may sail for weeks without a spout, and then be suddenly saluted by what seems thousands on thousands.

Broad on both bows, two or three miles off, in a vast semicircle, a continuous chain of jets up-played and sparkled; not the Right Whale’s split boughs, but the Sperm Whale’s single forward-slanting curled bush of mist. Rising on a sea-hill, we beheld that host of vapory spouts, through a bluish haze, like a thousand cheerful chimneys of some dense metropolis descried on a balmy autumnal morning. As armies hastening through a mountain defile, so did this fleet seem hurrying the Straits; their wings contracting, they swam on in a solid crescentic centre.

Crowding all sail, stun-sail on stun-sail, we pressed after; when of a sudden Tashtego’s voice hailed our wake. Corresponding to the crescent in our van, we beheld another astern—detached white vapors hovering like spouts that did not quite come and go. Ahab levelled his glass, wheeled upon his pivot—“Aloft there, rig whips and buckets to wet the sails;—Malays, sir, and after us!”

But the swift Pequod, herself in hot chase, found these tawny philanthropists but riding-whips and rowels. Pacing to and fro, Ahab in one turn beheld the monsters he chased; in the other, the bloodthirsty pirates chasing him; and glancing on the green walls of the watery defile—the gate to his vengeance—he was both chasing and being chased to his deadly end, infernally cheered on with curses. When these conceits had passed, his brow was left gaunt and ribbed, like a black sand beach gnawn by stormy tide.

The crew troubled little; dropping the pirates astern, we shot by vivid green Cockatoo Point and emerged upon broad waters. Still driving in the whales’ wake, at length they seemed abating; we neared them; the wind dying, word was passed to spring to the boats. But no sooner did the herd, notified by some wonderful instinct of the keels after them, rally and form in close battalions, their spouts like flashing lines of stacked bayonets, than they moved on with redoubled velocity.

Stripped to shirts and drawers we sprang to the white-ash. After hours’ pulling we were for renouncing the chase, when a general pausing commotion showed them gallied. The compact columns broke into rout, expanding in vast irregular circles, aimlessly swimming, their short thick spoutings betraying panic; some, paralysed, floated like dismantled water-logged ships. Best withhold amazement; there is no folly of beasts not outdone by the madness of men.

The herd, though much in motion, neither advanced nor retreated, but as a whole remained; the boats separated for lone whales on the outskirts. In three minutes Queequeg’s harpoon flew; the fish, running with us like light, steered straight for the heart of the herd. Thus dragged deeper and deeper into the frantic shoal, we bade adieu to circumspect life and only existed in a delirious throb. Our boat, mobbed by to-and-fro rushing monsters, was like a ship among ice-isles in tempest. Not daunted, Queequeg steered manfully; Starbuck stood in the bows, lance in hand, pricking aside whatever he could reach; the oarsmen shouted—“Out of the way, Commodore!—Hard down with your tail, there!”

All whaleboats carry druggs—crossed wooden blocks with a long line looped to a harpoon—to “wing” more whales than can be killed at once. Two we darted; the fettered whales ran off, cramped like malefactors with chain and ball. On flinging the third, the block caught under a seat, tore it out, and wounded both sides of the boat; drawers and shirts stuffed in stayed the leaks.

Advancing inward, our whale’s way diminished; the dire disorders waned. When the harpoon drew out and the tow-ward whale vanished, with his tapering momentum we glided into the innermost heart of the shoal—as if from a mountain torrent we had slid into a serene valley lake. This central expanse presented the sleek—a smooth satin-like surface; we were in the enchanted calm said to lurk at every commotion’s heart. In the distracted distance the outer circles went round and round, pods shoulder to shoulder, as if a Titanic circus-rider might arch the middle backs. Hemmed by a living wall, we must watch for a breach. Small tame cows and calves—the women and children—came snuffling like household dogs, touching our gunwales; Queequeg patted their foreheads; Starbuck scratched their backs with his lance, yet refrained from darting.

Far beneath this wondrous surface another world appeared: nursing mothers and those large with young floated suspended; the lake was singularly transparent. The infants looked up towards us, but not at us, as if we were but a bit of Gulf-weed. One little fellow, hardly a day old, some fourteen feet long, six round, frisked; his delicate side-fins and palms of flukes still crumpled like a baby’s ears newly arrived. “Line! line!” cried Queequeg; “him fast!”—and Starbuck saw long coils of Madame Leviathan’s umbilical cord, tethering the cub to its dam. Some of the subtlest secrets of the seas seemed divulged. We saw young Leviathan amours in the deep.

And though surrounded by circle upon circle of affrights, these creatures at the centre freely and fearlessly indulged in all peaceful concernments; yea, serenely revelled in dalliance and delight. But even so, amid the tornadoed Atlantic of my being, do I myself still for ever centrally disport in mute calm; and while ponderous planets of unwaning woe revolve round me, deep down and deep inland there I still bathe me in eternal mildness of joy.

Meanwhile, frantic spectacles afar showed the other boats drugging on the frontier. At last there came one more appalling sight. A whale, hamstrung at the tail-tendon by a cutting-spade and broken away, had entangled himself in the harpoon-line; the spade, working loose, was tossed by his flailing tail, wounding and murdering his own comrades. Recalled from fright, the herd on our lake’s margin crowded; the sleek heaved; nurseries vanished; the central circles thickened; a low hum advanced; then like the block-ice of the Hudson breaking up, the entire host came tumbling upon their inner centre, to pile in one common mountain. Instantly Starbuck took the stern—“Oars! Oars!—gripe your oars, and clutch your souls, now!—Shove him off, you Queequeg—the whale there!—prick him!—hit him! Stand up—stay so! Spring, men—pull; never mind their backs—scrape them!”

Jammed between two vast bulks like a narrow Dardanelles, we shot by desperate endeavor into a fleeting opening, watched for another, and, after many hair-breadth escapes, at last glided into what had been an outer circle, now crossed by random whales all making for one centre—Queequeg’s hat whisked clean from his head by a sudden pair of flukes.

Riotous commotion resolved itself to system; clumped in one dense body, they renewed their onward flight with augmented fleetness. Further pursuit was useless. The boats lingered to pick up drugged whales and a waifed fish of Flask’s. Yet the result proved that sagacious saying—the more whales the less fish. Of all the drugged whales only one was captured; the rest escaped for the time, to be taken, as will hereafter be seen, by some other craft than the Pequod.

## Chapter 2

Though immense herds are sometimes met, small detached bands are often observed—schools—embracing from twenty to fifty. Two sorts: those almost entirely of females, and those mustering none but young vigorous males—forty-barrel-bulls.

In cavalier attendance on the females, you invariably see one full-grown male, not old; upon alarm, he gallantly falls in the rear and covers the flight of his ladies. A luxurious Ottoman, swimming with the solaces of a harem, he is of the largest proportions, while his concubines, even at full growth, are not more than a third his bulk—comparatively delicate, yet hereditarily entitled to en bon point. On their leisured rambles, they promenade the Equator, cheat summer in northern seas, then anticipate coolness in Oriental waters.

If any unwarrantably pert young Leviathan presume to draw close to a lady, with what prodigious fury the Bashaw assails and chases him! High times, indeed, if young rakes invade domestic bliss; though, do what the Bashaw will, he cannot keep the most notorious Lothario out of his bed; for, alas! all fish bed in common. For love, whales fence with their long lower jaws, locking like elks; heads are furrowed, fins scolloped, mouths wrenched.

As youth declines and dumps increase; reflection lends her pauses; lassitude overtakes the sated Turk: love of ease and virtue supplants love for maidens; he forswears, disbands the harem, and, grown a sulky exemplary soul, goes alone saying his prayers, warning each young Leviathan from amorous errors. Hence the harem’s lord is the schoolmaster—satirically so; after going to school, he preaches the folly of it.

Aged Sperm Whales betake them to solitude; a lone whale proves almost universally an ancient one—like moss-bearded Boone, wifing Nature in the wilderness of waters.

The forty-barrel-bull schools are larger and a contrast: like a mob of collegians, full of fight, fun, and wickedness, tumbling round the world at a rollicking rate; proverbially the most dangerous to encounter, save the wondrous grizzled whales that fight like fiends. Struck, a bull is deserted by all his comrades; but strike a member of the harem, and her companions swim around her with every token of concern, lingering so near and so long as themselves to fall a prey.

## Chapter 3

The waif and waif-pole call for the whale fishery’s grand badge and law. The American fishermen, though no nation has written codes (save Holland of 1695), have two terse fundamentals, engravable on a Queen Anne’s farthing:

I. A Fast-Fish belongs to the party fast to it.

II. A Loose-Fish is fair game for anybody who can soonest catch it.

What plays the mischief with this admirable brevity is the volume of commentaries. A fish is fast when connected with an occupied ship or boat by any controllable medium, or when it bears a waif under a plainly evinced ability and intention to take it alongside. Yet Coke-upon-Littleton of the fist often expounds the rest.

There was a curious English whale-trover case. After a hard chase and harpooning, the plaintiffs, through peril of their lives, forsook lines and even their boat. The defendants came up, struck, killed, seized, and appropriated—retaining line, harpoons, and boat still attached. Mr. Erskine, for the defendants; Lord Ellenborough judging. Erskine illustrated by a crim. con.: the husband who had once harpooned the lady and abandoned her; she became a Loose-Fish; the subsequent gentleman re-harpooning had the property, harpoon and all. The learned judge awarded the boat to the plaintiffs (abandoned to save life), but the whale, harpoons, and line to the defendants: the fish was a Loose-Fish at capture; and when a fish makes off with your gear, it acquires a property in it; whoever takes the fish afterwards has right to the gear.

Ploughed to the primary rock, these twin laws are the fundamentals of all human jurisprudence. Possession is half the law? Oft it is the whole. What are Russian serfs and Republican slaves but Fast-Fish? The widow’s last mite to the rapacious landlord—a Fast-Fish. The undetected villain’s marble mansion with a door-plate for a waif—a Fast-Fish. What to John Bull is poor Ireland, and to Brother Jonathan, Texas? Fast-Fish.

If Fast-Fish be widely applicable, Loose-Fish is still more so. America in 1492—a Loose-Fish waifed by Columbus. Poland to the Czar, Greece to the Turk, India to England, and what at last will Mexico be to the United States? All Loose-Fish. What are the Rights of Man and the Liberties of the World but Loose-Fish? What all men’s minds and opinions but Loose-Fish? What is the great globe itself but a Loose-Fish? And what are you, reader, but a Loose-Fish and a Fast-Fish, too?

## Chapter 4

“De balena vero sufficit, si rex habeat caput, et regina caudam.”—Bracton. That is: of all whales captured on England’s coast, the King, as honorary Grand Harpooneer, must have the head, the Queen the tail—a division much like halving an apple; no remainder. The law, in modified form, yet in force, offers a strange anomaly to Fast- and Loose-Fish; so let royalty have a separate car.

Within two years certain mariners of the Cinque Ports killed and beached a fine whale, promising themselves £150; when up steps a learned Christian gentleman with Blackstone under arm, lays it on the whale’s head—“Hands off! This fish is a Fast-Fish. I seize it as the Lord Warden’s.” “Who is the Lord Warden?”—“The Duke.” “But the Duke had nothing to do with taking this fish.”—“It is his.” “We have been at great trouble, peril, expense—are all to go to the Duke’s benefit?”—“It is his.” “Is the Duke so very poor?”—“It is his.” “Won’t the Duke be content with a quarter or a half?”—“It is his.” The whale was seized and sold; his Grace the Duke of Wellington received the money. An honest clergyman wrote begging consideration for the mariners. His Grace replied he had already done so, received the money, and would be obliged if the reverend gentleman would decline meddling with other people’s business.

Plowdon gives the reason: the whale belongs to King and Queen “because of its superior excellence.” But why should the King have the head, and the Queen the tail? William Prynne, in “Queen-Gold,” says, “Ye tail is ye Queen’s, that ye Queen’s wardrobe may be supplied with ye whalebone.” A sagacious lawyer’s sad mistake; the black limber bone is in the head, not the tail. Is the Queen a mermaid, to be presented with a tail? Allegory may lurk.

Two royal fish the law styles—the whale and the sturgeon—both royal property under certain limitations. By inference, the sturgeon must be divided as the whale; the King receiving the highly dense elastic head—congeniality symbolical, perhaps. There is a reason in all things, even in law.

## Chapter 5

“In vain it was to rake for Ambergriese in the paunch of this Leviathan, insufferable fetor denying not inquiry.”—Sir T. Browne. A week or two after the last lowering, as we sailed over a sleepy, vapory, mid-day sea, the many noses on the Pequod’s deck proved more vigilant than the three pairs of eyes aloft. A peculiar, not pleasant smell was smelt. “Some of those drugged whales we tickled the other day,” said Stubb; “I thought they would keel up.”

The vapors slid aside; a ship lay becalmed, French colors at her peak; and by the eddying vulture sea-fowl, it was plain the whale alongside was blasted—died and floated unappropriated; an unsavory odor—worse than a plague city. Coming nearer, the Frenchman had a second fish—one of those dyspeptic whales that die prodigiously indigested; almost bankrupt of oil, yet not to be turned up by a knowing fisherman.

Stubb vowed he recognised his cutting spade-pole entangled round one whale’s tail. “There’s a Crappo content with our leavings—the drugged whale—and scraping dry bones of the other. Poor devil! Pass a hat; make him a present of a little oil. Though—” and he sniffed—“maybe there’s ambergris. I wonder if our old man has thought of that. Yes, I’m for it.”

A calm entrapped us in the smell. Stubb called his crew and pulled for the stranger. Her stem was carved and painted like a green thorned stalk ending in a red bulb; upon her head-boards: “Bouton de Rose”—Rose-bud. “A wooden rose-bud, eh?” cried Stubb, hand to nose; “how like all creation it smells!”

He must pull to starboard, close to the blasted whale, to hail. “Bouton-de-Rose, ahoy! any of you that speak English?” “Yes,” rejoined a Guernsey-man—the chief-mate. “Have ye seen the White Whale—Moby Dick?” “Never heard of such a whale. Cachalot Blanche?—no.” Stubb trumpeted back to Ahab—“No, Sir! No!”—and returned.

The Guernsey-man, in the chains with a cutting-spade, had slung his nose in a bag. “Broke it?” said Stubb. “I wish it was broken, or I had none! What are you holding yours for?” “It’s a wax nose; I have to hold it on. Air rather gardenny; throw us a bunch of posies, Bouton-de-Rose.” The sailors in tasselled red caps worked slow, talked fast, with noses like jib-booms; some ran aloft for air; some smoked stems broken short; some held oakum dipped in coal-tar to their nostrils. From the round-house a fiery face thrust out—the tormented surgeon, yelling remonstrances, self-immured to avoid the pest.

The Guernsey-man detested his captain, a delicate, moustached gentleman in red velvet vest with watch-seals; a Cologne manufacturer on his first voyage; a conceited ignoramus, said the mate. Sounding him, Stubb perceived no suspicion of ambergris; so, under cover of interpretation, they concocted a plan—Guernsey to tell the Captain what he pleased, as from Stubb; Stubb to utter any nonsense uppermost.

Introduced, Stubb eyed the vest and seals. “Tell him he looks babyish to me.” The Guernsey-man, in French, said instead that only yesterday a ship’s captain, chief-mate, and six sailors had died of a fever from a blasted whale. The Captain started, eager. “Now,” whispered the mate. “Tell him,” said Stubb, “he’s no more fit to command a whale-ship than a St. Jago monkey. In fact, tell him from me he’s a baboon.” The interpreter said: “The other whale, the dried one, is far more deadly; as we value our lives, cut loose.” Instantly the Captain ran forward, and loudly commanded his crew to desist and cast loose all cables and chains.

“What now?”—“Tell him I’ve diddled him.” The interpreter: “He is happy to have been of any service.” The Captain, grateful, invited Stubb below to a bottle of Bordeaux. “Thank him; tell him it’s against my principles to drink with the man I’ve diddled. If Monsieur would live another day to drink, best drop all four boats and pull the ship away; it’s so calm they won’t drift.” Stubb over the side again, hailed that he had a long tow-line and would help by towing the lighter whale off. While the French boats towed their ship one way, Stubb benevolently towed the whale the other, slacking an unusually long line. A breeze sprang; he feigned to cast off; the Frenchman increased his distance; the Pequod slid between him and Stubb’s whale. Stubb pulled to the floating body, hailed his ship, and set to reaping the fruit of his cunning.

With sharp boat-spade he excavated behind the side fin, as if digging a sea-cellar; ribs rang like Roman tiles in fat loam; the crew in high excitement, as gold-hunters; fowls diving and screaming. The horrible nosegay increased—when suddenly, from the plague’s heart, there stole a faint perfume, flowing through the bad smells without blending, as one river within another. “I have it, I have it,” cried Stubb, striking—“a purse! a purse!” He thrust both hands and drew out handfuls like ripe Windsor soap, or rich mottled old cheese; unctuous; easily dented; a hue between yellow and ash. Ambergris—worth a guinea an ounce. Some six handfuls were obtained; more lost to sea—when impatient Ahab’s loud command bade Stubb desist and come on board, else the ship would bid them good-bye.

## Chapter 6

Ambergris—grey amber by French name, yet distinct from amber. Amber is hard, transparent, brittle, odorless; found on coasts and inland soils; for pipe-mouths, beads, ornaments. Ambergris is soft, waxy, highly fragrant and spicy; used in perfumery, pastiles, precious candles, hair-powders, pomatum; the Turks cook with it and carry it to Mecca, as frankincense to St. Peter’s; a few grains dropped into claret flavor it. Who would think such fine folk regale themselves with an essence found in the inglorious bowels of a sick whale! Yet so it is. By some, ambergris is cause; by others, effect, of whale dyspepsia. How to cure such a dyspepsia but by administering three or four boat-loads of Brandreth’s pills, and then running like men blasting rocks?

Certain hard, round, bony plates were found in this ambergris; Stubb took them for sailors’ trowser-buttons; they proved pieces of small squid bones embalmed.

That incorruption should be found in the heart of such decay—is this nothing? Bethink thee of St. Paul to the Corinthians—sown in dishonor, raised in glory; remember Paracelsus on what maketh the best musk; forget not that Cologne-water, in rudimental stages, is of all ill savors the worst.

One charge to repel. It is slanderously hinted that all whales always smell bad. The stigma is traceable to the first Greenland whalemen in London, who, not trying out at sea as the Southern ships do, but thrusting fresh blubber through cask bung-holes and carrying it home, unload in the dock a savor like excavating a grave-yard for a Lying-in Hospital. Add Smeerenberg—Schmerenburgh—the Dutch fat-berg on Greenland’s coast, a village of furnaces and fat-kettles; when in blast, no pleasant savor. But all this is different with a South Sea Sperm Whaler; in four years, after filling her hold, she perhaps consumes not fifty days boiling; the oil, casked, is nearly scentless. Living or dead, if but decently treated, whales as a species are by no means of ill odor; nor can whalemen be recognised by the nose. Indeed, the motion of a Sperm Whale’s flukes above water dispenses a perfume, as when a musk-scented lady rustles her dress in a warm parlor. What then liken the Sperm Whale to for fragrance, considering his magnitude? To that famous elephant, with jewelled tusks, redolent with myrrh, led out of an Indian town to do honor to Alexander the Great.
