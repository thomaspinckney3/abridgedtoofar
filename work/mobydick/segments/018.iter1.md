It was quite late when the little Moss came snugly to anchor; so we could attend to no business but a supper and a bed. Peter Coffin had sent us to his cousin Hosea Hussey of the Try Pots, famous for chowders; but his sea-directions—keep the yellow warehouse to starboard till you open a white church to larboard, then keep that to larboard till three points starboard, and then ask—set us beating about in the dark till we came to something there was no mistaking.

Two enormous wooden pots painted black, suspended by asses’ ears, swung from the cross-trees of an old top-mast before an old doorway; the other horns sawed, so the spar looked not a little like a gallows. Perhaps I was over sensitive, but as I gazed up to the two remaining horns—one for Queequeg, and one for me, thinks I—there came a vague misgiving. A Coffin for my Innkeeper; tombstones in the whalemen’s chapel; and here a gallows and a pair of prodigious black pots! Are these throwing out oblique hints touching Tophet?

A freckled woman in a yellow gown under a dull red lamp, briskly scolding a man in a purple woollen shirt—“Get along with ye, or I’ll be combing ye!” “There’s Mrs. Hussey,” said I. Mr. Hosea being from home, she was entirely competent; she ushered us to a table spread with the relics of a late repast, turned, and said—“Clam or Cod?”

“What’s that about Cods, ma’am?” said I, with much politeness. “Clam or Cod?” she repeated. “A clam for supper? a cold clam; is that what you mean, Mrs. Hussey? but that’s a rather cold and clammy reception in the winter time, ain’t it, Mrs. Hussey?” But she, eager to resume scolding the purple-shirted man, seemed to hear nothing but “clam,” bawled “clam for two,” and disappeared. “Queequeg,” said I, “do you think that we can make out a supper for us both on one clam?”

However, a warm savory steam belied that cheerless prospect; and when the smoking chowder came in, the mystery was delightfully explained. Oh, sweet friends! hearken to me. It was made of small juicy clams, scarcely bigger than hazel nuts, mixed with pounded ship biscuit, and salted pork cut up into little flakes; the whole enriched with butter, and plentifully seasoned with pepper and salt. Our appetites sharpened by the frosty voyage, and the chowder being surpassingly excellent, we despatched it with expedition; then, bethinking me of Mrs. Hussey’s “clam and cod,” I stepped to the kitchen door, uttered “cod” with great emphasis, and in due time a fine cod-chowder was placed before us.

We resumed business; and while plying our spoons in the bowl, thinks I to myself, I wonder now if this here has any effect on the head? What’s that stultifying saying about chowder-headed people? “But look, Queequeg, ain’t that a live eel in your bowl? Where’s your harpoon?”

Fishiest of all fishy places was the Try Pots: chowder for breakfast, and chowder for dinner, and chowder for supper, till you looked for fish-bones coming through your clothes. The area before the house was paved with clam-shells. Mrs. Hussey wore a polished necklace of codfish vertebra; Hosea Hussey had his account books bound in superior old shark-skin. There was a fishy flavor to the milk, too, which I could not at all account for, till one morning happening to take a stroll along the beach among some fishermen’s boats, I saw Hosea’s brindled cow feeding on fish remnants, and marching along the sand with each foot in a cod’s decapitated head, looking very slip-shod, I assure ye.

Supper concluded, we received a lamp and directions bedward; but as Queequeg was about to precede me upstairs, the lady reached forth her arm and demanded his harpoon; she allowed no harpoon in her chambers. “Why not?” said I; “every true whaleman sleeps with his harpoon—but why not?” “Because it’s dangerous,” says she. “Ever since young Stiggs coming from that unfort’nt v’y’ge of his, when he was gone four years and a half, with only three barrels of ile, was found dead in my first floor back, with his harpoon in his side; ever since then I allow no boarders to take sich dangerous weepons in their rooms at night.”

“But the chowder; clam or cod to-morrow for breakfast, men?” “Both,” says I; “and let’s have a couple of smoked herring by way of variety.”
