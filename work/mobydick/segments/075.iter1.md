In cutting-in there is much scampering; everything to be done everywhere at once. We must go back a little. Upon first breaking ground in the whale’s back, my friend Queequeg, as harpooneer, descended upon the monster to insert the blubber-hook. Oftentimes he must stay there till the whole flensing is done, the whale lying mostly submerged, the worked-on parts alone awash. So, ten feet below the deck, half on the whale and half in the sea as the vast mass revolved like a tread-mill, Queequeg floundered—in Highland costume of shirt and socks—while I, his bowsman, held him from the ship’s steep side by the monkey-rope: a canvas belt round his waist, and—Stubb’s improvement—made fast at the other end to my own.

It was a humorously perilous wedding. For better or worse we were wedded; should Queequeg sink, usage and honor forbade cutting the cord, but dragged me after. So an elongated Siamese ligature united us. So strongly and metaphysically did I conceive of my situation then, that while earnestly watching his motions, I seemed distinctly to perceive that my own individuality was now merged in a joint stock company of two; that my free will had received a mortal wound; and that another’s mistake or misfortune might plunge innocent me into unmerited disaster and death. Therefore, I saw that here was a sort of interregnum in Providence; for its even-handed equity never could have so gross an injustice. And yet still further pondering—while I jerked him now and then from between the whale and ship, which would threaten to jam him—still further pondering, I say, I saw that this situation of mine was the precise situation of every mortal that breathes; only, in most cases, he, one way or other, has this Siamese connexion with a plurality of other mortals. If your banker breaks, you snap; if your apothecary sends poison in your pills, you die. Handle the monkey-rope heedfully as I would, sometimes he jerked so that I came near sliding overboard; and do what I would, I had but one end of it.

Unappalled by last night’s massacre, sharks swarmed like bees, freshly allured by the pent blood now flowing. Right among them was Queequeg, pushing them aside with his feet; the miscellaneously carnivorous shark, drawn by such prey, will seldom touch a man. Nevertheless, wise to look sharp: besides my jerks, Tashtego and Daggoo, from the stages above, flourished keen spades over his head, slaughtering what they could reach—disinterested and indiscreet; for, half-hidden in blood-muddled water, their zealous steel came nearer amputating a leg than a tail. Poor Queequeg, iron hook in hand, strained and gasped, and, I think, prayed to Yojo.

Well, well, my dear comrade and twin-brother, thought I, as I drew in and then slacked off the rope to every swell of the sea—what matters it, after all? Are you not the precious image of each and all of us men in this whaling world? That unsounded ocean you gasp in, is Life; those sharks, your foes; those spades, your friends; and what between sharks and spades you are in a sad pickle and peril, poor lad. But courage! there is good cheer in store for you, Queequeg. For now, as with blue lips and blood-shot eyes the exhausted savage at last climbs up the chains and stands dripping and trembling, the steward advances and hands him—what? Some hot Cognac? No! a cup of tepid ginger and water.

“Ginger!—what the devil is ginger? Sea-coal? firewood?—lucifer matches?—tinder?—gunpowder?—what the devil is ginger, I say, that you offer this cup to our poor Queequeg here.” There was, Stubb averred, some sneaking Temperance Society in it. He carried the kannakin to Starbuck, who owned it “poor stuff enough.” “It was Aunt Charity,” cried Dough-Boy; “she bade me never give the harpooneers any spirits, only this ginger-jub.” “Ginger-jub! you gingerly rascal!—but I hope I do no wrong, Mr. Starbuck; the captain’s orders—grog for the harpooneer on a whale.” “Enough,” said Starbuck; “only don’t hit him—go down with him, and get what thou wantest thyself.” Stubb returned with a dark flask for Queequeg, and Aunt Charity’s tea-caddy—for the waves.
