Hand in hand, ship and breeze blew on; soon the Pequod rocked, and through the glass the stranger proved a whaleship. Too far to windward, she shot by; so signals were set. She answered: the Jeroboam of Nantucket. Squaring her yards, she ranged abeam and lowered a boat; but as Starbuck rigged the side-ladder, the captain in the stern waved it off. A malignant epidemic raged aboard; though himself untainted and a rifle-shot off, Captain Mayhew held to shore-quarantine punctilio and would not come aboard. His boat kept a few yards off, now shoved ahead by a rolling wave, now dropping back.

At an oar sat a small, short, youngish man, freckled, redundant yellow hair, in a long-skirted, cabalistic coat; delirium fanatic in his eyes. “That’s he!” cried Stubb. Gabriel, as the Town-Ho had told, had been a great prophet among the Neskyeuna Shakers, descending from heaven through a trap-door with the seventh vial. He shipped as green-hand, and once off soundings declared himself the archangel Gabriel, and commanded the captain to jump overboard. He published his manifesto, whereby he set himself forth as the deliverer of the isles of the sea and vicar-general of all Oceanica. His preternatural earnestness, and the terrors of real delirium, invested him with sacred awe among the ignorant crew; they feared him, and protected him; the captain could not land him. Since the epidemic, he had carried a higher hand, declaring the plague his own to loose or bind; the poor devils cringed, sometimes rendering him homage as to a god. Such things may seem incredible; but, however wondrous, they are true. Nor is the history of fanatics half so striking in respect to the measureless self-deception of the fanatic himself, as his measureless power of deceiving and bedevilling so many others.

“I fear not thy epidemic,” Ahab called. “Come on board.” Gabriel sprang up: “Think of the fevers! Beware the horrible plague!” A headlong wave shot the boat ahead, drowning Mayhew’s words. When she drifted back Ahab hailed, “Hast thou seen the White Whale?”—“Think of thy whale-boat, stoven and sunk! Beware the horrible tail!” Again a wave tore them on.

When the sea’s caprice abated, Mayhew began the dark story of Moby Dick—Gabriel breaking in whenever the name arose. Not long from home, the Jeroboam had been warned of the White Whale’s havoc. Gabriel denounced any attack, pronouncing him the Shaker God incarnate. Yet, a year or two later, when Moby Dick was fairly sighted, Macey, the chief mate, burned to engage. The captain let him go; five men manned his boat. Gabriel, at the main-royal, tossed his arm and hurled prophecies of doom. Macey stood in the bow, reckless, essaying his lance, when a broad white shadow rose; its quick fanning took the oarsmen’s breath. Next instant the mate, so full of furious life, was smitten bodily into the air, and fell fifty yards off. Not a chip of the boat was harmed, nor a hair of any head; but the mate for ever sank. It is well to parenthesize here, that of the fatal accidents in the Sperm-Whale Fishery, this kind is perhaps almost as frequent as any. Sometimes, nothing is injured but the man who is thus annihilated; oftener the boat’s bow is knocked off, or the thigh-board, in which the headsman stands, is torn from its place and accompanies the body. But strangest of all is the circumstance, that in more instances than one, when the body has been recovered, not a single mark of violence is discernible; the man being stark dead.

“The vial! the vial!” shrieked Gabriel; he called the crew from further hunting; and the terrible event clothed him with added influence, as if he had fore-announced it specifically. He became a nameless terror aboard.

Mayhew ending, he asked Ahab if he intended to hunt the White Whale. “Aye,” said Ahab. Gabriel sprang up, pointing down: “Think of the blasphemer—dead, and down there!—beware of the blasphemer’s end!” Ahab turned aside. “Captain, my letter-bag—there is a letter for one of thy officers,” he said; and Starbuck fetched a sorely tumbled, mould-spotted missive, damp from a dark locker. Of such a letter, Death himself might well have been the post-boy. Ahab spelled it out: “Mr. Harry Macey, Ship Jeroboam.”—“It’s Macey—and he’s dead!” sighed Mayhew. “From his wife—let me have it.” “Nay, keep it thyself,” cried Gabriel to Ahab; “thou art soon going that way.” “Curses throttle thee!” yelled Ahab. Starbuck split the end of a long spade-pole to hold the letter clear of contagion; Ahab reached it toward the boat. The oarsmen, expectant, ceased rowing; the boat drifted astern; and, as if by magic, the letter ranged along Gabriel’s eager hand. He clutched, impaled it on a boat-knife, and sent it, thus loaded, back to the Pequod. It fell at Ahab’s feet. Gabriel screamed to give way, and the mutinous boat shot from our side.

The men, returning to the whale’s jacket, muttered many a strange hint of this wild affair, while the hoisted head jogged and the cut blubber bled.
