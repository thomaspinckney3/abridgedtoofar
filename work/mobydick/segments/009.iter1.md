If Queequeg among the polite astonished me by candle-light, daylight in New Bedford quickly accustomed me.

Nigh the docks, any seaport shows queer nondescripts; but in New Bedford actual cannibals stand chatting at corners—savages, some who yet carry on their bones unholy flesh. It makes a stranger stare.

Besides Feegeeans and the wild whaling specimens reeling unheeded, there arrive weekly scores of green Vermonters and New Hampshire men—stalwart fellows who have felled forests and now drop the axe for the whale-lance. No town-bred dandy matches a downright bumpkin—mowing in buckskin gloves in the dog-days—who, bespeaking a sea-outfit, orders bell-buttons to his waistcoats and straps to his canvas trowsers. Ah, poor Hay-Seed! how bitterly will burst those straps in the first howling gale, when thou art driven, straps, buttons, and all, down the throat of the tempest.

Think not the town has only harpooneers, cannibals, and bumpkins. It is a land of oil, true; but no Canaan. Yet nowhere in America will you find more patrician-like houses, parks and gardens more opulent, than in New Bedford. Go gaze upon the iron emblematical harpoons round yonder lofty mansion, and your question is answered. Yes; all these brave houses and flowery gardens came from the Atlantic, Pacific, and Indian oceans. One and all, they were harpooned and dragged up hither from the bottom of the sea. Can Herr Alexander perform a feat like that?

In New Bedford, fathers, they say, give whales for dowers to their daughters, and portion off their nieces with a few porpoises a-piece; every house has reservoirs of oil, and every night they recklessly burn their lengths in spermaceti candles.

In summer the town is sweet with fine maples; in August the horse-chestnuts, candelabra-wise, proffer cones of blossoms; art has superinduced bright terraces of flowers upon barren refuse rocks.

And the women bloom like their own red roses—carnation perennial as sunlight in the seventh heavens. Elsewhere match that bloom of theirs, ye cannot, save in Salem, where they tell me the young girls breathe such musk, their sailor sweethearts smell them miles off shore, as though they were drawing nigh the odorous Moluccas instead of the Puritanic sands.
