What the white whale was to Ahab has been hinted; what, at times, he was to me remains unsaid. It was the whiteness of the whale that above all things appalled me. How shall I render it? In many things whiteness refines and enhances; marbles, japonicas, pearls; banners and chargers and ermine and the alb of priests; white belts of wampum, the spotlessness of the divine in Persian flame and in visions of St. John: white robes, a great white throne, the Holy One white like wool. Yet for all these accumulated associations, there lurks an elusive something in the innermost idea of this hue that strikes more of panic to the soul than the redness of blood.

Divorced from kindly associations and coupled with what is terrible, whiteness heightens terror to the furthest bounds. Witness the white bear and the white shark—their ghastly whiteness imparting an abhorrent mildness to their dumb gloating. Not the heraldic tiger so staggers courage as the shrouded ghostliness of those pale marauders. The French, alluding to the white, silent stillness of death, call that shark Requin—from Requiem.

Bethink thee of the albatross: in its wondrous bodily whiteness chiefly lurks the spell; I remember the first I ever saw—regal, unspotted, hooked Roman bill; vast archangel wings as if to embrace some holy ark; its strange eyes peeped me to secrets that took hold of God. “Goney!” cried a sailor; but Coleridge had naught to do with that first awe. The truth was in the bird’s whiteness.

So, too, the White Steed of the Prairies—milk-white, small-headed, bluff-chested, an apparition imperial and archangelical—whose spiritual whiteness clothed him with divineness that enforced a nameless terror. And yet there are other whitenesses that lose strange glory and acquire a repelling chill: the Albino, invested in all-pervading pallor, more hideous than deformity; the White Squall; the White Hoods of Ghent masked in their snowy faction; the marble pallor of the dead, the shroud’s hue, and ghosts rising in a milk-white fog; and even Death, riding his pallid horse.

Name alone can summon a spectralness: White Tower; White Mountains; the White Sea; “the tall pale man” of the Hartz. Lima has taken the white veil—her woe frozen in rigid pallor that admits not the greenness of decay.

To the common apprehension this phenomenon is not prime in terror; yet often the very thing that appals is the whiteness. A mariner, called by night to view his ship sailing through a midnight sea of milky whiteness, feels a silent dread, as if shoals of combed white bears swam around him; the lead assures soundings, but heart and helm go down till blue water is under him again. The backwoodsman views with indifference a prairie sheeted with snow; but not so the sailor in Antarctic scenes, where boundless churchyards grin with icy monuments and splintered crosses.

“White-lead chapter,” sayest thou? “White flag hung out from a craven soul?” Tell me, then, why a strong colt, smelling only the musk of a buffalo robe, starts and snorts with bursting eyes though he never knew the goring bisons of Oregon. Here behold, even in a brute, the instinct of the demonism in the world. So to me the milky sea, the frost-festoons, the windrowed snows—are the shaken robe to the frightened colt.

But not yet the incantation is solved. Why should that which is emblem of the Christian’s Deity be also intensifier of the appalling? Is it that by its indefiniteness it shadows forth the heartless voids and immensities of the universe, stabbing us with annihilation at the sight of the milky way? Or that whiteness is not so much a colour as the visible absence of colour, and the concrete of all colours; hence the dumb blankness, full of meaning, in a wide landscape of snows—a colourless, all-colour of atheism from which we shrink? And when we remember the philosophers who tell us all earthly hues—sunset tinges, butterfly cheeks—are but subtile deceits laid on from without; that Nature paints like the harlot; and that the mystical cosmetic that produces every hue—Light itself—forever remains white; and that, operating unmediated, it would touch even roses with its own blank tinge—pondering all this, the palsied universe lies before us a leper; and like travellers in Lapland refusing coloured glasses, the infidel gazes himself blind at the monumental white shroud that wraps all the prospect round him. And of all these things the Albino whale was the symbol. Wonder ye then at the fiery hunt?
