Had you descended from the try-works to the forecastle, you had thought yourself in an illuminated shrine of canonized kings and counsellors: mariners lay in triangular vaults; lamps flashed on hooded eyes.

In merchantmen, oil is scarcer than queens’ milk; the sailor dresses, eats, and gropes in the dark. But the whaleman lives in light. He makes his berth an Aladdin’s lamp, and lays him down in it; so that in the pitchiest night the ship’s black hull still houses an illumination. He replenishes his handful of lamps at the copper cooler. He burns the purest oil, unmanufactured, unvitiated—a fluid unknown to solar, lunar, or astral contrivances ashore—sweet as April grass-butter; he hunts his own light to be sure of its freshness.
