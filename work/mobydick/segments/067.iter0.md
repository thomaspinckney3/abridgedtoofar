Stubb’s whale had been killed far from the ship; in a flat calm we made a tandem of three boats and, with eighteen men, thirty-six arms, and one hundred and eighty thumbs and fingers, slowly towed the inert, heaving bulk toward the Pequod. Three dim lights in the main-rigging guided us, and as we neared, Ahab dropped more lanterns, issued the customary orders for mooring, handed his light to a seaman, and vanished to his cabin. Though hot in the chase, he now showed some obscure impatience, as if the dead body only reminded him that Moby Dick yet lived, and a thousand other whales were nothing to his monomaniac end. Tied by the head to the stern and by the tail to the bows, the black hull of whale and ship lay close, yoked like colossal bullocks—one reclining, one standing.

A word for the flukes: the surest hold is the tail, but, being denser, it sinks beyond hand-reach; so a small line with a float and a mid-weight is led cleverly around, girdling the mass so the chain can follow and lock at the smallest part, hard by the lobes.

If Ahab grew still, Stubb—flush with conquest—bustled. “A steak, a steak, ere I sleep! You, Daggoo! overboard, and cut me one from his small!” About midnight it was cooked; standing at the capstan-head, lit by sperm-lanterns, Stubb set to his spermaceti supper, while thousands of sharks, wallowing blackly, turned on their backs to gouge out globular mouthfuls the size of a human head, leaving countersunk hollows as by a carpenter’s screw. The slapping of their tails jarred sleepers in their bunks. If ever you saw that sight, you would suspend judgment on the propriety of devil-worship.

But Stubb heeded neither sharks nor their smackings. “Cook, cook!—where’s that old Fleece?” The old black hobbled from the galley, tongs for a cane, and stood bowed before Stubb’s sideboard.

“Cook,” cried Stubb, “this steak is overdone. Don’t I always say a whale-steak must be tough? See those sharks prefer it tough and rare! Go preach to ’em: help themselves civilly, and keep quiet.” Taking a lantern, Fleece leaned over the bulwarks and began: “Fellow-critters: I’se ordered here to say dat you must stop dat dam noise. Massa Stubb say you can fill your dam bellies up to de hatchings; but you must stop dat dam racket!” “Cook!”—a slap—“you mustn’t swear when you’re preaching.” “Well, den—Belubed fellow-critters: you is all sharks, and by natur wery woracious; but to gobiern dat wicked natur, dat is de pint. You is sharks, sartin; but if you gobiern de shark in you, why den you be angel; for all angel is not’ing more dan de shark well goberned. Don’t be tearin’ de blubber out your neighbour’s mout. And, by Gor, none on you has de right to dat whale; dat whale belong to some one else.” Soon he despaired: “No use a-preachin’ to such dam g’uttons till dare bellies is full; and when full, dey sink on de coral and can’t hear not’ing, for eber and eber.” “Give the benediction, Fleece.” He lifted his hands over the mob: “Cussed fellow-critters! Kick up de damndest row as ever you can; fill your dam’ bellies ’till dey bust—and den die.”

“Now, cook—attend.” Stubb plied his fork. He cross-questioned Fleece on his age and birthplace; then, with sudden gravity, “Do you belong to the church?”—“Passed one once in Cape-Down.” Ahab-like catechism followed on heaven: Fleece pointed “up dere” with his tongs; Stubb mocked the lubber’s-hole to glory—“you don’t get there except the regular way, round by the rigging.” Then orders: “Next time, hold the steak in one hand, and show a live coal to it with the other; that done, dish it. To-morrow, when we are cutting in, stand by for the tips of his fins—put them in pickle. Have the ends of the flukes soused.” As Fleece limped off: “Cutlets for supper to-morrow in the mid-watch!—Avast! Whale-balls for breakfast—don’t forget.” And old Fleece, vanishing to his hammock, muttered: “Wish, by gor! whale eat him, ’stead of him eat whale. I’m bressed if he ain’t more of shark dan Massa Shark hisself.”

Outside, the sharks’ feast went on beneath the mild stars, and the ship and her yoked dead lay in their black communion till morning.
