South-eastward the long peninsula of Malacca runs, and from it in file stretch Sumatra, Java, Bally, and Timor—a vast green rampart, pierced here and there by sally-ports for ships and whales; conspicuous among them, the Straits of Sunda, dividing Sumatra from Java, buttressed by bold green Java Head like the central gateway to some walled empire rich in spices, silks, jewels, gold, and ivory. No fortresses frown there; the Orientals do not demand lowered topsails, yet they do not renounce more solid tribute.

With a fair wind the Pequod drew nigh these straits; Ahab purposed thence to the Javan sea, then northward by Sperm-Whale waters, inshore by the Philippines, and so to Japan for the great season; and after circling all known grounds, to descend upon the Line, where, foiled elsewhere, he firmly counted upon giving battle to Moby Dick. But in this zoned quest, does he touch no land? Nay. So Ahab. Mark this, too, in the whaler. While other hulls are loaded down with alien stuff, to be transferred to foreign wharves; the world-wandering whale-ship carries no cargo but herself and crew, their weapons and their wants. She is ballasted with utilities; not altogether with unusable pig-lead and kentledge. She carries years’ water in her—clear old prime Nantucket water; so that though other ships touch a score of ports, the world-wandering whaler may not sight one grain of soil. Tell them a new flood had come—“Well, boys, here’s the ark!”

Many Sperm Whales had been captured off Java; yet though the palmy cliffs loomed to starboard and the air smelt of fresh cinnamon, not a jet was descried—till at last, hailed from aloft, a spectacle of singular magnificence saluted us. Of late, hunted over all oceans, Sperm Whales now often herd in immense caravans; thus you may sail for weeks without a spout, and then be suddenly saluted by what seems thousands on thousands.

Broad on both bows, two or three miles off, in a vast semicircle, a continuous chain of jets up-played and sparkled; not the Right Whale’s split boughs, but the Sperm Whale’s single forward-slanting curled bush of mist. Rising on a sea-hill, we beheld that host of vapory spouts, through a bluish haze, like a thousand cheerful chimneys of some dense metropolis descried on a balmy autumnal morning. As armies hastening through a mountain defile, so did this fleet seem hurrying the Straits; their wings contracting, they swam on in a solid crescentic centre.

Crowding all sail, stun-sail on stun-sail, we pressed after; and who could tell whether, in that congregated caravan, Moby Dick himself might not temporarily be swimming, like the worshipped white-elephant in the coronation procession of the Siamese!—when of a sudden Tashtego’s voice hailed our wake. Corresponding to the crescent in our van, we beheld another astern—detached white vapors hovering like spouts that did not quite come and go. Ahab levelled his glass, wheeled upon his pivot—“Aloft there, rig whips and buckets to wet the sails;—Malays, sir, and after us!”

But the swift Pequod, herself in hot chase, found these tawny philanthropists but riding-whips and rowels. Pacing to and fro, Ahab in one turn beheld the monsters he chased; in the other, the bloodthirsty pirates chasing him; and glancing on the green walls of the watery defile—the gate to his vengeance—he was both chasing and being chased to his deadly end, and not only that, but a herd of remorseless wild pirates and inhuman atheistical devils were infernally cheering him on with their curses. When these conceits had passed, his brow was left gaunt and ribbed, like a black sand beach gnawn by stormy tide.

The crew troubled little; steadily dropping the pirates astern, we shot by Cockatoo Point and emerged upon broad waters. Still driving in the whales’ wake, at length they seemed abating; we neared them; the wind dying, word was passed to spring to the boats. But no sooner did the herd, notified by some wonderful instinct of the keels after them, rally and form in close battalions, their spouts like flashing lines of stacked bayonets, than they moved on with redoubled velocity.

Stripped to shirts and drawers we sprang to the white-ash. After hours’ pulling we were for renouncing the chase, when a general pausing commotion showed them gallied. The compact columns broke into rout, expanding in vast irregular circles, aimlessly swimming, their short thick spoutings betraying panic; some, paralysed, floated like dismantled water-logged ships. Best, therefore, withhold any amazement at the strangely gallied whales before us, for there is no folly of the beasts of the earth which is not infinitely outdone by the madness of men.

The herd, though much in motion, neither advanced nor retreated, but as a whole remained; the boats separated for lone whales on the outskirts. In three minutes Queequeg’s harpoon flew; the fish, running with us like light, steered straight for the heart of the herd. Thus dragged deeper and deeper into the frantic shoal, we bade adieu to circumspect life and only existed in a delirious throb. Our boat, mobbed by to-and-fro rushing monsters, was like a ship among ice-isles in tempest. Not daunted, Queequeg steered manfully; Starbuck stood in the bows, lance in hand, pricking aside whatever he could reach; the oarsmen shouted—“Out of the way, Commodore!—Hard down with your tail, there!”

All whaleboats carry druggs—crossed wooden blocks with a long line looped to a harpoon—to “wing” more whales than can be killed at once. Two we darted; the fettered whales ran off, cramped like malefactors with chain and ball. On flinging the third, the block caught under a seat, tore it out, and wounded both sides of the boat; drawers and shirts stuffed in stayed the leaks.

Advancing inward, our whale’s way diminished; the dire disorders waned. When the harpoon drew out and the tow-ward whale vanished, with his tapering momentum we glided into the innermost heart of the shoal—as if from a mountain torrent we had slid into a serene valley lake. This central expanse presented the sleek—a smooth satin-like surface; we were in the enchanted calm said to lurk at every commotion’s heart. In the distracted distance the outer circles went round and round, pods shoulder to shoulder, as if a Titanic circus-rider might arch the middle backs. Hemmed by a living wall, we must watch for a breach. Small tame cows and calves—the women and children—came snuffling like household dogs, touching our gunwales; Queequeg patted their foreheads; Starbuck scratched their backs with his lance, yet refrained from darting.

Far beneath this wondrous surface another world appeared: nursing mothers and those large with young floated suspended; the lake was singularly transparent; and as human infants while suckling will calmly and fixedly gaze away from the breast, as if leading two different lives at the time; and while yet drawing mortal nourishment, be still spiritually feasting upon some unearthly reminiscence;—even so did the young of these whales seem looking up towards us, but not at us, as if we were but a bit of Gulfweed in their new-born sight. One little fellow, hardly a day old, some fourteen feet long, six round, frisked; his body seemed scarce yet recovered from that irksome position it had so lately occupied in the maternal reticule; where, tail to head, and all ready for the final spring, the unborn whale lies bent like a Tartar’s bow. The delicate side-fins, and the palms of his flukes, still freshly retained the plaited crumpled appearance of a baby’s ears newly arrived from foreign parts. “Line! line!” cried Queequeg; “him fast!”—and Starbuck saw long coils of Madame Leviathan’s umbilical cord, tethering the cub to its dam. We saw young Leviathan amours in the deep. The milk is very sweet and rich; it has been tasted by man; it might do well with strawberries. When overflowing with mutual esteem, the whales salute _more hominum_.

And though surrounded by circle upon circle of affrights, these creatures at the centre freely and fearlessly indulged in all peaceful concernments; yea, serenely revelled in dalliance and delight. But even so, amid the tornadoed Atlantic of my being, do I myself still for ever centrally disport in mute calm; and while ponderous planets of unwaning woe revolve round me, deep down and deep inland there I still bathe me in eternal mildness of joy.

Meanwhile, frantic spectacles afar showed the other boats drugging on the frontier. At last there came one more appalling sight. A whale, hamstrung at the tail-tendon by a cutting-spade and broken away, had entangled himself in the harpoon-line; the spade, working loose, was tossed by his flailing tail, wounding and murdering his own comrades. Recalled from fright, the herd on our lake’s margin crowded; the sleek heaved; nurseries vanished; the central circles thickened; a low hum advanced; then like the block-ice of the Hudson breaking up, the entire host came tumbling upon their inner centre, to pile in one common mountain. Instantly Starbuck took the stern—“Oars! Oars!—gripe your oars, and clutch your souls, now!—Shove him off, you Queequeg—the whale there!—prick him!—hit him! Stand up—stay so! Spring, men—pull; never mind their backs—scrape them!”

Jammed between two vast bulks like a narrow Dardanelles, we shot by desperate endeavor into a fleeting opening, watched for another, and, after many hair-breadth escapes, at last glided into what had been an outer circle, now crossed by random whales all making for one centre—Queequeg’s hat whisked clean from his head by a sudden pair of flukes.

Riotous commotion resolved itself to system; clumped in one dense body, they renewed their onward flight with augmented fleetness. Further pursuit was useless. The boats lingered to pick up drugged whales and a waifed fish of Flask’s. Yet the result proved that sagacious saying—the more whales the less fish. Of all the drugged whales only one was captured; the rest escaped for the time, to be taken, as will hereafter be seen, by some other craft than the Pequod.
