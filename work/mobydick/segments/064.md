If to Starbuck the Squid was portent, to Queequeg it was omen. “When you see him ’quid,” said the savage, honing his harpoon in the hoisted bow, “then you quick see him ’parm whale.”

The next day was still and sultry; the crew, spell-bound by vacancy, drowsed. It was my turn at the foremast-head; with my shoulders leaning against the slackened royal shrouds, to and fro I idly swayed in what seemed an enchanted air. No resolution could withstand it; in that dreamy mood, losing all consciousness, at last my soul went out of my body; though my body still continued to sway as a pendulum will, long after the power which first moved it is withdrawn. Ere forgetfulness altogether came over me, I had noticed that the seamen at the main and mizzen-mast-heads were already drowsy. So that at last all three of us lifelessly swung from the spars, and for every swing that we made there was a nod from below from the slumbering helmsman. The waves, too, nodded their indolent crests; and across the wide trance of the sea, east nodded to west, and the sun over all. Suddenly bubbles seemed bursting beneath my closed eyes; like vices my hands grasped the shrouds; some invisible, gracious agency preserved me; with a shock I came back to life. And lo! close under our lee, not forty fathoms off, a gigantic Sperm Whale rolled like the capsized hull of a frigate, his broad glossy back glistening like a mirror; lazily undulating, and ever and anon tranquilly spouting his vapory jet, like a portly burgher smoking his pipe of a warm afternoon. But that pipe, poor whale, was thy last. As if struck by some enchanter’s wand, the sleepy ship and every sleeper started; voices from all parts, with the three notes from aloft, hailed his regular spout.

“Clear away the boats! Luff!” cried Ahab, dashing the helm down ere the helmsman could touch it. The whale turned majestically to leeward with steady tranquillity, making few ripples. Thinking him not yet alarmed, Ahab ordered no oar to be used, no word but whispers. So, seated like Ontario Indians, we silently paddled. Presently, the monster perpendicularly flitted his tail forty feet and sank. “There go flukes!” cried all. Stubb struck his match, lit his pipe—the respite of a sounding. When he rose again, Stubb’s boat had the vantage; paddles were dropped; oars came loudly into play. Still puffing, Stubb cheered on.

A mighty change had come over the fish—“head out,” obliquely elevating that buoyant, sharp-cutting head. “Start her, start her, my men! Don’t hurry—take plenty of time—but start her; start her like thunder-claps, that’s all,” spluttered Stubb through smoke. “Start her, now; give ’em the long and strong stroke, Tashtego. Start her, Tash, my boy—start her, all; but keep cool—cucumbers is the word—easy, easy—only start her like grim death and grinning devils, and raise the buried dead perpendicular out of their graves, boys—that’s all. Start her!” “Woo-hoo! Wa-hee!” screamed the Gay-Header in reply, raising some old war-whoop to the skies; as every oarsman in the strained boat involuntarily bounced forward with the one tremendous leading stroke which the eager Indian gave. But his wild screams were answered by others quite as wild. “Kee-hee! Kee-hee!” yelled Daggoo, straining forwards and backwards on his seat, like a pacing tiger in his cage. “Ka-la! Koo-loo!” howled Queequeg, as if smacking his lips over a mouthful of Grenadier’s steak.

“Stand up, Tashtego!—give it to him!” The harpoon flew. “Stern all!” Back-water! Something went hot and hissing along every wrist—the magical line. Stubb had caught two extra turns round the loggerhead; a hempen blue smoke jetted up and mingled with his pipe. The blistering line passed through both his hands—like holding an enemy’s sharp two-edged sword by the blade, while he strives to wrest it free. “Wet the line! wet the line!” he cried. The tub-oarsman dashed sea-water with his hat; more turns; the line held; the boat flew like a shark all fins. Stubb and Tashtego changed places—stem for stern. From the vibrating line, tighter than a harpstring, you’d have thought the craft had two keels—one cleaving water, the other air; a cascade at the bows; the tall Gay-Header crouched low to bring down his centre of gravity.

“Haul in—haul in!” Hands pulled the boat up even while towed on; ranging by his flank, Stubb, knee in the clumsy cleat, darted dart after dart; at the word, the boat alternately sterned from the horrible wallow, then ranged for another fling. The red tide poured from all sides like brooks down a hill; he rolled not in brine but in blood, which bubbled and seethed for furlongs. The slanting sun on that crimson pond sent back its reflection into every face, so all glowed like red men. Jet after jet of white smoke shot from his spiracle; vehement puff after puff from Stubb’s mouth; at each dart he hauled in upon his crooked lance, straightened it by blows against the gunwale, and sent it again.

“Pull up—pull up!—close to!” Reaching far over, Stubb slowly churned his long sharp lance into the fish, carefully churning, as if feeling after a gold watch swallowed, fearful of breaking it ere hooking it out. That gold watch was the innermost life. Struck—he started into his “flurry,” horribly wallowing in blood, overwrapping himself in mad, boiling spray; the imperilled craft, dropping astern, struggled blindly from that frenzied twilight to clear air. Abating, he rolled out again; surging; spasmodically dilating and contracting his spout-hole with sharp, cracking respirations. At last, gush after gush of clotted red gore, purple lees of wine, shot into the frighted air and ran down his motionless flanks. His heart had burst. “He’s dead, Mr. Stubb,” said Daggoo. “Yes; both pipes smoked out!” Stubb scattered his dead ashes over the water, and thoughtfully eyed the vast corpse he had made.
