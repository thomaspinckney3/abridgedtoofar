By the Bashee isles we emerged at last upon the great South Sea. There is, one knows not what sweet mystery about this sea, whose gently awful stirrings seem to speak of some hidden soul beneath; for here, millions of mixed shades and shadows, drowned dreams, somnambulisms, reveries; all that we call lives and souls, lie dreaming, dreaming, still; tossing like slumberers in their beds; the ever-rolling waves but made so by their restlessness. It rolls the midmost waters of the world, the Indian ocean and Atlantic but its arms; the same waves wash Californian towns and lave Asiatic lands older than Abraham; between float coral archipelagoes and impenetrable Japans. Thus this mysterious, divine Pacific zones the world, makes all coasts one bay; seems the tide-beating heart of earth. Lifted by those eternal swells, you needs must own the seductive god, bowing your head to Pan.

But few thoughts of Pan stirred Ahab’s brain; standing like iron beside the mizen, with one nostril he unthinkingly snuffed the sugary musk from the Bashee isles (in whose sweet woods mild lovers must be walking), with the other consciously inhaled the salt breath of the new-found sea wherein the hated White Whale must be swimming. Launched at length upon these almost final waters, the old man’s purpose intensified itself. His firm lips met like the lips of a vice; the Delta of his forehead’s veins swelled like overladen brooks; in his very sleep, his ringing cry ran through the hull, “Stern all! the White Whale spouts thick blood!”
