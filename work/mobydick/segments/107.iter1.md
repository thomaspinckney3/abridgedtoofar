From his mighty bulk the whale affords a most congenial theme whereon to enlarge, amplify, and generally expatiate. Would you, you could not compress him. By good rights he should only be treated of in imperial folio. Having already described him in most of his present habitatory and anatomical peculiarities, it now remains to magnify him in an archæological, fossiliferous, and antediluvian point of view. Fain am I to stagger to this emprise under the weightiest words of the dictionary. And here be it said, that whenever it has been convenient to consult one in the course of these dissertations, I have invariably used a huge quarto edition of Johnson, expressly purchased for that purpose; because that famous lexicographer’s uncommon personal bulk more fitted him to compile a lexicon to be used by a whale author like me.

One often hears of writers that rise and swell with their subject, though it may seem but an ordinary one. How, then, with me, writing of this Leviathan? Unconsciously my chirography expands into placard capitals. Give me a condor’s quill! Give me Vesuvius’ crater for an inkstand! Friends, hold my arms! For in the mere act of penning my thoughts of this Leviathan, they weary me, and make me faint with their outreaching comprehensiveness of sweep, as if to include the whole circle of the sciences, and all the generations of whales, and men, and mastodons, past, present, and to come, with all the revolving panoramas of empire on earth, and throughout the whole universe, not excluding its suburbs. Such, and so magnifying, is the virtue of a large and liberal theme! We expand to its bulk. To produce a mighty book, you must choose a mighty theme. No great and enduring volume can ever be written on the flea, though many there be who have tried it.

Ere entering upon the subject of Fossil Whales, I present my credentials as a geologist, by stating that in my miscellaneous time I have been a stone-mason, and also a great digger of ditches, canals and wells, wine-vaults, cellars, and cisterns of all sorts. Likewise, by way of preliminary, I remind the reader, that while earlier strata show monsters now all but extinct, the subsequent relics called the Tertiary formations seem intercepted links; all the Fossil Whales hitherto discovered belong to the Tertiary period; and though none precisely answer to any known species of the present time, they are sufficiently akin to justify their taking rank as Cetacean fossils.

Detached broken fossils of pre-adamite whales—fragments of bones and skeletons—have, within these thirty years, turned up across Europe and America; among the more curious, part of a skull disinterred in 1779 in the Rue Dauphine in Paris, and bones found when excavating the great docks of Antwerp in Napoleon’s time. Cuvier pronounced these fragments to have belonged to some utterly unknown Leviathanic species.

But by far the most wonderful of all Cetacean relics was the almost complete vast skeleton of an extinct monster, found in 1842, on the plantation of Judge Creagh, in Alabama. The awe-stricken slaves took it for the bones of a fallen angel; the Alabama doctors declared it a huge reptile, and named it Basilosaurus. But specimen bones carried to Owen, the English Anatomist, proved it a whale, though of a departed species—a significant illustration that the skeleton furnishes but little clue to the shape of his fully invested body. So Owen rechristened the monster Zeuglodon, and pronounced it, in substance, one of the most extraordinary creatures which the mutations of the globe have blotted out of existence.

When I stand among these mighty Leviathan skeletons, skulls, tusks, jaws, ribs, and vertebræ—partially resembling existing sea-monsters, yet bearing, on the other hand, akinities to annihilated antichronical seniors—I am, by a flood, borne back to that wondrous period, ere time itself can be said to have begun; for time began with man. Here Saturn’s grey chaos rolls over me, and I obtain dim, shuddering glimpses into those Polar eternities; when wedged bastions of ice pressed hard upon what are now the Tropics; and in all the 25,000 miles of this world’s circumference, not an inhabitable hand’s breadth of land was visible. Then the whole world was the whale’s; and, king of creation, he left his wake along the present lines of the Andes and the Himmalehs. Who can show a pedigree like Leviathan? Ahab’s harpoon had shed older blood than the Pharaoh’s. Methuselah seems a school-boy. I look round to shake hands with Shem. I am horror-struck at this antemosaic, unsourced existence of the unspeakable terrors of the whale, which, having been before all time, must needs exist after all humane ages are over.

But not alone has this Leviathan left his pre-adamite traces in the stereotype plates of nature, and in limestone and marl bequeathed his ancient bust; upon Egyptian tablets, too, whose antiquity claims for them an almost fossiliferous character, we find the unmistakable print of his fin. In an apartment of the great temple of Denderah, upon a granite ceiling, a sculptured and painted planisphere abounds in centaurs, griffins, and dolphins; gliding among them, old Leviathan swam as of yore—there swimming in that planisphere, centuries before Solomon was cradled.

Nor must there be omitted another strange attestation of the whale’s antiquity, set down by the venerable John Leo, the old Barbary traveller: a Sea-side Temple whose Rafters and Beams were Whale-Bones; a Rib arched so high a man upon a camel’s back could not reach its crown; the Common People imputing a secret Power of God that no Whale could pass it without immediate death—though, in truth, rocks on either side shot two miles into the sea and wounded them. This Rib, said to have lain there a hundred years, they kept for a Miracle; and some did not scruple to assert that the Prophet Jonas was cast forth by the Whale at the base of the Temple. In this Afric Temple of the Whale I leave you, reader, and if you be a Nantucketer, and a whaleman, you will silently worship there.
