Next morning Stubb told Flask his dream: Ahab’s ivory leg kicked him; kicking back, he kicked his own leg off; Ahab seemed a pyramid and he, like a blazing fool, kept kicking at it. But—Flask, you know how curious all dreams are—through all his rage he thought the insult less than it seemed: “Why, it’s not a real leg, only a false leg. There’s a mighty difference between a living thump and a dead thump. That’s what makes a blow from the hand, Flask, fifty times more savage to bear than a blow from a cane. The living member—that makes the living insult, my little man.” And still he thought, “it was only a playful cudgelling—in fact, a whaleboning—not a base kick.” And the small foot’s end—“whittled down to a point.”

Then a badger-haired old merman with a hump slewed him round, forever muttering “Wise Stubb, wise Stubb,” a sort of eating of his own gums like a chimney hag, and fell to arguing the insult. Kicked with an ivory leg, and by a great man—“It’s an honor; I consider it an honor. In old England the greatest lords think it great glory to be slapped by a queen, and made garter-knights of; but, be your boast, Stubb, that ye were kicked by old Ahab, and made a wise man of. Be kicked by him; account his kicks honors; and on no account kick back; for you can’t help yourself, wise Stubb. Don’t you see that pyramid?”

Stubb woke in his hammock. “What do you think of that, Flask?” “Foolish,” said Flask. “May be,” said Stubb, “but it’s made a wise man of me. D’ye see Ahab yonder? Best let the old man alone; never speak to him, whatever he says.—Hark!”

“Mast-head, there! Look sharp, all of ye! There are whales hereabouts! If ye see a white one, split your lungs for him!”

“A white whale—did ye mark that, man?” whispered Stubb. “There’s something special in the wind. Ahab has that that’s bloody on his mind. But, mum; he comes this way.”
