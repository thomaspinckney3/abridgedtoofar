Next morning, Monday, after disposing of the embalmed head to a barber for a block, I settled our bill—with Queequeg’s money; landlord and boarders grinned at our sudden intimacy. We borrowed a wheelbarrow for my carpet-bag and his canvas sack and hammock, and down we went to the little Nantucket packet, the Moss, wheeling by turns, Queequeg now and then stopping to adjust the sheath on his harpoon barbs. Why lug it ashore, I asked, when ships find their own irons? He said he loved his own: of assured stuff, well tried, intimate with whales’ hearts; as inland mowers go with their own scythes, so he with his steel.

He told of the first wheelbarrow he ever saw at Sag Harbor—how, lashing his chest upon it, he shouldered the barrow and marched up the wharf. “Didn’t the people laugh?” I cried. He answered with another story: at Rokovoko, where the High Priest first dips his consecrating fingers in the calabash before the wedding feast, a punctilious sea-captain, thinking himself above a mere king in the king’s own house, coolly washed his hands in the sacred punchbowl. “Now,” said Queequeg, “what you tink now?—Didn’t our people laugh?”

Aboard at last, the Moss slipped down the Acushnet. New Bedford rose; casks on casks; ships silent; forges to melt the pitch—one perilous voyage ending only to begin another; such the endlessness, yea, the intolerableness of all earthly effort. Gaining open water, the breeze freshened; the little Moss tossed foam like a colt his snortings. How I snuffed that Tartar air!—how I spurned that turnpike earth!—that common highway all over dented with the marks of slavish heels and hoofs; and turned me to admire the magnanimity of the sea which will permit no records. At the same foam-fountain, Queequeg seemed to drink and reel with me. His dusky nostrils swelled apart; he showed his filed and pointed teeth. On, on we flew; and our offing gained, the Moss did homage to the blast; ducked and dived her bows as a slave before the Sultan. Sideways leaning, we sideways darted; every ropeyarn tingling like a wire; the two tall masts buckling like Indian canes in land tornadoes.

Passengers—boobies and bumpkins, green to the core—jeered at our companionship, as though a white man were anything more dignified than a whitewashed negro. Queequeg caught one of these young saplings mimicking him behind his back. I thought the bumpkin’s hour of doom was come. Dropping his harpoon, the brawny savage caught him in his arms, and by an almost miraculous dexterity and strength, sent him high up bodily into the air; then slightly tapping his stern in mid-somerset, the fellow landed with bursting lungs upon his feet; whereupon Queequeg, turning his back upon him, lighted his tomahawk pipe and passed it to me for a puff. The Captain stormed; Queequeg twisted his tattooed face: “him bevy small-e fish-e; Queequeg no kill-e so small-e fish-e; Queequeg kill-e big whale!” Then the mainsail’s weather-sheet parted; the boom flew from side to side, sweeping the after deck; the greenhorn went overboard; all hands panic-struck. Queequeg dropped to his knees, crawled under the boom’s path, whipped hold of a rope, secured one end, and flinging the other like a lasso, caught it round the spar as it swept over his head; at the next jerk it was trapped, and all was safe. The schooner luffed; while the hands cleared away the stern boat, Queequeg, stripped to the waist, sprang from the side with a long living arc, swam through the freezing foam, then shot perpendicularly, glanced around, and dived. He rose again, one arm striking out, the other dragging a lifeless form. They were picked up; the bumpkin restored; the captain begged his pardon. From that hour I clove to Queequeg like a barnacle—yea, till poor Queequeg took his last long dive.

Was there ever such unconsciousness? He did not seem to think that he at all deserved a medal from the Humane and Magnanimous Societies. He only asked for water—fresh water—something to wipe the brine off; then, dry clothes, pipe, and mild eyes that seemed to say—“It’s a mutual, joint-stock world, in all meridians. We cannibals must help these Christians.”
