Merry Christmas.

Towards noon, the riggers were dismissed, the Pequod hauled from the wharf; thoughtful Charity came off with her last gifts—a night-cap for Stubb and a spare Bible for the steward. Then Peleg and Bildad issued forth.

“Now, Mr. Starbuck, are you sure everything is right? Captain Ahab is all ready—just spoke to him. Call all hands—blast ’em!” cried Peleg.

“No need of profane words, friend Peleg,” said Bildad; “away, Starbuck, and do our bidding.”

How now! Here upon the very point of starting for the voyage, Captain Peleg and Captain Bildad were going it with a high hand on the quarter-deck, just as if they were to be joint-commanders at sea, as well as to all appearances in port. And, as for Captain Ahab, no sign of him was yet to be seen; only, they said he was in the cabin; and as, not yet completely recovered—so they said—and as it was not his proper business, but the pilot’s, to get the ship under weigh, therefore, Captain Ahab stayed below. And all this seemed natural enough; especially as in the merchant service many captains never show themselves on deck for a considerable time after heaving up the anchor.

Peleg did most of the roaring; Bildad, a licensed pilot, looked forward for the anchor, now and then chanting a dismal stave of psalmody to cheer the windlass hands, who roared a chorus about the girls in Booble Alley. Nevertheless, not three days previous, Bildad had told them that no profane songs would be allowed on board the Pequod, particularly in getting under weigh; and Charity, his sister, had placed a small choice copy of Watts in each seaman’s berth.

“Man the capstan! Blood and thunder!—jump!” Peleg cried, stumping along the bar, using his leg very freely. I paused a moment on my handspike, thinking of the perils we both ran in starting with such a devil for a pilot; I was comforting myself, however, with the thought that in pious Bildad might be found some salvation, spite of his seven hundred and seventy-seventh lay; when I felt a sudden sharp poke in my rear—my first kick. “Spring, thou sheep-head!—spring thy eyes out!” roared Peleg, while imperturbable Bildad led off with his psalm.

At last the anchor up, sails set—we glided out. It was a short, cold Christmas; the freezing spray cased us in ice as in polished armor. The long rows of teeth on the bulwarks glistened in the moonlight; and like the white ivory tusks of some huge elephant, vast curving icicles depended from the bows. Lank Bildad headed the first watch, and ever and anon his steady notes were heard:

“Sweet fields beyond the swelling flood,
Stand dressed in living green.
So to the Jews old Canaan stood,
While Jordan rolled between.”

Never did those sweet words sound more sweetly. Spite of this frigid winter night in the boisterous Atlantic, spite of my wet feet and wetter jacket, there was yet, it then seemed to me, many a pleasant haven in store.

We gained an offing; the pilots were needed no more; the stout sail-boat ranged alongside. Loath to depart a ship bound beyond both stormy Capes, poor old Bildad lingered long; paced the deck with anxious strides; ran down into the cabin; again came on deck, and looked to windward; looked towards the wide and endless waters; looked towards the land; and at last grasped stout Peleg’s hand, and, holding up a lantern, as if to say, “Nevertheless, friend Peleg, I can stand it.” Peleg took it more like a philosopher; yet when the lantern came too near, a tear twinkled.

“Captain Bildad—come, old shipmate, we must go. Back the main-yard! Boat ahoy!—Luck to ye, Starbuck—Mr. Stubb—Mr. Flask—good-bye and good luck to ye all—and this day three years I’ll have a hot supper smoking for ye in old Nantucket. Hurrah and away!”

“God bless ye, and have ye in His holy keeping,” murmured Bildad. “I hope ye’ll have fine weather now, so that Captain Ahab may soon be moving among ye—a pleasant sun is all he needs. Mind the cooper—don’t waste spare staves. Don’t whale it too much a’ Lord’s days; but don’t miss a fair chance either. Beware of fornication if ye touch at the islands. The sail-needles are in the green locker! Don’t keep that cheese too long down in the hold, Mr. Starbuck; it’ll spoil. Be careful with the butter—twenty cents the pound it was, and mind ye, if—”

“Come, come, Captain Bildad; away!” cried Peleg, hurrying him over.

Ship and boat diverged; a screaming gull flew overhead; the two hulls wildly rolled; we gave three heavy-hearted cheers, and blindly plunged like fate into the lone Atlantic.
