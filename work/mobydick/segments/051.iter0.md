The phantoms—so they then seemed—flitted noiseless on the starboard side, casting loose the tackles of the captain’s spare boat. Tall and swart by its bows stood one with a single white tooth wickedly protruding from steel-like lips; a funereal Chinese jacket and wide black trowsers clothed him; but, strangest of all, a glistening white plaited turban crowned the ebonness—his own living hair coiled round and round. Less swart, his companions were tiger-yellow men of the Manillas—fellows whom some honest mariners hold for the devil’s spies afloat.

“All ready there, Fedallah?” cried Ahab.

“Ready,” hissed the white-turbaned old man.

“Lower away then; d’ye hear?—Lower away there, I say!”

With the sheaves whirring, three boats wallowed into the sea; the sailors, goat-like, leaped to them from the rolling hull. Hardly had they shot clear when a fourth keel rounded under the stern—Ahab’s—rowed by the five strangers. He stood erect, hailing, “Spread yourselves!” But, with eyes riveted upon Fedallah and his crew, the other boats stared.

“Never heed yonder yellow boys,” cried Flask, sweeping his great steering-oar. “There!—there again! There she blows right ahead, boys!”

“What think ye of those yellow boys, sir?” bawled Stubb towards Starbuck, whose face, flinty-set, never turned.

“Smuggled aboard,” came the grave quick whisper; “but never mind, Mr. Stubb—(seethe her, my lads!)—duty and profit, hand in hand!”

“Aye, aye; I thought as much,” soliloquized Stubb. “The White Whale’s at the bottom of it. Well then—give way, men! It ain’t the White Whale to-day!”

To soothe his uneasy crew he preached in that queer mingled fun-and-fury which was his: “Pull, pull, my fine hearts-alive; pull, my little ones!—the more the merrier. So—so—long and strong! The devil fetch ye, ye ragamuffins, ye’re asleep! Draw knives and pull with the blades between your teeth! That’s it—my steel-bits—start her, my silver-spoons!”

I set down Stubb’s exordium thus large, for it shows his religion of rowing: fiercest words salted with laughter; fury a mere spice to the joke; the idle indolent yawn of the man bewitching the crew to deadly work.

Meanwhile, ranging most to windward, Ahab’s boat kept foremost; a potent crew pulled him—those tiger-yellow creatures of his rose and fell like five trip-hammers; Fedallah, stripped to the waist, bent at the harpooneer oar; and Ahab, one arm thrown back like a fencer’s, steered as in a thousand lowerings ere the White Whale had torn him. All at once his outstretched arm signaled; the five oars peaked; boat and crew sat motionless. The whales had irregularly settled; from his closer ken, Ahab had seen it.

“Every man look out along his oars!” cried Starbuck. “Thou, Queequeg, stand up!”

The savage sprang to the triangular bow-box and gazed intently. Astern, Starbuck himself, cool and adroit, balanced on the stern platform, silently eyeing the vast blue eye of the sea. Near by, little King-Post, not content with the loggerhead’s palm-breadth, mounted Daggoo’s shoulders; the gigantic negro rolled his noble form to the sea’s rhythm, while flaxen Flask, on his back, looked a snow-flake on basalt.

Stubb, for his part, would solace the lull with his pipe; but even as he struck fire on his palm, Tashtego, star-staring to windward, dropped like light to his thwart and cried, “Down, down all, and give way!—there they are!”

To a landsman nothing were visible then but a troubled bit of greenish-white water and thin puffs of vapor blowing to leeward like scud. Yet under that wavering air, and half beneath a thin sheet of sea, the whales swam; their spoutings their forerunning couriers.

All four boats drove for the dancing whiteness. “Pull, pull, my good boys,” whispered Starbuck with those keen needle-eyes of his dead ahead—his silence cut now and then by a whisper harsh with command or soft with entreaty. Not so Flask: he raved and stamped on his hat, flinging it seaward, roaring to be beached on their black backs. “Look at that chap now,” drawled Stubb between his unlighted pipe. “He’s got fits—that Flask has. Merrily, babes—pull and burst your livers, but take it easy!”

What words Ahab poured into his tiger crew are best omitted here; only infidel sharks might give ear when with tornado brow and foam-glued lips he leaped after his prey.

The sight was full of awe—the omnipotent swells shouldering our low gunwales; the tipped knife-edge pauses and sled-like slides; cries of headsmen and harpooneers; the shuddering gasps of oarsmen; while the ivory Pequod bore down with outstretched sails like a wild hen after her screaming brood. Stranger emotions no raw recruit feels in his first battle than he who first pulls into the charmed, churned circle of the hunted sperm whale.

The dun cloud-shadows deepening made the white waters telling clear. Our sail drew; with the rising wind we rushed so mad that the lee oars scarce could keep from tearing out. Mist swallowed ship and boats. “Give way,” whispered Starbuck, hauling his sheet; “time to kill a fish ere the squall! There’s white water—close! Spring!”

Cries on either beam told the other boats were fast. Hardly heard, when Starbuck’s lightning whisper: “Stand up!” and Queequeg sprang, iron poised. None of us oarsmen, backs turned to peril, saw the life-and-death thing, but reading the mate’s face we felt the instant. “That’s his hump. There—there—give it to him!” A short rushing; the iron leapt. Then, in one welded commotion—an astern push; a forward shock as on a ledge; sail collapsing; a scalding jet; an earthquake underfoot; and we, half-suffocated, were tossed helter-skelter into the white curdling cream. Squall, whale, and harpoon all blended; the whale, but grazed, escaped.

Swamped, yet nearly whole, the boat floated. We swam her round, crossed oars for thwarts, and tumbled in, sitting knee-deep in sea; the craft seemed a coral boat grown from the bottom. The wind howled; bucklers of waves clashed; the squall roared, forked, and crackled like a white prairie-fire in which, unconsumed, we were burning—immortal in these jaws of death! Vain to hail the others. Night thickened with rack and scud; no Pequod. Baling was forbid by the rising sea; oars were life-preservers now. Starbuck, after many failures, got the lantern lit; set it on a waif-pole and gave it Queequeg—the standard-bearer of our forlorn hope. There he sat, holding up that imbecile candle in the heart of that almighty forlornness; the sign and symbol of a man without faith, hopelessly holding up hope in the midst of despair.

Dawn, and still the mist spread. The empty lantern lay crushed. Suddenly Queequeg sprang, hand to ear. We too heard faint creakings of ropes. Nearer; the fog parted to a huge vague form. Affrighted, we leaped for our lives as the ship loomed right upon us. Our abandoned boat gaped beneath her bows like a chip at a cataract’s base; the vast hull rolled over it; it weltered astern; we swam for it, were dashed against, and at last were taken up and landed aboard. Ere the squall’s close the other boats had cut loose and come in. The ship had given us up, but cruised still, if haply she might light on some token of our perishing—an oar or a lance pole.
