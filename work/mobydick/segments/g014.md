## Chapter 1

A few days after the Frenchman, a most significant event befell the most insignificant of the Pequod’s crew—little Pip, the tambourine boy; bright of nature, over tender-hearted, loving life and its peaceable securities, and therefore sadly blurred by the panic-striking business into which he had unaccountably been entrapped.

Stubb’s after-oarsman having sprained his hand, Pip was put in his place. The first lowering he only trembled; the second, when the struck whale gave its customary rap right under his seat, he leaped overboard, and the slack line, sweeping his chest, entangled him; the whale started; in an instant Pip came foaming up to the chocks, dragged by turns of the strangling line. Tashtego’s knife hovered—“Cut?”—“Damn him, cut!” roared Stubb. The fish was lost, and Pip was saved.

Recovered, the poor little negro was rated and exhorted. Officially and otherwise Stubb ended with a command: “Stick to the boat, Pip, or by the Lord, I won’t pick you up if you jump; mind that. We can’t afford to lose whales by the likes of you; a whale would sell for thirty times what you would, Pip, in Alabama.” So the charity of man is often thrift in disguise.

But we are all in the hands of the Gods; and Pip jumped again. This time he did not breast out the line, and when the whale ran, Pip was left astern, like a hurried traveller’s trunk. A bright, blue day; the spangled calm; the horizon hammered thin as gold-beater’s skin; his ebon head bobbed like a head of cloves. Stubb’s inexorable back was turned on him; the whale was winged; and in three minutes a mile of shoreless ocean widened round Pip’s ringing, miserable horizon. By chance, the ship herself at last rescued him.

From that hour the little negro went about the deck an idiot, so they said. The sea had jeeringly kept his finite body up, but drowned the infinite of his soul. Not drowned entirely—carried down alive to wondrous depths: strange primal shapes gliding before his passive eyes; the miser-merman Wisdom revealing his hoards; the omnipresent coral insects heaving the orbs; he saw God’s foot upon the treadle of the loom, and spoke it; therefore they called him mad. So man’s insanity is heaven’s sense; and, wandering from all mortal reason, he comes at last to that celestial thought which to reason is absurd; and weal or woe, feels then uncompromised, indifferent as his God.

Blame not Stubb too hardly; such things are common in the fishery; and in the sequel it will be seen what like abandonment befell myself.



## Chapter 2

Stubb’s whale, so dearly purchased, was brought to the Pequod’s side; all the cutting and hoisting done, even to baling the Heidelburgh Tun. The sperm cooled and crystallized so that, sitting cross-legged before a great bath of it, I found soft lumps rolling in the liquid part. Our business was to squeeze them back to fluid. A sweet and unctuous duty! After but minutes, my fingers felt like eels, serpentine and spiralising.

Under a blue, tranquil sky; the ship gliding indolently; my hands bathing among those gentle globules lately woven; as they broke to my touch and discharged all their opulence like ripe grapes their wine; as I snuffed that uncontaminated aroma—literally like spring violets—I forgot our horrible oath; in that inexpressible sperm, I washed my hands and my heart of it; I almost credited the Paracelsan superstition that sperm allays the heat of anger; I felt divinely free from all ill-will.

Squeeze! squeeze! squeeze! all the morning long; I squeezed that sperm till I almost melted into it; till a strange sort of insanity came over me; and I unwittingly squeezed my co-laborers’ hands in it, mistaking their hands for the gentle globules. Such abounding, affectionate, friendly feeling did this avocation beget, that I continually squeezed their hands and looked sentimentally up, as much as to say—Oh my dear fellow beings, why should we longer cherish acerbities! Let us squeeze hands all round; nay, let us squeeze ourselves universally into the very milk and sperm of kindness.

Would that I could keep squeezing for ever! For man at last must lower, or shift, his conceit of felicity, placing it in the wife, the heart, the bed, the table, the saddle, the fireside, the country. In visions of the night I saw long rows of angels in paradise, each with his hands in a jar of spermaceti.

Of kindred matters: white-horse (tough, tendinous, yet oily slabs), plum-pudding (mottled, ruby-dotted flesh that tempts the palate), slobgollion (ineffably oozy strings after much squeezing), gurry (right-whalemen’s dark glue), and the whalemen’s nippers (leathern squilgees of tail) all pass through the blubber-room, where, by lantern’s gloom, the pike-and-gaffman and the shoeless spade-man hew the sheets—a scene where toes are scarce among veterans.



## Chapter 3

At a certain juncture of this post-mortemizing, had you strolled forward you had scanned, lying in the lee scuppers, a strange black cone—longer than a Kentuckian is tall, a foot in girth, jet-black as Yojo. An idol its likeness was in old times—such as Queen Maachah’s in Judea, burnt for an abomination at the brook Kedron.

Behold the sailor called the mincer. With two allies he backs the grandissimus, staggers with it, extends it on the forecastle, and cylindrically peels its dark pelt as an African hunter strips a boa. Then he turns the pelt inside out like a pantaloon-leg, stretches it till near doubled in diameter, and hangs it in the rigging to dry. Taken down, he removes a length, cuts two arm-slits at the broad end, and lengthwise slips himself bodily into it. So stands the mincer invested in the full canonicals of his calling; this immemorial cassock alone will adequately protect him at his office.

That office consists in mincing the horse-pieces of blubber for the pots; performed at a wooden horse against the bulwarks, with a tub beneath, whereto the slices fall as fast as the sheets from a rapt orator’s desk. Arrayed in decent black; in a conspicuous pulpit; intent on bible leaves—what a candidate for an archbishopric; what a lad for a Pope, were this mincer!

“Bible leaves! Bible leaves!”—so cry the mates, bidding him cut as thin as may be, that boiling may be sped, the yield increased, and, it may be, the quality refined.



## Chapter 4

Outwardly, an American whaler is marked by her try-works—brick and mortar set upon oak and hemp, as if a brick-kiln were shipped to sea. Between fore and main, upon strengthened timbers, rises the mass, cased in wood, covered by a battened hatch; within, two vast try-pots shine like silver punch-bowls when polished with soapstone and sand. The furnaces’ iron mouths open beneath; a shallow reservoir of water beneath the works keeps the deck from firing; the smoke takes its way astern.

About nine at night, the Pequod’s try-works were first started. Stubb oversaw. First wood, then the whale’s own crisp scraps fed the flames. Like a plethoric burning martyr, the whale supplies his own fuel and burns by his own body; would that he consumed his own smoke! for it is horrible—a Hindoo, pyre-like reek, an argument for the pit.

By midnight all was in full operation; clear of the carcass; sail made; wind freshening; the ocean’s darkness intense; but licked up by fierce flames forking from the sooty flues, emblazoning the rigging like Greek fire. The hatch removed made a broad hearth: there the pagan harpooneers, stokers of the whale-ship, with pronged poles pitched hissing blubber into the scalding pots; the oil leaped to each pitch; the watch lounged by the windlass, their tawny faces, matted beards, barbaric teeth revealed by the capricious glow. The rushing Pequod, freighted with savages, burning a corpse, plunging into blackness, seemed the material counterpart of her monomaniac commander’s soul.

So seemed it to me at the helm; long hours I silently guided the way of this fire-ship. Wrapped in darkness myself, I the better saw others’ redness and madness. Capering fiend-shapes begat kindred visions as drowsiness came. Starting from a brief standing sleep, I felt something fatally wrong. The tiller smote my side; the sails began to shake; yet no compass glimmered before me—only jet gloom and ghastly flashes. Uppermost, that what I stood on rushed from all havens astern. Convulsively I grasped the tiller, crazily conceiving it inverted—till, lo! I had in sleep turned my body, fronting the stern, my back to prow and compass. I faced about in the instant, and saved the ship from flying up into the wind.

Look not too long in the face of the fire, O man! Never dream with thy hand on the helm! Turn not thy back to the compass; accept the first hint of the hitching tiller; believe not the artificial fire, when its redness makes all things ghastly. To-morrow the natural sun—the only true lamp—will show gentler relief.

Nevertheless, the sun hides not Virginia’s Dismal Swamp, nor Rome’s Campagna, nor Sahara, nor the ocean—dark side of the earth. He who hath more joy than sorrow cannot be true—or is undeveloped. With books the same. The truest of men was the Man of Sorrows; the truest of books, Solomon’s; Ecclesiastes is the fine hammered steel of woe. “All is vanity.” ALL.

Give not thyself up, then, to fire; there is a wisdom that is woe; but there is a woe that is madness. And there is a Catskill eagle in some souls, that can alike dive into the blackest gorges, and soar out again into sunny spaces; and even if for ever flying within the gorge, that gorge is in the mountains, and his lowest swoop is still higher than other birds upon the plain.



## Chapter 5

Had you descended from the try-works to the forecastle, you had thought yourself in some illuminated shrine of canonized kings and counsellors. They lay in triangular oaken vaults, each mariner a chiselled muteness; a score of lamps flashing on hooded eyes.

In merchantmen, oil is scarcer than queens’ milk; the sailor dresses, eats, and gropes in the dark. But the whaleman, seeking the food of light, lives in light. He makes his berth an Aladdin’s lamp; and, with entire freedom, fills his handful of lamps—old bottles and vials often—at the copper cooler, as mugs at a vat. He burns the purest oil, unmanufactured, unvitiated—sweet as April grass-butter; he hunts his own light to be sure of its freshness and genuineness.



## Chapter 6

When the last drop was bailed, and spermaceti, oil, and bone had passed the fire like Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, remained the romance of stowing down. While still warm, like hot punch, the oil is received into six-barrel casks; and while the ship pitches at midnight, the enormous casks are slewed, headed over end for end, and perilously scoot across the greasy deck till man-handled and stayed; then, rap, rap go all the hammers round the hoops, for now, ex officio, every sailor is a cooper.

At length, when the last pint is casked and cool, the great hatches are unsealed; the bowels of the ship are thrown open; and down go the casks to their final rest in the sea-dark hold. Hatches replaced and hermetically closed, closet-like, the visible leviathan vanishes again beneath.

Most remarkable is the quick transfiguration. One day the planks stream blood and oil; on the quarter-deck the head lies profanely piled; rusty casks lumber as in a brewery; smoke has besooted bulwarks; the mariners go unctuous; the ship herself seems leviathan; and the din is deafening. A day or two after—save for boats and try-works—you had sworn a neat merchantman. The unmanufactured sperm has a singularly cleansing virtue; the ashes yield a potent lye; hands with buckets and rags make tidiness; soot is brushed; gear cleansed and stowed; the great hatch scrubbed and set over the try-works; every cask out of sight; every tackle coiled; then the crew themselves ablute, shift from top to toe, and issue forth fresh and aglow as bridegrooms new-leaped from Holland.

Now, elated, they pace and prattle of parlors and carpets; propose matted decks; tea by moonlight on the piazza of the forecastle. Hint of oil and bone were audacity—“Away, and bring us napkins!” Yet aloft at the three mast-heads stand three men intent on more whales, that will soil the furniture again. Many a time, after ninety-six hours of uninterrupted labor—rowed wrists swollen on the Line; chains heaved; blubber slashed; sweated and smoked by sun and try-works—they cleanse the ship to a spotless dairy-room; and just as they button clean frocks—“There she blows!”—and away they fly to fight another whale, and go through all again. Oh! my friends, but this is man-killing! Yet this is life.



## Chapter 7

Ahab, pacing between binnacle and mainmast, often paused—now nailing his glance to the compass, now to the riveted gold coin he had set in the mast. One morning he newly studied the strange figures: the equatorial doubloon from Quito—REPUBLICA DEL ECUADOR: QUITO—its three Andean peaks (flame, tower, cock), and the arched zodiac, the sun entering Libra.

“There’s something ever egotistical in mountain-tops and towers,” he murmured. “The firm tower, that is Ahab; the volcano, that is Ahab; the courageous fowl, that, too, is Ahab; all are Ahab; and this round gold is but the image of the rounder globe, which, like a magician’s glass, mirrors back each man’s mysterious self. See—he enters the sign of storms, the equinox! From storm to storm! Born in throes, ’tis fit that man should live in pains and die in pangs! So be it, then!”

Starbuck, leaning by, read otherwise: a dark vale between three mighty peaks, almost the Trinity in faint earthly symbol. In this vale of Death God girds us round; and over all our gloom the sun of Righteousness shines a beacon. “This coin speaks wisely, mildly, truly—but sadly. I will quit it, lest Truth shake me falsely.”

Stubb by the try-works: “Faces nine fathoms long from a piece of gold I’d spend quick on Negro Hill. What’s in this doubloon? Signs and wonders! The zodiac! Books give words and facts; we supply thoughts. Look you, Doubloon—your zodiac is man’s life: Aries begets us; Taurus bumps us first; Gemini, Virtue and Vice; then Cancer drags us back; Leo bites and paws; we escape and hail Virgo—our first love; we marry; then Libra—happiness weighed and found wanting; while we’re sad, Scorpio stings our rear; we pluck the shafts, when Sagittarius showers arrows; stand aside! Capricorn, the battering-ram, tosses us; Aquarius drowns us; Pisces—we sleep. The sun goes through it jollily each year, and so does jolly Stubb. Adieu, Doubloon!”

Flask: “A round thing of gold; whoever raises a certain whale gets it. Worth sixteen dollars—nine hundred and sixty cigars. Here goes Flask aloft to spy ’em out.”

The old Manxman shuffled round the mast, muttering: “In a month and a day, when the sun stands in one of these signs. The horse-shoe opposite—Leo, the roaring and devouring lion. Ship, old ship! my old head shakes to think of thee.”

Queequeg, tattooed like the zodiac itself, compared the coin to his own thigh-bone—found Sagittarius, or some king’s old button; shook his head.

Fedallah came silently, made a sign to the sign, and bowed; a fire-worshipper acknowledging the sun.

Then Pip—poor boy!—with that unearthly idiot face, who had watched them all—came to read: “I look, you look, he looks; we look, ye look, they look.” He said it again—getting it by heart. Then, with sudden cawing rapture: “And I, you, and he; and we, ye, and they, are all bats; and I’m a crow, especially when I stand a’top of this pine tree here. Caw! caw! Where’s the scare-crow? There he stands; two bones stuck into a pair of old trowsers, and two more poked into the sleeves of an old jacket.”

“Here’s the ship’s navel,” he ran on, “and they are all on fire to unscrew it. Unscrew your navel—what then? But if it stays nailed—ugly, too; when aught’s nailed to the mast, things grow desperate. Ha, ha! old Ahab! the White Whale; he’ll nail ye!” Then, wandering to Tolland memories: a pine cut down with a silver ring grown over; so, in the resurrection, men shall fish up this old mast and find a doubloon lodged with bedded oysters for bark. “Oh, the gold! the precious, precious, gold! Hish! hish! God goes ’mong the worlds blackberrying. Cook! ho, cook! and cook us! Jenny! hey, hey, get your hoe-cake done!” And Stubb, over-witty for his own sanity, slipped away from Pip’s too-crazy wit, leaving him muttering about the nailed coin and nailed fates.



## Chapter 8

“Ship, ahoy! Hast seen the White Whale?” Ahab hailed an English whaler bearing down astern—the Samuel Enderby of London. Trumpet to mouth, in his hoisted quarter-boat, ivory leg plain; and in the stranger’s bow sat a burly, good-natured man of sixty, one sleeve empty and streaming.

“Hast seen the White Whale?”—“See you this?” And the stranger captain held up a white arm of sperm-whale bone, ending in a wooden mallet-head.

“Man my boat!” cried Ahab; and in a minute he was alongside. But there was no Pequod contrivance here to hoist a one-legged man aboard. The English officers swung down man-ropes, then bethought them; their captain cried, “Avast! Over with the cutting-tackle!” The curved blubber-hook, clean and dry, was lowered; Ahab slid his solitary thigh into its crook, helped haul on the running part, and was landed on the capstan. Ivory crossed with ivory—“Aye, hearty! let us shake bones together!—an arm and a leg!—an arm that never can shrink; a leg that never can run. Where did’st thou see the White Whale? how long ago?”

“There,” said the Englishman, sighting along his ivory like a telescope. “On the Line, last season.”

“And he took that arm off, did he? and that leg, too?”

“He was the cause. Spin me the yarn.”

“It was my first cruise on the Line; ignorant of the White Whale. We lowered for a pod; my boat fastened to a regular circus-horse. Up breaches a bouncing great whale, with a milky-white head and hump, all crows’ feet and wrinkles—harpoons stuck in near his starboard fin.”

“It was he, it was he!” cried Ahab. “Aye—mine—my irons!—but on!”

“The old great-grandfather snaps at my fast-line to part it; it fouls in his teeth; we pull—bounce—plump upon his hump, instead of the other’s. Seeing what a noble great whale he was, spite his boiling rage, I resolved to capture him. I jumped into my mate Mounttop’s boat, gunwale to gunwale; snatched the first iron and gave it him. Hearts alive!—next instant I was blind as a bat; both eyes out with black foam; his tail up like a marble steeple; down it came like a Lima tower, cutting my boat in two; flukes first, he backed through the wreck. I struck out; seized my harpoon-pole sticking in him; a combing sea dashed me off; the fish darted; the barb of the second iron, towing near, caught me here—bore me down to Hell’s flames—when thank God the barb ripped along the flesh, came out nigh my wrist, and up I floated. Dr. Bunger there will tell you the rest.”

The round-faced surgeon, in faded frock, marlingspike and pill-box in hand, bowed: “A shocking bad wound. We stood our old Sammy off to the northward for cooler airs; I sat up nights; was very severe with him in diet—”

“Oh, very severe!” chimed the patient. “Hot rum-toddies till he couldn’t see to bandage; sent me to bed half seas over. A great watcher is Dr. Bunger—Bunger, you dog, laugh!”

Imperturbable Bunger bowed to Ahab: “Spite my severest endeavors, the wound worsened; more than two feet several inches long—I measured with the lead-line; it grew black; off it came. But I shipped not that ivory arm; it’s against rule—that club-hammer end he had put to knock some one’s brains out, I suppose, as he tried mine once.” He doffed his hat, showing a bowl-like cavity without scar. “The captain will tell how that came.” “No, his mother did—born with it,” said Boomer. “Bunger, when you die, you ought to die in pickle.”

“What became of the White Whale?” cried Ahab.

“After he sounded we saw him not for some time; nor knew then his name till afterwards. Since then crossed his wake twice.”

“But could not fasten?”—“Didn’t want to. Ain’t one limb enough? Moby Dick doesn’t so much bite as swallow.”

“Well then,” interposed Bunger, gravely mathematical, bowing to both, “give him your left arm for bait to get the right. Know, gentlemen, that the whale’s digestive organs are so inscrutably constructed by Providence that it is quite impossible for him to completely digest even a man’s arm; and he knows it too. What you take for malice is awkwardness; he only thinks to terrify by feints; like my juggling patient in Ceylon who made believe swallow jack-knives till one dropped in earnest; a twelvemonth after I gave him an emetic and he heaved it up in small tacks.”

“No, thank ye, Bunger,” said Boomer. “He’s welcome to the arm he has, but not to another. There’s glory and a ship-load of sperm in him; but he’s best let alone. Don’t you think so, Captain?”—glancing at the ivory leg.

“He is. But he will still be hunted. What is best let alone most allures. He’s all a magnet. How long since thou saw’st him? Which way heading?”

“Bless my soul—and curse the foul fiend’s,” cried Bunger, sniffing Ahab like a dog. “This man’s blood—bring the thermometer!—it’s at the boiling point! His pulse makes these planks beat!” He drew a lancet.

“Avast!” roared Ahab, dashing him to the bulwarks. “Man the boat! Which way heading?”

“East, I think,” cried Boomer. “Is your Captain crazy?” he whispered Fedallah. But Fedallah laid a finger to lip, slid to the steering-oar; Ahab swung the cutting-tackle; the Manilla men sprang to oars; and, with his back to the stranger and face flint-set to his own, Ahab stood upright till alongside the Pequod, unheeding the Englishman’s hail.
