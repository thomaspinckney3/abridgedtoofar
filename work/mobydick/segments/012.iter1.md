He rose and, in a mild voice of unassuming authority, ordered the people to condense: “Starboard gangway, there! side away to larboard—larboard gangway to starboard! Midships! midships!” Kneeling in the pulpit’s bows, hands folded across his chest, eyes uplifted and closed, he prayed as if at the bottom of the sea. Then, in prolonged solemn tones, like the continual tolling of a bell in a fog, he read and then burst forth singing, the congregation joining till the storm without seemed but an accompaniment:

“The ribs and terrors in the whale, Arched over me a dismal gloom,
While all God’s sun-lit waves rolled by, And lift me deepening down
to doom.

“I saw the opening maw of hell, With endless pains and sorrows there;
Which none but they that feel can tell— Oh, I was plunging to
despair.

“In black distress, I called my God, When I could scarce believe him
mine, He bowed his ear to my complaints— No more the whale did me
confine.

“With speed he flew to my relief, As on a radiant dolphin borne;
Awful, yet bright, as lightning shone The face of my Deliverer God.

“My song for ever shall record That terrible, that joyful hour; I
give the glory to my God, His all the mercy and the power.”

Turning the leaves, he clinched his text: “Beloved shipmates, clinch the last verse of the first chapter of Jonah—‘And God had prepared a great fish to swallow up Jonah.’”

“Shipmates, this little book—four chapters, four yarns—is one of the smallest strands in the mighty cable; yet what depths it sounds! What is its lesson? Two-stranded: to us all as sinful men; and to me, as a pilot of the living God. As men, it is the story of sin, hard-heartedness, suddenly awakened fears, swift punishment, repentance, prayers, deliverance, and joy. All the things that God would have us do are hard for us to do; and if we obey God, we must disobey ourselves.

“With his sin of disobedience in him, Jonah flouts God by fleeing. He skulks at Joppa, seeks a ship for Tarshish—world-wide away; miserable man! with slouched hat and guilty eye. The mariners mark him; he looks the fugitive—no baggage, no friends with adieux; and, in their gamesome but still serious way, one whispers to the other—‘Jack, he’s robbed a widow;’ or, ‘Joe, do you mark him; he’s a bigamist;’ or, ‘Harry lad, I guess he’s the adulterer that broke jail in old Gomorrah.’ He goes to the cabin. ‘We sail with the next coming tide,’ says the Captain, eyeing him: ‘Soon enough for any honest man that goes a passenger.’ Stab! But Jonah answers, ‘I’ll sail with ye—the passage money how much is that?—I’ll pay now.’ Note it well, shipmates: ‘he paid the fare thereof.’ In this world, sin that pays its way can travel freely; whereas Virtue, if a pauper, is stopped at all frontiers. The Captain rings every coin—‘Not a forger, any way,’—and Jonah is put down.

“In his close state-room a swinging lamp, straight in itself, shows all else awry; so his conscience hangs in him. ‘Straight upwards, so it burns; but the chambers of my soul are all in crookedness!’ Like one who after a night of drunken revelry hies to his bed, still reeling, but with conscience yet pricking him, as the plungings of the Roman race-horse but so much the more strike his steel tags into him; as one who in that miserable plight turns and turns in giddy anguish, praying God for annihilation until the fit be passed; and at last, amid the whirl of woe, he feels a deep stupor steal over him, as over the man who bleeds to death, for conscience is the wound, and there’s naught to staunch it; so, after sore wrestlings in his berth, Jonah’s prodigy of ponderous misery drags him drowning down to sleep. At last, the tide: the ship, the first of smugglers—the contraband is Jonah—glides to sea. A dreadful storm comes on; in all this raging tumult, Jonah sleeps his hideous sleep, till the master cries in his dead ear, ‘What meanest thou, O, sleeper! arise!’

“The sailors cast lots; the lot is Jonah’s. ‘What is thine occupation? whence comest thou? thy country? what people?’ He answers more than asked: ‘I am a Hebrew; I fear the Lord, the God of heaven, who hath made the sea and the dry land!’ Fear him then indeed! He confesses; and when he cries, ‘Take me and cast me forth,’ they mercifully try all else; in vain. With one hand invoking God, with the other they lay hold of Jonah; and behold him taken as an anchor and dropped into the sea. Instantly an oily calmness floats from the east; the sea is still; he goes down in the whirling heart of the commotion into the yawning jaws; the whale shoots-to all his ivory teeth.

“Then Jonah prays out of the fish’s belly. Mark his prayer: not clamorous for pardon; he leaves deliverance to God; spite of all pains, he will still look towards His holy temple. Here is true repentance—grateful for punishment. God speaks to the fish; from the shuddering cold and blackness the whale breeches toward the warm and pleasant sun, and ‘vomited out Jonah upon the dry land.’

“Shipmates,”—here the storm’s howling seemed to add power to him; his chest heaved as with a ground-swell; his tossed arms were warring elements; thunder rolled from his brow—“I place Jonah not for copying in sin, but for copying in repentance.”

He turned the leaves, communed silently; then leaned and bowed with manly humility: “God has laid but one hand upon you; both his hands press upon me. Hear the other, more awful lesson to me, a pilot of the living God. Bidden to sound unwelcome truths in wicked Nineveh, Jonah fled from his mission; but God is everywhere; Tarshish he never reached. God came upon him in the whale; and when the word of the Lord came a second time—Jonah, bruised and beaten—his ears, like two sea-shells, still multitudinously murmuring of the ocean—Jonah did the Almighty’s bidding. And what was that, shipmates? To preach the Truth to the face of Falsehood! That was it!

“This, shipmates, this is that other lesson; and woe to that pilot who slights it. Woe to him whom this world charms from Gospel duty! Woe to him who seeks to pour oil upon the waters when God has brewed them into a gale! Woe to him who seeks to please rather than to appal! Woe to him whose good name is more to him than goodness! Woe to him who, in this world, courts not dishonor! Woe to him who would not be true, even though to be false were salvation! Yea, woe to him who, as the great Pilot Paul has it, while preaching to others is himself a castaway!”

Then joy leaped in his eyes: “But oh! shipmates! on the starboard hand of every woe there is a sure delight; and higher the top of that delight than the bottom of the woe is deep. Delight is to him—a far, far upward and inward delight—who against the proud gods and commodores of this earth, ever stands forth his own inexorable self. Delight is to him whose strong arms yet support him, when the ship of this base treacherous world has gone down beneath him. Delight is to him who gives no quarter in the truth, and kills, burns, and destroys all sin though he pluck it from under Senators’ and Judges’ robes. Delight,—top-gallant delight is to him, who acknowledges no law or lord but the Lord his God, and is only a patriot to heaven. And eternal delight will be his, who, laying him down, can say—O Father! chiefly known to me by Thy rod—mortal or immortal, here I die. I have striven to be Thine more than this world’s, or mine own. Yet this is nothing: I leave eternity to Thee; for what is man that he should live out the lifetime of his God?”

He waved a benediction, covered his face with his hands, and so remained kneeling till all had departed, and he was left alone.
