Ishmael, feeling a damp, drizzly November in his soul, chooses the sea over self-destruction and drifts to New Bedford in search of remedy and work. At the rough Spouter-Inn he meets Queequeg, a tattooed harpooneer from the South Seas; after an awkward, comical night they become sworn brothers. They attend the Whalemen’s Chapel where Father Mapple thunders Jonah’s lesson of obedience and repentance, warning against fleeing a divine summons. From Nantucket they ship aboard the Pequod, a weathered, trophy-studded bark owned by the flinty Quaker partners Peleg and Bildad, who bargain souls as crisply as wages. Ragged Elijah’s ominous hints, Bulkington’s restless return to sea, and dockside whispers of a captain named Ahab stain their departure with prophecy, and on a frigid Christmas morning the Pequod leaves hymn-drenched shore. For days Ahab is only a rumor pacing the quarter-deck until he appears at last—scar-seamed, lightning-branded, and stumping on an ivory leg, a man fused to a single purpose. Beneath him stand the three mates—Starbuck prudent, Stubb jovial, Flask pugnacious—and the harpooneers Queequeg, Tashtego, and Daggoo, a floating democracy with a fatal captain.

In a blazing quarter-deck scene Ahab nails a Spanish ounce of gold to the mast and swears the crew to hunt the White Whale, Moby Dick, the brute that razed him and in his mind embodies a hidden malice behind the world’s mask, which he vows to strike through. Starbuck protests that vengeance on a dumb beast is blasphemy, but blood and superstition boil as the men drink from harpoon sockets and bind themselves to Ahab’s doom. The first lowerings display the sea’s beauty and treachery at once: a squall swamps the boats and nearly drowns Ishmael while Ahab watches, impassive, from the deck. From the ship’s shadow a fourth boat materializes—Ahab’s own—manned by Fedallah and a crew of silent phantoms whose dark presence and riddling glances thicken the voyage’s fatal air. Weeks pass as the Pequod steams through gales and tropic calms, and Ishmael braids cetology with philosophy—cataloging whales, pondering the appalling purity of whiteness, and retelling in Lima the Town-Ho’s mutiny in which Moby Dick destroys a tyrant. Amid meadows of golden brit and the pastoral mowing of Right Whales, Ahab’s vendetta only hardens, and when a vast pale squid heaves up like a formless omen the crew whispers that the White Whale is near. Shipboard life settles into a rhythm of watch and rope, hymn and oath, as Ishmael and Queequeg weave a sword-mat and he imagines necessity the warp, free will the shuttle, and chance the indifferent sword beating the pattern. Gams with other ships yield broken instruments and rumors; the Jeroboam brings Gabriel, a prophet-mad sailor who damns any that would strike the White Whale, hardening rumor into dread.

In the Indian Ocean the perilous craft sharpens into muscle and iron: coils of whale-line that can garrote a man, barbs forged of steel and will, and the red work of cutting-in while sharks riot at the hull. Stubb kills a great fish and presides over a smoky midnight feast as old Fleece scolds the sharks and the deck runs with blubber and hymnlike labor, while Ishmael files omens beside anatomy, comparing skulls and spines between scalpels and sermons. Ahab’s inward weather darkens, and Fedallah whispers prophecies—that two hearses shall pass upon the sea and that hemp alone can kill him—binding the captain tighter to a script he pretends to author. Perth the ruined blacksmith tempers a bespoke harpoon in the pagan blood of the harpooneers while a typhoon crowns the masts with St. Elmo’s blue fire and Ahab toasts the unpitying elements. He smashes his quadrant, calls the heavens mute, and, when the compass needles spin wild, remagnetizes them by hand, trusting henceforth to dead reckoning and an iron will. Castaway Pip leaps from a lowering and comes back mad, a cracked bell whose wild metaphysics Ahab strangely cherishes even as Starbuck weighs the right to stop a monomaniac and, musket in hand, cannot. The carpenter turns whalebone to prostheses and coffin-planks, and the whole ship seems snared in an invisible tow-line Ahab names Fate. The Pequod races and parts from the jubilant Bachelor fat with oil, meets the Delight sewing a dead sailor into canvas, and later refuses the Rachel’s plea to help find a lost boy. When Queequeg sickens he orders a canoe-coffin carved with his tattooed cosmology; he recovers, and the box is rigged as a life-buoy none yet imagine will be needed. Ahab’s eyes burn so long at the masthead they seem nailed there with the doubloon promised to the man who first sights Moby Dick, and storms, sermons, and smithy sparks braid into a single cable of foreboding until at last the cry comes: there she blows, a hump like a snow-hill in motion.

The chase rages over three days as the White Whale breaches and batters, boats are stove and lines snarl, and Fedallah vanishes only to reappear lashed dead to the leviathan’s back, the first hearse fulfilled. On the second day Moby Dick rams the Pequod and swims off in a traveling wake of foam while Starbuck begs for home and Ahab refuses, hoisting himself on a new ivory leg and old vows. On the third day Ahab drives in his iron for hate’s sake, the running hemp bows him like a string and snaps him from the thwart, and the whale wheels to smash the Pequod to kindling. As the ship founders, Tashtego nails the flag to the sinking mast and snags a sky-hawk in the streaming canvas, the second hearse of American wood sinking in a feathered shroud. All collapses, and then the sea rolls on as it rolled five thousand years ago.

Held up by Queequeg’s coffin, now a life-buoy, Ishmael alone survives the vortex of ropes, iron, faith, and fury and floats through a long dirge of sky and foam. The devious-cruising Rachel, still hunting her lost children, finds and rescues another orphan and bears him away from the wreck’s mute circle. In the Pequod’s wake the book’s braided themes tighten: a working world of ropes, iron, and human song pressed into one man’s monomania, while man’s instruments and ideas—quadrants, compasses, sermons, and songs—glitter and fail before the whale’s blind forehead and the ocean’s old indifference, and the only witness left to tell it counts his heartbeats on a borrowed coffin.
