Ishmael, a melancholy wanderer, goes to sea to cure his spleen, befriends the tattooed harpooner Queequeg at the Spouter‑Inn, hears Father Mapple’s sermon on Jonah, and together they sign aboard the Nantucket whaler Pequod.
Owners Peleg and Bildad warn darkly of Captain Ahab, and the prophet‑like Elijah hints at doom as the ship sails with its captain still unseen.
At last the scarred, ivory‑legged Ahab appears, nails a gold doubloon to the mast, and binds the crew with grog and oaths to hunt the White Whale, Moby Dick, who maimed him.
His secret Parsee, Fedallah, and a tigerish stowaway crew slip from the hold as Starbuck the humane mate, with Stubb and Flask, uneasily counterpoise Ahab’s mounting monomania.
Life aboard unspools in gales and calms, mast‑head vigils and whale‑boat descents, interleaved with Ishmael’s meditations on whales, chance, necessity, and the terror of whiteness, as the Pequod gams with the ghostly Albatross and hears the Town‑Ho’s tale of Radney slain by Moby Dick.
The men study the lethal intricacies of the whale‑line and crowded boat as Stubb leads classic chases—“Wet the line!”—to the whale’s bloody flurry and a shark‑torn night feast where old Fleece even preaches to the sharks.
Amid hazards of the crotch and second iron, Ishmael and Queequeg are bound by a “monkey‑rope” while sharks and spades rave about; then the crew cuts in blubber, bales spermaceti—where Queequeg coolly saves Tashtego—and hangs the sperm and right whales’ heads to ponder eyes, ears, brows, and the battering‑ram forehead.
Ahab broods over the Sphinx‑like head as men whisper about Fedallah and fate; they gam with the Jeroboam, whose fanatic Gabriel warns of Moby Dick, race and mock the Jungfrau, and charge gallied herds off Sunda through drugged whales and hairbreadth escapes.
Ishmael codifies “Fast‑Fish and Loose‑Fish,” Stubb extorts ambergris from the French Rose‑Bud, and Pip, twice overboard, returns mad, a cracked seer whom Ahab strangely cherishes.
Under hell‑glowing try‑works the crew “squeezes” spermaceti into a brief brotherhood as Ahab’s will hardens; the blacksmith forges a special harpoon that Ahab tempers in the harpooneers’ blood and brandishes through a typhoon wreathed in corpse‑candles.
Ahab smashes his quadrant, reverses treacherous compasses by magnetizing a new needle, and—shadowed by Fedallah’s prophecies and omens like a hawk stealing his hat—refuses the Rachel’s plea to search for her lost boy and drives for the Line.
Portents multiply—the sea’s golden meadows of brit and the rising of a colossal squid—until at last Moby Dick breaches and Ahab gives chase.
For three days the White Whale smashes boats, snaps lines, reveals the dragged corpse of the Parsee, and batters the Pequod’s bows while the crews grimly re‑rig and return.
On the third day Ahab hurls his last iron; the fouled line snakes round his neck like a strangling bowstring, whipping him from the boat and dragging him to the deep as Moby Dick turns to stove the ship.
The Pequod founders—Tashtego still nailing the flag with a hawk entangled in it—and the sea rolls on, while Ishmael alone survives on Queequeg’s coffin‑turned‑lifebuoy until the Rachel, still searching for her own lost, rescues “another orphan.”
