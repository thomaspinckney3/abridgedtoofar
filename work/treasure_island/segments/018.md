Council of War

There was a great rush of feet across the deck. Slipping outside my barrel, I dived behind the fore-sail, doubled toward the stern, and came out upon the open deck in time to join the rush for the weather bow.

A belt of fog had lifted with the moon. Away to the south-west we saw two low hills about a couple of miles apart, and rising behind one of them a third and higher, its peak still buried in fog—all three sharp and conical. So much I saw, almost in a dream, for I had not yet recovered from my horrid fear. Then I heard Captain Smollett issuing orders. The Hispaniola was laid a couple of points nearer the wind, on a course to clear the island on the east.

"Now, men," said the captain, "has any one of you ever seen that land ahead?"

"I have, sir," said Silver. "I've watered there with a trader I was cook in. Skeleton Island they calls it. Three hills running south'ard—fore, main, and mizzen. The main, with the cloud on it, they usually calls the Spy-glass, by reason of a lookout they kept when they was in the anchorage cleaning."

"I have a chart here," says Captain Smollett. "See if that's the place."

Long John's eyes burned in his head as he took the chart, but by the fresh look of the paper I knew he was doomed to disappointment. This was not the map from Billy Bones's chest, but an accurate copy, complete in all things—except for the red crosses and the written notes. Sharp as must have been his annoyance, Silver had the strength of mind to hide it.

"Yes, sir," said he, "this is the spot, very prettily drawed out. Right you was, sir, to haul your wind and keep the weather of the island."

"Thank you, my man," says Captain Smollett. "You may go."

I was surprised at the coolness with which John avowed his knowledge, and half-frightened when I saw him drawing nearer to me. He did not know I had overheard from the apple barrel, yet I had taken such a horror of his cruelty and duplicity that I could scarce conceal a shudder when he laid his hand upon my arm.

"Ah," says he, "this here is a sweet spot for a lad to get ashore on. You'll bathe, and climb trees, and hunt goats. Why, it makes me young again. When you want to go a bit of exploring, you just ask old John, and he'll put up a snack for you to take along."

And clapping me in the friendliest way upon the shoulder, he hobbled off forward.

Captain Smollett, the squire, and Dr. Livesey were talking on the quarter-deck; anxious as I was to tell them, I durst not interrupt openly. While I was casting about for some excuse, Dr. Livesey called me to his side, as if to fetch his pipe; but as soon as I was near enough to speak unheard, I broke out: "Doctor, let me speak. Get the captain and squire down to the cabin, and then make some pretence to send for me. I have terrible news."

The doctor changed countenance a little, but next moment was master of himself. "Thank you, Jim," said he loudly, "that was all I wanted to know," as if he had asked a question.

He rejoined the others. Soon the captain piped all hands on deck.

"My lads," said Captain Smollett, "this land we have sighted is the place we have been sailing for. Mr. Trelawney, being a very open-handed gentleman, has just asked me a word or two; and as I was able to tell him every man on board has done his duty as I never ask to see better, why, he and I and the doctor are going below to drink your health and luck, and you'll have grog served out to drink ours. I think this handsome. And if you think as I do, you'll give a good sea-cheer for the gentleman that does it."

The cheer followed; but it rang out so full and hearty that I confess I could hardly believe these same men were plotting for our blood.

"One more cheer for Cap'n Smollett," cried Long John, and this also was given with a will.

Soon I was sent for to the cabin. The three sat round the table, a bottle of Spanish wine and some raisins before them, the doctor smoking with his wig on his lap—a sign, I knew, that he was agitated.

"Now, Hawkins," said the squire, "speak up."

I told the whole of Silver's conversation, as short as I could make it. Nobody interrupted, but kept their eyes on my face from first to last.

"Jim," said Dr. Livesey, "take a seat." They made me sit beside them, poured me wine, and all three, each with a bow, drank my health and their service to me, for my luck and courage.

"Now, captain," said the squire, "you were right, and I was wrong. I own myself an ass, and I await your orders."

"No more an ass than I, sir," returned the captain. "I never heard of a crew that meant to mutiny but what showed signs before. But this crew beats me."

"Captain," said the doctor, "that's Silver. A very remarkable man."

"He'd look remarkably well from a yard-arm, sir," returned the captain. "But this don't lead to anything. First point: we must go on, because we can't turn back. If I gave the word to go about, they would rise at once. Second, we have time—at least until this treasure's found. Third, there are faithful hands. It's got to come to blows sooner or later, and what I propose is to come to blows some fine day when they least expect it. We can count on your own home servants, Mr. Trelawney?"

"As upon myself," declared the squire.

"Three; ourselves make seven, counting Hawkins here."

"And to think they're all Englishmen!" broke out the squire. "Sir, I could find it in my heart to blow the ship up."

"Well, gentlemen," said the captain, "the best I can say is not much. We must lay to and keep a bright lookout. It would be pleasanter to come to blows. But there's no help for it till we know our men. Lay to, and whistle for a wind, that's my view."

"Jim here," said the doctor, "can help us more than anyone. The men are not shy with him, and Jim is a noticing lad."

"Hawkins, I put prodigious faith in you," added the squire.

I began to feel pretty desperate, for I felt altogether helpless; and yet, by an odd train of circumstances, it was indeed through me that safety came. Meantime, there were only seven out of the twenty-six on whom we knew we could rely; and out of these seven one was a boy, so the grown men on our side were six to their nineteen.
