Assessment

The newer models retain more original wording, but every completed new chapter is longer than the existing abridgment. Six of seven completed chapter drafts exceed target by more than 20%. Sonnet's Gatsby I is 5,616 words versus 5,891 in the source: only a 4.7% reduction. This is not yet a useful replacement for the existing abridgments.

There are real preservation gains and real regressions. Sonnet restores Daisy's missing exit in Gatsby I; Opus restores the conditional schoolmaster setup in Loomings. But Opus removes the Catherine exception from 'all present' drinking in Gatsby II, Sonnet drops the shaving-lather setup while retaining its callback, and both new Loomings drafts keep the perdition punchline after cutting its religious premise.

Opus at medium effort completed its two remaining requests in 55.72 and 61.83 seconds. On the three chapters completed by both models, recorded usage cost was $0.4763 for Opus versus $0.2106 for Sonnet, with total call times of 117.55 versus 90.09 seconds. Sonnet was cheaper on this sample; the quality tradeoffs do not establish an overall winner.

The original maximum-effort Opus call returned no prose within Forage's 420-second timeout. It was not retried. The remaining Opus calls used medium effort, so this is an adaptive pilot. The timeout could reflect long reasoning or provider/transport latency; it does not establish poor literary quality. Five successful calls cost $0.8695 in recorded usage; failed-call billing is unknown, with a conservative total bound of $3.57.

The likely next improvement is in the pipeline's compression control: the prompt says to hit target, while the specification gives preservation priority over word count. The new models' own notes invoke signature preservation to justify excess length. That is evidence of a prompt-policy tension, not proof of its causal effect. Use deterministic counts, explicit preservation priorities, and source-aware continuity checks before considering a full-book regeneration. No prompt changes or additional paid trials were performed here.

Method and limits

Identical saved source, plan, cut inventory, and actual Forage drafting prompts for both new models. The first Opus call used the configured maximum reasoning effort and hit Forage's 420-second no-text timeout. The remaining two Opus calls used the provider's default medium effort, with the same 128,000-token ceiling. Sonnet uses its default reasoning and the prompt's 32,000-token limit. Moby-Dick's two chapters share one request. Gatsby's two lean drafting requests are independent. This was an adaptive pilot, not a uniform maximum-effort benchmark.

The existing outputs include earlier drafting and repair passes using GPT-5, Opus 4.7 and Sonnet 4.6. New first drafts and historical final outputs differ in prompt version and repair history. This comparison cannot isolate model version as the cause of every difference. No paid judge, retries, fallback models or repair passes were used.

Quality review is source-aware editorial assessment by the coding assistant, with a checklist recorded before generation. Four chapters and at most one completed sample per model do not establish a general ranking. The failed call provides no evidence of Opus's literary quality on Gatsby I. Its final usage was not returned, so its cost is unknown; the conservative upper bound reserves its entire token allowance.

Word counts use whitespace splitting, consistent with the pipeline. Five-gram retention measures how often five consecutive draft words also occur in the source, ignoring case and most punctuation. It is a measure of wording retention, not a factual-accuracy or literary-quality score.
