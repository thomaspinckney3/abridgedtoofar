EDITOR'S PREFACE

Written in 1905 as sequel to Mont-Saint-Michel and Chartres, this volume was privately printed in 1906 for comment. Its plan had been stated: "Any schoolboy could see that man as a force must be measured by motion from a fixed point," the point of 1150–1250—Amiens and Aquinas—as unit; hence "Mont-Saint-Michel and Chartres: a Study of Thirteenth-Century Unity" and "The Education of Henry Adams: a Study of Twentieth-Century Multiplicity."

Chartres was finished in 1904; the Education proved harder. St. Augustine worked from multiplicity to unity, while he must reverse it. Trying to work his historical theory into the close, he issued A Letter to American Teachers (1910); illness in 1912 ended work.

In 1913 the Institute of Architects published Chartres; the Education was widely quoted. Holding it incomplete, he preferred silence; "silence next to good-temper was the mark of sense. After midsummer, 1914, the rule was made absolute."

The Massachusetts Historical Society now prints the Education as printed in 1907, with only Adams’s marginal corrections, to place both volumes within reach of students.

HENRY CABOT LODGE

September, 1918
