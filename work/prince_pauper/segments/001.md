In the ancient city of London, on an autumn day in the sixteenth century, a boy was born to a poor family named Canty, who did not want him; and that same day a child was born to a rich family named Tudor, who did. All England wanted him and went mad for joy; banners waved and pageants marched by day, bonfires blazed and revellers made merry by night. No talk in all England but of the new baby, Edward Tudor, Prince of Wales, who lay lapped in silks and satins, unconscious—and not caring—while lords and ladies tended him. But of Tom Canty, lapped in poor rags, none spoke save the paupers he had come to trouble.
