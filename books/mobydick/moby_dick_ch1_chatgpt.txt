Abridged Chapter 1

Call me Ishmael. Some years ago—never mind how long precisely—having little or no money in my purse, and nothing particular to interest me on shore, I thought I would sail about a little and see the watery part of the world. It is my way of driving off the spleen and regulating the circulation. Whenever I grow grim about the mouth; whenever it is a damp, drizzly November in my soul; whenever I find myself pausing before coffin warehouses, and bringing up the rear of every funeral I meet; and especially when my hypos so prevail that it requires a strong moral principle to keep me from methodically knocking people’s hats off—then I account it high time to get to sea. This is my substitute for pistol and ball. With a philosophical flourish Cato throws himself upon his sword; I quietly take to the ship.

There now is your insular city of Manhattan, belted round by wharves as Indian isles by coral reefs—commerce surrounds it with her surf. Right and left, the streets take you waterward. Posted like silent sentinels, thousands of mortal men stand fixed in ocean reveries. Inlanders all, they must get as nigh the water as possible without falling in. Take almost any path in the country, and ten to one it carries you down to a pool in the stream. Place a man upon the prairie, remove him to the desert, or set before him the painted image of a brook—still he turns instinctively toward the water. Meditation and water are wedded for ever. Why did the old Persians hold the sea holy? Why did the Greeks give it a god? And why did Narcissus drown in gazing upon the fountain? Because in water we behold the ungraspable phantom of life.

I never go to sea as a passenger; for to go as a passenger you must needs have a purse, and a purse is but a rag unless you have something in it. Nor do I go as Commodore, or Captain, or Cook. I abandon the glory and dignity of such offices, and go as a simple sailor, right before the mast, plumb down into the forecastle. True, they order me about, and make me jump from spar to spar like a grasshopper in a May meadow. It touches one’s sense of honor; especially if you come of an old established family. But what of that? Does the archangel think the less of me for obeying orders? Who ain’t a slave? The universal thump is passed round, and all hands must rub each other’s shoulder-blades, and be content.

Besides, they pay sailors, whereas passengers pay. The act of paying is perhaps the most uncomfortable infliction entailed upon us; but being paid—what will compare with it? The urbane activity with which a man receives money is marvellous, considering that we so earnestly believe it the root of all earthly ills, and that no monied man may enter heaven. Ah! how cheerfully we consign ourselves to perdition!

And another thing. In this world, head winds are far more prevalent than winds from astern; so the Commodore on the quarter-deck often gets his atmosphere at second hand from the sailors in the forecastle. He thinks he breathes it first; but not so. In much the same way do the commonalty lead their leaders in many other things, at the same time that the leaders little suspect it.

Wherefore, having repeatedly smelt the sea as a merchant sailor, I now take it into my head to go on a whaling voyage—the invisible police officer of the Fates knows why. Doubtless it formed part of the grand programme of Providence drawn up long ago, a brief interlude between more extensive performances. I take it the bill ran somewhat thus:

“Grand Contested Election for the Presidency of the United States.”
“Whaling Voyage by one Ishmael.”
“Bloody Battle in Afghanistan.”

Yet chief among my motives was the overwhelming idea of the great whale himself. Such a portentous and mysterious monster roused all my curiosity: the wild and distant seas where he rolled his island bulk; the nameless perils that attended him; the thousand remote and savage impressions of the sea. I am tormented with an everlasting itch for things remote. I love to sail forbidden seas, and land on barbarous coasts. Not ignoring what is good, I am quick to perceive a horror, and could still be social with it—would they let me. For in the wild conceits that swayed me to my purpose, there floated into my inmost soul endless processions of whales, and, mid most of them all, one grand hooded phantom, like a snow hill in the air.
